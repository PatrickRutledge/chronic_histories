Teaching objective: Evaluate chronic productive cough with blood-streaked sputum in a 39-year-old poet who has been an invalid for years and has taken a prescribed opioid since adolescence: test for tuberculosis first (three sputum samples for acid-fast smear, nucleic acid amplification and culture, an interferon-gamma release assay, and masking until the smears are negative), then confirm bronchiectasis by CT and work up its cause on the BTS 2019 guideline (blood count, immunoglobulins, Aspergillus serology, sputum bacterial and mycobacterial cultures including nontuberculous mycobacteria), with spirometry, airway clearance, vaccines, an exacerbation plan with a 14-day antibiotic, and clear rules for when hemoptysis needs emergency care; review long-term opioid therapy respectfully on the CDC 2022 guideline (benefits and harms, no forced taper, naloxone, the prescription monitoring program, urine drug testing, and DSM-5-TR criteria, under which tolerance and withdrawal from prescribed use do not count, so physiological dependence is not opioid use disorder; buprenorphine would be offered if it were present), and support the slow reduction she chooses; recognize and treat prolonged grief disorder with grief-focused psychotherapy, measurement-based follow-up and a safety assessment with safety planning; check bone health (vitamin D, DXA) and weight; and protect her writing. Her real illness remains undiagnosed; this record shows one modern possibility, not an answer.
