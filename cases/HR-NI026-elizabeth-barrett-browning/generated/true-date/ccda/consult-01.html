Assessment

  • Bronchiectasis, left lower lobe and lingula (J47.9). The CT confirms cylindrical bronchiectasis confined to the left lower lobe and lingula, which explains the daily sputum, the crackles, the mild obstruction and the streaks of blood from inflamed bronchial walls. The pattern and her history point to damage from a severe infection years ago. Normal immunoglobulins and a negative Aspergillus IgE exclude antibody deficiency and allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis; nothing in her history suggests cystic fibrosis, a ciliary disorder or connective tissue disease, so those tests are not needed now. Haemophilus influenzae is the usual organism. Bronchiectasis Severity Index 3: mild. [S]
  • Tuberculosis, nontuberculous mycobacteria, and other lesions (J47.9). Three negative smears, a negative NAAT and a negative IGRA make tuberculosis very unlikely, and she no longer needs to mask; the cultures will be final in six to eight weeks. Nontuberculous mycobacteria, which favour thin women with bronchiectasis, cannot be excluded until the cultures are final; the tree-in-bud change is more likely from retained mucus, and the diagnosis would need two positive sputum cultures with compatible imaging. The CT shows no sequestration, mass or other lesion, so bronchoscopy is not needed. [S]
  • Morphine and the lungs (Z79.891). At 20 mg a day she shows no sedation or slowed breathing and her oxygen saturation is normal, so the dose is not dangerous to her lungs in itself. But morphine suppresses cough, and in bronchiectasis the cough is how she clears sputum; once airway clearance is established, a slow reduction would be reasonable if she wishes it. There is no pulmonary reason to change the dose urgently, and it should never be stopped abruptly. Codeine and other cough suppressants should be avoided, and naloxone kept at home. [S]
  • Blood in the sputum. Streaking only, from bronchiectasis, with no lesion needing treatment. Bronchial artery embolization is for large or repeated bleeding and is not needed. [S]
