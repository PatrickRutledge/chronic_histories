## Editorial resolutions

- **Recent-death rule.** She died in 1861, so the rule does not apply. Claims are limited to what the fetched sources state. Claims about opium and morphine, about her grief, and about proposed psychiatric explanations are marked sensitive.
- **The nature of her chest illness is not documented as a diagnosis.** In her lifetime her physicians called it an affection of the lungs that was not tubercular consumption (CLM-0011), and later she was told one lung was very slightly affected (CLM-0013). Modern proposals are **R** claims kept apart: hypokalemic periodic paralysis (Buchanan and Weiss 2011; CLM-0020), the conjectures listed by Penn State (CLM-0021), and pulmonary sequestration (Quirarte-Ruvalcaba 2024; CLM-0022). None is placed on the problem list as a historical diagnosis. The brief named tuberculosis and bronchiectasis as possibilities; no fetched source proposes bronchiectasis specifically, so it is not stated as a historical claim.
- **Choosing the modern diagnosis (judgment call).** The modern episode diagnoses non-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis of the left lower lobe and lingula with Haemophilus influenzae, after tuberculosis and nontuberculous mycobacteria are excluded. It fits the presenting pattern (years of productive cough, streaks of blood, breathlessness, winter worsening) and teaches the most: testing for tuberculosis first, the BTS cause work-up, airway clearance and exacerbation care. The CT is written to look for and exclude a sequestration, in answer to the 2024 proposal. The problem (J47.9) is linked to CLM-0005, CLM-0011 and CLM-0022 with placement both_with_link, and its display says the historical illness is disputed. The record does not claim this was her real illness.
- **Opioids: what the evidence supports (judgment call).** The sources show prescribed opium from 1821 (CLM-0004), a prescribed daily dose in 1845 that her medical man said she could not do without (CLM-0013), and her own statement in 1846 that she never increased upon the prescribed quantity and that it should be reduced only slowly (CLM-0015). This supports long-term prescribed use with physiological dependence. Under DSM-5-TR, tolerance and withdrawal on prescribed use do not count toward opioid use disorder, and no fetched source documents taking more than prescribed, craving, failed efforts to cut down, or harm from use; the evidence does not support a diagnosis of opioid use disorder, and none is made. “Opium addiction” appears only as one of several modern conjectures (CLM-0021, R, disputed), and the 2024 overdose suggestion is recorded as R (CLM-0022). The modern problem is Z79.891 (long-term use of an opiate). The modern episode continues the dose without a forced taper, adds naloxone, monitoring-program checks and a urine drug test, and documents a DSM-5-TR assessment with no disorder; buprenorphine is named as the treatment that would be offered if opioid use disorder developed. The slow reductions (20 to 18, 16 and then 14 mg a day) are her own choice, echoing her 1846 wish to “do with less” slowly; that letter is after the index date and appears in no intake document.
- **Morphine dose.** Forty drops of laudanum is about 2 mL of a 1% morphine tincture, about 20 mg of morphine; the modern prescription is morphine sulfate oral solution 2 mg/mL, 5 mL twice daily (20 mg a day). Its start date, March 1, 2019 (about seven years before the index visit), is synthetic. The indication (cough, sleeplessness, palpitations) follows her own account; the CDC 2022 guideline is written for pain, and its principles for patients already on long-term opioids are applied, a judgment call.
- **Grief (judgment call).** Her bereavement and its effect on her are documented (CLM-0007, CLM-0008). The modern episode diagnoses prolonged grief disorder (F43.81) five years after the drowning, with a PHQ-9 of 12, and treats it with grief-focused psychotherapy; this is a synthetic modern diagnosis, not a claim about her real mental health. Because the problem has an F code and the PHQ-9 is 12, a safety assessment is recorded at intake (no ideation, intent or plan), with a safety plan, the 988 Lifeline, the morphine in a locked box and naloxone; safety is reviewed at every later visit.
- **Presenting problem.** The chronic cough with blood-streaked sputum at intake is its own problem (R05.3, chronic cough), resolved at the consultation one week later when J47.9 is recorded. The intake documents show only what was known then: the work-up, not its results.
- **Index age and dates.** The index visit on March 16, 2026 maps to March 16, 1845, when she was 39 and an invalid at 50 Wimpole Street. The synthetic date of birth is March 6, 1987 (shift 181 years). Visits on March 23, April 20, June 15, July 13, August 10 and October 19, 2026 map to the same days of 1845, all before her marriage, while she lived at Wimpole Street. The modern visits are a teaching episode, not a claim about where she was.
- **Dates in the sources.** The breaking of the blood-vessel is dated 1838 by Kenyon and by her own letter of October 1841 (“three years ago”), and 1837 by the Victorian Web timeline; the record uses 1838. The EBB Archive dates the family’s move to 50 Wimpole Street to 1838.
- **Name (judgment call).** In 1845 she was Elizabeth Barrett Barrett (Miss Barrett); the record uses her later name throughout, with identity.name_parts given “Elizabeth” and family “Barrett Browning” (family_first false), because Barrett is a family name, not a middle name, and she used both after her marriage. The notes call her Ms. Barrett Browning. Aliases: “Elizabeth Barrett Barrett” and “Elizabeth Barrett”, which appear in no other record.
- **Sources not used.** The British Library, the Poetry Foundation and Britannica refused automated access, the Browning Guide (browningguide.org) and an Armstrong Browning Library biography page could not be found, and the MDPI article page refused access (its abstract was read through Crossref). Young’s 1989 *BMJ* paper on her illnesses (PMID 2495055), the 1989 *BMJ* letters, Dally’s 1993 paper and the 2010 paper on her diary were not read and are not cited. A riding accident as the cause of the spinal injury is mentioned only as Kenyon reports it.

Boyd, Mrs. Martin, Dr. Chambers, Mr. Jago and the modern authors appear only in claims and sources. No modern decision, statement or health fact is attributed to any of them. - **Synthetic content.** All visits, laboratory values, vital signs, the CT, spirometry, DXA, culture and serology results, survey scores, the medicines and doses, the sputum volumes, her height (157 cm) and weights, her writing routine, the dog, and the health care agent are synthetic. - **Sick visit.** An exacerbation of bronchiectasis in August, chosen because it is the common event in this disease and because it tests the plan: sputum culture first, 14 days of amoxicillin chosen by the earlier culture, more airway clearance, emergency rules for blood, and holding the opioid dose without adding a cough suppressant. It implies no real diagnosis.
- **Preventive visit.** A periodic adult preventive visit at 39 (CPT 99395 with 99497 in plain text). Judgment calls: PCV20 for chronic lung disease; Tdap and the first hepatitis B dose; hepatitis B and C screening; cervical HPV testing because no earlier screening is recorded; HIV already tested with the tuberculosis work-up; mammography due at 40; lipids and diabetes screening not indicated, with reasons; advance care planning limited to her own general preferences, with an unnamed agent.
- **Focused visits.** The four chronic-care follow-ups (one week with the consultation, four weeks, three months, and seven months) are documented as focused visits on the BTS 2019 bronchiectasis guideline with the tuberculosis and NTM guidelines, the CDC 2022 opioid guideline, grief therapy with the CMID depression monograph, and the CMID osteoporosis monograph. One deviation is recorded instead of an invented result: no PHQ-9 was recorded between the three-month and seven-month visits, although the CMID calls for one at least monthly during active treatment; no harm judging by the PHQ-2 of 1 in July, her stable mood in August and the PHQ-9 of 5 at seven months; the psychologist’s scores are now entered and a monthly PHQ-9 is sent through the portal. Spirometry, the Bronchiectasis Severity Index, the yearly mycobacterial culture and the yearly urine drug test are due after the episode ends.
- **Consultation.** The pulmonology referral and the consultation by Ravindra Ellesmere-Park, MD (a fictional consultant), on the day of the one-week visit, are **S**. The consultant answers the five referral questions in line with what the record shows afterwards: masking stopped and cultures followed to a final negative result at eight weeks; bronchiectasis with a normal immune and Aspergillus work-up and no sequestration; airway clearance, vaccines and a 14-day amoxicillin plan, used in August; no macrolide; no pulmonary reason to change morphine urgently, with a slow reduction reasonable if she chooses it, which she did from the four-week visit; and emergency rules for blood, with no bronchial artery treatment.
- **DXA in a premenopausal woman (judgment call).** DXA was done for low weight, vitamin D deficiency, limited weight-bearing and long-term opioid therapy, and read by Z-scores, both within the expected range; no drug treatment was given.
