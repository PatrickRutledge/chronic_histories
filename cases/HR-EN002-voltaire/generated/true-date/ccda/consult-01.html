  • In a letter to the baron de Breteuil dated December 1723, he gave an account of the smallpox from which he was recovering: he fell ill on 4 November at Maisons; the disease declared itself after two days of fever with a slight eruption; the physician Gervasi had him take an emetic eight times and drink two hundred pints of lemonade, he had himself bled a second time on his own authority, and by the 15th he was out of danger. [D]
  • His biographer Jean Orieux, as summarized in a 1970 review in the Revue d'histoire de la pharmacie, describes a lifelong preoccupation with illness and remedies: he suffered from colic, took as many as eight purges and twelve enemas in a month, was persuaded he would die before forty, and really did suffer from his kidneys and bladder.   • He returned to Paris on 10 February 1778, for the first time in 28 years, to direct rehearsals of his tragedy Irène; more than 300 people called on him the day after his arrival. On 30 March he went to the Académie amid acclamations and was crowned in his box at a performance of Irène. His deteriorating health did not permit his return to Ferney. [D]
  • His secretary Jean-Louis Wagnière recorded that on 25 February 1778, at a quarter past noon, while dictating from his bed, he coughed three times and said that he was spitting blood, and that blood then spurted from his mouth and nose. A declaration in his hand of 2 March 1778 states that he had been attacked for four days by a vomiting of blood, and the blood loss continued for several days. [D]

Assessment

  • Gross hematuria with difficult urination (R31.0). The bleeding comes from a solid bladder tumor whose appearance and size, with wall thickening and perivesical stranding on CT, suggest a muscle-invasive cancer; the keratinizing plaques and squamous atypia on cytology raise the possibility of squamous histology. The friable prostatic vessels may add to the bleeding. The upper tracts are clear and the chest CT shows no metastasis. [S]
  • Chronic urinary retention (R33.8). Chronic high-pressure retention from prostatic obstruction, made worse by the anticholinergic sleep aid and possibly by clot and the tumor's bulk; kidney function has recovered with drainage. [S]
  • Benign prostatic hyperplasia with lower urinary tract symptoms (N40.1). PSA 6.4 ng/mL with a gland of about 78 mL gives a PSA density of 0.08, low, and the gland is smooth. Retention, catheterization, and bleeding all raise the PSA. At 83, with a bladder tumor that will determine his treatment and outlook, finding a prostate cancer now would not change management, so I do not advise prostate MRI or biopsy. [S]
