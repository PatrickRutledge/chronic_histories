Assessment

  • Pernicious anemia with B12 deficiency neuropathy (D51.0). Established. Very low B12 with raised MMA confirms deficiency, and intrinsic factor antibodies are highly specific for pernicious anemia. The mild leukopenia and thrombocytopenia, raised LDH, and raised bilirubin come from ineffective red cell production in the marrow. The reticulocyte rise to 6.8 percent at one week, with counts already climbing, confirms the diagnosis. A bone marrow examination is not needed unless the counts fail to normalize by eight weeks. The neurologic signs show dorsal column involvement, so treatment must be by injection and must not lapse. [S]
  • Autoimmune gastritis and gastric risk. Pernicious anemia is the late stage of autoimmune gastritis, which raises the risk of type 1 gastric neuroendocrine tumors and gastric adenocarcinoma. She has never had an endoscopy. [S]
  • Associated conditions. Autoimmune thyroid disease is common with autoimmune gastritis; her TSH is normal. Iron deficiency often appears as red cell production recovers, because absorption of dietary iron is reduced without gastric acid. [S]

Data reviewed: The presenting blood count, reticulocytes, B12, folate, MMA, intrinsic factor antibody, LDH, bilirubin, iron studies, TSH, and kidney function; today's blood count, reticulocyte count of 6.8 percent, and potassium of 3.7 mmol/L. [S]

Plan of Treatment

  • Continue cyanocobalamin 1000 micrograms intramuscularly: weekly for four weeks after the daily week, then monthly for life. Oral B12 is not advised for her while neurologic signs are present, and injections should never be stopped. [S]
