# Teaching Note — Annie Oakley pilot

> **SYNTHETIC RECORD, NOT A REAL MEDICAL RECORD**

## Why this case matters

This case shows how historical evidence can support a transparent fictional modern episode without presenting interpretation or generated content as documented fact.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish documented (**D**), retrospective (**R**), and synthetic (**S**) content.
2. Keep historical and active problems distinct.
3. Connect measurable goals and interventions to guidelines.
4. Preserve strengths, function, identity, and agency.

## Destigmatization message

A health condition is one part of a person’s life and does not erase achievement, relationships, purpose, or agency.

## Discussion questions

- Which facts support concern without establishing a modern diagnosis?
- Which entries belong in active problems, historical problems, or both?
- What additional evidence would change an evidence grade?
- How should an EHR distinguish synthetic and historical content?

## Evidence-grade summary

- **D:** Annie Oakley was born Phoebe Ann Moses (also spelled Mosey) on August 13, 1860, in Darke County, Ohio, to Jacob Moses and Susan Wise Moses. Her father died when she was six; two sources give pneumonia as the cause, and the Garst Museum says he died from exposure to winter weather. Her mother was left to support a large family. She became a celebrated exhibition sharpshooter who toured with a Wild West show in the United States and Europe, and in later years she gave exhibitions and taught women to shoot. On October 29, 1901, the show's train collided head-on with another train in North Carolina; Oakley's back was injured, and the accident ended her touring with the show. Late in 1922 she was severely injured in an automobile accident in Florida, reported as fractures of the hip and ankle, after which she had to wear a brace on her leg; her recovery took more than a year. Her health declined in the years after the accident, and in 1925 she moved back to Darke County, Ohio. Oakley died on November 3, 1926, in Greenville, Ohio, aged 66; the Garst Museum and HistoryNet give pernicious anemia as the cause of death.
- **R:** None recorded.
- **S:** Shifted identity, fictional care team, modern encounters, results, plans, and outcomes.
