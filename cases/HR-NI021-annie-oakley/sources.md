## Editorial resolutions

- **Index age.** Oakley died at 66. An index at 65 or 66 would put the true-frame visits in 1926, during her final illness and within weeks of her death. The modern episode presents her at 64. The synthetic date of birth is August 13, 1961, and the date shift is 101 years. The index visit is February 9, 2026 (true frame February 9, 1925) and the last visit is July 27, 2026 (true frame July 27, 1925). All visits come before her birthday, so she is 64 throughout and the preventive visit is the adult type. The visits fall in the year she moved back to Ohio, more than a year before her death, and do not contradict documented events.
- **Modern diagnosis.** Pernicious anemia is the cause of death given by the Garst Museum and HistoryNet (**D**). How it was diagnosed in 1926 is not recorded. The synthetic episode diagnoses it from B12, MMA, and intrinsic factor antibodies, with early neurologic involvement. Treatment brings remission. The real 1926 outcome remains only in the historical timeline.
- **Injuries.** The 1901 train collision and her back injury are documented. The 1922 automobile accident and her long recovery are documented in two sources. The hip and ankle fractures and the leg brace come from HistoryNet alone, so that claim is marked probable. Reports that she was temporarily paralyzed after the 1901 wreck and had several spinal operations appeared in search summaries but were not confirmed in a fetched source, and they are not used. The synthetic chronic low back pain is linked to both injuries, with no claim about its real nature.
- **Gastric cancer and neuroendocrine tumor risk.** The AGA 2021 update advises endoscopy with topographic biopsies for new pernicious anemia, to rule out gastric neoplasia including neuroendocrine tumors. The synthetic endoscopy finds advanced atrophic gastritis with intestinal metaplasia and ECL-cell hyperplasia. It finds no neuroendocrine tumor, dysplasia, or cancer, and the plan is surveillance in about three years. No tumor was added, so that the record does not insinuate an undocumented real diagnosis.
- **Not used.** A suggestion that lead from ammunition caused her anemia appeared only in a genealogy forum. It is not used, and no blood lead test was added. A death date of November 2 on a records site conflicts with the museum sources and is not used. Her husband is not named and no health facts about him are recorded. The synthetic social history says only that she is married.
- **Family history.** Her father's death of pneumonia after winter exposure is recorded as reported. Her mother's health is not recorded in the cited sources.
- **Sick and preventive visits.** Two visits were added: an unscheduled sick visit on May 26, 2026 (true frame May 26, 1925) for acute uncomplicated cystitis, and an adult preventive visit on July 27, 2026 (true frame July 27, 1925). Both are synthetic. The cystitis does not echo a documented illness. It was chosen because its treatment interacts with the case: a fluoroquinolone is avoided because of her neuropathy and her iron tablets. The preventive visit adds a firearm safe-storage check that fits her occupation. Its advance care planning names no real person as her health care agent.
- **Focused visits.** The three chronic-care follow-ups (one week, eight weeks, and five months) are documented as focused visits on the CMID anemia monograph, the AGA atrophic gastritis update, and the ACP back pain guideline. Each lists every item the protocols call for at that point, with its interval and status. One deviation is recorded: the blood count about four weeks after starting oral iron was not drawn. The five-month note records it as missed, with the good response that day and the corrective action.
- **Consultation.** The hematology consultation is synthetic and takes place at the one-week visit. Its recommendations (injections without lapse, eight-week blood count and MMA, no repeat B12, iron studies, endoscopy with surveillance by findings, yearly TSH, and avoiding nitrous oxide) agree with what happened afterwards.
- Oakley died in 1926, so the recent-death review rule does not apply. Ordinary clinical, provenance, and safe-messaging reviews remain required.
