Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Index age and dates. The episode presents him at 36, in the spring after his October 1964 admission for exhaustion and after the Selma to Montgomery march. The synthetic date of birth is January 15, 1990, so the shift is 61 years. The index visit is April 7, 2026 (true frame April 7, 1965) and the last visit September 15, 2026 (true frame September 15, 1965). His day-to-day whereabouts in 1965 were not verified; the dates avoid the documented events in the King Institute chronology (Selma in March), and the episode places him in the Atlanta clinic between journeys. A late-1964 index was not possible because every visit must fall on or before November 1, 2026.
  2. Name. The display name "Martin Luther King Jr." would split wrongly at the last space, so `identity.name_parts` gives the given names "Martin" and "Luther" and the family name "King." The schema has no suffix field (name_parts allows only given, family and family_first), so "Jr." is not carried in the coded given and family names; it stays in the display name, which the FHIR name text and every note use. The record HR-ML014, checked for comparison, carries no suffix in its display name. The owner may wish to add a suffix field. Aliases are "Michael King," his name at birth, and "Martin Luther King"; a whole-word search found neither in another record except HR-ML035, which lists this record in its historical mentions. The notes call him "Dr. King," after his doctorate.
  3. Naming other figures. The reformer Martin Luther, his namesake, is named in CLM-0002, and `identity.historical_mentions` lists HR-RE004. No other figure with a record is named.
  4. Living people. His children are never named; the record says only that he had four young children, and that a daughter was unhurt in the 1956 bombing, as the chronology says. Coretta Scott King appears only in documented claims (the marriage and the telephone call about the prize); no statement, decision or health fact of any relative is used in the modern episode. The driver quoted by NPR, the psychologist and the historians are cited as sources; no other living person is named, and the driver's remark about another family member is left out. The assailant of 1958 is not named in the claims, although the King Institute entry cited carries her name in its title.
  5. Childhood self-injury (correction). The roster's "suicide attempt at 12" overstates the sources. The editors of his papers record that, grieving his grandmother and remorseful, he reacted "impulsively" with an act of self-injury at home (quoted in source 5), and that neither he nor his father described it as a suicide attempt. The claim (CLM-0003) records this once, marked sensitive, as an impulsive act of self-injury; for safe messaging the method is quoted only in the source list of this dossier, not in the claim, these resolutions or the notes; the modern record calls it an impulsive act of self-injury in grief and weighs it in the safety assessment without asserting suicidal intent.
  6. Exhaustion admission (correction). The brief's "exhaustion and a viral infection" is recorded as exhaustion and a respiratory infection, as the Atlanta Journal-Constitution reported; the King Institute says only that he checked in for a rest.
  7. Depression (R). Garrow's account of "morbid depression," quoted in TIME, and Pike's 2024 summary are graded R, probable, and marked sensitive; Pike's statement that people close to him tried to arrange care shortly before his death, which he refused, is a separate later claim (CLM-0017, R, possible) kept out of the intake documents; no historical diagnosis is adopted. TIME's remark about who disputes Garrow's claim is paraphrased as "others have disputed" so that no statement is attributed to living relatives. Branch's trilogy was not consulted directly.
  8. Diagnosis chosen. DSM-5-TR major depressive disorder, single episode, moderate (F32.1), with Z65.8 and Z56.6 for threat, surveillance and work strain, rather than an adjustment disorder, because the synthetic presentation meets the criteria for major depression, which excludes an adjustment disorder; single episode, because no earlier episode is documented before the index date. Insomnia disorder (G47.00) is coded alongside. PTSD is screened (PC-PTSD-5 2, then 1) and not diagnosed: his vigilance is treated as proportionate to a real and continuing threat. These are clinical judgments.
  9. Safety. The PHQ-9 of 17 (item 9 scored 0) requires a safety assessment. It records no suicidal ideation, separates his calm expectation of being killed from a wish to die, and weighs the childhood act, the anonymous letter (CLM-0009, sensitive, not quoted) and the depression. "No firearm in the home, by his report" is synthetic. The 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline is in the plan and safety is reviewed at every later visit.
  10. Equity and privacy. Racism, segregation, jailing and threat are recorded as causes of stress, not personal failings. Because of the documented surveillance and his documented fear that depression could be used against him, the plan restricts the chart, limits documentation, offers a therapist of his choosing (including a Black clinician) and uses secure video. His faith is treated as a strength in therapy.
  11. Smoking. The NPR account is graded D, probable, and marked sensitive. A pack a day since his twenties is synthetic. Varenicline was chosen by shared decision under the USPSTF 2021 recommendation and the EAGLES trial, started three weeks after sertraline, with a second twelve-week course after a lapse.
  12. Cardiovascular risk. Stage 1 hypertension is confirmed by home readings and treated with lifestyle changes at low estimated risk; the PREVENT equations are named for the estimate because the Pooled Cohort Equations do not apply under 40, and no numeric risk is stated. LDL 142 then 127 mg/dL does not call for a statin. The autopsy report of an aged heart (CLM-0015) is secondhand, graded R, possible, and history only; it is not used to justify any modern finding. The old thoracotomy scar is a documented anchor; its occasional aching is synthetic.
  13. Recent-death rule. He died in 1968, so the rule for deaths after 1990 does not apply. The assassination is recorded as history only, with no foreshadowing in the modern episode.
  14. Sick and preventive visits. A periodic adult preventive visit (age 36) on May 12, 2026 (true frame May 12, 1965), at which varenicline started, and a same-day sick visit on August 4, 2026 (true frame August 4, 1965) were added. The sick visit is acute bronchitis in a recent quitter, chosen because it tests his abstinence, aggravates the scar and teaches no antibiotic, no chest X-ray with normal vital signs, and no dextromethorphan with sertraline; it is not modeled on a documented illness.
  15. Focused visits. The four chronic-care follow-ups (two, eight, fourteen and twenty-three weeks) are documented as focused visits on the CMID depression monograph with the APA 2019 and VA/DoD guidelines (PHQ-9, MDQ, GAD-7, suicide-risk reassessment, SSRI tolerability, adherence, psychiatry follow-up), the ACP and AASM insomnia guidance (Insomnia Severity Index and sleep diary in the notes, STOP-Bang), the VA/DoD PTSD guideline (PC-PTSD-5 at intake and three months), the USPSTF tobacco recommendation with EAGLES (smoking status, varenicline review), and the CMID hypertension and hyperlipidemia monographs with the ACC/AHA guidelines (home readings, urine albumin, fundi, ECG and baseline laboratory tests, risk estimate, lipids). One protocol deviation is recorded: the telephone tolerability check due two weeks after sertraline started did not reach him while he travelled, and his tolerability was checked a week later at the preventive visit, without harm; the corrective action is recorded. The Insomnia Severity Index, STOP-Bang and home blood pressure averages are in the notes only, not stored as observations.
  16. Consultation. The psychiatry consultation by the fictional Dr. Lindqvist-Bankole on the second visit is synthetic. Its recommendations (CBT with CBT for insomnia, sertraline 25 then 50 mg, no hypnotic, safety review at every visit, repeat PTSD screening, varenicline after sertraline, a work plan, privacy measures and video follow-up) are those the later visits carry out.
