Social, functional, and mental status

Social history

  • S Baptist minister and the president of a national civil rights organization; travels most weeks, speaks several times a day, and works late into the night
  • S Married, with four young children; lives in Atlanta; his faith and his congregation are central to his life
  • S Lives under sustained threat: his home was bombed nine years ago, he was stabbed seven years ago, he receives threats by telephone and mail, and he has reason to believe his calls are monitored
  • S Tobacco: about a pack of cigarettes a day since his twenties, kept out of public view and away from his children; has never tried to quit with help. Alcohol: occasional, AUDIT-C 2. No other drugs
  • S Racism and segregation have shaped his daily life, his work and his family's safety; he has been jailed repeatedly for nonviolent protest

Disposition
No suicidal ideation, intent, plan or past suicide attempt. He expects that he may be killed and has accepted the risk, which is a realistic appraisal, not a wish to die.
