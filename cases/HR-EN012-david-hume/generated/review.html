Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Birth date. Hume gave his birth as April 26, 1711, Old Style. Scotland then used the Julian calendar, so the preferred date is the Gregorian equivalent, May 7, 1711, and the Old Style date is kept as an alternative. The synthetic date of birth, May 7, 1962, keeps the Gregorian month and day; the date shift is 251 years.
  2. Index age. Hume died at 65, and his bowel disorder began in spring 1775, when he was 63 or 64. The modern episode presents him at 64, at the documented onset, so that a full diagnostic, treatment, and early survivorship course can unfold before his death. The index visit is May 11, 2026 (true frame May 11, 1775) and the last visit is October 28, 2026 (true frame October 28, 1775). All visits fall in 1775 in the true frame, before his journey to London and Bath in April 1776 and his death in August 1776, and so do not contradict documented events.
  3. Modern diagnosis. His illness is documented (D) as a bowel disorder with diarrhoea, a hemorrhage his physician inferred, wasting, and a contested diagnosis in his lifetime. No examination was made after death. Colon cancer is a retrospective interpretation (R, possible). The synthetic episode gives him a biopsy-proven, curatively resected stage IIIB sigmoid cancer. The problem is linked to both the documented illness and the R claim, and its display and provenance say the historical diagnosis remains a retrospective interpretation. The modern diagnosis is marked confirmed because a biopsy confirms it within the synthetic episode.
  4. Liver spread. Spread to the liver is sometimes suggested in secondary accounts, but no fetched source confirmed it, so it is not asserted. The synthetic staging CT includes an indeterminate liver lesion that MRI shows to be a hemangioma. This is a teaching point on characterizing small liver lesions. It does not confirm liver spread.
  5. Equanimity. His documented composure is recorded only in claims, with the exact words from My Own Life that were checked against the fetched text. No new words are attributed to him. Synthetic notes describe him as calm and wanting plain facts.
  6. Family history. The father's cause of death is not recorded. The mother's illness is recorded only as a physician's letter reports Hume's own statement. The synthetic notes use that report as uncertain family history to justify offering genetic counseling. No germline result is recorded.
  7. Youthful illness. The 1729 to 1734 illness is recorded in past medical history with its contemporary label and is flagged sensitive. No modern psychiatric diagnosis is inferred, and it plays no part in the synthetic episode.
  8. Not used. The 1988 article on his last illness could not be read (PubMed lists no abstract). Accounts of his last days by named friends are not used, so that no statements are attributed to real people. His physicians are not named in claims.
  9. Sick and preventive visits. Two visits were added: an unscheduled sick visit on September 8, 2026 (true frame September 8, 1775) for a viral upper respiratory infection during the third CAPOX cycle, and an adult preventive visit on October 28, 2026 (true frame October 28, 1775). Both are synthetic. The cold does not echo a documented illness. It was chosen to teach the same-day blood count and fever rules for a patient on chemotherapy. The preventive visit is the adult type because he is 64. It adds a synthetic positive AUDIT-C (4) with brief counseling, based on his synthetic wine intake and not on any documented drinking. Its advance care planning names no real person as his health care agent.
  10. Focused visits. The three chronic-care follow-ups (three weeks, nine weeks, and about five months after presentation) are documented as focused visits on the CMID colorectal cancer and anemia monographs and the NCCN colon guideline. Each lists every item the protocol calls for at that point, with its interval and status. One deviation is recorded: the distress and depression screen was not done at the diagnosis visit. The postoperative visit records it as missed, with a negative screen that day and the corrective action. The DPYD result before cycle 1 is reported in prose as normal. It is not an observation, because the oncology team ordered and resulted it.
  11. Consultation. The medical oncology consultation is synthetic and takes place at the second visit, after colonoscopy and CT and before surgery. Its recommendations (liver MRI, surgery first, intravenous iron, three months of CAPOX for low-risk stage III disease, DPYD genotyping, genetic counseling, and the surveillance schedule) agree with what happened afterwards. The genetic counseling appointment is still pending at the end of the episode.
  12. Hume died in 1776, so the recent-death review rule does not apply. Ordinary clinical, provenance, and safe-messaging reviews remain required.
