Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. The historical diagnosis is not settled. Her chronic intestinal illness is described in reference works and essays as intestinal tuberculosis, and several give tuberculosis as the cause of death (sources 2, 3, 4, 6); later writers call it Crohn disease (sources 4, 5, 6, 7), a disease not described as a distinct entity until long after her death. No peer-reviewed article proposing either diagnosis was found (a PubMed search returned none), and no test from her lifetime is reported. Both are graded R with certainty disputed. The modern problem is linked to the documented early-1915 symptoms (D) and to both interpretations (R), with placement both_with_link. It is marked confirmed only because endoscopy, histology, imaging, and negative tuberculosis tests confirm it within the synthetic episode; this does not assert that she had Crohn disease. The synthetic workup deliberately excludes intestinal tuberculosis, the conflicting historical diagnosis, before any immunosuppression.
  2. Childhood pneumonia and immunity. Pneumonia at about two (1895) and frail health afterwards are documented (D). The usual account that it weakened her immune system is a biographical interpretation, graded R possible. One fetched source instead attributes her low immunity to inflammatory bowel disease.
  3. Names and places. The national library authority record gives the civil-register names Marie-Juliette Olga and death at Mézy-sur-Seine. One reference biography gives "Juliette Marie Olga" and death in Paris, and another gives "Julie-Marie Olga" and "the Paris area."   6. Choice of therapy. Decided: risankizumab, an interleukin-23 inhibitor, as early advanced therapy for moderate to severe ileocolonic disease (Montreal A2 L3 B1, SES-CD 14), with a short prednisone bridge. The alternatives (infliximab with azathioprine, adalimumab, ustekinumab, vedolizumab) were offered in the consultation. The reasons recorded: efficacy in ileocolonic disease, no need for an immunomodulator, a low rate of serious infection after tuberculosis had been excluded, and home injection that suits her work. The trade-off, recorded as hers to accept, is more limited pregnancy data than for anti-TNF drugs; the pregnancy counseling says the options will be reviewed before any planned pregnancy. The AGA 2021 guideline predates risankizumab's approval for Crohn disease; the ACG 2025 and ECCO 2024 guidelines and the label cover it.
  7. Tuberculosis exclusion. Negative interferon-gamma release assay, normal chest X-ray, small non-caseating granulomas, negative acid-fast stain and TB PCR, and a mycobacterial culture with no growth at five weeks and negative at six. The prednisone bridge and risankizumab began after the first results and before the final culture; the consultation records this as reasonable with the culture to be reviewed, and it was.
