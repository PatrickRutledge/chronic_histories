Assessment

  • Crohn disease of the terminal ileum and colon, Montreal A2 L3 B1, moderate to severe (K50.80). Long ileal ulcers, skip lesions, a spared rectum, small non-caseating granulomas, and the MR findings confirm Crohn disease. Intestinal tuberculosis can look the same; against it here are a negative interferon-gamma release assay, a normal chest X-ray, no caseating or confluent granulomas, no necrotic nodes, and negative acid-fast stain and TB PCR. That is enough to start treatment, but the mycobacterial culture must be checked when final. Diagnosis before 30, extensive deep ulcers, and weight loss predict a disabling course, so she should start an advanced therapy now. [S]
  • Choice of therapy. Options discussed with her: infliximab with azathioprine, adalimumab, ustekinumab, risankizumab, and vedolizumab. She chose risankizumab: effective for induction and maintenance in ileocolonic disease, given as three infusions and then a home injection every eight weeks, without an added immunomodulator and with a low rate of serious infection. The trade-off she accepted is that data in pregnancy are more limited than for anti-TNF drugs. A short prednisone bridge will control symptoms until risankizumab acts. [S]
  • Screening before immunosuppression. Tuberculosis screen negative. Hepatitis B surface antigen and core antibody negative, but surface antibody below 10 after an infant series: a booster is advised. Immune to measles and varicella, so no live vaccine is needed and treatment need not wait. [S]
  • Anemia, nutrition, and bone. Iron deficiency from blood loss and inflammation is best treated intravenously. Moderate malnutrition needs dietitian support. Vitamin D deficiency, low weight, and a steroid course call for vitamin D, calcium during the steroid, and a baseline DXA. [S]
  • Targets, fertility, and pregnancy. The targets are clinical response early, steroid-free remission and a normal CRP by about week 12, a normal fecal calprotectin by about six months, and endoscopic healing at six to twelve months. Crohn disease in remission does not reduce fertility; the main risk to a pregnancy is active disease, so a pregnancy should be planned in remission. [S]
