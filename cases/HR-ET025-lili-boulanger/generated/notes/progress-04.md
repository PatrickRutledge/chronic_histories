## Assessment and plan

1. **Acute norovirus gastroenteritis** (A08.11). Sudden vomiting then watery diarrhea, with ill contacts, is typical of a viral infection rather than a Crohn flare, which builds over days with her usual right-sided pain and sometimes blood. Stool Clostridioides difficile testing is negative and the gastrointestinal PCR panel is positive for norovirus. CRP 11 mg/L, a modest rise that fits the infection; potassium 3.4 mmol/L and creatinine 0.9 mg/dL, mild dehydration. Liver tests normal. **S**
   - Oral rehydration solution in small, frequent amounts; she drank 500 mL in clinic without vomiting, so intravenous fluids were not needed.
   - No antibiotics or antidiarrheal drugs; acetaminophen for aches.
   - Hand washing and bleach-based cleaning, since norovirus spreads easily; stay away from rehearsals until 48 hours after symptoms stop.
   - Return the same day for blood in the stool, inability to keep fluids down, fever of 38.5 °C or more, or right lower quadrant pain.
2. **Crohn disease on risankizumab** (K50.80). No sign of a flare. The next dose, the first maintenance injection in two weeks, can be given on time if she has recovered. **S**
   - Continue the plan; calprotectin will be measured after recovery rather than now, because infection raises it.
