Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Lili Boulanger was born Marie-Juliette Olga Boulanger on August 21, 1893, in the 9th arrondissement of Paris. [D]
  • She was a composer, the sister of the composer and teacher Nadia Boulanger (1887–1979). [D]
  • At about two years old, in 1895, she had pneumonia, described as bronchial pneumonia; her health was frail from a very young age and she remained sickly for the rest of her life. [D]
  • The usual account is that the childhood pneumonia left her immune system weakened, so that she never fully recovered. [R]
  • In 1913, at 19, she became the first woman to win the Premier Grand Prix de Rome for musical composition, with the cantata Faust et Hélène; she had been the only woman among the five finalists that May. [D]
  • She went to the Villa Medici in Rome in 1914. The outbreak of war made her leave Rome for Nice, where she composed the song cycle Clairières dans le ciel; she stayed in Rome again in 1916 and then returned to France. [D]
  • Early in 1915, living in Paris, she began to have headaches, loss of appetite, and fever, followed by symptoms resembling colitis and appendicitis; she spent much of her time in bed but kept composing. [D]
