Assessment

  • Primary adrenal insufficiency (Addison's disease) (E27.1). Confirmed. Cortisol does not rise after cosyntropin (peak 3.9 ug/dL, against a threshold of 18), and the ACTH of 548 pg/mL shows the failure is in the adrenal glands rather than the pituitary. The high renin with low aldosterone shows mineralocorticoid deficiency too, which accounts for the low sodium, high potassium, salt craving and postural drop. The pigmentation, the raised eosinophils, the mild anemia and the back ache all fit. Treatment should start today without waiting for the cause. [S]
  • Cause and differential (E27.1). In a woman of 40 in this country autoimmune adrenalitis is by far the commonest cause, and 21-hydroxylase antibodies will show it in most cases. If they are negative, the adrenal glands should be imaged by CT to look for enlargement or calcification, and tuberculosis tested for, because tuberculosis remains the main infectious cause worldwide. Lymphoma can rarely infiltrate both adrenals, and her weight loss and evening feverishness deserve to be taken seriously; but with no nodes or enlarged spleen, a normal LDH and blood count apart from the eosinophils, and a normal chest X-ray, it is unlikely. Low-grade feverishness and weight loss are common in untreated adrenal insufficiency and should settle on replacement; if they do not, or if nodes appear, CT of the chest, abdomen and pelvis is needed. [S]
  • Mild normocytic anemia (D64.9). With normal iron, vitamin B12 and folate, this is the anemia of untreated adrenal insufficiency and should recover on replacement. [S]
