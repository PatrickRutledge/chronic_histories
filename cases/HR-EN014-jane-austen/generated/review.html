Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Recent-death rule. Austen died in 1817, so the rule does not apply. Every historical claim is limited to what the fetched sources state, and none is marked sensitive. Her death is recorded in one sentence (CLM-0013) and not used in the modern episode.
  2. The cause of her illness is not documented. No diagnosis was recorded in her lifetime, and Cassandra destroyed letters with medical detail (CLM-0016). The proposed causes are recorded as R claims and kept apart: Addison’s disease (Cope 1964, and the Jane Austen Society’s “possibly”; CLM-0014, CLM-0015), Hodgkin’s lymphoma (Upfal 2005; CLM-0016), disseminated bovine tuberculosis (White 2009; CLM-0017), systemic lupus erythematosus (Sanders and Graham 2021; CLM-0018), and arsenic, cancer and tuberculosis as reported by the British Library (CLM-0019). Each is graded disputed or possible, and none is placed on the problem list as a historical diagnosis.
  3. Choosing the modern diagnosis (judgment call). The modern episode diagnoses primary adrenal insufficiency, as the brief suggested, because the presenting pattern the letters describe (tiredness, back pain, bilious attacks with nausea, fevers, and a changed colour) is a classic presentation of it and teaches the most: recognizing it from clues such as low sodium, high potassium, raised eosinophils and pigmentation, confirming it, replacing two hormones, and preventing crisis. Upfal’s and White’s objections are respected in the consultation: lymphoma and tuberculosis are kept in the differential and closed with evidence (positive 21-hydroxylase antibodies, a normal chest X-ray and LDH, no nodes or spleen, and full recovery of weight on replacement), and the antibody result makes the cause autoimmune rather than tuberculous. The problem (E27.1) is linked to CLM-0014 and CLM-0015 with placement both_with_link, and its display says the historical cause is disputed. The record does not claim this was her real illness.
  4. Presenting problem. The tiredness, weight loss, pigmentation, nausea, back ache and electrolyte changes at intake are a separate problem (R53.83, other fatigue), resolved at the consultation three days later, when E27.1 is recorded. The intake documents show only what was known then. The back ache echoes her letter of 8 September 1816 and the colour her letter of 23 March 1817; both are after the index date, so those claims are linked only to later timeline events and appear in no intake document.
  5. Index age and dates. The index visit on March 16, 2026 maps to March 16, 1816, when she was 40; the 1913 Family Record dates the first break in her health to about March 1816 (month precision). The synthetic date of birth is December 16, 1985 (shift 210 years). The visits on March 19, April 2, April 20, April 30, July 27, and October 12, 2026 map to the same days of 1816, when she lived at Chawton. No visit falls in May or June, to leave room for the three-week journey to Cheltenham in May 1816 (exact dates not confirmed in a fetched source). The modern visits are a teaching episode, not a claim about where she was.
  6. Wording of the letters. Quotations are taken from the 1913 Family Record and corroborated in the 1892 Coolidge selection (Brabourne text) and, for “black and white and every wrong colour,” in the title of Sanders and Graham’s article. Deirdre Le Faye’s edition of the letters was not consulted, and an Open University reading-experience record that cites it (page 335) did not quote the health passage; spelling follows the Family Record. Jane Austen’s House pages returned “forbidden” and are not cited. A Hektoen International essay that attributes a typhus theory to Cope was not used, because the peer-reviewed abstracts contradict it.
  7. Real people. Her parents, Cassandra, her brothers Edward and Charles, her nieces Fanny Knight and Caroline Austen, Mr. Lyford, and the authors Cope, Upfal, White, Sanders, Graham and Tuppen appear only in claims and sources. No modern decision, statement, or health fact is attributed to any real person. Her household with her mother and sister is described only as a household; the family history records her father’s death with the cause not recorded in the cited source.
  8. Synthetic content. All visits, laboratory values, vital signs, the chest X-ray, the cosyntropin test, survey scores, the medicines and doses, the emergency kit, the sore throat, her height (170 cm) and weights, her writing routine, and the health care agent are synthetic. Qualitative results (rapid streptococcal test, chest X-ray, HIV, hepatitis B and C screening, HPV test) appear only in the note text.
  9. Sick visit. Group A streptococcal pharyngitis a month after diagnosis, chosen because a febrile illness is the commonest reason for stress dosing and because the tender neck nodes it causes must be told apart from the lymphoma in the differential (they are rechecked and gone at six weeks). It teaches the sick-day rules, checking for adrenal crisis, and first-line amoxicillin. It implies no real diagnosis.
  10. Preventive visit. A periodic adult preventive visit at 40 (CPT 99396 with 99497 in plain text). Judgment calls: a first screening mammogram at 40 under the 2024 USPSTF recommendation; cervical HPV testing because no earlier screening is recorded; lipid screening at 40 for statin assessment; one-time hepatitis B screening before the first hepatitis B vaccine dose; Tdap because no booster is recorded; HPV vaccine and folic acid not indicated, with reasons; advance care planning limited to her own general preferences and an emergency hydrocortisone letter, with an unnamed agent.
  11. Focused visits. The four chronic-care follow-ups (three days with the consultation, two weeks, six weeks, and seven months) are documented as focused visits on the Endocrine Society 2016 guideline, the CMID anemia monograph, and the ACG 2023 celiac guideline. One deviation is recorded instead of an invented result: at the two-week visit blood pressure was taken seated only, not lying and standing; no harm resulted (normal sodium and potassium that day, and no postural drop at six weeks), and postural blood pressure was added to her visit template. The medical ID was ordered at two weeks rather than at diagnosis. Vitamin B12, A1c and celiac serology are due at one year, after the episode ends, and the yearly endocrinology review is due after it.
  12. Consultation. The endocrinology referral and the consultation by Lucien Varga-Whitlock, MD (a fictional consultant), on the day of the three-day visit, are S. The consultant answers the five referral questions in line with what the record shows afterwards: the diagnosis confirmed by the cosyntropin test, hydrocortisone 15 mg and fludrocortisone 0.1 mg started that day and never changed, antibodies at two weeks (positive, so no imaging or tuberculosis testing), lymphoma kept in the differential until weight and symptoms recovered (closed at seven months without CT), the sick-day rules she used correctly in the sore throat, and yearly endocrinology review and autoimmune screening.
