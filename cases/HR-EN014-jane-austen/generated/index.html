Teaching objective: Recognize primary adrenal insufficiency in a 40-year-old writer whose tiredness, weight loss, nausea, back ache and darkening skin could be put down to overwork or low mood, and care for it on the Endocrine Society 2016 guideline: low sodium, high potassium and raised eosinophils as clues; urgent testing with an 8 am cortisol and ACTH and a cosyntropin stimulation test, without delaying treatment if she becomes unstable; renin and aldosterone for mineralocorticoid deficiency; 21-hydroxylase antibodies for the cause, with adrenal imaging and tuberculosis testing only if they are negative; lymphoma kept in the differential (examination for nodes and spleen, LDH, chest X-ray, and weight and symptoms on treatment) because a retrospective diagnosis of her real illness proposes it; hydrocortisone in two daily doses and fludrocortisone adjusted by blood pressure lying and standing, electrolytes and renin; written sick-day rules, an emergency injection kit, a steroid card and a medical ID, then used in a febrile illness; screening for associated autoimmune disease; anemia work-up (CMID anemia monograph); and listening to the patient's own written account of her illness. Her real illness remains undiagnosed; this record shows one modern possibility, not an answer.
