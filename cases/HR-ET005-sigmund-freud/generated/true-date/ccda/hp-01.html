Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Sigmund Freud was born Sigismund Schlomo Freud on May 6, 1856, in Freiberg, Moravia, now in the Czech Republic; in 1860 his family settled in Vienna. [D]
  • In the 1880s he experimented with cocaine himself, praised it in his paper On Coca (1884), and urged others to use it before he realized it could be addictive. [D]
  • According to his personal physician's account, as summarized in a 2019 review, he still used cocaine regularly in the 1890s. [D]
  • He began smoking in his twenties, first cigarettes and then cigars, following the example of his father, and often smoked about twenty cigars a day; his biographer described it as an addiction rather than a habit. [D]
  • In 1894, at 38, his friend the physician Wilhelm Fliess attributed his irregular heartbeat to smoking and told him to stop. He tried repeatedly and failed: one attempt ended within seven weeks, and a later one lasted fourteen months before he started again, describing the abstinence as torture. [D]
  • In 1923, at 67, he was diagnosed with cancer of the right upper jaw and palate, a squamous cell carcinoma; a first excision in April 1923 was followed by radiation treatment. [D]
  • Later in 1923 the oral surgeon Hans Pichler performed a radical operation that removed part of the upper jaw and soft palate, and made the obturator prosthesis that closed the defect; Freud called it "the monster." Pichler provided most of his later prosthetic care. [D]
  • He kept smoking cigars after the cancer was diagnosed and refused to give them up. [D]
  • At 73, after a heart illness, he stopped smoking for 23 days and then gradually started again. [D]
