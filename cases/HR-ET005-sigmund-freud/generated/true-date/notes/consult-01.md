## History

Mr. Freud, a 74-year-old physician and writer, is seen at the request of Dr. Reinholt. Seven years ago he had a right partial maxillectomy for palatal cancer, without radiation, and has worn the same obturator since. Over the past year it has loosened; liquids leak into his nose when he drinks, his speech becomes nasal as the day goes on, and a sore spot at the front of the defect hurts when he chews. He cleans the obturator by rinsing it and wears it day and night. He has smoked about fourteen cigars a day this week, down from twenty, on varenicline. He has not noticed any lump or bleeding. **S**

## Assessment

1. **Worn obturator with a poor seal** (Z90.09). After seven years the acrylic is worn and porous and the tissues have changed, so the bulb no longer seals at the back. A chairside reline with a soft tissue conditioner restores the seal for now, but a new definitive obturator is needed. **S**
2. **Pressure ulcer at the defect margin** (Z90.09). A shallow ulcer under an overextended flange, with no induration or mass, is a pressure ulcer. The flange was relieved today. It should heal within two weeks; if it does not, his head and neck surgeon should see it and take a biopsy, because new or persistent lesions in a smoker with a past oral cancer may be a recurrence or a second primary. **S**
3. **Hypernasal speech and nasal leakage** (R49.21). Both come from the poor seal and improved at once with the reline. A short course of speech-language therapy, starting now with the relined obturator and continuing after the new one is fitted, will help him use it well. **S**
4. **Dental care, nicotine replacement and smoking**. He has two areas of root caries and a high caries risk. Smoking stains and roughens the acrylic, encourages Candida, slows healing and raises the risk of a second cancer in the mouth. Nicotine gum sticks to acrylic, so a lozenge, dissolved on the side away from the defect, is better; the patch has no effect on the prosthesis. No radiation was given, so there is no risk of osteoradionecrosis and extractions can be done in the usual way if needed. **S**

## Recommendations

1. Chairside reline with a soft tissue conditioner and relief of the anterior flange done today.
2. New definitive obturator: impressions in about four weeks and fitting about six weeks later.
3. Recheck the ulcer in two weeks; his head and neck surgeon and a biopsy if it has not healed.
4. Speech-language therapy sessions to improve resonance and swallowing with the relined and then the new obturator.
5. Brush the obturator twice daily, soak it overnight in a denture cleanser, and leave it out for at least six hours each night once the new one fits well.
6. High-fluoride toothpaste at night, treatment of the two root caries, and dental cleaning every three months.
7. Nicotine lozenge rather than gum, and the patch, are both suitable with the obturator; stopping smoking will protect the tissues and the prosthesis.
