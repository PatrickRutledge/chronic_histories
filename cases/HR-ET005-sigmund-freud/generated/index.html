Teaching objective: Treating tobacco dependence in a man who has already had a smoking-related cancer, without judgment: primary care for a 74-year-old physician and writer, seven years after a partial maxillectomy for squamous cell carcinoma of the palate, who still smokes about twenty cigars a day after many failed attempts to stop. The episode covers an assessment of readiness that does not blame him; starting varenicline before he is ready to set a quit date and adding combination nicotine replacement (ATS 2020, USPSTF 2021), with the point that cigars count; behavioral support; the risk of second primary cancers; head and neck cancer survivorship (NCCN, ACS 2016): yearly examination after five years, imaging only for symptoms or as screening supports, and lung cancer screening eligibility when the history is of cigars rather than cigarettes; maxillofacial prosthodontic care of the obturator, with speech, swallowing and dental care; nutrition and weight; and chronic pain managed without long-term opioids (CDC 2022). There is no radiation in the synthetic history, so there is no osteoradionecrosis risk.

Social, functional, and mental status

Social history

  • S Physician who works as a psychotherapist and writer; sees patients in the mornings and writes in the afternoons and evenings
  • S Tobacco: started with cigarettes in his twenties, then cigars; about twenty cigars a day for about fifty years, inhaling some of the smoke; first cigar within 30 minutes of waking; many attempts to stop, the longest about fourteen months; says cigars help him work
  • S Alcohol: rarely, a glass of wine at dinner; no drug use now; experimented with cocaine as a young doctor and has used none for decades
  • S Married; lives with his household in the city; walks daily
  • S Eats soft foods because chewing hurts; liquids sometimes leak into his nose; meals take a long time
  • S Strengths: his work and writing, a disciplined daily routine, intellectual curiosity, and long experience of living with the prosthesis

Functional status

  • S Independent in all activities; speech is harder to understand when the obturator leaks, which tires him during long working days

Mental status

  • S Alert, precise and ironic; speech hypernasal; mood steady; PHQ-2 1; no cognitive concerns

Allergies and medications

Allergies

  • S No known allergy

Medications

  • S Varenicline 0.5 mg oral tablet: one tablet daily for three days, then one tablet twice daily for four days
  • S Varenicline 1 mg oral tablet, one tablet twice daily after food; planned for at least 24 weeks
  • S Nicotine 21 mg/24 hours transdermal patch, one patch each morning from the quit date, for six weeks
  • S Nicotine 14 mg/24 hours transdermal patch, one patch each morning for four weeks, then stop
  • S Nicotine 4 mg oral lozenge, one lozenge as needed for cravings, up to 10 a day, dissolved on the side away from the obturator; gum avoided because it sticks to the prosthesis
  • S Acetaminophen 500 mg oral tablet, two tablets three times daily (3,000 mg a day) for pain at the maxillectomy site
  • S Nystatin 100,000 units/mL oral suspension, 5 mL swished around the mouth and defect for as long as possible and then swallowed, four times daily with the obturator out, for 14 days
  • S Atorvastatin 20 mg oral tablet, one tablet daily
