Assessment

  • Worn obturator with a poor seal (Z90.09). After seven years the acrylic is worn and porous and the tissues have changed, so the bulb no longer seals at the back. A chairside reline with a soft tissue conditioner restores the seal for now, but a new definitive obturator is needed. [S]
  • Pressure ulcer at the defect margin (Z90.09). A shallow ulcer under an overextended flange, with no induration or mass, is a pressure ulcer. The flange was relieved today. It should heal within two weeks; if it does not, his head and neck surgeon should see it and take a biopsy, because new or persistent lesions in a smoker with a past oral cancer may be a recurrence or a second primary. [S]
  • Hypernasal speech and nasal leakage (R49.21). Both come from the poor seal and improved at once with the reline. A short course of speech-language therapy, starting now with the relined obturator and continuing after the new one is fitted, will help him use it well. [S]
  • Dental care, nicotine replacement and smoking. He has two areas of root caries and a high caries risk. Smoking stains and roughens the acrylic, encourages Candida, slows healing and raises the risk of a second cancer in the mouth. Nicotine gum sticks to acrylic, so a lozenge, dissolved on the side away from the defect, is better; the patch has no effect on the prosthesis. No radiation was given, so there is no risk of osteoradionecrosis and extractions can be done in the usual way if needed. [S]

Data reviewed: Dr. Reinholt's intake note and today's two-week note. Weight 58.2 kg today, from 58.4 kg; albumin 3.4 g/dL and hemoglobin 13.2 g/dL at intake. Surveillance letter from his head and neck surgeon ten months ago: no recurrence. [S]
