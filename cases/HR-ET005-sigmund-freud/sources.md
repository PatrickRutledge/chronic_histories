## Editorial resolutions

- **Sources.** The case uses the Library of Congress exhibition, three Freud Museum London pages, a museum object description, a History.com article, the 1972 Consumers Union report that quotes Ernest Jones's biography, and PubMed records of six articles (abstracts only; one has no abstract). The biographies by Ernest Jones and by Max Schur were not read and are cited only as these sources report them. Anti-tobacco advocacy pages found in searches were not used.
- **Not verified and left out.** The exact number of operations (33 and 34 are both reported; the claim says more than thirty); the name Příbor (the sources say only that Freiberg is now in the Czech Republic); the details and dates of the radium and X-ray treatments; the cardiac symptoms with nicotine-free cigars found only in search summaries; and the name of the surgeon who did the first excision. None is a claim beyond what the cited sources state.
- **Real historical people.** Hans Pichler (CLM-0007), Max Schur (CLM-0013) and Wilhelm Fliess (CLM-0005) appear only in documented history claims. Nothing in the modern episode is attributed to them. His father appears only in the documented family history (a smoker until his death at 81). His daughter, named in one source in connection with his death, is not named or given a role. No living relative is named.
- **Cocaine (CLM-0002, CLM-0003, sensitive).** His experiments and his paper in the 1880s are documented; the regular use in the 1890s rests on his physician's account as summarized in a 2019 review, so its certainty is “probable.” They are history only. The modern episode records no current drug use and uses no drug use code. The hypothesis that his palatal disease was a cocaine-induced lesion (CLM-0011) is graded R and disputed and is not used.
- **Death (CLM-0013).** Recorded once in one plain sentence, as sensitive, as history only, without dose or detail beyond the cited sources. The modern advance care planning records only his own synthetic wishes and is not a comment on his death.
- **Index age and dates.** The index visit on June 1, 2026 maps to June 1, 1930, when he was 74: seven years after the 1923 diagnosis and radical operation, and after the heart illness at 73 that made him stop smoking for 23 days (CLM-0010). The synthetic date of birth is May 6, 1952 (shift 96 years). The chronic-care visits on June 15, July 13, August 24 and October 26, the sick visit on September 14 and the wellness visit on September 28, 2026 all map to the same days of 1930, eight years before his emigration; no documented event in the cited sources falls in those months. Past medical history includes only events up to 1929.
- **Visit type.** At 74 the preventive visit is a subsequent annual wellness visit (HCPCS G0439 with CPT 99497 in plain text).
- **Synthetic cancer history differs from the real one.** In the modern episode the palatal cancer was treated seven years ago by one partial maxillectomy with clear margins (pT2 N0) and no radiation, with no recurrence. His real course included radiation after the first excision and more than thirty later procedures (CLM-0006, CLM-0008). The simpler synthetic history was chosen so that the episode teaches survivorship more than five years after surgery; it also means there is no osteoradionecrosis or radiation-related monitoring, which the record marks as not indicated with that reason.
- **Consultant specialty.** The consultant is Clara Vasquez-Lindqvist, DDS, in maxillofacial prosthodontics rather than head and neck surgical oncology: the problem at intake was a worn obturator causing pain, leakage, hypernasal speech and weight loss, which is prosthodontic care, while cancer surveillance stays with his established head and neck surgeon (a yearly examination, not a new consultation).
- **Synthetic clinical picture.** Everything in the modern episode is synthetic: the cigar count and its fall (twenty, fourteen, six, then none from the quit date), the one slip, the exhaled carbon monoxide readings (narrative), every score (pain 6 to 2, PHQ-2, Mini-Cog), all laboratory values, the ulcer, the reline and the new obturator, the speech therapy, the Candida stomatitis, the screening CT (Lung-RADS 2 with mild emphysema and coronary calcification) and aortic ultrasound, the medicines and the outcomes. Height (172 cm) and weights are synthetic.
- **Clinical judgment calls.** (1) Varenicline was started before he was ready to set a quit date, to cut down first, as the ATS 2020 guideline suggests, and continued to at least 24 weeks. (2) Combination nicotine replacement (patch and lozenge) was added from the quit date; a lozenge rather than gum because gum sticks to acrylic. (3) Cigars were treated like any other tobacco. (4) Lung cancer screening was offered by shared decision, because the USPSTF criteria count cigarette pack-years and he smoked cigarettes only briefly, but fifty years of heavy cigar smoking and a smoking-related cancer carry risk; this is a judgment, not a guideline rule. (5) No routine head and neck imaging seven years after treatment without symptoms. (6) Pain was treated with scheduled acetaminophen up to 3,000 mg a day and a better prosthesis, without opioids. (7) A moderate-intensity statin was started at 74 by shared decision for a high estimated risk; coronary calcification on the screening CT later supported it. (8) The pressure ulcer was watched for two weeks after the flange was relieved, with a biopsy planned if it did not heal.
- **Sick visit.** Candida stomatitis under the new obturator, worn day and night, chosen because it is common with prostheses and tests the record: an examination looking for recurrence, nystatin rather than fluconazole, prosthesis hygiene and overnight removal, and a biopsy plan for any area that did not heal. It is not a historical claim.
- **Wellness visit.** Judgment calls: low-dose CT and one-time aortic ultrasound ordered (both done two weeks later); colorectal screening up to date; high-dose influenza and PCV20 given; RSV vaccine deferred to 75; atorvastatin started; advance care planning records an unnamed agent and his wish for good pain relief and no treatment that only prolongs suffering, with no orders.
- **Focused visits.** The four chronic-care follow-ups (two weeks with the consultation, six weeks, twelve weeks and about five months) are documented as focused visits on the ATS 2020 and USPSTF 2021 tobacco guidance, the NCCN and ACS head and neck survivorship guidelines, the CDC 2022 opioid guideline and USPSTF statin and lung screening recommendations. Two deviations are recorded instead of invented results: (1) he had not called the quitline two weeks after intake; no harm resulted (he had already cut down by a third), and the tobacco treatment nurse enrolled him directly during the visit; (2) speech-language therapy planned at the consultation had not started by six weeks because the clinic cancelled the first appointment; no harm resulted (speech was clearer with the reline), the sessions were rebooked with a reminder, and four were done by twelve weeks. Synthetic findings introduced: nausea and vivid dreams on varenicline, a pressure ulcer that healed, one slip in the second week after the quit date, mild redness at patch sites, two root caries, and the screening CT findings.
- **Consultation.** The maxillofacial prosthodontics referral and the consultation by Clara Vasquez-Lindqvist, DDS (a fictional consultant), on the day of the two-week visit, are **S**. The consultant answers the five referral questions in line with what the record shows afterwards: a chairside reline and flange relief that day and a new definitive obturator (impressions at about five weeks, fitted at twelve weeks); a pressure ulcer to be rechecked in two weeks with a biopsy if it persisted (healed); speech-language therapy (delayed, a recorded deviation, then completed); prosthesis hygiene, high-fluoride toothpaste, treatment of two root caries and three-monthly cleaning; and a nicotine lozenge rather than gum. Her advice to leave the obturator out at night was not followed at first, which led to the Candida stomatitis at the sick visit.
- **Aliases.** “Sigismund Schlomo Freud,” his birth name in the Library of Congress exhibition, appears in no other record.
