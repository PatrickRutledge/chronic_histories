## Assessment and plan

1. **Thiazide-associated hyponatremia and hypokalemia with orthostatic hypotension** (E87.1). Sodium 129 mmol/L, potassium 3.3 mmol/L and creatinine 1.05 mg/dL (eGFR 57), from chlorthalidone together with four days of little food and salt, which leaves the kidneys unable to clear water; she is also low in volume, which causes the drop in pressure on standing. Mild and without confusion, so it can be managed at home with a recheck; chlorthalidone should not be restarted, since older women who develop thiazide hyponatremia often develop it again. A near-fall on a blood pressure medicine is exactly the risk the falls plan is meant to catch. **S**
   - Stop chlorthalidone for good; record thiazide hyponatremia as a reason not to use it again.
   - Continue amlodipine 5 mg.
   - Eat normally, with soup or broth and some salt for a few days, and fruit, vegetables and beans for potassium; drink to thirst rather than large amounts of tea or water.
   - Stand up slowly: sit on the edge of the bed or chair first; use the cane.
   - Sodium and potassium again in one week at the clinic laboratory.
   - Go to the emergency department for confusion, vomiting, a fall, fainting or a severe headache.
   - For the cold: rest, warm drinks, honey and ginger as she likes; no decongestant tablets, which raise blood pressure, and no anti-inflammatory cold remedies.
2. **Hypertension** (I10). Pressure is low today because of volume loss; it will rise again off the diuretic. **S**
   - Home readings once daily; bring them to the three-month review.
