## Assessment

1. **Blood pressure medicines and target** (I10). Stage 2 hypertension, confirmed at home, with ECG evidence of left ventricular hypertrophy and a 10-year cardiovascular risk of about 14%. She is fit, independent and has no orthostatic drop, so the target of below 130/80 mmHg applies to her as to younger adults, reached gradually. Start two first-line medicines: amlodipine 5 mg and chlorthalidone 12.5 mg. In a 71-year-old woman a thiazide can lower sodium, especially when she eats little, so check sodium and potassium in about four weeks, check standing pressure at every visit, and teach a sick-day rule to stop chlorthalidone if she cannot eat or drink normally. If sodium falls, stop it and use an angiotensin receptor blocker instead. An echocardiogram is reasonable for stage 2 hypertension with ECG voltage. **S**
2. **Falls** (Z91.81). STEADI shows high risk: a fall last year, fear of falling, a Timed Up and Go of 15 seconds, a below-average chair stand and poor tandem balance. No orthostatic drop, no sedating or anticholinergic medicines, and her vision is adequate. The stairs to her rooms and worn slip-on shoes are the main hazards. Strength and balance exercise reduces falls in community-dwelling older adults. **S**
3. **Knee osteoarthritis** (M17.0). Clinical bilateral knee osteoarthritis; imaging is not needed. Exercise therapy, then tai chi, topical diclofenac, weight loss of at least 5%, and a cane in the right hand are all strongly recommended. Avoid oral anti-inflammatory medicines, which raise blood pressure and harm the kidneys. An intra-articular corticosteroid injection is an option for a flare. Her own massage is welcome and fits the exercise plan. **S**
4. **Her own remedies**. Liquorice root contains glycyrrhizin, which raises blood pressure and lowers potassium; that would add to the potassium loss from chlorthalidone, so pausing it is wise. Ginger tea in food amounts is fine. Her liniment contains camphor and methyl salicylate; used on one knee, or at a different time from the diclofenac gel, and never under a heat pad, it is safe. Her knowledge of her remedies is good, and she should be asked about any new one rather than told to stop. **S**
5. **Mood, loneliness, home and costs**. PHQ-9 of 6 reflects loneliness and fear of falling rather than depression. A social prescribing link worker, a tai chi group and a lunch club address loneliness and falls together. An occupational therapy home visit for a stair rail and bath board. Generic medicines in 90-day supplies, a free blood pressure monitor and help with transport keep costs down. **S**
