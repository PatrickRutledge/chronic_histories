Teaching objective: Hypertension with knee osteoarthritis and fall risk in a 71-year-old Black Jamaican British woman, a nurse and healer living alone in London on a modest fixed income. The episode covers a very high blood pressure found at a visit for headaches, without a hypertensive emergency; confirmation with home readings; baseline tests (metabolic panel, lipids, hemoglobin A1c, TSH, urine albumin, ECG, fundi and an echocardiogram for stage 2 hypertension); two first-line medicines of different classes, a calcium channel blocker and a thiazide-like diuretic, chosen under the 1876 AHA/ACC guideline, with a target below 130/80 mmHg; thiazide-associated hyponatremia and orthostatic hypotension with a near-fall during a cold, and the change to an angiotensin receptor blocker; a moderate-intensity statin; stroke risk reduction; knee osteoarthritis managed under the ACR/AF 1870 guideline with exercise therapy, tai chi, topical diclofenac, weight loss, a cane and an injection offered; falls under CDC STEADI and the USPSTF; bone health; her own healing knowledge and remedies respected and checked for interactions (liquorice root raises blood pressure and lowers potassium); loneliness and the cost of care; and her public work. It is taught with dignity and attention to equity: her expertise as a healer is respected, she is addressed as she wishes, and access barriers (cost of a cuff, transport, stairs) are solved rather than blamed on her.

Social, functional, and mental status

Social history

  • S Nurse, healer and writer; asked to be called Mrs. Seacole; known to many former soldiers and their families; still gives nursing advice to neighbours who call on her
  • S Widowed for many years, with no children; lives alone in rented rooms up two flights of stairs in Paddington; friends visit, but she goes out less since her fall and some weeks speaks to few people
  • S Income: a modest fixed income from savings and the fund raised for her; careful with money since her bankruptcy, and worried about the cost of medicines, a blood pressure cuff and taxis
  • S Her own remedies: ginger tea most days; a liquorice root infusion for coughs on and off; a liniment she makes with camphor and oil of wintergreen, which she massages into her knees; she shared these openly and wants them respected
  • S Diet: home cooking with rice, peas, fish, vegetables and fruit, and salted fish and pickled foods several times a week; tea with sugar; never smoked; a small glass of wine on occasions
  • S Strengths, supports and purpose: decades of nursing and healing, courage, humour, her writing and her friendships with old soldiers; she wants to keep walking to see her friends
