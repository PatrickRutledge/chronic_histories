Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Plato names him in the Protagoras as 'Hippocrates of Cos, the Asclepiad', a physician who took pupils for a fee, and in the Phaedrus cites his method; Aristotle calls him 'the Great Hippocrates'. These are the earliest witnesses to him. [D]
  • The three ancient lives (by Soranus, a late writer, and in the Suda and Tzetzes, all written many centuries after him) say that he was born on Cos in 460 BC, that his father Heraclides was a physician of the Asclepiad guild and taught him, and that he travelled widely in Greece; the Loeb editor finds 'a certain amount of fable' in them but nothing improbable in their broad outline. [D]
  • The Hippocratic Corpus is not the work of one man. The Loeb editor describes it as the remains of a library of works 'of various dates and of different schools', a great part of which is 'neither by Hippocrates himself nor by his school'; Aristotle ascribed one of its treatises to Polybus, and Hippocrates is 'a name without writings'. Which treatises, if any, he wrote is still debated. [D]
  • Modern medicine regards him as its founder: reviews call him 'the father of modern medicine' and 'the founding father of clinical medicine', for medicine based on observation of clinical signs rather than on religious or magical beliefs. [D]
