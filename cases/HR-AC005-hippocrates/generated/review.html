Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Sources and how little is knowable. Nothing written about him survives from his own lifetime except a few references: Plato names him twice and Aristotle once. The three ancient lives (Soranus, the Suda, Tzetzes) were written many centuries later and mix fact with legend; the Loeb editor and the Suda's modern editors say so. Claims are graded D only where an ancient witness plainly reports a fact that modern scholars accept (a physician and teacher of Cos, an Asclepiad, born about 460 BC; died in Thessaly and buried at Larissa); the length of his life is graded R, disputed. Nothing about his own health is documented (CLM-0007), and the record does not invent any: the chart is thin on purpose. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy had no entry at the address tried (it returned not found), the National Library of Medicine page tried is no longer maintained and has no content, and Britannica could not be read; the Loeb introduction, the Suda On Line and two peer-reviewed reviews are used instead.
  2. Name. The display name is “Hippocrates.” He is known by a single name, so `identity.name_parts` has an empty given array and the name as the family name, as in the other single-name records. “Hippocrates of Kos” is the alias and appears in no other record. The sources spell the island both Cos and Kos; the record uses each source's spelling.
  3. Dates (rule 28). Historical dates use signed astronomical years: birth -0459 (about 460 BC, approximate), with the Loeb editor's note that Aulus Gellius would put it before 470 BC recorded as unresolved; death -0369 (about 370 BC, approximate), following the roster and the ninety-year tradition, with 377 BC (two medical reviews) and about 356 BC (the Suda's 104 years) recorded as unresolved alternatives. The brief's range of 83 to over 100 years is corrected to the traditions the Loeb editor gives (85, 90, 104 or 109). Calendars are “unknown” because no ancient date for him is a calendar date. The record has no true-date variant (docs/date-shifting.md).
  4. Index age and dates. The index visit on March 2, 2026 maps to March 2, 371 BC, at 89 (synthetic date of birth January 10, 1937; shift 2,396 years), one year before the approximate death year so that every visit (March to September 371 BC) falls within his life under the ninety-year tradition. If the 85-year tradition were right, he would have died before these dates; no tradition is certain, and the record says so. The setting at Larissa, where the Suda says he died and was buried, is assumed; where he lived in his last years is not recorded.
  5. The Hippocratic Corpus (CLM-0005, D). The Corpus was written by many authors, of different schools, over a long period, and a great part of it is neither by him nor by his school. No sentence from the Corpus is attributed to him in the record, and nothing in it is used as a fact about his health.
  6. Thin chart (rule 26). Because nothing about his health is documented, the modern episode is built around normal aging: an independent 89-year-old physician whose wellness screening at his previous practice noted slower walking, leading to a comprehensive geriatric assessment, care centered on the Age-Friendly 4Ms, and one mild common condition (knee osteoarthritis) so that the chronic-care visits have a purpose. Every finding is synthetic and is not a claim about his real body or mind.
  7. Presenting problem. The slower gait, knee pain and near-fall found at intake are recorded as their own problem (R26.81), resolved at the consultation when knee osteoarthritis with reduced strength and balance, with diphenhydramine at night adding to the risk, was diagnosed. The brief's “annual wellness visit leads to the episode” is read as a wellness screening at his previous practice a year before, which prompted the intake assessment; this clinic's subsequent annual wellness visit falls a year after it.
  8. Frailty scales. The Clinical Frailty Scale, the FRAIL scale, the Timed Up and Go, gait speed, the 30-second chair stand, the four-stage balance test, the MNA-SF and the whispered voice test are recorded as narrative because they have no LOINC code used in the record; grip strength, MoCA, Mini-Cog, GDS-15, PHQ-2, KOOS JR, the pain score, the DXA T-scores and FRAX are coded.
  9. Clinical judgment calls. (1) Knee osteoarthritis diagnosed on the ACR clinical criteria without an X-ray, as ACR/AF 2019 and the CMID allow. (2) Over-the-counter naproxen and diphenhydramine stopped (Beers 2023); acetaminophen limited to 3,000 mg a day at 89; topical diclofenac, whose blood levels are low, accepted. (3) A sleep routine rather than any sleep medicine. (4) Vitamin B12 and TSH checked once for slowing gait; both normal. (5) DXA in a man of 89 at risk of falls (BHOF and the Endocrine Society), although USPSTF finds the evidence insufficient for screening men. (6) No osteoporosis medicine at a FRAX risk of 11 percent (major) and 2.6 percent (hip), below the BHOF thresholds; repeat DXA in two years. (7) Twelve weeks of a strength and balance program based on Otago, then tai chi, rather than a walking aid, at his choice. (8) PCV20 after PPSV23 at 65, a single RSV dose, two recombinant zoster doses and high-dose influenza vaccine. (9) Cancer, diabetes, lipid, hepatitis C and HIV screening not indicated at 89. (10) Pre-frailty (FRAIL 1) reassessed after the program (FRAIL 0).
  10. Relatives and other real people. The ancient lives name his father; no relative appears in the modern episode. The health care agent is “a long-time friend,” unnamed. No real person has a statement, decision or health fact in the modern episode. No family medical history is recorded.
  11. Synthetic clinical picture. Everything in the modern episode is synthetic: every result, the DXA, the frailty and mobility measures, the examination findings, the scores, the medicines, the vaccines and the outcomes. Height (168 cm) and weights are synthetic.
  12. Annual wellness visit. A subsequent annual wellness visit at 89 (HCPCS G0439 with CPT 99497 in plain text): the health risk assessment, Mini-Cog 5, PHQ-2 0, hearing and vision, falls and home safety, the prevention plan with screening not indicated at his age, RSV and the first recombinant zoster dose, and advance care planning centered on what matters most to him (full treatment for a reversible illness; comfort-focused care at home, without resuscitation, ventilation or tube feeding, if he could no longer recognize or speak with people).
  13. Sick visit. A common cold, chosen because it is common at his age, tests the deprescribing plan (a nighttime cold remedy with diphenhydramine and a decongestant made him unsteady at night; saline, honey and acetaminophen instead; no antibiotics) and the falls plan, and points at no documented illness. It is not a historical claim.
  14. Focused visits. The four chronic-care follow-ups (two weeks with the consultation, eight weeks, four months and seven months) are documented as focused visits on the CMID osteoarthritis and osteoporosis monographs with ACR/AF 2019, BHOF 2022 and the Endocrine Society 2012, the AGS Beers Criteria 2023, STEADI (AGS 2025) and the World Falls Guidelines, the ICFSR frailty guideline, and the Age-Friendly 4Ms. One deviation is recorded instead of an invented result: the KOOS JR was not given at eight weeks because the form was left out of the visit packet; no harm was likely (the pain score and examination recorded that day both showed improvement, and the score at four months was 74, from 62); KOOS JR was added to the visit template. Synthetic findings introduced: low bone mass on DXA with vitamin D repleted from 24 to 33 ng/mL, gait speed 0.78 to 0.96 m/s, Timed Up and Go 13.8 to 10.9 seconds, KOOS JR 62 to 76, and no falls.
  15. Consultation. The geriatric medicine referral and the consultation by Aristide Lindqvist-Bamba, MD (a fictional consultant), on the day of the two-week visit, are S. The consultant answers the five referral questions in line with what the record shows afterwards: knee osteoarthritis with weakness and diphenhydramine as the causes, no X-ray; naproxen and diphenhydramine kept stopped, with topical diclofenac, acetaminophen and a sleep routine; pre-frailty treated with twelve weeks of strength and balance training and then tai chi; a home safety check, a second stair rail and proper shoes; and DXA with FRAX, with medicine only above the thresholds (not reached).
