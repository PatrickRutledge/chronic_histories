Assessment

  • Low rectal adenocarcinoma, mismatch repair deficient, cT3bN1M0 (stage IIIB) (C20). Locally advanced adenocarcinoma of the low rectum with no distant spread. For a mismatch repair-proficient tumor at this level the standard would be total neoadjuvant therapy (chemoradiation and chemotherapy) followed by total mesorectal excision, and because the lower edge is only about 1 cm above the anorectal junction the operation would most likely be an abdominoperineal resection with a permanent colostomy. This tumor is mismatch repair deficient, which changes everything: in the dostarlimab trial every patient with this kind of tumor who completed six months of treatment had a clinical complete response, and in the larger follow-up study nearly all kept their rectum without radiation or surgery, with few regrowths. The NCCN and ASCO guidelines now recommend checkpoint inhibitor immunotherapy for mismatch repair-deficient locally advanced rectal cancer. The aim is cure with the rectum and sphincter preserved; radiation and surgery remain available if the response is incomplete or the tumor regrows. [S]
  • Iron deficiency anemia from blood loss (D50.0). From the tumor's bleeding; oral iron is enough while the bleeding settles with treatment. [S]

Data reviewed: Dr. Vasquez-Thornbury's intake note and results. Colonoscopy: ulcerated mass on the posterior and left wall of the lower rectum, 4 to 9 cm from the anal verge, about half the circumference; the rest of the colon normal. Pathology: adenocarcinoma, moderately differentiated; loss of MSH2 and MSH6 on immunohistochemistry with MLH1 and PMS2 retained; microsatellite instability high.
