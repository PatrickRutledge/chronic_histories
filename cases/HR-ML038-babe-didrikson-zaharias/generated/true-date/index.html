Teaching objective: Recognize early-onset rectal cancer in a fit woman of 41, below the screening age, whose rectal bleeding and change in bowel habit could be put down to hemorrhoids, and care for it on the NCCN rectal cancer guideline and the ASCO guidance on locally advanced rectal cancer: a digital rectal examination and prompt colonoscopy with biopsy; mismatch repair testing of every colorectal cancer; germline genetic testing because she is under 50; staging by pelvic MRI and CT, with CEA; a multidisciplinary tumor board; and, because the tumor is mismatch repair deficient, PD-1 blockade with dostarlimab and a watch-and-wait strategy after a clinical complete response, sparing her the abdominoperineal resection and permanent colostomy that a tumor this low would otherwise need. The episode also covers immune-related adverse events (hypothyroidism), iron deficiency anemia, sexual health and body image, return to elite sport, screening of first-degree relatives, the USPSTF screening age of 45, and survivorship care, set against her documented operation with a permanent colostomy in 1880 and her return to win a national championship afterwards.
