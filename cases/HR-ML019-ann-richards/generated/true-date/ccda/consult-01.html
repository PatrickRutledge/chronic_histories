Assessment

  • Low bone mass with high fracture risk (M85.89). By T-score this is low bone mass, not osteoporosis: the lowest value is -2.3 at the lumbar spine, and none is -2.5 or lower. The phrase early osteoporosis has no defined meaning and should not be used. A hand fracture is not one of the fractures that establishes osteoporosis on its own (hip or vertebra, or in low bone mass a proximal humerus, pelvis, or distal forearm fracture), but it followed a fall from standing height, so it is correctly entered as a previous fracture in FRAX. With that and her mother's hip fracture, FRAX is 22 percent for a major osteoporotic fracture and 3.6 percent for hip fracture, above the thresholds of 20 and 3 percent. Treatment is indicated by the BHOF 1993 and Endocrine Society thresholds, and AACE 1991 would call this osteoporosis on clinical grounds. By AACE 1991 she is in the very-high-risk group because of a fall with injury and a fracture within about the past year; she does not meet the Endocrine Society very-high-risk criteria, which center on severe or multiple vertebral fractures. [S]
  • Drug choice. Zoledronic acid 5 mg once a year is a first-line choice in both the high-risk and very-high-risk groups of AACE 1991 and is an initial therapy in the Endocrine Society guideline, with proven reduction of hip, vertebral, and nonvertebral fractures. Once-yearly dosing suits her travel. Anabolic therapy was discussed; with no vertebral fracture and no T-score below -2.5 it is not needed first, and it remains the option if she fractures on treatment. An oral bisphosphonate is a reasonable alternative. A selective estrogen receptor modulator is not suitable, because it has not been shown to reduce hip fractures and her hip risk is above threshold. Before the first dose, 25-hydroxyvitamin D must be 30 ng/mL or more, calcium and eGFR must be normal (creatinine clearance 35 mL/min or more), and a routine dental examination should be done. [S]
  • Secondary causes. Normal calcium, PTH, phosphate, magnesium, alkaline phosphatase, kidney function, TSH, and blood count make hyperparathyroidism, osteomalacia, thyroid excess, and myeloma unlikely. Vitamin D of 24 ng/mL is insufficient and is being corrected. The 24-hour urine calcium is still needed to exclude hypercalciuria before treatment. Celiac serology and protein electrophoresis are not needed without bowel symptoms, anemia, or an unexplained abnormal result. [S]
  • Falls (Z91.81). Falls cause most of her fracture risk outside the spine. Her tandem stance of 8 seconds shows impaired balance; a supervised balance and strength program is the most effective fall-prevention step. [S]
  • Alcohol use disorder in sustained remission (F10.21). Heavy alcohol use and smoking harm bone; her long abstinence from both removes them as current risk factors, so neither is entered as current in FRAX. Remission also lowers her fall risk. No change to the drug plan; avoid sedating medicines. [S]

  • Acetaminophen for the aches and fever that can follow the first dose. [S]
  • Continue vitamin D3 1971 units daily and about 1200 mg of calcium a day, mostly from food. [S]
  • Supervised balance and strength program now, then a home program; weight-bearing exercise most days. [S]
  • Fasting morning CTX three months after the first dose to confirm response; calcium and creatinine before each yearly dose; 25-hydroxyvitamin D yearly. [S]
