# Teaching Note — Ann Richards pilot

> **SYNTHETIC RECORD, NOT A REAL MEDICAL RECORD**

## Why this case matters

This case shows how historical evidence can support a transparent fictional modern episode without presenting interpretation or generated content as documented fact.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish documented (**D**), retrospective (**R**), and synthetic (**S**) content.
2. Keep historical and active problems distinct.
3. Connect measurable goals and interventions to guidelines.
4. Preserve strengths, function, identity, and agency.

## Destigmatization message

A health condition is one part of a person’s life and does not erase achievement, relationships, purpose, or agency.

## Discussion questions

- Which facts support concern without establishing a modern diagnosis?
- Which entries belong in active problems, historical problems, or both?
- What additional evidence would change an evidence grade?
- How should an EHR distinguish synthetic and historical content?

## Evidence-grade summary

- **D:** Ann Richards was born Dorothy Ann Willis on September 1, 1933, in Lakeview, near Waco, Texas. She was elected Texas state treasurer in 1982, gave the keynote address at the 1988 Democratic National Convention, was elected governor of Texas in 1990, and served one term after losing her bid for re-election in 1994. She spoke openly about her past heavy drinking ("I smoked like a chimney and drank like a fish"), entered treatment, and emerged sober; one biography dates the treatment to 1980 and describes it as successful. She said publicly that her mother had osteoporosis, broke "her arm, her hip," and developed a hump, and that osteoporosis was not the cause of her mother's death but led to it. Because of her mother's fractures, she asked her doctor at an annual checkup to test her bone density. She had a bone density test after falling and breaking her hand. CBS News (2003) and a publisher's summary date this to 1996, and CBS says she fell twice and broke her hand and her ankle; a Publishers Weekly review dates the hand fracture to 1994. The bone density test showed osteopenia, which CBS News and the publisher's summary call an early stage of osteoporosis; in other interviews she said she had osteoporosis or early osteoporosis. CBS News reported in 2003 that she had shrunk three-quarters of an inch; in a 2002 television interview she said "my neck bones were collapsing and I literally was shrinking." She took a prescription medicine, began weight-bearing exercise and weight training, and changed her diet; CBS News reported in 2003 that her bone density had stabilized, and that she was a spokesperson for the maker of the medicine she took. In 2003 she published I'm Not Slowing Down: Winning My Battle with Osteoporosis, written with Richard U. Levine, MD, to encourage women and men to ask their doctors for a bone density test. She was found to have esophageal cancer in March 2006, received chemotherapy, and died at her home in Austin on September 13, 2006, at 73.
- **R:** None recorded.
- **S:** Shifted identity, fictional care team, modern encounters, results, plans, and outcomes.
