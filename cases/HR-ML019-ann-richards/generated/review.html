Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Recent-death rule. Richards died in 2006. The case uses only what she made public herself (her 2003 book as described in reviews, her television interviews, and press coverage of her osteoporosis), her public account of heavy drinking and recovery, and basic biographical facts from state archives and obituaries. No medical records, physician statements, family accounts, or speculation about undisclosed conditions are used. Everything clinical in the modern episode is synthetic: her DXA T-scores, FRAX, laboratory results, vertebral assessment, fall tests, treatment, and preventive care. Claims about alcohol and tobacco are marked sensitive. No sick visit is added.
  2. Fracture and diagnosis. The sources agree that she broke her hand in a fall and then had a bone density test. CBS News (2003) and the publisher's book description date this to 1996; the Publishers Weekly review dates the hand fracture to 1994. CBS (2003) alone says she fell twice and also broke her ankle. The case uses 1996 with the conflict recorded, and treats the ankle fracture as reported in one source. The published label is osteopenia, “an early stage of osteoporosis”; in some interviews she said she had osteoporosis. Her T-scores were not found in any fetched source (the book itself was not accessed), so none are used.
  3. Precision in the modern episode. The synthetic T-scores (lumbar spine -2.3, femoral neck -2.0, total hip -1.7) are in the low bone mass range, matching the published osteopenia. The notes say plainly that this is not osteoporosis by T-score and that “early osteoporosis” is not a defined category. Treatment rests on FRAX (22 percent major osteoporotic, 3.6 percent hip, above the 20 and 3 percent thresholds), with her mother's hip fracture (her own public account) and her hand fracture entered. Judgment call: the hand fracture followed a fall from standing height, so it is entered as a previous fracture in FRAX, while the notes explain that a hand fracture does not by itself establish osteoporosis; without it, FRAX would fall below the thresholds. The FRAX values are synthetic estimates, not a recorded calculation from her life. Problem code M85.89 follows the densitometric category; the notes add that AACE 2020 would call this osteoporosis on clinical grounds.
  4. Drug choice and the spokesperson role. She publicly took a prescription medicine and was a spokesperson for its maker. The record names no brand, does not name or use her medicine, and repeats no promotional claim. The synthetic therapy (zoledronic acid once a year) was chosen strictly by AACE 2020, the Endocrine Society, and BHOF for her synthetic risk (hip risk above threshold, so a drug with proven hip fracture reduction), with once-yearly dosing for her travel. The consultant notes, in general terms, that a selective estrogen receptor modulator is not suitable for a woman whose hip risk is above threshold; this is guideline reasoning about the synthetic patient, not a comment on her real care. Calcium from food, vitamin D, weight-bearing and balance exercise, falls prevention, the secondary-causes work-up, DXA two years after starting, and the drug-holiday rules follow the CMID and the cited guidelines.
  5. Alcohol use disorder. Her heavy drinking and recovery are public in her own words and in reference biographies (treatment in 1980). The case codes alcohol use disorder in sustained remission (F10.21), so a structured safety assessment was added at intake with the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, and safety and recovery are reviewed at every later visit; it records no current risk and makes no claim about her real mental health. Who prompted her treatment is not recorded, because the sources attribute it to relatives and friends. Her past heavy smoking is from her own phrase; the quit timing (more than fifteen years before the index visit) is synthetic, and it decides that she is not eligible for lung cancer screening.
  6. Index age. The modern episode presents Richards at 63, the year after the fall and hand fracture. The synthetic date of birth is September 1, 1962 (shift 29 years). The index visit on February 10, 2026 maps to February 10, 1997; the chronic-care visits on March 10, May 12, and August 11, 2026 map to the same days of 1997; and the adult preventive visit on October 6, 2026 maps to October 6, 1997, when she was 64 (so `preventive_adult`). Every visit falls within her lifetime and after the documented fracture and test. Because she says she had been told of early bone loss after the earlier test and no report is available, the intake DXA serves as a new baseline. The modern record does not model her real treatment after the 1996 test; this is a judgment call for review.
  7. Synthetic clinical picture. Kept modest: no vertebral fracture, normal secondary-causes tests apart from vitamin D insufficiency (24 ng/mL, corrected to 38), normal mood and alcohol screens, no new falls or fractures. The 2 cm height loss matches the three-quarters of an inch reported in the press. The occupation (public speaker and policy adviser), living situation, diet, menopause timing, and vaccines are synthetic. Her later esophageal cancer and death are recorded as history only and are not used in the episode.
  8. Focused visits. The three chronic-care follow-ups (four weeks, three months, and six months after intake) are documented as focused visits on the CMID osteoporosis monograph, with AACE 2020, the Endocrine Society, BHOF 2022, and CDC STEADI, and each lists every item the protocol calls for at that point, with its interval and status. The four-week visit is the day of the endocrinology consultation; the three-month visit has the pre-infusion tests and the first zoledronic acid infusion; the six-month visit has the CTX response check three months after the dose. Protocol deviation recorded instead of an invented result: the 24-hour urine calcium ordered at intake was not collected before the four-week visit because she was traveling, and was collected about ten weeks late, before the first dose, with a normal result, no harm, and a corrective action.
  9. Consultation. The endocrinology referral and the consultation by Marcus Delgado, MD (a fictional consultant), on the day of the four-week visit, are S.
