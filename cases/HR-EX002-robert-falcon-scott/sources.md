## Historical evidence

1. Library of Congress. Name authority record n50005591, "Scott, Robert Falcon, 1868-1912." Born June 6, 1868, at Plymouth; died about March 29, 1912. https://id.loc.gov/authorities/names/n50005591.html
2. Royal Museums Greenwich. "Captain Robert Falcon Scott." Born in Devon into a seafaring family; joined his first ship at 13; the Discovery expedition of 1901 to 1904; married the sculptor Kathleen Bruce in 1908, one son; reached the South Pole on January 17 to 18, 1912; all five of the polar party died "from cold and hunger," the last three about 11 miles short of a depot; the tent found on November 12. https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/maritime-history/captain-robert-falcon-scott
3. Scott RF. *The Voyage of the 'Discovery'*, volume I. London: Smith, Elder, 1905 (Internet Archive scan). The October 1902 outbreak of scurvy at winter quarters after the western sledge journey; the surgeon's examination of everyone on board ("signs of scurvy in a good many, but in most cases it is only the merest indication"); cooked seal meat to replace part of the pemmican; "our scurvy is at an end" (October 20, 1902); and his remarks that scurvy "is now believed to be ptomaine poisoning." https://archive.org/details/voyageofdiscover001scot
4. Scott RF. *The Voyage of the 'Discovery'*, volume II. London: Smith, Elder, 1905 (Internet Archive scan), with his sledging diary of the southern journey. Weekly "examination of gums and legs"; the change from bacon to more seal meat in December 1902; his tobacco running low and trying tea leaves (December 20, 1902); the examination of January 14, 1903 ("I myself have distinctly red gums, and a very slight swelling in the ankles"); improvement on a double ration of seal meat (January 16); "our scurvy has been advancing again" and "my ankles are considerably swollen" at the end of the journey; and, after the return, "both legs much swollen and very uncomfortable gums" with an "extraordinary feeling of lassitude" that lasted many weeks. https://archive.org/details/voyageofdiscover002scot
5. *Scott's Last Expedition*, volume I, being the journals of Captain R. F. Scott, arranged by Leonard Huxley (London, 1913). Project Gutenberg eBook 11579. Markham's preface (battleship commands and confidential Admiralty work between the expeditions); the entries of January 16 and 17, 1912 at the Pole; March 18 and 19, 1912 ("My right foot has gone, nearly all the toes"; "Amputation is the least I can hope for now"); and the last entry, dated March 29, 1912 ("For God's sake look after our people."). https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11579
6. Butler AR. "The role of scurvy in Scott's return from the South Pole." *J R Coll Physicians Edinb* 2013;43(2):175–181. PMID 23734365. Abstract only. Scurvy may have contributed to the polar party's ill health, but their deaths were more likely caused by frostbite, malnutrition and hypothermia; many British doctors of the time followed the ptomaine theory and overlooked Holst and Frølich. Graded **R**. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23734365/

## Editorial resolutions

- **Index age and whereabouts.** The modern episode presents him at 41. The synthetic date of birth is 1984-06-06 (the true month and day), so the shift is 116 years. The index visit is January 13, 2026 (true frame January 13, 1910) and the last visit is May 26, 2026 (true frame May 26, 1910), about three weeks before the Terra Nova left Cardiff on June 15, 1910. A letter of November 24, 1909 places him at the expedition's London offices organizing and raising funds; his day-to-day whereabouts between January and June 1910 were not verified, and the episode assumes he stayed in or near London, which no fetched source contradicts. The roster gives his age as 43; he was 43 at his death.
- **Thin chart.** No documented health problem between the Discovery expedition and the Terra Nova expedition was found in a fetched source, so none is recorded. The brief's suggested presenting complaint of fatigue before the expedition is not supported and is not used. The documented health history is his scurvy on the Discovery southern journey and his own account of recovery. The modern episode is therefore built as a structured pre-expedition medical assessment, with one common, synthetic condition (stage 1 hypertension found at that assessment) to give the chronic-care visits a purpose, and a synthetic pipe-smoking history anchored to his documented tobacco use.
- **Scurvy on the Discovery expedition.** The brief asked whether he himself had signs. His own sledging diary records that he did: red gums and slight ankle swelling on January 14, 1903, improvement on more seal meat, swollen ankles again at the end of the journey, and swollen legs, sore gums and weeks of lassitude after the return (CLM-0005, CLM-0006, **D**). The October 1902 outbreak at winter quarters is recorded without naming the men affected. A companion's worse illness on the southern journey is mentioned only as the trigger for the change of diet, without his name or details.
- **The 1912 deaths.** Recorded as history only: the frostbite of his right foot from his own journal (CLM-0013), the deaths of all five men from cold and hunger (Royal Museums Greenwich), the last entry dated March 29, 1912, and the finding of the tent. His companions are not named and no health facts are given about them beyond the fact and order of their deaths. Interpretations of the causes (scurvy as a contributor, energy and vitamin C in the rations, unusually cold weather) are graded **R**. The death date is approximate, taken from the last journal entry. None of this appears in the modern episode.
- **Roster leads not adopted.** "Depression" and "depression in final days documented in diary" are not adopted: the last entries record a man facing death in a storm, not a diagnosis, and no source fetched makes a diagnosis. "Hypothermia (fatal)" is recorded only as the museum's "cold and hunger." The roster's "poor planning decisions" and its comparison with another expedition are value judgments, not clinical claims, and are not recorded. The rival expedition at the Pole is referred to without naming its leader.
- **Hypertension.** Synthetic. The office reading at intake is its own presenting problem (PRB-001, R03.0), resolved at the second visit when home readings confirm stage 1 hypertension (PRB-003, I10). At a low ten-year risk (about 5% by the Pooled Cohort Equations counting him as a smoker; PREVENT was not calculated exactly and is stated only as below the 7.5% threshold), lifestyle measures come first for three months, and losartan is started at the three-month visit when the home average is still 134/85. An ARB is preferred over a thiazide or a beta-blocker for cold and dehydration; this is a clinical judgment, not a guideline rule.
- **Tobacco.** His documented tobacco use on the southern journey (CLM-0008) anchors a synthetic pipe-smoking history coded F17.290. The amount and the quit course are synthetic. Varenicline is used rather than nicotine replacement because nicotine narrows skin vessels, a frostbite concern the WMS guideline addresses.
- **Personal history of scurvy.** Coded Z86.39 (personal history of other nutritional disease) so that the vitamin C supplement has an indication. The dose (250 mg a day when no fresh food is available) is a clinical judgment between the IOM recommended intake and the upper limit; it is not a guideline dose.
- **Energy and altitude figures.** The 5,000 to 6,500 kcal a day target is a clinical estimate for man-hauling consistent with the physiology review (Halsey and Stroud, abstract); the exact figures in the full text were not read. The plateau altitude is described without a number.
- **Guideline versions.** The brief named the 2019 WMS frostbite guideline; it has been superseded by the 2024 update (PMID 38577729), which is cited. The 2019 hypothermia guideline is current and cited. The 2024 WMS altitude guideline is added for the plateau. The 2025 AHA/ACC guideline is cited for hypertension with the CMID monograph, which still lists the 2017 guideline and the Pooled Cohort Equations.
- **Family.** No family medical history is recorded. His wife and son appear only in the documented marriage claim and in a household line with no health facts. His advance care plan names agents without naming them.
- **Aliases.** "Captain Robert Falcon Scott" and "Captain Scott" are recorded as aliases; neither appears in any other record. The record names no other figure who has a record in the dataset.
- **Sick and preventive visits.** A periodic adult preventive visit (age 41) on March 10, 2026 (true frame March 10, 1910) and a same-day sick visit on May 5, 2026 (true frame May 5, 1910) were added. The sick visit is acute viral gastroenteritis with mild dehydration two weeks after starting losartan, chosen to teach sick-day rules for an ARB, oral rehydration, and the expected rise in creatinine with fluid loss; it does not echo a documented illness.
- **Focused visits.** The four chronic-care follow-ups (two weeks, six weeks, three months, and pre-departure) are documented as focused visits on the CMID hypertension monograph and the 2025 AHA/ACC guideline, with rows for tobacco, mental health screening, and the expedition plan from the WMS guidelines. All results, the ECG, audiometry, and the dental and eye findings are synthetic. One protocol deviation is recorded: the urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio due at diagnosis was collected about four weeks late, with a normal result, no harm, and a corrective action. The two-to-four-week electrolyte check after starting losartan fell at the sick visit; the repeat at the pre-departure visit is recorded as completing it.
- **Consultation.** The wilderness and expedition medicine consultation by the fictional Dr. Nwankwo-Lund on the second visit is synthetic. Its recommendations (no exercise test, lifestyle first then losartan with sick-day rules, no tobacco, the nutrition and vitamin C plan, cold-injury training, altitude precautions, the mental health and sleep plan, the evacuation plan, and dental, hearing and eye checks) are those the later visits carry out. Clinicians named in the episode are fictional; no decision is attributed to any real expedition member.
