Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Index age and whereabouts. The modern episode presents him at 41. The synthetic date of birth is 1984-06-06 (the true month and day), so the shift is 116 years. The index visit is January 13, 2026 (true frame January 13, 1910) and the last visit is May 26, 2026 (true frame May 26, 1910), about three weeks before the Terra Nova left Cardiff on June 15, 1910. A letter of November 24, 1909 places him at the expedition's London offices organizing and raising funds; his day-to-day whereabouts between January and June 1910 were not verified, and the episode assumes he stayed in or near London, which no fetched source contradicts. The roster gives his age as 43; he was 43 at his death.
  2. Thin chart. No documented health problem between the Discovery expedition and the Terra Nova expedition was found in a fetched source, so none is recorded. The brief's suggested presenting complaint of fatigue before the expedition is not supported and is not used. The documented health history is his scurvy on the Discovery southern journey and his own account of recovery. The modern episode is therefore built as a structured pre-expedition medical assessment, with one common, synthetic condition (stage 1 hypertension found at that assessment) to give the chronic-care visits a purpose, and a synthetic pipe-smoking history anchored to his documented tobacco use.
  3. Scurvy on the Discovery expedition. The brief asked whether he himself had signs. His own sledging diary records that he did: red gums and slight ankle swelling on January 14, 1903, improvement on more seal meat, swollen ankles again at the end of the journey, and swollen legs, sore gums and weeks of lassitude after the return (CLM-0005, CLM-0006, D). The October 1902 outbreak at winter quarters is recorded without naming the men affected. A companion's worse illness on the southern journey is mentioned only as the trigger for the change of diet, without his name or details.
  4. The 1912 deaths. Recorded as history only: the frostbite of his right foot from his own journal (CLM-0013), the deaths of all five men from cold and hunger (Royal Museums Greenwich), the last entry dated March 29, 1912, and the finding of the tent. His companions are not named and no health facts are given about them beyond the fact and order of their deaths. Interpretations of the causes (scurvy as a contributor, energy and vitamin C in the rations, unusually cold weather) are graded R. The death date is approximate, taken from the last journal entry. None of this appears in the modern episode.
  5. Roster leads not adopted. "Depression" and "depression in final days documented in diary" are not adopted: the last entries record a man facing death in a storm, not a diagnosis, and no source fetched makes a diagnosis. "Hypothermia (fatal)" is recorded only as the museum's "cold and hunger." The roster's "poor planning decisions" and its comparison with another expedition are value judgments, not clinical claims, and are not recorded. The rival expedition at the Pole is referred to without naming its leader.
  6. Hypertension. Synthetic. The office reading at intake is its own presenting problem (PRB-001, R03.0), resolved at the second visit when home readings confirm stage 1 hypertension (PRB-003, I10). At a low ten-year risk (about 5% by the Pooled Cohort Equations counting him as a smoker; PREVENT was not calculated exactly and is stated only as below the 7.5% threshold), lifestyle measures come first for three months, and losartan is started at the three-month visit when the home average is still 134/85. An ARB is preferred over a thiazide or a beta-blocker for cold and dehydration; this is a clinical judgment, not a guideline rule.
  7. Tobacco. His documented tobacco use on the southern journey (CLM-0008) anchors a synthetic pipe-smoking history coded F17.290. The amount and the quit course are synthetic. Varenicline is used rather than nicotine replacement because nicotine narrows skin vessels, a frostbite concern the WMS guideline addresses.
  8. Personal history of scurvy. Coded Z86.39 (personal history of other nutritional disease) so that the vitamin C supplement has an indication. The dose (250 mg a day when no fresh food is available) is a clinical judgment between the IOM recommended intake and the upper limit; it is not a guideline dose.
  9. Energy and altitude figures. The 5,000 to 6,500 kcal a day target is a clinical estimate for man-hauling consistent with the physiology review (Halsey and Stroud, abstract); the exact figures in the full text were not read. The plateau altitude is described without a number.
  10. Guideline versions. The brief named the 2019 WMS frostbite guideline; it has been superseded by the 2024 update (PMID 38577729), which is cited. The 2019 hypothermia guideline is current and cited. The 2024 WMS altitude guideline is added for the plateau. The 2025 AHA/ACC guideline is cited for hypertension with the CMID monograph, which still lists the 2017 guideline and the Pooled Cohort Equations.
  11. Family. No family medical history is recorded. His wife and son appear only in the documented marriage claim and in a household line with no health facts. His advance care plan names agents without naming them.
  12. Aliases. "Captain Robert Falcon Scott" and "Captain Scott" are recorded as aliases; neither appears in any other record. The record names no other figure who has a record in the dataset.
  13. Sick and preventive visits. A periodic adult preventive visit (age 41) on March 10, 2026 (true frame March 10, 1910) and a same-day sick visit on May 5, 2026 (true frame May 5, 1910) were added. The sick visit is acute viral gastroenteritis with mild dehydration two weeks after starting losartan, chosen to teach sick-day rules for an ARB, oral rehydration, and the expected rise in creatinine with fluid loss; it does not echo a documented illness.
  14. Focused visits. The four chronic-care follow-ups (two weeks, six weeks, three months, and pre-departure) are documented as focused visits on the CMID hypertension monograph and the 2025 AHA/ACC guideline, with rows for tobacco, mental health screening, and the expedition plan from the WMS guidelines. All results, the ECG, audiometry, and the dental and eye findings are synthetic. One protocol deviation is recorded: the urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio due at diagnosis was collected about four weeks late, with a normal result, no harm, and a corrective action. The two-to-four-week electrolyte check after starting losartan fell at the sick visit; the repeat at the pre-departure visit is recorded as completing it.
  15. Consultation. The wilderness and expedition medicine consultation by the fictional Dr. Nwankwo-Lund on the second visit is synthetic. Its recommendations (no exercise test, lifestyle first then losartan with sick-day rules, no tobacco, the nutrition and vitamin C plan, cold-injury training, altitude precautions, the mental health and sleep plan, the evacuation plan, and dental, hearing and eye checks) are those the later visits carry out. Clinicians named in the episode are fictional; no decision is attributed to any real expedition member.
  16. Scott died in 1912, so the recent-death review rule does not apply. Ordinary clinical and provenance reviews remain required.

Retrospective claims (R)

ClaimStatementCertainty
CLM-0007In his account of the Discovery expedition he wrote that scurvy was then believed to be ptomaine poisoning from tainted meat. A modern historian of medicine notes that many British doctors of the time accepted this view and overlooked the experiments of Holst and Frølich on dietary deficiency, so medical advice given to him before his second expedition would probably not have helped him prevent scurvy.probable
CLM-0016A historian of medicine concludes that scurvy may have contributed to the general ill health of the polar party in 1912, but that their deaths were more likely caused by a combination of frostbite, malnutrition and hypothermia.probable
CLM-0017Physiologists reviewing the expedition conclude that rations of a different composition, giving more energy for the same weight, might have saved some of the party, and that more vitamin C, though hard to achieve at the time, could have improved their chances.possible
CLM-0018Atmospheric scientists comparing the party's temperature records with modern automatic weather stations found that daily minimum temperatures from late February to March 19, 1912 were on average 10 to 20 °F below those measured in the same region and season since 1985, and that this cold likely contributed substantially to the party's exhaustion and frostbite.probable
