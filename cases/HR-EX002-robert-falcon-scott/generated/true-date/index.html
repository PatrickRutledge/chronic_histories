Teaching objective: Primary care and wilderness and expedition medicine for a 41-year-old naval officer, five months before he leads a long Antarctic expedition with a winter in a hut and months of hauling sledges on foot: a structured pre-expedition medical assessment (cardiovascular risk and fitness, dental, hearing, vision, vaccinations); an elevated office blood pressure confirmed as stage 1 hypertension with home readings, lifestyle measures first, and an angiotensin receptor blocker chosen over a thiazide or a beta-blocker for cold and dehydration, with sick-day rules; stopping pipe tobacco with varenicline, because nicotine raises frostbite risk; nutrition planning for energy needs of man-hauling in extreme cold and vitamin C to prevent the scurvy he had on an earlier journey; frostbite and hypothermia prevention and field first aid training; altitude on the polar plateau; sleep and mental health in isolation with screening and a support plan; and an evacuation and communication plan. Built on the Wilderness Medical Society frostbite (1908), hypothermia (1903) and altitude (1908) guidelines, the 1909 AHA/ACC high blood pressure guideline, the CMID hypertension monograph, and the USPSTF tobacco, depression and anxiety recommendations.

Social, functional, and mental status

Social history

  • S Naval officer and polar explorer; leading a scientific expedition to the Antarctic that sails in about five months, with a voyage south, a winter in a hut, and sledge journeys of several months on foot; for much of the year no evacuation is possible
  • S Married, with a young son; lives in London and works long days raising funds and organizing the expedition
  • S Tobacco: smokes a pipe, about 25 g of tobacco a week, more on long working days, since his early twenties; first pipe within an hour of waking; no cigarettes
  • S Alcohol: a glass or two of wine with dinner on three or four evenings a week (AUDIT-C 3); no other drugs
  • S Exercise: walks fast across London every day; no regular endurance or strength training since his last expedition
  • S Sleep: six to seven hours, shorter when the expedition work presses; no snoring reported

Functional status

  • S Independent and fully active; no limitation on stairs, walking or lifting

Mental status

  • S Alert, reserved and precise; determined, with some worry about money and time; PHQ-9 score 3 and GAD-7 score 4

Allergies and medications

Allergies

  • S No known allergy

Medications

  • S Varenicline 1 mg tablet, one tablet by mouth twice a day after a one-week starter titration (0.5 mg once daily for 3 days, then twice daily for 4 days)
  • S Losartan 50 mg tablet, one tablet by mouth daily; stop during vomiting, diarrhea or dehydration and restart when eating and drinking normally
  • S Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) 250 mg tablet, one tablet by mouth daily whenever no fresh food is available, packed in every sledging ration
