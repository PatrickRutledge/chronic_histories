Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Booker T. Washington was born into slavery on the James Burroughs plantation in Franklin County, Virginia, in 1856; the National Park Service timeline gives the date as April 5, 1856. [D]
  • Washington wrote in Up from Slavery (1901) that he was not sure of the exact date of his birth and estimated the year as 1858 or 1859; the April 5, 1856 date traces to a report by his brother John after his death and was adopted by the Tuskegee trustees. [D]
  • After emancipation in 1865 Washington moved with his mother, Jane, and his family to Malden, West Virginia, where he worked in the salt and coal mines; a National Park Service release gives his mother's death as 1876. [D]
  • Washington opened Tuskegee Institute in Tuskegee, Alabama, on July 4, 1881, at age 25, and led it for the rest of his life; in March 1915 he sponsored National Negro Health Week. [D]
  • In 1911 Washington was beaten in New York City and received two large gashes in his head and a torn ear, recovering without apparent sequelae; he had chronic indigestion, especially when traveling, and spent several days in 1911 at the Battle Creek Sanitarium. [D]
