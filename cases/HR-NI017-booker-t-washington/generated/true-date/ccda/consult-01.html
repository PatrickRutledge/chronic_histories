Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Booker T. Washington was born into slavery on the James Burroughs plantation in Franklin County, Virginia, in 1856; the National Park Service timeline gives the date as April 5, 1856. [D]
  • Washington wrote in Up from Slavery (1901) that he was not sure of the exact date of his birth and estimated the year as 1858 or 1859; the April 5, 1856 date traces to a report by his brother John after his death and was adopted by the Tuskegee trustees. [D]
  • After emancipation in 1865 Washington moved with his mother, Jane, and his family to Malden, West Virginia, where he worked in the salt and coal mines; a National Park Service release gives his mother's death as 1876. [D]
  • Washington opened Tuskegee Institute in Tuskegee, Alabama, on July 4, 1881, at age 25, and led it for the rest of his life; in March 1915 he sponsored National Negro Health Week. [D]
  • In 1911 Washington was beaten in New York City and received two large gashes in his head and a torn ear, recovering without apparent sequelae; he had chronic indigestion, especially when traveling, and spent several days in 1911 at the Battle Creek Sanitarium. [D]

Assessment

  • Chronic kidney disease stage G3b A3 (N18.32). Small, echogenic, symmetric kidneys with severe hypertension, left ventricular hypertrophy, and hypertensive retinopathy fit long-standing hypertensive nephrosclerosis, and the disease is chronic, not acute. Albuminuria of 820 mg/g is high for nephrosclerosis alone, so the response to treatment is the test: a fall of at least 30 percent supports the diagnosis. One week after losartan, creatinine is 2.2 mg/dL (up 5 percent), eGFR 34, and potassium 4.6 mmol/L, so blockade can be pushed further. His risk of progression is very high. [S]
  • Severe hypertension with hypertensive kidney disease (I12.9). 172/104 mmHg, improving but far above target. The symmetric kidneys, the absence of an abdominal bruit, and the small creatinine rise on losartan argue against renal artery stenosis, and his potassium is normal. Secondary causes need testing only if blood pressure stays above target on three agents including a diuretic. [S]
  • Mild anemia of chronic kidney disease (D63.1). Hemoglobin 12.6 g/dL with ferritin 210 ng/mL and transferrin saturation 26 percent: iron replete, and well above any threshold for treatment. [S]
  • Mineral and bone disorder and acid-base status of chronic kidney disease. PTH 92 pg/mL is mildly raised with normal calcium and phosphate, and bicarbonate 21 to 22 mmol/L is above the level that needs alkali. Neither needs treatment now. [S]

Data reviewed: Today's kidney ultrasound: both kidneys small (9.4 and 9.6 cm) and echogenic, symmetric, without hydronephrosis. Plan of Treatment

  • Increase losartan to 100 mg daily today and continue amlodipine 10 mg daily. [S]
  • Start dapagliflozin 10 mg daily today: eGFR is above 20 and albuminuria above 200 mg/g. It is for kidney protection, so continue it even if eGFR later falls below 20. [S]
  • Accept a creatinine rise of up to 30 percent from 2.1 mg/dL after these changes; check potassium and creatinine in about three weeks. [S]
  • If blood pressure is still above target on full doses of losartan and amlodipine, add chlorthalidone 12.5 mg daily, which works at his level of kidney function. [S]
  • Target a standardized office systolic pressure below 120 mmHg if tolerated, with below 130/80 as the first step. [S]
  • Avoid NSAIDs; sick-day rule to hold losartan, dapagliflozin, and any diuretic on days with vomiting, diarrhea, or poor fluid intake. [S]
  • Sodium below 2 g a day, with a plan for meals while traveling. [S]
  • Urine albumin-creatinine ratio once the regimen is complete. Urinalysis with microscopy and further testing for other kidney diseases if albuminuria does not fall on treatment or kidney function changes unexpectedly. [S]
  • Calcium, phosphate, PTH, and a baseline 25-hydroxyvitamin D at about six months; hemoglobin at least yearly. No alkali, vitamin D analog, or iron now. [S]
  • Pneumococcal and hepatitis B vaccination for chronic kidney disease. [S]
  • Calculate the kidney failure risk once eGFR has settled on full therapy. [S]
