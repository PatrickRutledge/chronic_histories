## Editorial resolutions

- **Birth date.** Washington did not know his birth date. The National Park Service timeline gives April 5, 1856, which traces to his brother's report after his death; Washington himself estimated 1858 or 1859. The case uses April 5, 1856 as preferred, records 1858 or 1859 as a conflicting alternative, and keeps April 5 in the synthetic date of birth (1967-04-05).
- **Index age and date.** The modern episode presents Washington at 59, his age at the documented November 2, 1915 admission. That date cannot be used in the modern frame because the publication cutoff is November 1, 2026, and visits must also fall before his death on November 14 when shifted back by 111 years. The index visit is May 4, 2026 (true frame May 4, 1915), in the months of failing health the obituary describes, and the last visit is October 26, 2026 (true frame October 26, 1915). The 1915 admission and his death therefore fall after the index date and are left out of the past medical history; they remain in the historical timeline.
- **Modern diagnosis.** Bright's disease (1915) and malignant hypertension with kidney failure (2006 review, graded **R**) are linked to, but not equated with, the synthetic diagnosis of hypertensive chronic kidney disease stage G3b A3. The synthetic episode deliberately presents him earlier and without a hypertensive emergency, so outpatient treatment is appropriate; the intake blood pressure of 212/128 echoes, but is not, the documented 220/150.
- **Biased attribution.** Dr. Bastedo's 1915 “racial characteristics” remark and the syphilis rumor it fed are recorded as a sensitive claim (**D**) only with the negative Wassermann result and the 2006 review alongside them. The synthetic workup does not repeat syphilis testing.
- **Mother's death.** The NPS release gives 1876; other accounts give 1874. The case uses 1876 with that caveat and records no cause.
- **Not used.** His father was an unidentified white man, so no paternal history is recorded. His wives' deaths are not family medical history. His children's later illnesses happened after the index date and are not recorded. A 2006 statement that he died of hypertensive cardiomyopathy and nephrosclerosis appeared in search summaries but was not confirmed in a fetched source and is not used.
- Washington died in 1915, so the recent-death review rule does not apply. Ordinary clinical, provenance, and safe-messaging reviews remain required.
