## Editorial resolutions

- **Recent-death rule.** Roundtree died in 2023. The case uses only what he made public himself (his 2000 *PEOPLE* interview as quoted in 2023, and a 2019 television interview), the facts in his obituaries, and his manager’s public statement on the cause of death. No physician of his made a public statement that was found. The *PEOPLE* article names his physician and his surgeon; neither is named in the record. No medical records, accounts by relatives, or speculation about undisclosed conditions are used. His tumor size, grade, receptor status, node count, stage, examination findings, results, germline testing, chemotherapy regimen, and preventive care are synthetic. The recent-death review rule applies.
- **Men get breast cancer.** The record says so plainly. It treats a new lump in a man as needing a prompt work-up whatever his earlier history. His own public words (that he “chalked it up” to adolescent gynecomastia, that he felt “embarrassment,” that he kept treatment secret to keep working, and that men “sweep it under the rug”) frame the teaching on stigma and delay. The record answers them with information, confidentiality, and peer support, and never frames the diagnosis itself as embarrassing or unmanly.
- **Single or double mastectomy.** Sources conflict. His own account in *PEOPLE* describes a modified radical mastectomy of the left breast. The Associated Press obituary and the encyclopedia article (citing a 2001 *USA Today* interview that was not read) say a double mastectomy. The conflict is recorded as claim CLM-0006 (certainty disputed) and is not resolved. The history does not take a side, and neither does the synthetic record. The operation is coded as mastectomy with axillary clearance for the left-sided cancer, and the pathology describes the left breast only. The consultant states that removal of the other breast is not needed for cancer control and is his choice. Later examination findings describe the chest without saying whether the right breast remains. No acquired-absence code is used.
- **Index age and dates.** He found the lump at the end of September 1993, at 51. The synthetic date of birth is July 9, 1975 (shift 33 years). The index visit on September 29, 2026 maps to September 29, 1993. Imaging and biopsy on September 30, the diagnosis visit and surgical consultation on October 5, surgery on October 7, the two-week review on October 20, the echocardiogram on October 23, and the preventive visit on October 27, 2026 map to the same days of 1993. This follows his account: diagnosis given in person four or five days after the doctor visit, and surgery a day or two later. The six months of chemotherapy he described begin after the episode ends and are recorded only as a plan. Past medical history holds only the gynecomastia at 13.
- **Delay.** The public accounts show that he saw his doctor within days, so the record does not invent a long delay. The teaching on delay rests on his own statements that he first attributed the lump to gynecomastia and felt embarrassment, and on his later account of secrecy. The Costa Rica location in the 2019 interview, and the week before he returned home, are recorded in the claims and are not used in the synthetic notes.
- **Synthetic clinical picture.** Everything clinical is synthetic and typical of breast cancer in men: a retroareolar, strongly hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative invasive ductal carcinoma. It is grade 3 and 1.6 cm, with 2 of 15 nodes involved (pT1c pN1a, stage IIA). The grade and the two nodes make the chemotherapy he described a standard choice, and make a radiation oncology review after chemotherapy appropriate. The regimen (doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide, then weekly paclitaxel, about six months) is synthetic and matches only the duration he gave. Other results are normal or mild (PHQ-9 5 and 6, normal echocardiogram, normal blood tests), so that nothing reads as a claim about his undisclosed health. No sensitive-content claim was needed.
- **Germline result.** Germline testing is sent at diagnosis, as the ASCO guideline calls for in every man with breast cancer. The result is deliberately left pending at the end of the episode. Any result, positive or negative, would read as a claim about his genetics, and about a possible link to the pancreatic cancer that caused his death, which no source makes.
- **Pancreatic cancer.** His death from pancreatic cancer in 2023 is recorded as history only. It is not linked to the breast cancer, and the record does not mention it in the synthetic episode.
- **Relatives.** No relative is named or given any statement or health fact. The record has no family history entries. The advance care planning names an unnamed agent.
