Assessment

  • Invasive ductal carcinoma of the left breast, clinical stage IIA (cT1c cN1 M0), ER and PR positive, HER2 negative, grade 3 (C50.122). Men get breast cancer. In men it is usually retroareolar, as here, and usually hormone receptor-positive. He has early, operable disease with a biopsy-proven axillary node. Staging scans are not recommended for clinical stage IIA without symptoms or abnormal blood tests. Because the tumor is small and hormone receptor-positive, surgery first is reasonable; chemotherapy before surgery was discussed as an alternative, and surgery first gives the full pathology to guide treatment afterwards. With so little breast tissue in men, mastectomy is the usual operation, and with a biopsy-proven node, a level I and II axillary dissection is standard. [S]
  • Germline testing. Germline testing should be offered to every man with breast cancer. BRCA2 is the gene most often found. A variant would raise the risk of a new cancer in the other breast and of prostate and pancreatic cancer, and would matter for his relatives. It need not delay surgery, because the operation on the cancer side is the same either way. [S]
  • Concern about privacy and stigma. He feels embarrassed by a breast cancer diagnosis and fears it could cost him work. These are common reasons men delay care or keep it secret. He came in promptly, which is the right thing, and his care can be kept confidential. [S]

Data reviewed: Diagnostic mammography and ultrasound of both breasts and both axillae; core biopsy pathology of the breast mass and the axillary node with ER, PR, HER2, and Ki-67; today's preoperative blood count and chemistry, all normal. [S]
