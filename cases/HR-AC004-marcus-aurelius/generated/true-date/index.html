Teaching objective: Evaluate chronic epigastric and lower chest pain after meals, with poor appetite and poor sleep, in a 53-year-old man who leads a large organization through a long field assignment, as current guidance advises: exclude a cardiac cause first (an ECG, a structured estimate of the chance of coronary disease, no troponin for chronic pain without an acute episode, and CT coronary angiography chosen by risk); then upper endoscopy with gastric biopsies for Helicobacter pylori, because chest pain without heartburn after a cardiac evaluation calls for objective testing for reflux; reach a diagnosis of erosive reflux esophagitis and say why peptic ulcer and functional dyspepsia were set aside; treat with an eight-week course of a proton pump inhibitor, timed meals and head-of-bed elevation, then step down to on-demand use; review, with respect and without judgment, an unregulated opium-containing herbal remedy he has taken for years (a DSM-5-TR assessment, a drug-monitoring check, a urine drug test with consent, naloxone, no sedatives or alcohol with it, and no forced taper, as the CDC 170 guideline advises), and recognize how opioids slow the gut; treat chronic insomnia with cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia rather than hypnotics; and attend to the strain of command. The modern episode is a teaching case, not a claim about the cause of his real complaints, and it does not attribute opioid addiction to him.
