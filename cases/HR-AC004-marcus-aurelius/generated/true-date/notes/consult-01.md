This explains his symptoms: burning and pressure after a large meal eaten shortly before lying down, night waking with a sour taste, and relief with antacid, with coronary disease excluded this morning. There is no ulcer, so peptic ulcer disease is excluded, and functional dyspepsia is not the diagnosis when a structural cause is found. Grade B esophagitis heals in most patients with eight weeks of a proton pump inhibitor. **S**
2. **Helicobacter pylori**. The rapid urease test is negative off acid suppression and the stomach looks normal; histology will confirm. If it is negative, no eradication therapy is needed. **S**
3. **Opium-containing remedy and the gut** (F11.90). Opioids slow stomach emptying and can disturb esophageal motility, and in long-term users can cause an opioid-induced esophageal dysfunction; they also cause his constipation. The esophagitis seen today is a reflux injury and should be treated in its own right.
