Assessment

  • Chronic coronary disease with stable exertional angina (I25.118). His typical exertional angina and risk factors gave an intermediate-to-high pretest probability. Coronary CT angiography is a recommended first test at his age and gives the anatomy. It shows a flow-limiting proximal LAD stenosis (CT-FFR 0.70), which explains the angina, with nonobstructive right coronary disease and normal left ventricular function. Without left main disease or a reduced ejection fraction, revascularization in chronic coronary disease improves angina and quality of life but has not been shown to lower the risk of death or heart attack compared with optimized medical therapy. The first step is therefore a higher beta-blocker dose. If angina persists, angiography and a stent to the LAD are reasonable, by shared decision, with dual antiplatelet therapy for six months after an elective stent. [S]
  • Past heart murmur and episodes of exertional breathlessness. There is no murmur today, and the echocardiogram shows normal valves, a normal right heart, and no interatrial shunt, so there is no structural explanation such as an atrial septal defect. The present picture is fully explained by the coronary disease. [S]
  • Fitness for a remote polar expedition. If he is free of angina on treatment and a symptom-limited exercise test shows good capacity without ischemia, he can sail with precautions. The main risks are an acute coronary event far from a catheter laboratory, bleeding on dual antiplatelet therapy after injury, and the extra cardiac demand of cold and heavy work. Stopping smoking is the single most important change he can make. [S]
