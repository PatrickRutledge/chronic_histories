Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Ernest Henry Shackleton was born on February 15, 1874, at Kilkea, County Kildare, Ireland, the son of Dr. Henry Shackleton, of a well-known Quaker family. [D]
  • On the southern sledge journey of the Discovery expedition he became unable to pull the sledge because of breathlessness and weakness; the collapse was attributed to scurvy and "a sort of asthma," and he was invalided home against his will in 1903. [D]
  • On the Nimrod expedition (1907 to 1909), its medical officer Eric Marshall recorded in his diary on January 20, 1908, that Shackleton was very unwell after pulling on a rope, and twice heard a pulmonary systolic murmur. [D]
  • On the Imperial Trans-Antarctic (Endurance) expedition of 1914 to 1917, of fifty-six men three died in the Antarctic: Mackintosh, Hayward, and Spencer-Smith of the Ross Sea party. No member of the Endurance's own party died. [D]
  • His colleague Dr. James McIlroy later recalled that on one occasion Shackleton would not undress and let him listen to his heart. [D]
  • He was a heavy cigarette smoker for most of his life. [D]
  • After the war he returned to London. In 1921, with John Quiller Rowett taking on practically the whole financial responsibility, he turned his planned Arctic expedition into the Shackleton-Rowett expedition to the South; it was organized and equipped within three months, its sailing was postponed beyond August 20 by a strike of ships' joiners and other delays, and the Quest left St. Katharine's Dock in London on September 17, 1921. [D]
