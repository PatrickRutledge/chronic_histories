Teaching objective: Recognize and treat recurrent severe major depression in a 58-year-old Black singer, actor and activist under sustained political persecution, as the APA 2019 and VA/DoD 2022 guidelines and the CMID depression monograph advise: PHQ-9 and GAD-7, a structured diagnostic interview, bipolar screening with the MDQ before an antidepressant, a structured suicide-risk assessment with a safety plan, means counseling and the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, and tests to exclude medical causes; treating realistic fears of surveillance as realistic rather than as paranoia, and attending to the documented racial disparities in the diagnosis of depression, its treatment and access to ECT; behavioral activation with measurement-based care; adequate trials of an SSRI (sertraline to 200 mg) and an SNRI (venlafaxine to 225 mg), then aripiprazole augmentation for treatment-resistant depression with metabolic monitoring; and an informed consent discussion about modern ECT (general anesthesia, right unilateral ultrabrief pulse, a defined course with cognitive testing) and its alternatives (rTMS and esketamine), decided against for now and kept available. The modern record contrasts careful, consented, measured modern ECT with what is documented about the many treatments he received years later, without asserting the disputed allegations about his care. It is a teaching case, not a claim about his real illness or care.
