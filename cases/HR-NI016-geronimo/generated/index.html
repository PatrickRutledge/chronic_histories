Teaching objective: Chronic post-traumatic stress disorder and alcohol use disorder in a 77-year-old Chiricahua Apache elder who has lived for twenty years under federal custody far from his homeland, seen at a tribal health clinic near Fort Sill. The episode covers trauma-informed, culturally safe care with an interpreter, his own words and choices first, and traditional healing offered alongside clinic care without probing his beliefs, as the Indian Health Service policy asks; screening with the PC-PTSD-5, the PCL-5 and the AUDIT, and confirmation with the CAPS-5 interview under the VA/DoD 2023 and APA 2017 PTSD guidelines; telling grief apart from prolonged grief disorder by DSM-5-TR criteria and cultural norms, without turning mourning into a diagnosis; a structured safety assessment with the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline; cognitive processing therapy adapted for an older man who does not read English; sertraline at a low dose with a sodium check; why prazosin was not started in an older man who had fallen, although the VA/DoD guideline suggests it for nightmares; no benzodiazepines; naltrexone with liver and kidney checks and acamprosate as the alternative; fall risk, and pneumococcal, high-dose influenza, RSV, zoster and Tdap vaccines at his age.
