## Assessment

1. **Post-traumatic stress disorder, chronic** (F43.12). On the CAPS-5 he meets criteria in all four symptom clusters (intrusion, avoidance, negative changes in thinking and mood, and arousal), with a severity score of 44, lasting decades and impairing his sleep and daily life. The diagnosis is chronic post-traumatic stress disorder, rooted in the killing of his family, years of combat and captivity. His Mini-Cog of 4 does not suggest dementia; cognition should be rechecked once he sleeps better and drinks less. **S**
2. **Grief and prolonged grief disorder**. Prolonged grief disorder requires, nearly every day for at least the past month, intense yearning for or preoccupation with the dead with further symptoms such as identity disruption, disbelief or intense emotional pain, beyond what his culture would expect. His grief is lasting and deep but is not that daily, consuming yearning, and the way he mourns follows the customs of his people. The criteria are not met. His grief should be respected as grief, not treated as a disorder; the distress that brings him in is driven by the post-traumatic symptoms. **S**
3. **Psychotherapy** (F43.12). The VA/DoD guideline recommends an individual, manualized trauma-focused psychotherapy (cognitive processing therapy, prolonged exposure or EMDR) over medicine, and the APA guideline strongly recommends cognitive processing therapy and prolonged exposure. Cognitive processing therapy suits him: it can be given without the written trauma account, its worksheets can be done aloud through an interpreter, and it does not require him to retell the killings in detail, which he has said he will not do. For a man of 77: a steady pace, shorter sessions when he tires, a consistent interpreter, and video sessions when he cannot come in, which the guideline supports. The therapist should ask how he wishes to speak of those who died and follow his lead. **S**
4. **Medicines** (F43.12). Sertraline, paroxetine and venlafaxine are recommended by the VA/DoD guideline; the APA guideline suggests fluoxetine, paroxetine, sertraline and venlafaxine. Paroxetine is best avoided at his age because of its anticholinergic effects. If he wants a medicine, sertraline 25 mg rising to 50 mg is my choice, with sodium checked within the first month because SSRIs can lower sodium in older adults, and attention to bleeding and falls. Benzodiazepines should not be used. The VA/DoD guideline suggests prazosin for nightmares associated with PTSD, though not for PTSD as a whole; in a man of 77 who has just fallen and is drinking, its first-dose and standing drops in blood pressure outweigh the benefit for now. Reconsider it, with standing pressures, if nightmares persist after therapy. **S**
5. **Alcohol use disorder, moderate** (F10.20). I agree with four DSM-5-TR criteria. The VA/DoD guideline advises that a substance use disorder should not delay trauma-focused therapy, so both are treated together. Naltrexone suits him: AST is under three times the upper limit, his kidneys are normal, and he takes no opioids; liver tests should be repeated at four to six weeks. Acamprosate, dosed by kidney function, is the alternative if naltrexone cannot be used. He has no history of withdrawal seizures or delirium and has cut down without symptoms, so no withdrawal medicine is needed. **S**

## Recommendations

1. Diagnosis: chronic post-traumatic stress disorder, confirmed on the CAPS-5; prolonged grief disorder not diagnosed.
2. Cognitive processing therapy, twelve weekly sessions with the clinic's behavioral health therapist and a consistent interpreter, without the written trauma account, with worksheets done aloud and video sessions when travel is hard.
3. PCL-5 at each visit to follow progress.
4. If he wants a medicine: sertraline 25 mg each morning, raised to 50 mg after a sodium check within the first month; venlafaxine as the alternative, watching blood pressure; avoid paroxetine at his age.
5. No benzodiazepines; prazosin not now, and reconsidered with standing blood pressure checks if nightmares persist after therapy.
6. Naltrexone 50 mg daily with liver tests at four to six weeks; acamprosate if naltrexone cannot be used; continue thiamine.
7. Treat the PTSD and the drinking together; do not wait for him to stop drinking.
8. Review safety at every visit; traditional healing alongside clinic care whenever he wishes.
