Teaching objective: Polypharmacy and prescription opioid and sedative use disorders from several prescribers, in a 39-year-old touring singer who comes in for fatigue, weight gain, constipation, sleepiness and high blood pressure. The episode covers a brown-bag medication reconciliation and prescription drug monitoring program review that finds two opioids, two benzodiazepines and an appetite suppressant from five prescribers; a nonjudgmental DSM-5-TR assessment; naloxone at the first visit; a single-prescriber agreement; buprenorphine started in the office under the ASAM 1968 guideline, with the guideline's advice that taking a benzodiazepine is not a reason to withhold it; the benzodiazepines combined into diazepam and tapered slowly under the 1973 joint guideline; a structured safety assessment with means safety and the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline; hypertension confirmed with home readings and treated under the 1973 AHA/ACC guideline; obesity treated without stigma with tirzepatide, which also treats obstructive sleep apnea; STOP-Bang and in-laboratory polysomnography because opioids make home testing unreliable; opioid-induced constipation; glaucoma that rules out phentermine and anticholinergic sleep aids; acute back pain managed on buprenorphine; and care planned around a performing and touring schedule.
