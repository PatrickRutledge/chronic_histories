Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Helen Keller was born on June 27, 1880, in Tuscumbia, Alabama. [D]
  • In February 1882, at 19 months, she had a febrile illness that her doctor, as she later wrote, called acute congestion of the stomach and brain and thought she would not survive; the fever left suddenly, but she never saw or heard again. [D]
  • The cause of the 1882 illness is unknown; scarlet fever, meningitis, and rubella have been proposed. [R]
  • From March 1887 her teacher, a graduate of the Perkins School for the Blind, taught her language by spelling words into her hand; she learned the manual alphabet and raised print for blind readers, and began to learn speech at 10. [D]
  • In her own account, others spoke to her by spelling into her hand with the single-hand manual alphabet while she rested her hand lightly on theirs; she spoke for herself, read the lips of some people by touch, and read and wrote braille. [D]
  • In 1904 she graduated cum laude from Radcliffe College, the first deafblind person to earn a college degree. [D]
  • From 1924 she worked as a public spokesperson for the American Foundation for the Blind for the rest of her life, and from 1946 she made world tours to 35 countries in 11 years on behalf of people with disabilities. [D]
  • A receipt dated September 1911 records the purchase of an artificial eye for her. [D]
  • She lived at Arcan Ridge in Easton, Connecticut, from the late 1930s for the rest of her life. [D]
  • Her father died in 1896 and her mother in 1921. [D]
