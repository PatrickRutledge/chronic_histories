## Reason for consultation

Comprehensive geriatric assessment, adapted for deafblindness, for a 78-year-old woman after a fall at home. Please look for any contributor we have missed and advise on fall prevention, cognitive baseline, and planning ahead. **S**

## History

Ms. Keller is seen at the request of Dr. Strand, through a qualified tactile interpreter, in a double-length appointment; she answered for herself throughout. She missed the last step where the back-step handrail ends three weeks ago and sat down hard without injury; she had no warning symptoms. She has had no other falls in the past year. She notices that rising from low chairs takes more effort than it did a few years ago. She has no urinary incontinence, no nocturia beyond once, no weight loss, and no change in the feeling in her fingers or feet, which she checks closely because she reads and receives language by touch. She sleeps seven hours, drinks a glass of wine with dinner a few times a week, and takes no medicines. She is independent in all activities of daily living; her assistant drives, shops, and reads her print mail. **S**

## Assessment

1. **Fall at home with increased fall risk** (Z91.81). A mechanical fall at an environmental hazard, made more probable by reduced hip and knee strength and balance. There is no orthostatic hypotension, no medicine, no neuropathy, no cognitive impairment, and no continence or nutritional problem. Vision is not a modifiable factor here; she moves by touch, rails, and a sighted guide. **S**
2. **Cognitive screening**. Standard screening tools depend on vision or hearing, and none is validated for people who are deafblind. A tactile interview with fingerspelled word recall, spoken verbal fluency, and serial sevens, interpreted by a qualified tactile interpreter, shows no impairment. Use the same method each year so that any change is measured against her own baseline. **S**
3. **Lifelong deafblindness** (H54.0X55). Touch is her main channel for language and reading, and it is fully intact. Her greatest risks in illness are communication barriers: being examined without warning, consent taken without a qualified interpreter, or isolation in hospital. **S**
4. **Bone health**. Low bone mass with FRAX risk below the treatment thresholds; medicine is not indicated. **S**

## Recommendations

1. Home physical therapy for progressive strength and balance training twice a week for 12 weeks, then a daily home program; a qualified tactile interpreter at the first sessions and whenever new exercises are taught.
2. Occupational therapy home visit: handrails on both sides of every step to the bottom step, tactile edge strips, clear routes, vibrating smoke and carbon monoxide alarms with a bed shaker, and a wearable alert button.
3. Repeat Timed Up and Go, chair stand, and balance tests after the exercise program.
4. No vitamin D supplement for fall prevention, since her level is adequate; dietary calcium of about 1,200 mg a day.
5. Repeat the tactile cognitive check yearly at the wellness visit by the same method.
6. Write a hospital communication passport in braille and print with her, and record her health care agent and wishes; review them at her annual wellness visit.
