## Historical evidence

1. American Foundation for the Blind. “Helen Keller Biography.” Supports her birth in Tuscumbia, Alabama, on June 27, 1880; that at 19 months she “became deaf and blind as a result of an unknown illness, perhaps rubella or scarlet fever”; her teacher's manual signing into her hand and her learning of the manual and raised-print alphabets; her wish to learn to speak at 10; her use of a braille typewriter; her 1904 degree cum laude, the first by a deafblind person; a stroke in 1960; and her death at Arcan Ridge on June 1, 1968, a few weeks short of her 88th birthday. https://www.afb.org/about-afb/history/helen-keller/biography-and-chronology/biography
2. Perkins School for the Blind. “Helen Keller Timeline.” Supports her birth date; the February 1882 illness, “scarlet fever or meningitis,” after which she was deaf and blind at 19 months; her teacher's arrival on March 3, 1887; her father's death in 1896 and her mother's in 1921; the 1904 Radcliffe degree; her work for the American Foundation for the Blind from 1924 for the rest of her life; the move to Arcan Ridge in Easton in 1939; world tours to 35 countries in 11 years from 1946; a stroke in 1961, after which she retired from public appearances and work; and her death at Arcan Ridge on June 1, 1968. https://www.perkins.org/resource/helen-keller-timeline/
3. Keller H. *The Story of My Life*. New York: Doubleday, Page; 1903. Project Gutenberg eBook 2397. Her own account of the February illness that “they called … acute congestion of the stomach and brain,” when “the doctor thought I could not live” (chapter 1), and of how others spoke to her by spelling into her hand with the single-hand manual alphabet while she spoke for herself and read lips by touch (chapter 13). https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2397
4. Selsdon H. “Helen Keller: An Artificial Eye.” AFB Blog, July 29, 2015. A receipt dated September 1911 for “1 Artificial Eye” sold to Miss Helen Keller; early photographs show an oddly shaped left eye. https://afb.org/blog/entry/helen-keller-artificial-eye
5. Jensen L. “Helen Keller in Connecticut: The Last Years of a Legendary Crusader.” Connecticut History, a CTHumanities project, May 31, 2021. Supports her home at Arcan Ridge in Easton (dated 1936 in this article) and her death there in her sleep on June 1, 1968. https://connecticuthistory.org/helen-keller-in-connecticut-the-last-years-of-a-legendary-crusader

## Editorial resolutions

- **Index age and the strokes.** Her stroke is dated 1961 by Perkins and 1960 by the AFB; its type is not recorded. The index date is set before both: the episode presents her at 78, on March 9, 2026 (true frame March 9, 1959). The synthetic date of birth is June 27, 1947, a shift of 67 years. The last chronic-care visit is September 14, 2026 (true frame September 14, 1959), and the annual wellness visit is October 19, 2026 (true frame October 19, 1959). The stroke appears only in the historical timeline, and no stroke or cerebrovascular history is placed in the modern record.
- **Cause of deafblindness.** The illness at 19 months and her deafblindness afterward are documented (**D**), including her own account of the contemporary label. The cause is unknown; scarlet fever, meningitis, and rubella are retrospective suggestions (**R**, possible) and are not coded. Blindness and deafness are coded with codes that do not imply a cause (H54.0X55 and H91.93).
- **Artificial eye.** The 1911 receipt documents one artificial eye; which eye, and whether an eye was removed, is not recorded. Reports that both eyes were replaced were not verified and are not used. The modern record says only that she wears an artificial eye.
- **Communication.** The synthetic care follows her own documented methods: tactile fingerspelling with the one-hand manual alphabet, her own speech, lip-reading by touch with familiar people, and braille. The modern refreshable braille display is synthetic. Every visit uses a qualified tactile interpreter booked by the clinic; her assistant is never used as the interpreter, and at the sick visit she chose to wait for the on-call interpreter.
- **No chronic disease invented.** No chronic condition is added. The episode's problems are her documented deafblindness, the artificial eye, a synthetic fall, and a synthetic cystitis. The bone density result (low bone mass below treatment thresholds) is synthetic and not coded as a diagnosis.
- **Real people.** Her teacher, her long-time secretary-companion, and other real people are not named in the modern record, and no statement or health fact is attributed to them. The live-in assistant and the health care agent in the modern record are fictional.
- **Language about disability.** The record describes her deafblindness in plain clinical and functional terms and treats her communication methods as normal ways of communicating. It avoids triumph-over-tragedy wording. Her stated choices, including no interest in hearing technology evaluation, are recorded and respected.
- **Conflicting dates.** The move to Arcan Ridge is 1939 at Perkins and 1936 in the Connecticut History article; the claim says the late 1930s. The death place is given as Arcan Ridge in Easton; obituary datelines named Westport.
- **Sick and wellness visits.** An unscheduled sick visit on August 3, 2026 (true frame August 3, 1959) for acute uncomplicated cystitis, and a subsequent annual wellness visit on October 19, 2026, at 79. The cystitis does not echo a documented illness; it was chosen because it is common at her age, tests same-day interpreter access, and connects to night-time fall risk. The wellness visit's prior initial visit with a previous physician, the earlier colonoscopy, and the health care agent are synthetic.
- **Focused visits.** The three chronic-care follow-ups (two weeks, about three months, and six months) are documented as focused visits on CDC STEADI with the USPSTF falls recommendation, because the CMID has no falls monograph and covers no condition in her record. Each lists the STEADI items, bone health, and communication access with interval and status. One deviation is recorded as `deferred`, not filled: the occupational therapy home hazard visit was not done by the three-month review because of a waiting list; the main hazard had been corrected by an extended handrail, there was no fall, and the visit took place in July. Synthetic findings introduced: the fall, the test scores (Timed Up and Go, chair stand, tandem stance), strength grades, and normal hand sensation.
- **Consultation.** The geriatric medicine consultation on March 23, 2026 is synthetic. It answers the four referral questions; its recommendations (home physical therapy with an interpreter, an occupational therapy home visit, repeat testing after the program, no vitamin D supplement, a yearly tactile cognitive check by the same method, and a hospital communication passport) match what the later visits show.
- Keller died in 1968, so the recent-death review rule (deaths after 1990) does not apply. Ordinary clinical, provenance, and safe-messaging reviews remain required.
