Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Ida B. Wells was born enslaved on July 16, 1862, in Holly Springs, Mississippi, during the Civil War. [D]
  • In the yellow fever epidemic of 1878, when she was 16, both her parents and an infant brother died; she left school and took on the care of her surviving younger siblings, teaching to support them. [D]
  • She was an investigative journalist and editor who documented lynching in the United States, notably in A Red Record (1895), and campaigned against it for the rest of her life; she was also a suffragist and civil rights leader. [D]
  • On June 27, 1895, she married the Chicago attorney Ferdinand L. Barnett, kept her own name alongside his, and settled in Chicago; they had four children. [D]
  • She signed the call for the 1909 conference that led to the National Association for the Advancement of Colored People and is considered one of its founders, though she was left off the list of its founding committee and later drew away from it. [D]
  • In 1930 she ran, unsuccessfully, for the Illinois State Senate. [D]
  • She began writing her autobiography in her last years; it was published after her death as Crusade for Justice: The Autobiography of Ida B. Wells, edited by her youngest daughter, Alfreda M. Duster. [D]
