Teaching objective: Chronic kidney disease found in time in a 68-year-old Black woman, a journalist and civic leader in Chicago, who comes in tired with swollen ankles. The teaching contrast is her documented death from kidney failure after a brief illness: here the disease is found, staged and treated years before kidney failure. The episode covers the race-free CKD-EPI 2021 equations (creatinine, then creatinine with cystatin C) and the history of race-based eGFR delaying the recognition and referral of Black patients, as the NKF-ASN Task Force described; staging on the KDIGO 2024 heat map with the urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and the kidney failure risk equation; finding the cause (home blood pressure, diabetes screening, urinalysis, kidney ultrasound, and a monoclonal protein screen); APOL1 genetic testing offered with counseling and left to her choice; an ARB and an SGLT2 inhibitor with potassium and creatinine checks; blood pressure control; a statin; stopping naproxen; anemia and mineral and bone checks; vaccinations; a nephrology consultation; and shared decisions about kidney replacement options should the disease progress, under the KDIGO 2024 guideline, the 2025 AHA/ACC blood pressure guideline and the CMID chronic kidney disease monograph.
