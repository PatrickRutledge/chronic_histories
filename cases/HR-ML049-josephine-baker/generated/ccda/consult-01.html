Assessment

  • Cause of the breathlessness (T45.525A). Ticagrelor-related breathlessness, with some deconditioning. The pattern is typical: air hunger at rest beginning within days of the start, without effort-related chest symptoms, with a normal oxygen saturation, clear lungs, normal filling pressures and no anemia. It is not a sign of heart damage or of a blocked stent, but it is distressing and leads some patients to stop their medicine. There is no sign of heart failure or of ongoing ischemia. [S]
  • Heart function and further testing (I25.10). The ejection fraction of 52% with inferior hypokinesis matches the infarct in the right coronary territory; function is at the lower limit of normal. No further imaging or stress testing is needed now. Repeat the echocardiogram only if her symptoms change. [S]
  • Antiplatelet therapy (I25.10). Ticagrelor is preferred over clopidogrel after an acute coronary syndrome, but changing to clopidogrel is appropriate when ticagrelor causes breathlessness that affects adherence. Change with a 600 mg loading dose of clopidogrel 24 hours after the last ticagrelor dose, then 75 mg daily, without a gap. Her bleeding risk is not high, so dual antiplatelet therapy should continue for twelve months from the infarction, then aspirin 81 mg alone. [S]
  • Beta-blocker, ACE inhibitor and targets (I10). Metoprolol succinate is appropriate for the first year after the infarction and, because she has high blood pressure, beyond it. Lisinopril is indicated for high blood pressure; raise it to 10 mg for a target below 130/80 mmHg, with potassium and creatinine one to two weeks later. Hormone therapy is not used to prevent heart disease. Women are less often given full secondary prevention and cardiac rehabilitation after a heart attack, and more often have breathlessness, tiredness or other symptoms besides chest pain; her plan should be complete, and her account of her symptoms taken seriously.
