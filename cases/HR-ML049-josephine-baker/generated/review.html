Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Sources. The case relies on the French Ministry of the Armed Forces, the National Park Service, a La Croix dossier reproduced by the Château des Milandes, Women in World History, the Women's History Network, the INA archive, Biography.com, a Library of Congress research guide and the National Women's History Museum. The National Park Service page is hosted by a historic site named for another figure in the dataset; the publisher is given as the National Park Service only, so that name does not appear.
  2. Recent-death rule. She died in 1975, so the rule does not apply. A sick visit is included.
  3. Index age and dates (correction to the brief). The brief proposed an index at 57 to 58 in 1964, after a heart attack often said to have happened that year. No reliable source read dates a heart attack to 1964; the claim appears only on low-quality sites. La Croix records a heart attack on the day of her eviction from Les Milandes in March 1969, called a new one, with a hospital admission in Périgueux. The index visit is therefore April 14, 2026, which maps to April 14, 1969, when she was 62, three weeks after the modern heart attack placed on March 24 (mapping to March 1969). The synthetic date of birth is 1963-06-03, a shift of 57 years. The visits on April 28, May 26, June 16, July 21, September 15 and October 20, 2026 map to the same days of 1969. Where she was day to day in 1969 is not documented in the sources read; nothing places a visit in conflict with a documented event. The presenting pattern chosen is the early post-infarction follow-up visit with breathlessness on exertion.
  4. Morocco illness. The long stay in hospital in Casablanca from June 1941 to December 1942, with five operations, is D (La Croix, with Women in World History agreeing that she was critically ill and had several operations). The pregnancy loss is disputed (stillbirth in Women in World History, miscarriage in the Women's History Network), and the hysterectomy, peritonitis and septicemia rest on the Women's History Network alone, so that claim (CLM-0008) is R, certainty “possible,” and marked sensitive. Whether her ovaries were removed is not recorded in any source read.
  5. Surgical menopause (correction to the brief). The brief called the hysterectomy a documented surgical menopause. A hysterectomy alone does not cause menopause unless the ovaries are removed or lose their blood supply, and the sources do not say. The record lists the absence of the uterus (Z90.710, verification provisional, linked to CLM-0008) and treats a possible early menopause as a risk-enhancing factor for heart disease and osteoporosis, citing the AHA statement on the menopause transition. Hormone therapy is not used for prevention.
