Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • She was born Freda Josephine McDonald on June 3, 1906, in St. Louis, Missouri, to Carrie McDonald and Eddie Carson, into a family with little money. [D]
  • She grew up in poor neighborhoods of St. Louis and began working as a child; she married twice before the age of 16 and kept the surname of her second husband, William Howard Baker. [D]
  • She danced in New York in the musicals Shuffle Along and The Chocolate Dandies, came to Paris with her troupe in 1925 to perform at the Théâtre des Champs-Elysées, and became the most famous and highest-paid American performer in France. [D]
  • She married a Frenchman in 1937 and became a French citizen; the National Park Service gives 1936. [D]
  • During the Second World War she worked for the intelligence services of Free France with the rank of sub-lieutenant, gathering information and carrying messages written in invisible ink on her sheet music; on August 18, 1961, she received the Legion of Honor and the Croix de guerre with palm. [D]
  • She bought a château at Les Milandes in the Périgord, where she made a "village of the world" and raised twelve children of different nationalities and religions whom she had adopted during her tours with Jo Bouillon, her husband; the children were called the "Rainbow Tribe". [D]
  • In North Africa during the war she was taken urgently to hospital in Casablanca, where she stayed in bed from June 1941 to December 1942, had five operations and was several times reported to be dying; she continued her intelligence work from her room. [D]
  • The illness followed the loss of a pregnancy in 1941, given as a stillbirth in Casablanca by one source and as a miscarriage by another; the second account says a severe infection required a hysterectomy, after which she developed peritonitis and septicemia. La Croix says she was unable to have children. [R]
  • At the March on Washington for Jobs and Freedom on August 28, 1963, she was one of the few women to speak; she came in her military uniform with her decorations. [D]
  • Les Milandes ran into heavy debt; she toured repeatedly to pay it, and on June 2, 1964, she made a televised appeal for donations, saying her work no longer covered debts of about 200 million old francs. [D]
  • In March 1969, after a court ordered her eviction, she was removed from the château and sat for seven hours in the rain on its steps; she had a new heart attack and ended the day in hospital in Périgueux. Les Milandes was sold at auction that year. [D]
