Teaching objective: Secondary prevention in the first months after a non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction in a 62-year-old Black woman, an entertainer, veteran and civil rights activist under heavy financial strain, seen three weeks after a stent with breathlessness on exertion. The episode covers the care women, and Black women in particular, are less likely to receive after a heart attack, as AHA statements and registry data show: a full cardiology review, echocardiography, cardiac rehabilitation and every guideline-directed medicine. It teaches dual antiplatelet therapy for twelve months, recognizing ticagrelor-related breathlessness and switching to clopidogrel with a loading dose rather than stopping; high-intensity atorvastatin with an LDL goal below 70 mg/dL and a single lipoprotein(a) measurement; a beta-blocker for the first year and an ACE inhibitor for high blood pressure, with potassium and kidney checks (one missed check recorded honestly); depression screening after a heart attack with the PHQ-2 and PHQ-9, as the AHA advises; a nosebleed on dual antiplatelet therapy managed without stopping it; a graded return to the stage after an exercise test; the hysterectomy of her thirties as a possible early menopause and a risk-enhancing factor; and trauma-informed, respectful care that takes her account of her body seriously, under the 1966 AHA/ACC chronic coronary disease guideline, the 1968 ACC/AHA acute coronary syndromes guideline and the AHA statement on myocardial infarction in women.

Social, functional, and mental status

Social history

  • S Entertainer, singer and dancer for more than forty years, in Paris and on tours around the world; has stopped performing while she recovers and wants to return to the stage
  • S Veteran of the wartime Resistance, decorated for intelligence work, and a civil rights activist who has spoken against segregation all her life
  • S Mother of twelve adopted children, some still at school; the family recently lost the home and estate where she raised them, and now lives in a rented house
  • S Finances: large debts from the estate; income only from performing, which has stopped since the heart attack; she is anxious about the cost of medicines and of the children's needs
  • S Tobacco: never. Alcohol: rarely, a glass of champagne on occasion. No drug use
  • S Diet: irregular meals around rehearsals and travel, often late at night; enjoys cooking for her family; little salt added
  • S Says she waited until the morning to go to hospital, thinking the pressure in her chest was the strain of the eviction; asks that her account of her body be taken seriously and that she be told plainly what each medicine is for

Functional status

  • S Independent in all daily activities; breathless after one flight of stairs or ten minutes of brisk walking, which before the heart attack never limited her; has not danced since

Mental status

  • S Alert, warm and direct; worried about her family and her debts, and grieving the loss of her home; sleeps poorly; PHQ-9 score 7 with no thoughts of self-harm

Procedures and immunizations

Procedures

  • S Coronary angiography and percutaneous coronary intervention in the hospital for a non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction: 90% stenosis of the mid right coronary artery treated with one drug-eluting stent; mild disease elsewhere. Performed at a regional hospital before she established care
  • S Twelve-lead electrocardiogram: sinus rhythm at 64 per minute, small Q waves in II, III and aVF from the recent inferior infarction, no acute ST change, normal QT
  • S Outpatient cardiac rehabilitation, phase 2: 36 sessions over twelve weeks with exercise training, nutrition, stress management and psychological support; all sessions completed
  • S Transthoracic echocardiography: ejection fraction 52%, hypokinesis of the basal and mid inferior wall, normal right ventricle, no thrombus, normal valves, normal estimated filling pressures, no pericardial effusion
  • S Symptom-limited exercise treadmill test on her usual medicines (Bruce protocol): 9 minutes, about 10 METs, peak heart rate 132 per minute on metoprolol, normal blood pressure response (124/76 to 172/80), no angina, no ischemic ST change, no arrhythmia
  • S Advance care planning conversation, about 20 minutes, with her consent
