Teaching objective: Mild dementia due to early-onset Alzheimer's disease in a 63-year-old retired boxer with type 2 diabetes and hypertension, seen in Los Angeles with his care partner. The episode follows the 1982 Alzheimer's Association DETeCD-ADRD guideline: a structured history from him and an informant, the MoCA, MMSE and CDR, function, reversible causes (B12, TSH, depression, anticholinergic medicines, sleep apnea and hearing), brain MRI, and amyloid PET to confirm the cause; how Alzheimer's disease differs from traumatic encephalopathy syndrome, a research diagnosis for people with long exposure to repetitive head impacts; why he is not a candidate for lecanemab or donanemab under the appropriate use recommendations (more than four cerebral microbleeds), and why APOE genotyping was not needed for that decision; donepezil, its pulse and stomach side effects, and a dose step back; deintensifying diabetes treatment under the ADA Standards for older adults (stopping the sulfonylurea; a goal under 8.0%) with sick-day rules for metformin; blood pressure; driving and firearm safety; hearing aids; caregiver support, respite and the Medicare GUIDE model he may join once on Medicare; and advance care planning while he can take part.
