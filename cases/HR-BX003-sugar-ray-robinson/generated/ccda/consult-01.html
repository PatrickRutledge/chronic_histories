Assessment

  • Stage and pattern (R41.3). Mild dementia (global CDR 1, MMSE 22, MoCA 18) with an amnestic presentation and progressive course, independent in personal care but dependent for complex tasks. With bilateral hippocampal atrophy, this is most consistent with Alzheimer's disease beginning before 65. Depression, thyroid disease, B12 deficiency and anticholinergic drugs have been excluded or addressed; sleep apnea risk is low; hearing loss is a treatable contributor. [S]
  • Traumatic encephalopathy syndrome and vascular disease (R41.3). Twenty-five years of professional boxing is substantial exposure to repetitive head impacts. Under the 2021 NINDS consensus criteria, traumatic encephalopathy syndrome is a research diagnosis for people with that exposure and progressive cognitive or neurobehavioral impairment; it is not meant to guide routine clinical care, and the underlying disease, chronic traumatic encephalopathy, can be confirmed only after death. He has no neurobehavioral dysregulation or parkinsonism, and the amnestic pattern with hippocampal atrophy points to Alzheimer's disease; repetitive head impacts may still add to his difficulty, as the microbleeds and cavum septi pellucidi suggest past injury. Vascular change is slight and does not explain his dementia. [S]
  • Biomarkers and APOE. Confirming Alzheimer's pathology will settle the diagnosis, which he wants, inform counseling and planning, and distinguish it from a mainly traumatic process. Amyloid PET is the test I advise. APOE genotyping informs the risk of amyloid-related imaging abnormalities for anti-amyloid therapy; since he is not a candidate, it would not change his care, and it carries implications for relatives. It can be offered with genetic counseling if he wishes. [S]
  • Anti-amyloid therapy. By stage and MMSE (20 to 30 for donanemab, 22 to 30 for lecanemab) he would otherwise fit, and he takes no anticoagulant. But both the lecanemab and donanemab appropriate use recommendations exclude people with more than four cerebral microbleeds on the pre-treatment MRI because of the risk of bleeding and amyloid-related imaging abnormalities. With six microbleeds he does not qualify, whatever his amyloid status. [S]
  • Diabetes and blood pressure (E11.9). Hemoglobin A1c 6.4% on glipizide is tighter than needed: for an older adult with cognitive impairment the ADA Standards advise a goal under 8.0% and avoiding drugs that cause hypoglycemia, which harms memory and raises the risk of falls. Blood pressure is well controlled. [S]
  • Treatment, safety and support. If amyloid is confirmed, a cholinesterase inhibitor offers modest benefit: donepezil 5 mg, then 10 mg after four to six weeks if tolerated, after a baseline ECG, watching for nausea, loose stools, vivid dreams and a slow pulse. Memantine is for moderate dementia. With CDR 1 and getting lost while driving, his driving risk is high and he should not drive. A firearm should not be kept in the home of a person with dementia. Hearing aids, exercise, social activity and support for his care partner, including respite, matter as much as medicine. [S]
