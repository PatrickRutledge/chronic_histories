Teaching objective: Trauma-informed primary care for a 37-year-old professional heavyweight boxer who grew up in poverty with adverse childhood experiences, spent time in prison as a young man, and does not read or write, seen after a high blood pressure reading at a boxing commission check. The episode follows the 2025 AHA/ACC blood pressure guideline and the CMID hypertension monograph: out-of-office confirmation by 24-hour ambulatory monitoring before the diagnosis, baseline tests including urine albumin and an ECG, an echocardiogram for stage 2 hypertension, a single-pill combination of two first-line drugs, and potassium and creatinine checks; the AHA/ACC eligibility statement for competitive athletes with hypertension; alcohol and drug screening (AUDIT, DAST-10) with nonjudgmental motivational counseling, naltrexone for alcohol use disorder, and a structured safety assessment; why naloxone is not prescribed when there is no opioid use; ACE-informed history taken with choice and control; health literacy universal precautions (spoken and picture-based plans, teach-back, read-aloud consent); cardiovascular risk; and preventive care. It makes no claim about his real health and does not draw on the disputed circumstances of his death.
