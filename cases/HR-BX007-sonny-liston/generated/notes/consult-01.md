## Assessment

1. **Hypertension confirmed, stage 2** (I10). Ambulatory averages are well above the thresholds for hypertension (daytime 135/85 and nighttime 120/70 mmHg) and agree with his office readings, so this is sustained hypertension in the stage 2 range, not a white-coat effect. **S**
2. **Mild concentric left ventricular hypertrophy**. A wall thickness of 12 mm with normal relaxation can be seen with heavy strength training, but concentric thickening with high ambulatory pressures most likely reflects hypertension, at least in part. It is mild, with normal function and no sign of another cardiomyopathy. A repeat echocardiogram after about a year of control will show whether it regresses. **S**
3. **Secondary causes**. Normal potassium, kidney function, urine albumin and thyroid tests, no bruits or radio-femoral delay, and no drugs or supplements that raise blood pressure. Alcohol is the one contributor found, and cutting down should help. Screening for primary aldosteronism is not needed now; it would be if his pressure proved resistant or his potassium fell. A sleep study is not needed with a STOP-Bang of 2. **S**
4. **Alcohol and blood pressure** (F10.20). Heavy drinking raises blood pressure, and reducing it is part of his treatment; I support Dr. Achebe-Whitford's plan. **S**

## Recommendations

1. Start a single-pill combination of amlodipine 5 mg and valsartan 160 mg each morning: two first-line drugs, as the 2025 guideline recommends for stage 2 hypertension, with a calcium channel blocker as part of the first combination, and an angiotensin receptor blocker rather than an ACE inhibitor because angioedema is more common with ACE inhibitors in Black patients.
2. Check potassium and creatinine in two to four weeks; if his pressure is still above 130/80 mmHg at four weeks, raise amlodipine to 10 mg in the same combination.
3. Home monitoring with a validated upper-arm monitor with a large cuff and a talking, large-number display, two readings each morning sent to the clinic in whatever way works for him.
4. Avoid diuretics for now, as boxers often dehydrate to make weight; avoid beta-blockers, which blunt exercise capacity and are not first-line; avoid decongestants, anti-inflammatory pain pills and stimulant or weight-cutting supplements.
5. Lower-salt eating and reduced drinking, alongside his training.
6. No bouts until his blood pressure is controlled; then clearance by the commission physician with his readings, and a blood pressure check before each bout. He can keep training meanwhile.
7. Repeat echocardiogram after about a year of control to look for regression of the hypertrophy; lipids, glucose and urine albumin yearly.
