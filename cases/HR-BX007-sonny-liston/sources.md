## Historical evidence

1. Cochran RB. “Sonny Liston (1932?–1970),” *Encyclopedia of Arkansas* (Central Arkansas Library System). Supports his birth on May 8, “probably 1932,” in rural St. Francis County to Tobe and Helen (Baskin) Liston, African American sharecroppers; “twenty-two siblings and half-siblings” in one account; heavy farm work and “many beatings”; “virtually no schooling”; his move to St. Louis at thirteen; a 1950 sentence of five years in the Missouri State Penitentiary and parole in 1952, with boxing begun in prison at the urging of Catholic chaplains; the title won in Chicago on September 25, 1962, by a first-round knockout and lost in Miami Beach on February 25, 1964, when he did not answer the bell for the seventh round, claiming a shoulder injury; the move to Las Vegas in 1966; his last fight on June 29, 1970; a record of “fifty wins, thirty-nine by knockout, and four losses”; that his wife found him dead on January 5, 1971, that “He had been dead for some time,” and that the coroner chose December 30, 1970, as the date of death; and “rumors of drug peddling, heroin use, and murder, but no answers.” https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/sonny-liston-2569/
2. McLean M. “Liston, Charles (‘Sonny’),” *Scribner Encyclopedia of American Lives, Thematic Series: Sports Figures* (via Encyclopedia.com). Supports 8 May 1932 with “some debate concerning the exact date of his birth” and other suggested years of 1927 or 1928; the ninth of ten children of a tenant farmer; that his father beat him regularly and had him working full-time in the cotton fields by the age of eight; that he “never learned to read or write”; the Missouri State Penitentiary in Jefferson City, a prison chaplain who encouraged him to box, and parole on 30 October 1952; his professional debut on 2 September 1953; a broken jaw in a 1954 loss; the heavyweight title from 1962 to 1964 and a second loss in 1965; the International Boxing Hall of Fame in 1991; heavy drinking, reckless driving and frequent arrests; and that his death was “ascribed to pulmonary congestion and heart failure,” with traces of heroin, “needle marks on his arm,” and heroin and marijuana in the house, whether natural, accidental or intentional “remains a mystery.” https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/liston-charles-sonny-0
3. Tallent A. “The Mysterious Birth of Sonny Liston,” *The Sweet Science*, February 2, 2005 (read through the site's public feed). Supports that Arkansas did not require birth certificates until 1965; that he filed for one in 1953 giving May 8, 1932; that his mother gave several dates; that the 1930 census card for his family does not list him, so he “could not have been born before late 1930”; and that he gave his age as 22 in 1950 and testified in 1960 that he was born in 1933. https://tss.ib.tv/boxing/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/1639
4. Assael S. “After Sonny Liston died,” *Andscape*, October 13, 2016. Used only for the coroner's conclusion of natural causes, the heroin metabolites in his blood, and friends' rejection of an accidental overdose. The author's own argument about the death is not adopted. https://andscape.com/features/after-sonny-liston-died
5. HISTMED Master Roster v3. Internal project source; not treated as independent corroboration.

## Editorial resolutions

- **Sources.** The case relies on the *Encyclopedia of Arkansas*, the *Scribner Encyclopedia of American Lives*, *The Sweet Science* (birth-date evidence) and *Andscape* (the coroner's conclusion). The International Boxing Hall of Fame site refused the connection; the *New York Times* could not be fetched; Sports Illustrated's archive returned errors; the *Las Vegas Review-Journal* refused access; and Nick Tosches's and Paul Gallender's biographies and Shaun Assael's book were not read. Wikipedia and forum pages were read only for leads and are not cited.
- **Thin chart (rule 26).** Little about his health is documented: a broken jaw in 1954, a shoulder injury cited in 1964, a description of heavy drinking, and the findings at his death. The record does not pad this. The modern episode is built around a common, defensible condition (hypertension found at a check, in a heavyweight boxer in his late thirties) with the screening and preventive care his documented life calls for: alcohol and drug use, adverse childhood experiences, low literacy and cardiovascular risk. Hypertension, the echocardiogram findings and every value are synthetic; no hypertension is documented in his life.
- **Birth date (rule 24).** The brief's “probably around 1930 to 1932” is supported, and the sources do disagree. The preferred date is May 8, 1932, the date he filed in 1953 and used for official purposes, which both encyclopedias use with a question. Conflicting evidence is recorded: his family's 1930 census card does not list him (so probably born no earlier than late 1930; the roster gives 1930), his mother gave several dates, he gave his age as 22 in 1950 and testified to 1933 in 1960, and some sources suggest 1927 or 1928. The 1940 census is cited only in pages that could not be checked and is not used. The synthetic date of birth keeps May 8: 1989-05-08 (shift 57).
- **Index age (judgment call).** The brief suggested about 38 to 40 in 1968 to 1970. With the preferred birth year of 1932, those years map to ages 36 to 38, and an episode at 38 would run from May to October 1970, within weeks of his death. He is therefore 37 at the index visit on June 9, 2026, which maps to June 9, 1969, in Las Vegas (where he lived from 1966) while he was still boxing; the visits on June 9, June 23, July 21, August 11, September 8, September 22 and October 20, 2026 (and the ambulatory monitoring on June 17 and 18) map to the same days of 1969, more than a year before his death. By the census evidence he may have been a year or two older.
- **Name and alias.** The display name is Sonny Liston; the aliases are Charles Liston and Charles L. Liston, which appear in no other record. The opponent to whom he lost the title in 1964 and 1965 has a record in this dataset and is not named: the record says only that he lost the title and the rematch. The champion he beat in 1962, his trainers and other boxers are not named. No other figure with a record is named.
- **Death and the cause (rule: record as R; adopt no theory).** The documented facts (found on January 5, 1971; date of death set by the coroner as December 30, 1970; death ascribed to pulmonary congestion and heart failure with a finding of natural causes; traces of heroin and a needle mark; heroin and marijuana in the house) are recorded once, in plain words (CLM-0007, D, sensitive). The dispute (natural causes, accidental overdose or homicide) is CLM-0008, R, disputed, and no theory, including murder, is adopted. The brief's “lung congestion and heart failure” matches the encyclopedia's wording, “pulmonary congestion and heart failure.” These claims are linked only to the death, after the episode, and do not appear in intake documents.
- **Substance use (judgment calls).** The roster's “probable heroin dependency” is recorded as R, disputed (CLM-0009), and not adopted: no source read documents heroin or other opioid use during his life. In the modern episode the DAST-10 is 0 and no opioid use is reported or suspected, so naloxone is not prescribed; the plan states that naloxone and overdose education would follow any reported or suspected opioid use. The documented description of heavy drinking (CLM-0006) anchors a synthetic AUDIT of 16 and a diagnosis of moderate alcohol use disorder (four DSM-5-TR criteria; F10.20, placed both_with_link), treated with a brief intervention, a goal he set himself, and naltrexone at his choice after four weeks, when cutting down alone had not controlled weekend drinking, with a wallet card noting that naltrexone blocks opioid pain medicines. The ICD-10-CM display for F10.20 is “Alcohol dependence, uncomplicated”; the record uses DSM-5-TR wording (moderate alcohol use disorder), and moderate severity is the level at which the APA and VA/DoD guidelines recommend offering naltrexone. Counseling is written to be nonjudgmental.
