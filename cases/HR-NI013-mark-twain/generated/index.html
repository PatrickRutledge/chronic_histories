Teaching objective: Prolonged grief disorder in a 69-year-old writer and humorist a year after his wife's death, after the earlier deaths of a son and a daughter. The episode covers the DSM-5-TR criteria and the PG-13-R; telling prolonged grief from normal grief and from major depression with the PHQ-9 and a clinical interview, and the decision not to start an antidepressant when major depression is absent; a structured safety assessment for passive thoughts of death in an older bereaved man, who is at high risk of suicide, with means safety and the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline; prolonged grief disorder therapy (Shear) in sixteen weekly sessions; sleep without sedative-hypnotics; tobacco use disorder in a cigar smoker since boyhood, with counseling and varenicline under the USPSTF recommendation; exertional chest tightness evaluated with an ECG and CT coronary angiography under the 2021 chest pain guideline, and nonobstructive coronary disease treated with a high-intensity statin and aspirin; gout with allopurinol started low and raised to a urate below 6 mg/dL, with colchicine prophylaxis and a flare treated without stopping allopurinol, under the ACR 2020 guideline; and social connection and his writing as part of recovery.

Disposition
Outpatient care with weekly visits and a telephone call in three days. Passive thoughts of death (wishing he could join his wife) without intent, plan or preparation; no past suicide attempt. Older widowed men are at high risk of suicide in the years after a loss (a Danish register study found the highest rise in suicide risk in the oldest bereaved men), and he has several further risk factors: repeated bereavement, living through the anniversary of her death, insomnia, alcohol each evening, chronic illness and social withdrawal. Means safety: no firearms in the house; medicines kept in small supplies; he agreed to keep whisky to one glass and none on bad nights. A written safety plan was made with him, and the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988) was saved in his phone. Safety is reviewed at every visit

This assessment is synthetic and does not convert historical behavior into a present-day fact.
