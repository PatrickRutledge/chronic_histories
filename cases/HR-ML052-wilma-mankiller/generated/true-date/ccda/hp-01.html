Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Wilma Pearl Mankiller was born on November 18, 1945, in Tahlequah, Oklahoma, the capital of the Cherokee Nation, the sixth of eleven children; her father was a full-blood Cherokee. A Cherokee Nation release gave her birthplace as the W.W. Hastings Indian Hospital in Tahlequah; the Oklahoma Hall of Fame says she was born at Rock Mountain in Adair County. [D]
  • As a child she moved with her family from Mankiller Flats in Adair County to San Francisco under the Bureau of Indian Affairs relocation program (1956 or 1957 by different accounts); she later described the move as her own little Trail of Tears. [D]
  • The occupation of Alcatraz Island in 1969 and her work with American Indian communities in California in the 1970s set the course of her activism. [D]
  • She returned to eastern Oklahoma with her two daughters in the later 1970s (1976 or 1977 by different accounts), worked for the Cherokee Nation, and lived on her grandfather's allotment, Mankiller Flats, in Adair County. [D]
  • In 1979 she was nearly killed in a head-on car crash on a curve, in which the driver of the other car, one of her best friends, died. She needed about 17 operations; accounts say her leg was barely saved from amputation and that she was told she might never walk again. The Oklahoma Hall of Fame dates the crash to 1978 and describes shattered legs, broken ribs and a crushed face. [D]
  • Her recovery was made harder by the emotional trauma of her friend's death in the crash. [D]
