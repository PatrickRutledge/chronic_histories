Assessment

  • Generalized myasthenia gravis, AChR antibody positive, MGFA class IIa, MG-ADL 7, without thymoma (G70.00). The diagnosis is confirmed by a positive antibody and a reproducible decrement on nerve stimulation; MuSK antibodies and single-fiber EMG are not needed. The weakness is mild to moderate and mainly ocular and limb, with mild bulbar involvement. Breathing measurements are normal, and there is no sign of impending crisis. [S]
  • Immunotherapy (Z79.52). Pyridostigmine alone is unlikely to control generalized disease. Prednisone is the first immunotherapy; starting at 10 mg and raising it by 10 mg a week to 40 mg lowers the risk of the early worsening that high starting doses can cause. A steroid-sparing agent is advised from the start because she is likely to need treatment for years. Azathioprine suits a woman of childbearing age better than mycophenolate, which must be avoided in pregnancy; it needs TPMT activity first, hepatitis B and tuberculosis screening, and regular blood counts and liver tests. Polycystic kidney disease with normal function does not change these doses. Pneumococcal and recombinant zoster vaccines should be given before immunosuppression, and live vaccines avoided. [S]
  • Thymectomy. In non-thymomatous AChR antibody-positive generalized MG between 18 and 50, thymectomy early in the disease improves outcomes and lowers immunotherapy needs (MGTX). It is elective and should be done once she is stable. Her healed rib fractures do not prevent a robotic approach from the right; she should see thoracic surgery in about four weeks, with surgery once prednisone has taken effect, about two to three months from now. [S]
  • Newer targeted therapies. Efgartigimod, an FcRn blocker, and the complement inhibitors eculizumab and ravulizumab work well in AChR antibody-positive generalized MG, but the guidance places complement inhibitors after other immunotherapies have failed, and her disease is not refractory. They are kept in reserve. A complement inhibitor would need meningococcal vaccination first. [S]
  • Safety, medicines to avoid and kidney considerations (Q61.2). Fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides, macrolides, telithromycin, magnesium by vein, beta-blockers and neuromuscular blocking drugs can worsen MG; statins and iodinated contrast need caution. Her kidney function is normal, so drug doses and contrast are not limited by it, but NSAIDs should be avoided for her kidneys and a beta-blocker should not be chosen if her blood pressure ever needs treatment. [S]
