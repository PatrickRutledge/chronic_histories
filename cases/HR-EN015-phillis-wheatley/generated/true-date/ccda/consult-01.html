Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Phillis Wheatley was born in West Africa, probably in the Senegambia region (present-day Senegal and the Gambia), about 1753 or 1754. Nothing is known of her early life before she was kidnapped and trafficked as a child. [D]
  • In 1761 the child arrived in Boston aboard the slave ship Phillis (on 11 July, by one scholarly chronology) and was bought by the Boston merchant John Wheatley and his wife Susanna (or Susannah), in whose household she was enslaved as a domestic servant and companion. She was named Phillis after the ship and Wheatley after the family. The family judged her to be about seven because she was shedding her front teeth. [D]
  • When she was bought in 1761 she was described as a slender, frail child, "evidently suffering from a change of climate"; the slave ship's captain is said to have sold her cheaply because he believed she was dying. One reference work describes her as "the frail, asthmatic child" and says she remained small and delicate. [D]
  • In the Wheatley household she was taught to read and write and within about sixteen months could read the Bible; she went on to read Greek and Latin classics and British literature. Her first known poem was published in the Newport Mercury in December 1767, and her elegy on the evangelist George Whitefield, published in 1770, brought her wide renown. [D]
  • Proposals to publish a volume of her poems in Boston in 1772 found too few subscribers, and the volume was instead prepared for publication in London with the support of Selina Hastings, Countess of Huntingdon. [D]
