Assessment

  • Relapsing Plasmodium vivax malaria (B51.9). Attacks months after leaving areas where P. vivax predominates, after blood-stage treatment alone, with P. vivax DNA detected between attacks, are relapses from dormant liver stages (hypnozoites). The PCR detects parasites below the threshold of the smear. Mildly low hemoglobin and platelets fit recent attacks. No further diagnostic test is needed. Central American P. vivax is chloroquine-sensitive. With a normal quantitative G6PD, primaquine 30 mg base daily for 14 days is appropriate; at 68 kg that dose meets the total recommended dose. [S]
  • Pre-travel care for French Guiana and the French Antilles (Z71.84). French Guiana requires proof of yellow fever vaccination from all arriving travelers, and CDC recommends it; one dose gives long-lasting protection. Hepatitis A, hepatitis B (if not immune) and typhoid vaccines are recommended for a long stay. Malaria is transmitted in parts of French Guiana, mostly P. vivax with some P. falciparum and chloroquine resistance, so chloroquine is not suitable for prophylaxis there. Dengue, chikungunya and rickettsial infections including typhus are prevented by vector avoidance; no dengue vaccine is recommended for travelers. [S]
