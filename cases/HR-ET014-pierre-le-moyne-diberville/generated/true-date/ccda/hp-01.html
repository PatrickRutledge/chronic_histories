Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Pierre Le Moyne d'Iberville was born on July 16, 1661, in Montreal (Ville-Marie), New France, and was baptized there on July 20, 1661. He was the third son of Charles Le Moyne de Longueuil et de Châteauguay and Catherine Thierry. [D]
  • The name d'Iberville came from a fief held by his father's family near Dieppe, and d'Ardillières from a property he acquired in Aunis near Rochefort. He was the first person born in Canada to receive the cross of the order of Saint-Louis. [D]
  • He fought the English in Hudson Bay and James Bay from 1686: the capture of the James Bay posts in 1686, the Albany River in 1688 to 1689, New Severn in 1690, York Fort (renamed Fort Bourbon) in 1694, and in 1697 the naval battle near York Factory in which his ship, the Pélican, defeated English warships and was itself lost, followed by the surrender of the fort. [D]
  • His biographer records that scurvy and exposure killed 25 of the English in the winter of 1688 to 1689 on the Albany River, and that in 1694 at York Fort scurvy killed many men on both sides. [D]
  • In 1696 to 1697 he led a campaign against the English: Fort William Henry at Pemaquid capitulated on August 15, 1696, and in Newfoundland St John's surrendered on November 30 after a brief siege; the campaign destroyed 36 settlements, killed about 200 people and took about 700 prisoners. [D]
  • He led the expeditions that began the French colony of Louisiana: in April 1699 he chose a site near the entrance of Biloxi Bay, near present-day Ocean Springs, Mississippi, for Fort Maurepas; in 1700 he built a fort on the Mississippi; and on his third voyage he began Fort Saint-Louis at Mobile. He left Louisiana in April 1702 and did not return. [D]
  • Sailing from La Rochelle on September 29, 1701, on his third voyage to Louisiana, he was forced by illness to put in at Pensacola. [D]
  • His biographer writes that he had attacks of malaria that recurred several times from 1702 to 1705 and that his deteriorating health thwarted planned expeditions to Louisiana; 64 Parishes writes that illness kept him from the expedition of 1703. [D]
  • Despite his poor health, he was well enough in 1705 to be entrusted with preparing an armament, and early in 1706 he sailed from France with a squadron of twelve vessels against the English in the West Indies. [D]
  • His biographer places the births of three of his children at La Rochelle, in 1698, 1701 and 1705. [D]
