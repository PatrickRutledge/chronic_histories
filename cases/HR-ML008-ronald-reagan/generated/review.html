Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Recent-death rule. Reagan died in 2004. The case uses only what Reagan, his physicians, and his presidential library made public, plus contemporary news reports of those disclosures. His stage of dementia, medicines, bone density, and the caregiver burden scores are synthetic. The recent-death review rule applies.
  2. Index age. The modern episode presents Reagan at 90, his age one month after the January 2001 hip fracture. The synthetic date of birth is February 6, 1936 (shift 25 years). The repair is recorded in the modern frame on January 13, 2026, which maps to the documented operation on January 13, 2001; the index visit on February 10, 2026 maps to February 10, 2001, the last chronic-care visit on July 14, 2026 maps to July 14, 2001, and the annual wellness visit on October 13, 2026 maps to October 13, 2001. Past medical history and family history include only events before the index date.
  3. Hip fracture coding. The fracture followed a fall at home in a man of 89 and is coded as an osteoporotic fracture (M80.051D), following the ICD-10-CM rule that a fracture from minor trauma in a patient with osteoporosis takes an M80 code. The fracture pattern (intertrochanteric) and the sliding hip screw are synthetic choices consistent with the reported pin, plate, and screws.
  4. Caregiver. His letter says the family often bears a heavy burden; that statement is the only historical anchor for the caregiver content. The spouse in the episode is unnamed, and her Zarit scores, sleep, and respite are synthetic. The Zarit Burden Interview, FAST stage, Lawton IADL, Timed Up and Go, and NPI-Q have no verified LOINC code in the terminology server and are recorded as narrative; MoCA, the Barthel Index, and GDS-15 are coded.
  5. Depression screening. GDS-15 is used because CMID lists it for dementia; all scores are below 5, so no safety assessment is required. The dementia code F02.B0 does not trigger the safety-assessment rule.
  6. Annual wellness visit. A subsequent Medicare annual wellness visit was added on October 13, 2026 (true frame October 13, 2001, well before his death in 2004), three months after the six-month review. No sick visit was added: under the recent-death rule the case adds no synthetic illness episode, so the wellness visit records no new diagnosis, fall, infection, or hospital stay. Judgment calls: (1) cognition is assessed by direct observation and the established diagnosis rather than a Mini-Cog, which would add nothing in known moderate dementia and could distress him; the formal MoCA and FAST staging stay at the yearly dementia review; (2) the PHQ-2 (score 0) is asked of him directly with caregiver corroboration to meet the same-day depression-screen requirement, while the GDS-15 continues at the dementia reviews; (3) no scored alcohol screen is used, because the social history already records no alcohol since the fracture; (4) the health risk assessment, history, and corroboration are attributed to “his caregivers,” not to the spouse, so that no statement or health fact is attributed to a real relative, and the spouse stays unnamed; (5) advance care planning reviews the existing agent and POLST and covers education about later-stage dementia, but records no new or specific end-of-life decision, so nothing in it reads as a claim about his real choices; the health care agent is not named; (6) hearing and vision are recorded only as “no new concern,” with no test result, and hearing aids are not recorded because no fetched source covers them; (7) vaccines not on record (RSV, recombinant zoster, tetanus, COVID-19) are listed as due rather than given, and only the yearly influenza vaccine is recorded as given.
  7. Focused visits. The three chronic-care follow-ups (two weeks, three months, and six months after the first visit) are documented as focused visits on the CMID Alzheimer’s disease, hip/pelvic fracture, and osteoporosis monographs, and each lists every item those protocols call for at that point, with its interval and status. The two-week visit is the same day as the neurology consultation. Synthetic details introduced, all consistent with the existing record: the home safety changes (the ones the annual wellness visit already lists), home physical therapy finishing before the six-month review, a creatinine clearance of about 50 mL/min calculated from the existing creatinine and weight, neurology’s decision not to add memantine during recovery, and the POLST being completed at the three-month visit (no contents of the POLST are recorded). Protocol deviations recorded instead of invented results: (1) no Fracture Liaison Service referral from the hospital stay, with primary care carrying out the same steps; (2) phosphorus and 24-hour urine calcium missing from the secondary-causes workup before zoledronic acid, with phosphorus added to the tests before the second infusion; (3) no vertebral fracture assessment with the DXA although both triggers apply; and (4) no FRAX calculated with the DXA. Each is judged to have caused no harm because the hip fracture already made treatment necessary, and (3) and (4) move to the next DXA. The DXA was done at three months rather than at the first outpatient visit, by plan, so that it fell with the first infusion. Bone-turnover markers are marked not indicated because a clinic infusion assures adherence. Timed Up and Go is recorded only where the record already has a value (24, 16, and 15 seconds); in its place the two- and three-month visits record the STEADI fall history, gait, and postural blood pressure without numbers. The recent-death rule is kept: no new illness, symptom, or finding is added, and the caregiver burden score is the one already recorded, attributed to his caregivers and not to a named relative.
  8. Consultation. The neurology referral and consultation (Rafael Quintero, MD, a fictional consultant, on the day of the two-week visit) are S. The consultant answers the four referral questions with what the focused visits already record: FAST stage 5 confirmed, donepezil continued with a pulse check (heart rate 62, no fainting), no antipsychotic or sedative for the mild evening restlessness, memantine deferred to the yearly review, and no repeat brain imaging, laboratory workup, or anti-amyloid therapy. Judgment calls: (1) the examination adds only normal findings needed for the assessment (no rest tremor or rigidity) to those in Dr. Ellison's same-day note; (2) history and agreement are attributed to “his caregivers,” the spouse stays unnamed and is given no statement, and the caregiver burden score is the one already recorded; (3) the advance care planning done the same day stays in the primary-care note, and the consultation adds nothing about his choices; (4) the consultant's own text adds no family history, past head injury, or other private health detail, under the recent-death rule.
  9. Mother’s cause of death. The Reagan Library states that Nelle Reagan died of complications of Alzheimer’s disease. Another 1962 account is reported to give a different cause; it was not reviewed, so the claim is graded D with certainty probable.
  10. Not used. Pneumonia as the immediate cause of death was reported by news outlets but was not found in a fetched source and is not recorded. Speculation that the 1989 head injury or his time in office related to his dementia is not recorded. The 1981 gunshot wound and the cancer operations are listed only as history.
