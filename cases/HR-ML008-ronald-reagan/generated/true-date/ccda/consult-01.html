History of Present Illness

Mr. Reagan, a 90-year-old man with Alzheimer's disease diagnosed many years ago, is seen at the request of Dr. Ellison six weeks after a fall at home and internal fixation of a right hip fracture. He takes part in the history as he is able, and his caregivers add detail. Assessment

  • Dementia due to Alzheimer's disease, moderate (F02.B0). FAST stage 5 is confirmed: he needs help choosing clothes and with all instrumental activities, in keeping with a MoCA of 11. The course and examination fit Alzheimer's disease; there are no hallucinations, parkinsonism, or focal signs to suggest another cause. By his caregivers' account he is back to his usual cognition since the sedating hospital medicines were stopped, and there is no delirium. [S]
  • Donepezil benefit and safety. Worth continuing. Heart rate 62 with no fainting, no orthostatic drop, and no nausea or weight loss. Stopping a long-standing cholinesterase inhibitor at this stage can bring a noticeable decline. [S]
  • Evening restlessness and repeated questions. Mild and settled by routine, with no hallucinations or aggression. Antipsychotics would add risks of falls, stroke, and death in dementia, and sedatives and anticholinergic drugs would add falls and confusion, so none is indicated. [S]
  • Memantine and further testing. Memantine is an option at the moderate stage, but adding a medicine during recovery from the fracture is best avoided; revisit at the yearly review. The diagnostic workup was done when the disease was first diagnosed, TSH is normal, and there are no new neurologic signs, so no repeat brain imaging or laboratory workup is needed. At the moderate stage he is outside the eligibility range for anti-amyloid therapy. [S]

Plan of Treatment

  • Continue donepezil 10 mg each morning; check the pulse at each visit, and reduce or stop it if the heart rate falls below 50 or he faints. [S]
  • Keep the quiet evening routine, with daylight and activity during the day and short, calm answers to repeated questions. [S]
  • No antipsychotic, benzodiazepine, or sedating antihistamine for the restlessness; if it worsens, look first for pain, constipation, infection, or a new medicine. [S]
