# Teaching Note — Ronald Reagan pilot

> **SYNTHETIC RECORD, NOT A REAL MEDICAL RECORD**

## Why this case matters

This case shows how historical evidence can support a transparent fictional modern episode without presenting interpretation or generated content as documented fact.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish documented (**D**), retrospective (**R**), and synthetic (**S**) content.
2. Keep historical and active problems distinct.
3. Connect measurable goals and interventions to guidelines.
4. Preserve strengths, function, identity, and agency.

## Destigmatization message

A health condition is one part of a person’s life and does not erase achievement, relationships, purpose, or agency.

## Discussion questions

- Which facts support concern without establishing a modern diagnosis?
- Which entries belong in active problems, historical problems, or both?
- What additional evidence would change an evidence grade?
- How should an EHR distinguish synthetic and historical content?

## Evidence-grade summary

- **D:** Ronald Reagan was born on February 6, 1911, in Tampico, Illinois, to Jack and Nelle Reagan; his older brother Neil was born in 1908. Reagan died at his home in the Bel Air district of Los Angeles on June 5, 2004, at age 93, after a long illness with Alzheimer's disease. In a letter to the American people made public on November 5, 1994, Reagan wrote that he had recently been told he was one of the millions of Americans who would be afflicted with Alzheimer's disease, and that as the disease progresses the family often bears a heavy burden. Reagan's personal physicians stated in November 1994 that his annual examinations included memory testing, that over the preceding twelve months they had seen findings suggesting possible early Alzheimer's disease, and that after further testing and observation they concluded he was entering its early stages. Reagan was thrown from a horse in Mexico on July 4, 1989; at a routine examination at the Mayo Clinic in September 1989, doctors found fluid on his brain attributed to the fall, and it was removed surgically at St. Mary's Hospital in Rochester, Minnesota. Reagan fell at home on January 12, 2001, and broke his right hip; the next day, at St. John's Health Center in Santa Monica, surgeons repaired it with a pin, plate, and screws in a 65-minute operation. He went home on January 20, 2001, and continued physical therapy at home, with no weight-bearing sessions for at least two weeks. Reagan survived an assassination attempt in 1981, had an operation for colon cancer in 1985, and had prostate and skin-cancer surgery in 1987. Reagan's father, Jack, died of a heart attack on May 18, 1941, and his brother, Neil, died of heart failure on December 11, 1996. Reagan's mother, Nelle, died on July 25, 1962, of complications of Alzheimer's disease, according to the Reagan Library.
- **R:** None recorded.
- **S:** Shifted identity, fictional care team, modern encounters, results, plans, and outcomes.
