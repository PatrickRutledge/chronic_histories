Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Recent-death rule. Ifill died in 2016, so the rule applies. The case uses only what was reported publicly during her life (the Associated Press report of her April leave) and in obituaries and official statements at her death (The Washington Post, CNN, PBS NewsHour, NPR, CBS News). She kept her illness private; the cause of death was given by her family, and the timing of the diagnosis, the reason for the April leave and her work through chemotherapy come from friends quoted in the obituaries (CLM-0006, graded probable). No sick visit is added. Her death is recorded once (CLM-0007), in plain words. No claim concerns mental health or substance use; the modern mood screens are synthetic and below the threshold for a safety assessment.
  2. What is not public. The stage, histology, molecular result and treatment of her cancer were never made public, beyond friends’ references to chemotherapy. Every clinical detail in the modern episode (the bleeding, the biopsy and its histology, the mismatch repair result, the nodal stage, the surgery, dostarlimab, the hypothyroidism, all values) is synthetic and is not a claim about her real illness.
  3. Real people. No relative is named; the family member who gave the cause of death is referred to only as her family. No decision, statement or health fact is attributed to any relative. Her co-anchor and the president she interviewed appear only in history claims about her career; friends quoted in the obituaries are cited as the obituaries’ sources and have no role in the modern episode. The modern family history is left empty so that no health fact is attributed to a real relative, and the Lynch syndrome work-up never assigns a genetic result to anyone: the tumor’s MLH1 loss is explained by promoter methylation, so germline and cascade testing are not needed.

(3) The New York Times obituary and the Britannica entry could not be fetched and are not cited.

  6. Synthetic clinical picture and teaching points. The presenting problem is postmenopausal bleeding (N95.0), resolved at the consultation when the biopsy gives the diagnosis. The diagnosis is endometrioid, low-grade (grade 2) and mismatch repair deficient, staged FIGO 2023 IIIC2ii at surgery with the molecular class MMRd (POLE wild-type, p53 wild-type), chosen so that the treatment teaches the current standard for mismatch repair deficient advanced disease (dostarlimab with carboplatin and paclitaxel, the RUBY regimen, which covers stage IIIC2 regardless of measurable disease; pembrolizumab with the same chemotherapy is the alternative). The teaching points on equity are explicit in the plan and the teaching objective: Black women in the United States have roughly twice the endometrial cancer mortality of White women, with more non-endometrioid and p53-abnormal tumors, later stage, and delays in evaluating bleeding, and the disparity is not fully explained by subtype and stage (Clarke 2022; Doll 2020). The modern episode models the opposite of delay: any bleeding after menopause is examined and sampled at the first visit, together with transvaginal ultrasound, as the 2026 ACOG update recommends instead of relying on a thin endometrium.
