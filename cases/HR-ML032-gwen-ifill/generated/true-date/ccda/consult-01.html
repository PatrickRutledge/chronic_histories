Assessment

  • Endometrioid adenocarcinoma of the endometrium, FIGO grade 2, mismatch repair deficient (C54.1). The biopsy shows a low-grade endometrioid cancer that has lost MLH1 and PMS2, with a wild-type p53 pattern. The enlarged right pelvic and left para-aortic nodes on CT suggest stage IIIC disease; the final FIGO 2013 stage comes from surgery. The molecular class needs POLE sequencing on the surgical specimen as well: a POLE-mutated tumor carries an excellent outlook, a p53-abnormal one a poor outlook, and a mismatch repair deficient tumor responds strongly to immunotherapy. She is fit, and the disease on CT can be removed with a minimally invasive operation: total hysterectomy with removal of both tubes and ovaries, washings, sentinel lymph node mapping with indocyanine green, and removal of the enlarged pelvic and para-aortic nodes. If nodal disease is confirmed, I recommend dostarlimab with carboplatin and paclitaxel every three weeks for six cycles, then dostarlimab alone every six weeks for up to three years: for mismatch repair deficient advanced endometrial cancer this combination sharply lowers the risk of progression compared with chemotherapy alone. Radiation would be reserved for nodal disease left after treatment. The loss of MLH1 is the universal Lynch syndrome tumor screen; in most women of her age it comes from MLH1 promoter methylation in the tumor, which is acquired, not inherited. If methylation is found, germline testing is not needed; if it is not, she should have germline testing after counseling, and if a variant is found, her blood relatives should be offered cascade testing through a letter she can share.
