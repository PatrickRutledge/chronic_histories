  • Amelia Mary Earhart was born on July 24, 1897, in Atchison, Kansas, to Amy Otis and Edwin Stanton Earhart. [D]
  • After visiting her sister in Toronto at Christmas 1917, she moved there in February 1918 and worked in the Voluntary Aid Detachment at the Spadina Military Hospital, often on twelve-hour shifts, going wherever an extra pair of hands was needed. [D]
  • During the 1918 influenza epidemic she worked nights on the ward that held the influenza patients; within weeks she fell ill herself, with severe sinus pain and terrible headaches. Her case was serious enough that doctors operated, opening into her sinuses so that they could be drained regularly. It was a month before she was well enough to leave hospital, and she went to recover at her sister's home in Massachusetts. [D]
  • She suffered from chronic sinus problems for the rest of her life. [D]
  • She entered Columbia University as a pre-medical student in the fall of 1919 and left in 1920. She took her first airplane ride in December 1920, with the pilot Frank Hawks, and became a licensed pilot in the early 1920s: Purdue University dates her pilot's license to May 1923, and the Encyclopedia of the Great Plains to 1921.
