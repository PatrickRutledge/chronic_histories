Assessment

  • Chronic rhinosinusitis without nasal polyps (J32.9). Her symptoms for more than 12 weeks with edema and pus in the middle meatus on endoscopy confirm chronic rhinosinusitis, as the AAO-HNS guideline requires; there are no polyps, and normal eosinophils and IgE without asthma suggest disease without type 2 inflammation. The worsening of the past five days is an acute exacerbation with pus, and a short course of amoxicillin-clavulanate, guided by the culture, is reasonable. An oral steroid is not advised, because the evidence for it is in disease with polyps. Saline irrigation and an intranasal steroid remain the basis of treatment. A CT is not needed now: there are no warning signs, and a CT is best done after about eight more weeks of medical therapy if symptoms persist, to judge whether surgery is appropriate and to plan it. Surgery is appropriate if the CT shows disease and the SNOT-22 stays at 20 or more after that therapy; it is her choice. The aching left upper teeth deserve a dental check, because a dental source can cause one-sided disease. Her old drainage operations do not change this plan; the CT will show any scarring. For flying: she must not fly while blocked or in pain, because a blocked sinus or ear cannot equalize on descent; the saline rinse and nasal steroid are acceptable for pilots; any new medicine needs a 48-hour ground trial; sedating antihistamines are no-go medicines. A decongestant does not make it safe to fly with a cold. [S]
