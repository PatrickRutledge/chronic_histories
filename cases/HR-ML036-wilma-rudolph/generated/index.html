Teaching objective: Recognize new progressive neurologic symptoms (morning headache, word-finding pauses and a clumsy hand) as a reason for prompt contrast MRI of the brain in a 54-year-old Black woman who survived childhood polio, and then manage a newly diagnosed glioblastoma to current standards: dexamethasone only for symptomatic edema, at the lowest dose and tapered early, with glucose and stomach protection; no seizure prophylaxis when no seizure has occurred; CT to exclude another primary; awake craniotomy with language and motor mapping for maximal safe resection and an MRI within 48 hours; the WHO CNS5 2021 integrated diagnosis with IDH, TERT, EGFR, chromosome 7 and 10 and MGMT promoter methylation; radiotherapy 60 Gy in 30 fractions with daily temozolomide and Pneumocystis prophylaxis, then adjuvant temozolomide with tumor-treating fields; blood count and liver test monitoring; RANO 2.0 response assessment that allows for pseudoprogression; venous thromboembolism awareness; fall prevention for a woman whose new right-sided weakness meets a left leg weakened by polio in childhood; and early palliative care with advance care planning alongside treatment, on the NCCN CNS cancers guideline, the EANO 2021 diffuse glioma guideline and the ASCO-SNO glioma guideline. Her childhood care, given at a Black medical school because care nearer home was not offered to her, frames the commitment to equal access to a brain tumor center and clinical trials.

  • S Lives in a suburb of Nashville; four grown children and a very large extended family nearby, and many friends from track and field
  • S Remembers being told as a child that she would never walk again; wants to be told plainly what is happening and to stay as independent and active as she can
  • S Never smoker; alcohol rarely; no drug use
