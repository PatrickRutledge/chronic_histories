Assessment

  • Left posterior frontal ring-enhancing mass, presumed high-grade glioma (D49.6). A single necrotic ring-enhancing mass with raised perfusion in a woman of 54 without cancer elsewhere on CT is most likely a glioblastoma. A solitary metastasis has been made unlikely by the CT; lymphoma would usually enhance solidly and restrict diffusion; an abscess would restrict diffusion in its center. The mass sits just behind the expressive language area and in front of the motor strip, and the edema explains much of her symptoms. Maximal safe resection, rather than biopsy alone, gives the tissue diagnosis, relieves the mass effect and improves survival, and an awake craniotomy with cortical and subcortical language and motor mapping lets the surgeons remove the tumor while protecting speech and hand function. A functional MRI with tractography beforehand will show the language areas and the motor tract. The tissue needs an integrated WHO CNS5 diagnosis: IDH1 R132H immunostaining, with IDH1 and IDH2 sequencing if it is negative because she is 54, ATRX and p53, the TERT promoter, EGFR amplification and chromosome 7 gain with 10 loss, which define glioblastoma when IDH is wild-type, CDKN2A, histone H3 and BRAF, and MGMT promoter methylation, which predicts benefit from temozolomide. If this is a glioblastoma, the standard after surgery is radiotherapy 60 Gy in 30 fractions with daily temozolomide 75 mg/m2, Pneumocystis prophylaxis and weekly blood counts, then six cycles of adjuvant temozolomide, with tumor-treating fields offered for the adjuvant phase, and screening for a clinical trial. Dexamethasone is right for edema causing symptoms and should be tapered as soon as possible after surgery. She has had no seizure, so she should not have a prophylactic antiseizure drug. Brain tumors carry a high risk of venous thromboembolism: she needs mechanical and then heparin prophylaxis in hospital after surgery, but not routinely at home. [S]
