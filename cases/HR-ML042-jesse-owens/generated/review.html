Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Recent-death rule. He died in 1980, so the rule does not apply. The claims are still limited to what the fetched sources state.
  2. Sources not used. The New York Times obituary (blocked), Britannica (blocked), Olympics.com (timed out) and the Smithsonian pages could not be read, and William J. Baker’s biography was not read; none is cited. The four gold medals rest on the Ohio State University Libraries and the archival clippings. The accounts on secondary websites that his cancer was “extremely aggressive and drug-resistant” were not found in a reliable fetched source and are not claimed. The histology (adenocarcinoma), the side (left lung) and the word “inoperable” come from contemporary newspaper clippings in the Ohio State University Archives, read by text extraction from scans; the quoted phrases were checked against the extracted text.
  3. Conflicts. The Ohio State University Libraries say he was the last of ten children; the clippings and his own account say nine. Both are recorded in CLM-0001. One clipping gives his time of death as 5:40 a.m. and another as 3:40 a.m.; neither is used. The clippings place his home in Paradise Valley and the Phoenix area; the library says Scottsdale; the record says the Phoenix area.
  4. Smoking history. “A pack-a-day smoker for the past 35 years” gives about 35 pack-years, which the modern record uses; the age at which he started is not documented. The claim is marked sensitive.
  5. Index age and dates. The synthetic date of birth is September 12, 1960 (shift 47 years). The index visit on May 11, 2026 maps to May 11, 1979, when he was 65 and living in the Phoenix area, seven months before the documented admission in December 1979. All visits map to May to October 1979, within his lifetime and before his documented diagnosis. Index age 65 was chosen, rather than 66, so that a full course of care fits between the index date and November 1, 2026 while staying before the documented diagnosis; he turns 66 before the wellness visit. The visits are not a claim about his real care in 1979.
  6. The teaching contrast. His documented cancer was an inoperable adenocarcinoma of the left lung found after breathlessness began. The modern episode finds an adenocarcinoma of the left upper lobe by low-dose screening while it is still stage IIB and resectable, because he meets the 2021 USPSTF criteria. This is a teaching choice, not a claim about what screening could have done in his lifetime; low-dose CT screening did not exist then. The synthetic history that no clinician had offered him screening illustrates the documented under-offer of screening to Black smokers and is not a claim about his real care.
  7. Presenting problem. The Lung-RADS 4X nodule (R91.1) is the presenting finding at intake and is resolved at the diagnosis visit on May 26, 2026, when the adenocarcinoma (C34.12, both_with_link to the documented diagnosis) is recorded. He is asymptomatic apart from a long-standing smoker’s cough, so he is a screening candidate rather than a diagnostic patient; the intake documents show only the same-day screening CT and the plan.
  8. Treatment choice. Stage IIB (cT1c N1 M0) with no EGFR or ALK alteration: three cycles of nivolumab with carboplatin and pemetrexed before surgery (CheckMate 816) were chosen over surgery first followed by adjuvant therapy, because treatment before surgery gives a pathologic response to guide later decisions and the ability to start systemic therapy at once. Perioperative regimens that add immunotherapy after surgery are alternatives; after a major pathologic response with negative nodes the record has him choose surveillance after discussion. These choices are clinical judgment calls for review. KRAS G12C is recorded for use only if the cancer recurs.
  9. Palliative care. Specialist palliative care was not requested, because the cancer was treated with curative intent and caused no symptom burden; symptom, distress and mood screening at each visit and advance care planning at the wellness visit stand in its place. This is recorded as not indicated in the monitoring table.
  10. Tobacco. He smokes at intake and starts varenicline at the diagnosis visit, quitting on the day of cycle 1. Nicotine dependence (F17.210) is resolved at the visit after surgery and replaced by nicotine dependence in early remission (F17.211, both_with_link).
