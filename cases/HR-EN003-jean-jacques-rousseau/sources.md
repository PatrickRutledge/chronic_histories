## Editorial resolutions

- **Birth date and calendar.** He was born at Geneva on June 28, 1712 (1911 Britannica). Geneva had followed the Gregorian calendar since its council's resolution of December 31, 1700 (source 12), so the date is Gregorian and is not converted. England had moved to the Gregorian calendar in 1752, so the dates of the Wootton stay are Gregorian as well.
- **Index age and dates.** He lived at Wootton from March 22, 1766, to May 1, 1767, and turned 54 in June 1766. The index visit on January 20, 2026 maps to January 20, 1767, at Wootton, after the quarrel and before his flight, when he was 54. The synthetic date of birth is June 28, 1971 (shift 259 years). The other visits (February 3, March 10, April 21, June 9, July 14, and September 22, 2026) map to the same days of 1767; the last four fall after his documented flight to France in May 1767. The true-frame dates lie within his lifetime and assert nothing about where he was; the modern episode does not reproduce the flight.
- **Not naming the man who arranged his stay in England.** That man has his own record in this dataset, so he is referred to only as a Scottish philosopher who arranged his stay in England, and the quarrel appears only as documented history.
- **Persecutory beliefs.** His beliefs and conduct (the conviction that his host was plotting against him, the fear of being spied on, the *Dialogues*, and the opening of the *Reveries*) are **D**, from his own writings and reference works. Calling them a delusion is **R** (possible), from the psychiatric tradition summarized in source 4, which also stresses that he had real enemies, his books were condemned, and his house was stoned. The modern episode gives him a synthetic diagnosis of delusional disorder, persecutory type, linked to both, and the notes acknowledge his real conflicts rather than treating every grievance as delusional.
- **Modern diagnosis decided.** Delusional disorder, persecutory type, rather than paranoid personality disorder (the beliefs are fixed and specific), schizophrenia (no hallucinations, disorganization, or negative symptoms), a mood disorder with psychotic features (mild depressive symptoms that followed the beliefs), or a medical or neurocognitive cause (normal examination, cognition, and tests over a long, stable course, so no brain imaging). The insomnia is recorded as part of the disorder rather than as a separate problem.
- **Antipsychotic.** Decided: aripiprazole 5 mg was offered at the consultation and declined; he declined again at the third visit, and chose it himself at the fourth visit, after urethroplasty, for sleep and worry. Aripiprazole was chosen for its low anticholinergic effect in a man with urinary retention. The outcome is a partial response, with less conviction and distress but persistent beliefs, which is realistic. No compulsory treatment was considered, because he was never at risk. The APA guideline was written for schizophrenia; its recommendations on antipsychotics, CBT for psychosis, and monitoring are applied to delusional disorder as a schizophrenia-spectrum condition, and the CMID depression and mood disorders monograph supplies the antipsychotic monitoring schedule.
- **Urinary disease.** His lifelong retention, self-treatment with bougies, the broken probe of 1761, and the 1758 rupture are **D**, from the *Confessions* and his letters as quoted. The cause is **R** (disputed): congenital narrowing, a penile stricture, a bladder-neck valve, psychogenic retention, and acute intermittent porphyria have all been proposed, and the autopsy found no lesion of the urinary tract (**D**). The modern episode gives him a synthetic 1.5 cm bulbar urethral stricture, idiopathic and probably congenital, treated by excision and primary anastomosis urethroplasty. This differs from the prostatic retention in another record in the dataset. The problem is linked to the documented and disputed claims, and its provenance says the stricture is not his historical diagnosis.
- **Hypospadias not used.** The brief for this record suggested a congenital anomaly such as hypospadias. No fetched source proposed hypospadias, so it is not asserted; the synthetic cystoscopy records a normal meatus, which teaches its exclusion on examination.
- **Hernia.** The documented "rupture" of 1758 does not give a site. The modern right inguinal hernia is synthetic and echoes it; it is managed by watchful waiting.

The 1778 suicide rumors are recorded with the evidence against them (the certificate of apoplexy and the 1897 skull examination). No current suicidal or violent ideation is recorded at any visit.
