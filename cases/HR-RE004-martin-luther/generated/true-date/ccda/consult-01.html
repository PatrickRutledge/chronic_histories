  • In May 1521, at the Wartburg, he wrote to Philipp Melanchthon that the Lord had afflicted him with painful constipation, and that he had not slept all night. [D]

  • On the evening of 6 July 1527 (Julian), when he was 43, he had an attack that began with a roaring tinnitus in his left ear, which increased and seemed to fill the left half of his head, followed by nausea, pallor, coldness and a state of collapse without loss of consciousness; he prayed and believed he would not survive the night. Assessment

  • Ménière disease of the left ear, definite (H81.02). He meets the AAO-HNS 1521 and Bárány Society criteria for definite Ménière disease: two or more spontaneous attacks of vertigo each lasting 20 minutes to 12 hours, low- to medium-frequency sensorineural hearing loss documented by audiometry in the left ear, and fluctuating tinnitus and fullness in that ear, not better explained by another vestibular diagnosis. The normal examination between attacks argues against a central cause. Vestibular migraine was considered: he has headaches, but no attack has carried migraine features, so the criteria for vestibular migraine are not met; the two can coexist, and a headache diary will show whether that is so. The asymmetric hearing loss calls for MRI to exclude a vestibular schwannoma. Vestibular function testing and electrocochleography are not needed to make the diagnosis. [S]
  • Maintenance treatment. Sodium restriction and limiting caffeine and alcohol are reasonable first steps. A diuretic is a guideline option for maintenance; hydrochlorothiazide also lowers urine calcium, which may help his stones, but his sodium should be checked, particularly if an SSRI is started. Betahistine is not FDA approved in the United States and the large BEMED trial found it no better than placebo, so it is not recommended. Vestibular suppressants such as meclizine should be limited to attacks, because regular use slows compensation. [S]
  • Hearing, tinnitus and balance. Hearing is fluctuating and word recognition is still good; no hearing aid is needed now. If unsteadiness persists between attacks, vestibular rehabilitation helps; it is not useful during an attack. If disabling attacks continue after about three months of these measures, an intratympanic steroid injection is the next step; gentamicin or surgery are reserved for refractory disease. [S]

Data reviewed: Dr. Brandt-Mensah's intake note, examination and tests (blood count, electrolytes, kidney function, calcium, uric acid and TSH normal) and his attack diary. Audiometry today: left low-frequency sensorineural hearing loss of 40 dB at 250 and 500 Hz and 30 dB at 1000 Hz, 15 to 20 dB above that; right ear normal; word recognition 88 percent left and 100 percent right; type A tympanograms on both sides.
