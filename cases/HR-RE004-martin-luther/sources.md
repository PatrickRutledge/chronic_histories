## Editorial resolutions

- **Calendar.** Electoral Saxony used the Julian (Old Style) calendar throughout his life and adopted the Gregorian calendar only in 1700. The sources give Julian dates. As in other records with Old Style dates, the record converts them for the true frame: his birth on 10 November 1483 (Julian) is 19 November 1483 in the proleptic Gregorian calendar (nine days' difference in the fifteenth century), and his death on 18 February 1546 (Julian) is 28 February 1546 (ten days' difference in the sixteenth century). The attack of 6 July 1527 (Julian) is 16 July 1527 (Gregorian) and the sermon of 22 April 1527 (Julian) is 2 May 1527. The conversions were checked by computing Julian day numbers. The preferred birth and death dates are the Gregorian ones, with the Julian dates as superseded alternatives; timeline dates carry Gregorian ISO values with the Julian date in the display text. Feldmann's 2009 letter gives the death as on the 17th; the 18th (Julian), given by the Stanford Encyclopedia and the Luther Memorials Foundation, is used.
- **Index age and dates.** The episode presents him at 43, the age at the documented attack of July 1527. The synthetic date of birth is November 19, 1982 (the Gregorian month and day), so the shift is 499 years. The index visit is July 28, 2026 (true frame 28 July 1527 Gregorian, 18 July Julian), twelve days after the attack; the last visit is October 27, 2026 (true frame 27 October 1527). He was in Wittenberg through this period, since he stayed when plague broke out there in August 1527. The plague, his household and the later return of his depression are history only and are not used in the modern episode.
- **Name.** "Martin Luther" splits at the last space, so no `name_parts` is needed. No alias is recorded: the birth-surname form "Luder" was not verified in a fetched source. The notes call him "Professor Luther," after his documented professorship at Wittenberg.
- **Ménière disease (R) and the differential.** Feldmann's reading of the 1527 attacks as Ménière disease of the left ear is graded **R**, probable; the older psychiatric and middle-ear readings and Takigami's otitis media are recorded in the same claim. Evers's suggestion that some attacks were vestibular migraine is graded **R**, possible. The modern episode diagnoses definite Ménière disease at the consultation by the AAO-HNS 2020 and Bárány criteria, with vestibular migraine considered and its criteria not met, and with an MRI excluding a vestibular schwannoma. The presenting attacks are a separate problem that resolves at the consultation.
- **Treatment decisions for Ménière disease.** Hydrochlorothiazide is chosen as the guideline's maintenance option, partly because it also lowers urine calcium in a calcium stone former; betahistine is not used because it is not FDA approved in the United States and the BEMED trial found no benefit over placebo; meclizine is limited to attacks; vestibular rehabilitation is used for imbalance between attacks; intratympanic steroid is held in reserve and is not needed because attacks stop. These are clinical judgments.
- **Depression.** His documented Anfechtungen (CLM-0003) and the depression of 1527 (CLM-0011, CLM-0012) are marked sensitive. No historical psychiatric diagnosis is adopted: Reiter's manic-depressive diagnosis and Grossmann's cyclothymia are recorded as **R**, disputed, with Braun and Loew's caution. The modern diagnosis is major depressive disorder, recurrent, moderate (F33.1), recurrent because of the documented earlier crises; the Mood Disorder Questionnaire is negative, so bipolar disorder is not diagnosed in the modern episode. The PHQ-9 at intake was 15 with item 9 scored 0; the safety assessment records no suicidal ideation, the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline is in the plan, and safety is reviewed at every later visit. Treatment is CBT with behavioral activation plus sertraline, by shared decision under the APA 2019 guideline; sodium is checked because an SSRI and a thiazide together can lower it.
- **Stones.** The 1526 stone is documented before the index date; Feldmann calls his stones bladder stones and Takigami uric acid stones (**R**). The sick visit, a synthetic 5 mm right distal ureteral stone, was chosen because it fits his documented recurrent stones and teaches medical expulsive therapy, a short NSAID course alongside sertraline and a thiazide, stone analysis and the 24-hour urine; it does not echo a dated documented attack. The stone type (calcium oxalate with calcium phosphate) and every result are synthetic. The Schmalkalden attack of 1537 is history only; its month was not verified in a fetched source, so the brief's "February 1537" is recorded only as 1537.
