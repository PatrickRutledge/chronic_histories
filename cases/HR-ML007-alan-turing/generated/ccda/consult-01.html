Assessment

  • Bilateral gynecomastia (N62). True gynecomastia, typical on examination and fully explained by a year of high-dose exogenous estrogen. hCG and AFP are normal and the scrotal ultrasound shows no mass, so a testicular tumor is excluded; liver, kidney, and thyroid disease are excluded. Breast imaging is not needed because the examination is typical and shows no discrete mass. Most gynecomastia of recent onset regresses once the cause is removed, though tissue present for many months may become fibrous and persist. [S]
  • Drug-induced hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (E23.1). Suppression of LH, FSH, and testosterone by exogenous estrogen, with estradiol already falling. Recovery is expected over two to six months. Testosterone therapy would suppress the axis again and is not indicated for a transient, drug-induced deficiency. Tamoxifen and aromatase inhibitors are not justified in general and are not needed here. The mildly raised prolactin is an expected estrogen effect and should be rechecked after the estrogen clears; no pituitary imaging is needed unless it stays raised. [S]
  • Adverse effects of high-dose estrogen given without free consent (T38.5X5A). An iatrogenic harm. He has no symptoms or signs of venous thrombosis, and his liver tests are normal. The treatment had no medical indication. [S]
