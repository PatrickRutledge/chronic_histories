## Assessment

1. **Ulnar neuropathy at the right elbow (cubital tunnel), post-traumatic** (G56.21). Moderately severe ulnar neuropathy at the elbow after a blow to the nerve at the cubital tunnel, with partial conduction block, focal demyelination, and some axonal loss (active denervation in the hand muscles). The studies meet AANEM criteria for localization at the elbow and exclude a lesion at the wrist, a C8 radiculopathy, and a lower trunk plexopathy. The nerve does not sublux, and there is no fracture, ganglion, or mass. Because the injury was acute and part of the deficit is conduction block, some recovery with protection of the nerve is likely, but the axonal loss and early wasting mean that recovery may be incomplete. **S**

## Recommendations

1. Conservative treatment for about three months from the injury: a night splint holding the elbow at about 45 degrees, a gel elbow pad, no leaning on the elbow, and no prolonged elbow bending.
2. A daytime hand-based anti-claw splint for the ring and little fingers to improve grasp, and hand therapy twice a week for strength, dexterity, and adapted tools: built-up brush handles, a lighter brush, and a palette fixed to the easel.
3. No corticosteroid injection, which the Cochrane review found no better than placebo.
4. Review at about three months from the injury with grip, pinch, two-point discrimination, and the QuickDASH; if motor strength has not clearly improved, or if weakness or wasting progress at any time, I would advise surgery rather than waiting longer.
5. If surgery is needed, in situ decompression of the ulnar nerve at the elbow, which the Cochrane review found as effective as transposition with fewer wound infections; anterior transposition only if the nerve subluxes at operation. Repeat nerve studies are not needed before surgery unless the picture changes.
6. Tendon transfers to correct the claw only if it persists a year or more after decompression.
7. He should keep drawing, writing, and teaching with his left hand without restriction, and paint with the adapted tools and help with heavy or prolonged right-hand tasks.
