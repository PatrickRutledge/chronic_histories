## Historical evidence

1. Georgia O’Keeffe Museum. Chronology (accessible site). Supports her birth on November 15, 1887, to Francis Calyxtus O’Keeffe and Ida Totto O’Keeffe at the family dairy farm near Sun Prairie, Wisconsin; her mother’s death in Charlottesville in May 1916 and her father’s in Petersburg in November 1918; two operations for benign breast cysts at Mount Sinai in 1927; admission to Doctor’s Hospital in February 1933 “suffering from psychoneurosis” and recuperation in Bermuda; failing eyesight beginning in 1968; loss of central vision early in 1971 with only peripheral sight retained; her last unassisted oil painting in 1972 and later work with assistance; Juan Hamilton as her assistant from 1973 and her work in clay from 1974; and her death at St. Vincent’s Hospital, Santa Fe, on March 6, 1986. https://access-ok.okeeffemuseum.org/chronology/
2. Georgia O’Keeffe Museum. Biography (accessible site). Supports her birth on November 15, 1887, as the second of seven children on a farm near Sun Prairie; macular degeneration and failing vision; the last unassisted oil painting in 1972; and her death in Santa Fe at 98. https://access-ok.okeeffemuseum.org/biography/
3. Whitney Museum of American Art. Exhibition text on her late watercolors. Supports macular degeneration beginning in early 1971 that eventually left her with only peripheral vision, work with assistants in her early eighties, and her death in Santa Fe at 98 on March 6, 1986. https://whitney.org/media/632
4. The Vision & Art Project (American Macular Degeneration Foundation). “Georgia O’Keeffe.” Secondary source. Quotes a 1972 letter, as cited in Jeffrey Hogrefe’s 1992 biography, in which she wrote that her left eye had become much more cloudy and her right eye seemed to be beginning to cloud; suggests first signs as early as 1964 (graded **R**, possible). https://visionandartproject.org/artists/georgia-okeeffe/
5. HISTMED Master Roster v3. Internal project source. Its clinical claims were provisional and were not treated as independent corroboration.

## Clinical guidance

1. American Academy of Ophthalmology. Age-Related Macular Degeneration Preferred Practice Pattern (2024; *Ophthalmology*, 2025). Prompt evaluation of new distortion, OCT and angiography, intravitreal anti-VEGF therapy with monthly loading and treat-and-extend dosing, pressure checks after injection, AREDS2 supplements for intermediate AMD, home Amsler grid monitoring, and low-vision rehabilitation. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ophtha.2024.12.018
2. CMID: Clinical Monitoring Intelligence Database (Aether Forge, 2026), cataract and glaucoma monographs. AMD is not one of the 30 CMS conditions, so only the general eye-examination items are used: best-corrected acuity and tonometry at each comprehensive eye examination, functional impairment assessment, and an annual comprehensive eye evaluation at age 65 or older. Depression and cognitive screening at 65 or older follow the general CMID items. https://patrickrutledge.github.io/topthirty/

## Editorial resolutions

- **Index age.** The modern episode presents O’Keeffe at 80, her age in 1968, when the museum chronology records that her eyesight began to fail. The synthetic date of birth is November 15, 1945 (shift 58 years), so the visits from March to July 2026 map to March to July 1968. Past medical history and family history include only events before the index date; the 1971 loss of central vision, her later work with assistants, and her death remain in the historical timeline.
- **Type of macular degeneration.** The sources say macular degeneration but not whether it was dry or neovascular. The modern episode makes the left eye neovascular and the right eye intermediate dry AMD, consistent with the 1972 letter describing the left eye as worse. Both are synthetic.
- **Teaching contrast.** Her documented course, from failing sight in 1968 to central vision loss in 1971, happened before anti-VEGF therapy existed. The synthetic episode shows what modern treatment and monitoring would offer; it does not claim that treatment would have changed her course.
- **1933 hospitalization.** The museum chronology records “psychoneurosis.” The term is kept as recorded and marked sensitive; no modern psychiatric diagnosis is inferred or coded, so no safety assessment is required.
- **Earlier onset.** The suggestion that first signs appeared as early as 1964 comes from a secondary advocacy source and is graded **R**, possible. It is kept off the problem list.
- **Medications.** Intravitreal aflibercept is recorded as procedures, because the schema has no intravitreal route. The AREDS2 supplement is recorded as an intervention, because RxNorm has no single clinical drug for the full AREDS2 formula. The medication list is therefore empty.
- **Not used.** Biographies’ accounts of her relationships, the 1978 lawsuit, and secondhand reports that her vision loss was kept quiet were not needed for the clinical teaching and are not recorded. Causes of her parents’ deaths were not given in the cited source.
- O’Keeffe died in 1986, so the recent-death review rule does not apply. Ordinary clinical, provenance, and safe-messaging reviews remain required.
