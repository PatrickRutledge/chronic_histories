History of Present Illness

Ms. O'Keeffe is an 80-year-old painter with neovascular age-related macular degeneration in the left eye, treated with intravitreal aflibercept on a treat-and-extend schedule, and intermediate dry macular degeneration in the right eye. She asked for a same-day visit. Yesterday, on her usual early walk, the sun was low and the ground looked flat and gray, and she did not see the edge of a rock. Her left foot rolled inward and she fell onto her left side on soft sand. She did not hit her head, did not lose consciousness, and had no dizziness, palpitations, or chest pain beforehand. She got up by herself and walked home slowly, about half a mile, with a limp. The outer ankle swelled within an hour. Ice and elevation helped; she has taken nothing for pain. This is her first fall in more than a year. Her daily Amsler grid checks show no new distortion in either eye, and her next retina visit is in about three weeks. She lives alone and was alone on the walk. [S]
