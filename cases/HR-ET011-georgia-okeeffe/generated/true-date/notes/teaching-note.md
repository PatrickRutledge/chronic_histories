# Teaching Note — Georgia O'Keeffe pilot

> **SYNTHETIC RECORD, NOT A REAL MEDICAL RECORD**

## Why this case matters

This case shows how historical evidence can support a transparent fictional modern episode without presenting interpretation or generated content as documented fact.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish documented (**D**), retrospective (**R**), and synthetic (**S**) content.
2. Keep historical and active problems distinct.
3. Connect measurable goals and interventions to guidelines.
4. Preserve strengths, function, identity, and agency.

## Destigmatization message

A health condition is one part of a person’s life and does not erase achievement, relationships, purpose, or agency.

## Discussion questions

- Which facts support concern without establishing a modern diagnosis?
- Which entries belong in active problems, historical problems, or both?
- What additional evidence would change an evidence grade?
- How should an EHR distinguish synthetic and historical content?

## Evidence-grade summary

- **D:** Georgia Totto O'Keeffe was born on November 15, 1887, to Francis Calyxtus O'Keeffe and Ida Totto O'Keeffe at the family dairy farm near Sun Prairie, Wisconsin, the second of seven children. O'Keeffe died at St. Vincent's Hospital in Santa Fe, New Mexico, on March 6, 1986, at age 98. O'Keeffe's mother died in Charlottesville, Virginia, in May 1916, and her father died in Petersburg, Virginia, in November 1918. In 1927 O'Keeffe was admitted twice to Mount Sinai Hospital in New York for surgery to remove benign breast cysts. In February 1933 O'Keeffe was admitted to Doctor's Hospital in New York, suffering from psychoneurosis, and recuperated in Bermuda in March and April. O'Keeffe's eyesight began to fail in 1968. Early in 1971 O'Keeffe lost her central vision and kept only peripheral sight; the O'Keeffe Museum and the Whitney Museum attribute her vision loss to macular degeneration. O'Keeffe completed her last unassisted oil painting in 1972 and continued in oil with assistance until 1977; she worked unassisted in watercolor and charcoal until 1978 and in graphite until 1984. From 1973 the potter Juan Hamilton was her assistant, and from 1974 he taught her to work in clay. In a 1972 letter O'Keeffe wrote that her left eye had become much more cloudy and that her right eye seemed to be beginning to cloud.
- **R:** The first signs of O'Keeffe's macular degeneration may have appeared as early as 1964.
- **S:** Shifted identity, fictional care team, modern encounters, results, plans, and outcomes.
