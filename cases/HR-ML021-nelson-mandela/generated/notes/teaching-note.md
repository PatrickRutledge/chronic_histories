# Teaching Note — Nelson Mandela pilot

> **SYNTHETIC RECORD, NOT A REAL MEDICAL RECORD**

## Why this case matters

This case shows how historical evidence can support a transparent fictional modern episode without presenting interpretation or generated content as documented fact.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish documented (**D**), retrospective (**R**), and synthetic (**S**) content.
2. Keep historical and active problems distinct.
3. Connect measurable goals and interventions to guidelines.
4. Preserve strengths, function, identity, and agency.

## Destigmatization message

A health condition is one part of a person’s life and does not erase achievement, relationships, purpose, or agency.

## Discussion questions

- Which facts support concern without establishing a modern diagnosis?
- Which entries belong in active problems, historical problems, or both?
- What additional evidence would change an evidence grade?
- How should an EHR distinguish synthetic and historical content?

## Evidence-grade summary

- **D:** Nelson Rolihlahla Mandela was born in Mvezo, in the Eastern Cape, on 18 July 1918. Mandela was convicted with seven co-accused in the Rivonia Trial on 11 June 1964 and sentenced to life imprisonment the next day. He was held on Robben Island, moved to Pollsmoor Prison on 31 March 1982 and to Victor Verster Prison near Paarl on 7 December 1988, and was released from Victor Verster on 11 February 1990. In 1985 Mandela had prostate surgery while imprisoned. His urologist said in July 2001 that the gland had been enlarged and was causing a urinary blockage, and that part of it was removed. Mandela was taken to hospital on 12 August 1988 and diagnosed with tuberculosis; public reports describe it as early tuberculosis, treated at the Constantiaberg Medi-clinic, after which he was moved to Victor Verster Prison. His doctors said publicly in 2001 that he had been monitored regularly with the prostate-specific antigen (PSA) test, that a slight rise in the PSA led to a biopsy of the prostate, and that the biopsy confirmed microscopic cancer within the gland. News reports said the raised blood level was first found in November 2000. His spokeswoman said in July 2001 that the cancer was not of a high grade and should not shorten his life span. He received hormonal treatment (anti-androgens) for a while and then began a seven-week course of radiotherapy with curative intent, limited to the prostate, on July 24, 2001; no surgery or chemotherapy was needed. His urologist called it a very early cancer that should be cured. In August 2001 his office said he would continue radiation treatment after a review of the options by two physicians, a radiotherapist, and five urologists, who considered the size and grade of the tumour, the radiological tests, and his response to hormonal treatment; his office said his general health remained good. In 2002 Mandela announced that his doctors had cleared him of prostate cancer. Mandela was inaugurated as South Africa's first democratically elected president on 10 May 1994 and stepped down in 1999 after one term. Mandela died at his home in Johannesburg on 5 December 2013.
- **R:** None recorded.
- **S:** Shifted identity, fictional care team, modern encounters, results, plans, and outcomes.
