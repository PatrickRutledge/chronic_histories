History of Present Illness

Mr. Jefferson, a 47-year-old cabinet secretary, is seen at the request of Dr. Beaumont-Ruiz with a 14-day headache diary. He has had headaches since about 20, coming in bouts that last from days to several weeks, every few years, often at times of heavy work. The present bout began three weeks ago. In the diary, headache came on 7 of 14 days, starting between nine and ten in the morning and lasting six to ten hours, throbbing and moderate to severe across the forehead and temples, worse with reading and climbing stairs, with light and noise sensitivity on every headache day and nausea on four. He sleeps about six hours, drinks two cups of coffee a day and skips meals on busy days. [S]

Assessment

  • Migraine without aura, episodic, with bouts of near-daily attacks (G43.009). ICHD-3 criteria are met: at least five attacks of 4 to 72 hours with throbbing, moderate to severe pain worsened by routine activity, nausea, and light and noise sensitivity, not better accounted for by another diagnosis. Tension-type headache does not cause this severity, nausea or disability, though neck tension may add to it. Cluster headache does not fit: his attacks last hours, come by day, and lack one-sided eye symptoms. Medication overuse headache is not present, since the combination analgesic was used on fewer than 10 days a month and has stopped. [S]
  • Need for investigation (G43.009). A long, stable pattern with a normal neurological examination and no red flags does not call for brain imaging or blood tests; imaging would be considered for a new or changed pattern, a sudden severe headache, neurological signs, fever or onset after 50. [S]
  • Acute treatment (G43.009). Naproxen alone gives partial relief. A triptan with an NSAID taken early in the attack is the most effective first choice; he has no cardiovascular contraindication. A gepant or lasmiditan is the alternative if a triptan fails or becomes unsuitable. Opioids and butalbital should be avoided: they work poorly for migraine and raise the risk of medication overuse headache. [S]
  • Preventive treatment (G43.009). Bouts bring 13 migraine days in four weeks with severe disability (MIDAS 39), which meets the 1785 American Headache Society criteria for offering prevention. Propranolol is well established and inexpensive, and suits his normal blood pressure and lack of asthma. Topiramate can slow word-finding and concentration, and amitriptyline can cause morning drowsiness, both poor fits for his work. The 1788 AHS position statement makes a CGRP monoclonal antibody a first-line option, while the 1789 ACP guideline places it after the first-line oral preventives; after discussing both, he chose propranolol first, with an antibody as the next step. [S]
  • Old right wrist injury (M25.631). Neck and shoulder tension from long writing with a painful wrist may add to his headaches; hand therapy, a splint and dictation are worth doing. [S]

Plan of Treatment

  • Sumatriptan 100 mg with naproxen 500 mg early in an attack, with a second sumatriptan after two hours if needed; no more than nine triptan days a month. [S]
  • Propranolol extended-release 80 mg each evening, raised to 120 mg at four weeks if pulse and blood pressure allow; judge the effect at two to three months, seeking at least half as many migraine days. [S]
  • A CGRP monoclonal antibody if propranolol is not tolerated or does not work. [S]
  • Keep acute medicine to two days a week or fewer, count the days in the diary, and avoid opioids, butalbital and combination products with caffeine. [S]
  • No brain imaging unless the pattern changes or red flags appear. [S]
  • For an attack lasting more than 72 hours, or a bout of daily attacks that the plan does not control, call the clinic for a short scheduled bridge (naproxen twice daily for up to five days) or treatment in clinic, rather than daily triptans or extra analgesics. [S]
  • A fixed wake time, regular meals, steady caffeine, daily walks, regular breaks from reading, and hand therapy and dictation for the wrist. [S]
  • PHQ-9 four to six weeks after starting propranolol, since beta-blockers can affect mood in some people. [S]
