## Assessment and plan

1. **Common cold** (J00). A viral cold of four days. Bacterial sinusitis is unlikely: symptoms are under ten days, are not worsening after improving, and there is no high fever or facial swelling. Antibiotics would not help. Facial pressure with a cold is expected and differs from his migraine. **S**
   - Saline nasal rinses, steam, fluids and honey for the cough.
   - Avoid decongestant tablets (pseudoephedrine or phenylephrine), which can raise blood pressure and pulse and blunt the effect of propranolol.
   - Avoid combination cold remedies that contain analgesics or caffeine, which add to his acute-medicine days.
   - Return if symptoms last ten days, worsen after improving, or bring a high fever, eye swelling, a severe one-sided headache or a stiff neck.
2. **Migraine without aura on propranolol** (G43.009). One migraine attack in the past week, treated well; acute medicine on one day so far this month. No red flags. **S**
