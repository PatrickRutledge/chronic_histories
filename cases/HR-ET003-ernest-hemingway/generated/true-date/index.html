Teaching objective: Several conditions that make each other worse, met in one man without judgment: a 55-year-old writer with severe alcohol use disorder, a year after a concussion with a skull fracture, whose routine intake tests show iron overload. The episode covers the diagnosis of hereditary hemochromatosis by transferrin saturation, ferritin and HFE genotyping; staging the liver with elastography and MRI instead of biopsy (AASLD 1940, ACG 1948); weekly phlebotomy to a ferritin of 50 to 100 ng/mL; avoiding iron and vitamin C supplements and raw shellfish; why alcohol multiplies the liver risk of iron overload; offering testing to first-degree relatives without attributing results to anyone; the AUDIT, DSM-5-TR criteria, brief intervention and naltrexone, with the patient choosing his own drinking goal (APA 1947); depression treated with psychotherapy and a carefully chosen SSRI (VA/DoD 1951); a structured suicide-risk assessment with respectful lethal-means counseling about firearms and the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (VA/DoD 1953); post-concussion symptoms managed by symptom (VA/DoD 1950) with cognitive screening and no diagnosis of chronic traumatic encephalopathy, which can be confirmed only after death; hypertension (CMID, ACC/AHA 1946); and diabetes screening.
