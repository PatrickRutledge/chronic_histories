## Historical evidence

1. IEEE History Center. “Nikola Tesla.” Engineering and Technology History Wiki. Supports his birth on 10 July 1856 “in what is now Croatia to Serbian parents” and death on 7 January 1943; that his father was a priest; the move to Budapest in 1881 to help install a telephone exchange; his arrival in New York in 1884; and that, with financial difficulties as his patents expired, “increasingly reclusive and eccentric, Tesla lived alone in a room in the New Yorker Hotel until he died in 1943.” https://ethw.org/Nikola_Tesla
2. Tesla N. “My Inventions.” *Electrical Experimenter*, February to June and October 1919, read in the scanned magazine text at the Internet Archive. His own account of his birth in the village of Smiljan; the boyhood “peculiar affliction due to the appearance of images, often accompanied by strong flashes of light”, which “caused me great discomfort and anxiety”, his first banishing of an image at about twelve, and flashes he “never had any control over” that peaked at about twenty-five; his “violent aversion against the earrings of women”, that “the sight of a pearl would almost give me a fit”, and that “I would not touch the hair of other people except, perhaps, at the point of a revolver”, with the remark “even now I am not insensible to some of these upsetting impulses”; that “I counted the steps in my walks and calculated the cubical contents of soup plates, coffee cups and pieces of food,—otherwise my meal was unenjoyable. All repeated acts or operations I performed had to be divisible by three and if I missed I felt impelled to do it all over again, even if it took hours”; his ability from about seventeen to visualize inventions without models or drawings; cholera in his youth; and “the complete breakdown of the nerves” in Budapest, with extreme sensitivity to sound, light, and vibration, a pulse that “varied from a few to two hundred and sixty beats”, twitching and tremors, a physician who called his malady “unique and incurable”, and his recovery. https://archive.org/details/my-inventions-and-other-works-jan.-oct.-1919-nikola-tesla
3. PBS. *Tesla: Master of Lightning*, “Tesla's Early Years.” Supports his birth as a subject of the Austro-Hungarian Empire in Lika to Serbian parents, his father an Orthodox priest, and cholera at seventeen. https://www.pbs.org/tesla/ll/ll_early.html
4. PBS. *Tesla: Master of Lightning*, “Coming to America.” Supports his claim that his hours at the Edison Machine Works ran from 10:30 a.m. to 5 a.m. and that “even into old age Tesla said he only slept two or three hours a night.” https://www.pbs.org/tesla/ll/ll_america.html
5. PBS. *Tesla: Master of Lightning*, “Tower of Dreams.” Supports the Wardenclyffe project under construction by 1901, its abandonment in 1905, and that “humiliated and defeated, Tesla experienced a complete nervous breakdown.” https://www.pbs.org/tesla/ll/ll_todre.html
6. PBS. *Tesla: Master of Lightning*, “Poet and Visionary.” Supports that after his turbine work he began “rescuing injured pigeons, and often taking them back to his hotel room to nurse them”, that this “prompted speculation about his mental well-being”, and that “his aversion to germs also heightened in this period, and he began to wash his hands compulsively and would eat only boiled foods.” https://www.pbs.org/tesla/ll/ll_poevis.html
7. Villarejo-Galende A, Herrero-San Martín A. “Nikola Tesla: flashes of inspiration” [in Spanish]. *Rev Neurol* 2013;56(2):109–114. PMID 23307357. The abstract reads the experiences in his autobiography as including migraine auras, synesthesias, obsessions, and compulsions. Only the abstract was read. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23307357/
8. HISTMED Master Roster v3. Internal project source. Its clinical claims (OCD, probable autism, photophobia, specific phobias, germophobia, financial ruin, malnutrition, pigeon attachment) were provisional and were not treated as independent corroboration.

## Editorial resolutions

- **Index age.** The episode presents him at 45, in New York while the Wardenclyffe tower and laboratory were being built (under construction by 1901, abandoned in 1905). The synthetic date of birth is July 10, 1980, so the whole-year shift is 124 years. The index visit is January 13, 2026 (true frame January 13, 1902). He lived to 1943, so all fall within his lifetime. He turns 46 during the episode; the preventive visit is `preventive_adult`. Past medical history includes only events before January 1902; the 1905 breakdown, the later germ aversion and pigeons, and his death stay in the claims and timeline and are omitted from intake documents.
- **Diagnosis.** His aversions, counting, calculating, and multiples-of-three rituals, and the images and flashes of light, are recorded in his own words from *My Inventions* (**D**, sensitive), as he described them and without comment on their strangeness. The later heightened germ aversion and compulsive handwashing are **D** from PBS. Calling any of this obsessive-compulsive disorder is a retrospective interpretation (**R**, possible: the 2013 neurology review and the roster), and no diagnosis was made in his lifetime. The synthetic diagnosis, F42.2 (mixed obsessional thoughts and acts), is placed both_with_link. The F42 code requires the safety assessment, which is present.
- **Autism.** The roster's “probable autism spectrum” is speculative. It is recorded only in CLM-0014 as a retrospective label that this record does not adopt; the modern episode makes no autism diagnosis and does not suggest one.
- **Visual imagery and flashes of light.** His boyhood images and flashes are recorded as history only. The 2013 review's reading of them as migraine auras and synesthesias is **R**. In the modern episode he describes deliberate visual imagery of his designs that he knows is his own imagination, which is recorded as not psychotic; he reports no flashes of light at the preventive visit, and no migraine or other diagnosis is added.
- **The Budapest illness.** His own account of “the complete breakdown of the nerves” in 1881 is **D** and is in the past medical history as he described it; no diagnosis is inferred from it. The physician he mentions is not named and no statement is attributed beyond his own report.
