History of Present Illness

Mr. Tesla is seen at the request of Dr. Kovač-Holt, two weeks after starting sertraline 50 mg, which he tolerates with brief nausea. He describes contamination, symmetry, and number obsessions since childhood that have waxed and waned and worsened over the past six months under work pressure, with washing, counting, repeating, and calculating compulsions that take three to four hours a day. He resists them at a cost of rising anxiety and recognizes them as excessive. He has no motor or vocal tics, no period of elevated mood or reduced need for sleep with racing thoughts beyond his long-standing short sleep, no hallucinations or fixed false beliefs, no hoarding, and no concern about his appearance. His vivid imagery of designs is voluntary and recognized as his own. His perfectionism is confined to the rituals he finds distressing and does not otherwise rule his relationships. He does not fear weight gain or dislike his body shape; he skips meals because calculating them is exhausting. He has never harmed himself and has no suicidal thoughts. He drinks rarely and uses no drugs. [S]

Plan of Treatment

  • Combine weekly exposure and response prevention with sertraline, given severe symptoms. [S]
  • Keep sertraline at 50 mg for now; if the response is inadequate after four to six weeks, raise it in 50 mg steps every four to six weeks as side effects allow, up to 200 mg, with a check for side effects about two weeks after each increase. [S]
  • Judge the response only after eight to twelve weeks with at least four to six weeks at the top tolerated dose; a fall of 35 percent or more in the Y-BOCS is a response. [S]
  • Rate the Y-BOCS about every four to six weeks during titration; screen mood and suicide risk at each visit. [S]
  • If the response is still inadequate after an adequate trial at the highest tolerated dose, ask psychiatry about a switch to another SSRI or clomipramine, or augmentation, with the monitoring each needs. [S]
  • Continue an effective dose for at least one to two years before any slow taper, with ERP booster sessions. [S]
  • Fixed mealtimes and dietitian support; examinations explained step by step, with hand hygiene offered, and no unplanned exposures outside therapy. [S]
