Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Franklin Delano Roosevelt was born on January 30, 1882, at Springwood, the family's estate in Hyde Park, New York. [D]
  • In the summer of 1921 Roosevelt developed a paralytic illness at Campobello; on August 25, 1921, Dr. Robert Lovett diagnosed infantile paralysis (polio), and Roosevelt remained paraplegic, using leg braces from early 1922. [D]
  • A 2003 Bayesian analysis of the recorded symptoms concluded that Guillain-Barré syndrome was more probable than paralytic polio as the cause of Roosevelt's 1921 illness; the conclusion is disputed and cannot be confirmed because no cerebrospinal fluid was examined. [R]
  • In May 1941 Roosevelt had severe iron deficiency anemia, with hemoglobin reported at 4.5 g/100 mL. [D]
  • At an examination at Bethesda Naval Hospital on March 27, 1944, Roosevelt's blood pressure was 186/108; he was breathless and cyanotic with rales, and X-rays and an electrocardiogram showed an enlarged heart, congestive heart failure, and a mitral murmur. His blood pressure had not been checked since February 27, 1941. [D]
  • After the March 1944 examination Roosevelt was treated with digitalis, a low-sodium and weight-reduction diet, and rest; his working hours were reduced, his cigarettes were limited to ten a day, and his evening cocktails were reduced. [D]
  • Roosevelt's father, James Roosevelt, died in 1900 during Franklin's first term at Harvard; his mother, Sara Delano Roosevelt, died at Springwood in September 1941. [D]
