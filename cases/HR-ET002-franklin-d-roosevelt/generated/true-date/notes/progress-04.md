## Assessment and plan

1. **Acute viral gastroenteritis with mild volume depletion** (A08.4). A likely norovirus-type illness given the short incubation, vomiting followed by watery diarrhea, and other people ill after the same meal. There is no blood in the stool, high fever, or severe pain, so stool testing and antibiotics are not indicated. Dry mucous membranes, low-normal blood pressure, and a 2.4 kg weight loss since June point to mild volume depletion that oral fluids can correct. **S**
   - Oral rehydration solution in small, frequent sips, about 200 mL after each loose stool, then a normal diet as tolerated.
   - Loperamide 4 mg once, then 2 mg after each loose stool, up to 8 mg a day, only while the stool has no blood and the temperature stays below 38.5 °C.
   - Hand washing with soap and water, because alcohol gel is less effective against norovirus; bathroom surfaces cleaned with a bleach-based cleaner.
   - Same-day basic metabolic panel.
2. **Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction** (I50.22). Euvolemic to dry, with no signs of congestion. Potassium 4.9 mmol/L is at the upper end of normal and creatinine 1.42 mg/dL is up from 1.24 in June, a rise below the threshold for acute kidney injury, consistent with volume depletion while on renin-angiotensin, mineralocorticoid, and SGLT2 blockade. **S**
   - Sick-day rule: hold empagliflozin, spironolactone, and sacubitril/valsartan from tonight until he has been eating and drinking normally for 24 hours, then restart all three at the usual doses.
   - Continue carvedilol 12.5 mg twice daily with food, because stopping a beta-blocker abruptly can destabilize heart failure. Skip a single dose only if the seated systolic pressure on his home monitor is below 90 mmHg or he is lightheaded at rest.
   - Continue atorvastatin.
   - After restarting, call for breathlessness, needing extra pillows, or new leg swelling.
   - If the diarrhea lasts beyond 48 hours or he cannot restart the held medicines by then, return for a repeat basic metabolic panel; otherwise potassium and creatinine are rechecked at the September review.
3. **Hypertensive heart disease** (I11.0). Blood pressure 102/64 mmHg seated, lower than his usual 120s systolic because of volume depletion. **S**
   - No change in long-term treatment; the temporary holds above cover today's lower pressure.
4. **Paraplegia after a paralytic illness at age 39; uses a manual wheelchair** (B91). Lightheadedness and frequent toileting raise his risk of a transfer fall and of moisture-related skin damage over pressure areas. **S**
   - Sit at the edge of the bed for a minute before each transfer, use the transfer board, and have someone present for transfers until he is drinking normally.
   - Barrier cream to the perineal and sacral skin after each stool, and a daily skin check with a mirror or a helper.
   - Keep up pressure relief in the wheelchair.
