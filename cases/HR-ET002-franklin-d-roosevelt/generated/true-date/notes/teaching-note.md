# Teaching Note — Franklin D. Roosevelt pilot

> **SYNTHETIC RECORD, NOT A REAL MEDICAL RECORD**

## Why this case matters

This case shows how historical evidence can support a transparent fictional modern episode without presenting interpretation or generated content as documented fact.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish documented (**D**), retrospective (**R**), and synthetic (**S**) content.
2. Keep historical and active problems distinct.
3. Connect measurable goals and interventions to guidelines.
4. Preserve strengths, function, identity, and agency.

## Destigmatization message

A health condition is one part of a person’s life and does not erase achievement, relationships, purpose, or agency.

## Discussion questions

- Which facts support concern without establishing a modern diagnosis?
- Which entries belong in active problems, historical problems, or both?
- What additional evidence would change an evidence grade?
- How should an EHR distinguish synthetic and historical content?

## Evidence-grade summary

- **D:** Franklin Delano Roosevelt was born on January 30, 1882, at Springwood, the family's estate in Hyde Park, New York. Roosevelt died of a cerebral hemorrhage at Warm Springs, Georgia, on April 12, 1945, aged 63. In the summer of 1921 Roosevelt developed a paralytic illness at Campobello; on August 25, 1921, Dr. Robert Lovett diagnosed infantile paralysis (polio), and Roosevelt remained paraplegic, using leg braces from early 1922. In May 1941 Roosevelt had severe iron deficiency anemia, with hemoglobin reported at 4.5 g/100 mL. At an examination at Bethesda Naval Hospital on March 27, 1944, Roosevelt's blood pressure was 186/108; he was breathless and cyanotic with rales, and X-rays and an electrocardiogram showed an enlarged heart, congestive heart failure, and a mitral murmur. His blood pressure had not been checked since February 27, 1941. After the March 1944 examination Roosevelt was treated with digitalis, a low-sodium and weight-reduction diet, and rest; his working hours were reduced, his cigarettes were limited to ten a day, and his evening cocktails were reduced. Roosevelt's blood pressure remained severely elevated, reported up to 260/150 in February 1945 and ranging from 170/88 to 240/130 at Warm Springs before his death. Roosevelt's father, James Roosevelt, died in 1900 during Franklin's first term at Harvard; his mother, Sara Delano Roosevelt, died at Springwood in September 1941.
- **R:** A 2003 Bayesian analysis of the recorded symptoms concluded that Guillain-Barré syndrome was more probable than paralytic polio as the cause of Roosevelt's 1921 illness; the conclusion is disputed and cannot be confirmed because no cerebrospinal fluid was examined.
- **S:** Shifted identity, fictional care team, modern encounters, results, plans, and outcomes.
