Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Index age. The modern episode presents Roosevelt at 62, his age at the March 27, 1944 examination. The index visit falls on March 27, so the true-date variant lands on the documented examination date. Past medical history and family history include only events before then; the 1945 readings and his death remain in the historical timeline.
  2. Modern diagnosis. Bruenn’s 1944 diagnoses of hypertension, hypertensive heart disease, and left ventricular failure are documented (D). The synthetic episode makes them a modern diagnosis of hypertensive heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, treated with therapies that did not exist in 1944.
  3. Contested cause of paralysis. The contemporaneous diagnosis was polio, so the problem list records sequelae of poliomyelitis (ICD-10-CM B91), verification provisional. The Guillain-Barré proposal is graded R and kept off the active problem list.
  4. Anemia. The 1941 anemia is documented in a secondary source and graded D with certainty probable. The modern iron deficiency is synthetic and is linked to it, not derived from it.
  5. Disability. He uses a wheelchair throughout the episode. Height is reported, weight is measured on a wheelchair scale, and heart failure symptoms are judged by wheelchair propulsion and orthopnea rather than walking distance.
  6. Sick and preventive visits. Two visits were added to the chronic-care schedule: an unscheduled sick visit on July 27, 2026 (true frame July 27, 1944) for a viral gastroenteritis, and a periodic adult preventive visit on October 23, 2026 (true frame October 23, 1944). Both are synthetic. He is 62, so the visit follows USPSTF adult preventive services rather than a Medicare wellness visit. The gastroenteritis does not echo a documented illness. It was chosen because volume depletion tests the sick-day handling of sacubitril/valsartan, spironolactone, and empagliflozin, with carvedilol continued, and because diarrhea raises transfer-fall and skin risks for a wheelchair user; the creatinine rise is kept below the threshold for acute kidney injury and recovers by the September review. The preventive visit uses the documented heavy smoking to make him eligible for lung cancer screening. It replaces gait and falls screening with transfer safety, pressure-injury, and shoulder checks. It records no skin-lesion finding, because the melanoma hypothesis is not used, and no new weakness or post-polio diagnosis, so the contested cause of paralysis is not extended. Hepatitis C and HIV screening, the low-dose CT, and the stool test are ordered, and their results are not recorded. Its advance care planning names no real person as his health care agent.
  7. Focused visits. The three chronic-care follow-ups (two weeks, three months, and six months) are documented as focused visits on the CMID heart failure, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and anemia monographs; the diabetes monograph has no prediabetes surveillance section, so yearly HbA1c follows the hypertension monograph and the USPSTF. Each visit lists every item the protocols call for at that point, with its interval and status. Synthetic findings introduced: the two-week examination (jugular venous pressure 9 cm, basal crackles, S3, 1+ edema) and later examinations, home weight and blood pressure logs, palpable foot pulses at six months, a cardiac rehabilitation referral in June adapted for a wheelchair user with arm ergometry and resistance bands (consistent with the band exercise recorded at the preventive visit), and his preference at six months to keep sacubitril/valsartan 49/51 mg and carvedilol 12.5 mg below target doses to protect transfer safety, which is how the goal of highest tolerated doses is read. Protocol deviations recorded instead of invented results: no ECG and no liver panel from the diagnostic workup; no basic metabolic panel one to two weeks after the April sacubitril/valsartan increase and spironolactone start; no panel one to two weeks after furosemide stopped in June; no panel within two weeks of restarting the medicines held during the July illness (the sick visit deferred it to September); magnesium never measured with any panel; the post-statin lipid panel drawn at 26 weeks instead of 4 to 12; and iron studies drawn ten weeks after intravenous iron instead of six to eight, a delay planned in April to spare a trip. Later results show no harm from any of them. Endoscopy for iron deficiency is recorded as not indicated, because ferritin 48 ng/mL is above the 30 ng/mL threshold for absolute iron deficiency.
  8. Consultation. The cardiology referral and Dr. Castellanos's consultation on the day of the two-week visit are synthetic and are written to agree with the record: the titration, spironolactone, intravenous iron, and pneumococcal vaccination that day, the later furosemide stop, cardiac rehabilitation, and the repeat echocardiogram. Synthetic details introduced: the echocardiogram description (dilated, globally hypokinetic left ventricle without regional wall motion abnormality or significant valve disease) alongside the recorded ejection fraction of 30 percent. Judgment calls: (1) an ischemic evaluation is deferred unless the ejection fraction fails to improve or angina develops; the ejection fraction later rose to 45 percent, so none is recorded; (2) the consultant notes that no ECG is on file and asks for one before any device decision, matching the deviation recorded at the visit; no device decision became necessary.
  9. Not used. The melanoma hypothesis about his final illness was not verified for this case and is not recorded. The completeness of Bruenn’s 1970 account is debated by historians; the case relies on the two sources that summarize it.
  10. Roosevelt died in 1945, so the recent-death review rule does not apply. Ordinary clinical, provenance, and safe-messaging reviews remain required.
