Please establish whether these are epileptic seizures, functional/dissociative seizures, syncope, or something else, ideally by capturing events on video-EEG, and advise on the antiseizure medicine, treatment, driving, and his studies. History of Present Illness

Mr. Madison is seen at the request of Dr. Pembroke-Achebe on the third day of video-EEG monitoring, and again this morning before discharge. He describes two years of spells that begin with a feeling of distance and heaviness, after which he cannot move or answer though he may hear what is said; they last two to ten minutes, end gradually, and leave him tearful and tired. There is no aura of rising sensation, déjà vu, automatisms he is aware of, tongue biting, incontinence, injury, or events from sleep. They began during a period of intense study and little sleep and are most frequent after long days. There is no history of febrile seizures, head injury, or meningitis, and no palpitations or exertional events. Levetiracetam for four months has made no difference and has made him irritable. He reports low mood and constant worry about his future and his health, without suicidal thoughts. He has not driven since his first primary care visit. [S]

Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • James Madison was born at Port Conway, Virginia, on 16 March 1751 in the Gregorian calendar (5 March, Old Style), the first of twelve children of James Madison and Nelly Conway Madison; his mother was visiting her own mother's home there. A family sketch he sent in 1834 gives the date as '5th of March, 1751 (O. S.)'. [D]
  • The Madison family's wealth lay in land, enslaved people, and tobacco. In the early 1760s the plantation's enslaved labor force built the brick house half a mile from the first family home, the estate later named Montpelier, in Orange County, Virginia. [D]
  • The Library of Congress describes him as an excellent student but a frail and sickly child. His health had always been delicate, and Williamsburg was regarded as unhealthy, which was among the reasons he went north to the College of New Jersey (Princeton) rather than to William and Mary. [D]
  • He entered the College of New Jersey in 1769, graduated in 1771, and stayed on several months to study Hebrew and other subjects under John Witherspoon. His biographer Gaillard Hunt wrote that for several months he spared only three hours in twenty-four for sleep, until he found his constitution unable to endure the strain. In his own late autobiography he wrote that 'his very infirm health, had been occasioned not a little by a doubled labor', accomplishing the studies of two years in one. A later account that he was excused from the commencement exercises cannot be confirmed from the college's records. [D]
  • He returned to Virginia in 1772 and studied law. In November 1772 he wrote to his college friend William Bradford: 'I am too dull and infirm now to look out for extraordinary things in this world, for I think my sensations for many months have intimated to me not to expect a long or healthy life'. The Miller Center describes these as some of the most morose letters of his life, written with failing health and the expectation of an early death; Hunt describes a solitary, purposeless period darkened by enfeebled health. [D]

Assessment

  • Functional/dissociative seizures, documented by video-EEG (F44.5). Both habitual events were captured with a normal waking EEG throughout, closed and resisting eyes, and a fluctuating course: with a typical history and normal interictal EEG and MRI, this is a documented diagnosis by the ILAE criteria. I find no evidence of epilepsy: focal impaired-awareness seizures of this length would be expected to change the EEG, and the events have not responded to levetiracetam. Syncope does not explain events of several minutes with closed eyes and a normal EEG. The onset under heavy study and little sleep, with anxiety and depression, fits the usual picture. I use the ILAE task force term functional/dissociative seizures with him. [S]
  • Taking an antiseizure medicine without a confirmed diagnosis (Z79.899). Levetiracetam is not needed and is probably adding to his irritability; it should be withdrawn. [S]
  • Major depressive disorder, single episode, moderate (F32.1). Depression and generalized anxiety are present and should be treated alongside the seizures. [S]

Data reviewed: Video-EEG for 72 hours with sleep deprivation, hyperventilation, and photic stimulation, on levetiracetam: two habitual events recorded (2 minutes 40 seconds and 6 minutes), with eyes closed and resisting opening, fluctuating unresponsiveness, and slow side-to-side head movements; the EEG showed a continuous normal waking posterior rhythm during both, with no ictal or postictal change; interictal EEG normal awake and asleep; he confirmed both events as typical. Plan of Treatment

  • Diagnosis: functional/dissociative seizures, documented level; not epilepsy and not syncope. [S]
  • Video-EEG was indicated and has captured his habitual events; no further EEG is needed unless a different kind of event appears. [S]
  • Withdraw levetiracetam over three weeks (250 mg twice daily for two weeks, 250 mg at night for one week, then stop), with no driving during the taper; record on his emergency card that typical events should not be treated with benzodiazepines or antiseizure medicines. [S]
  • Explain the diagnosis as I did yesterday: name it; say that it is real, common, and not epilepsy or put on; explain how the brain can switch off awareness under strain; say why antiseizure medicines do not help; say that it can improve with treatment; give a written summary and a reliable online resource; and review his questions at the next visit. [S]
  • Twelve weekly sessions of CBT-informed psychotherapy for functional seizures with an event diary, grounding techniques, and a relapse-prevention plan; treat the depression and anxiety in the same course, and add an SSRI such as sertraline if they persist, remembering that sertraline alone does not reduce the events. [S]
