Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Index age and whereabouts. The modern episode presents him at 55. The synthetic date of birth is 1970-07-16 (the true month and day), so the shift is 98 years. The index visit is February 10, 2026 (true frame February 10, 1928) and the last visit is June 9, 2026 (true frame June 9, 1928), a week before he left Uranienborg for the last time on June 16, 1928. Letters place him in Oslo in August 1927 after his lecture tour of Japan and again on May 28, 1928, and the museum of his home says he lived there until June 16, 1928; his day-to-day whereabouts between February and June 1928 were not verified. The period was chosen because it is the last documented stretch of his life at home and lets the final visit hold the brief's risk discussion about an Arctic rescue flight. The source for the letters is an auction catalogue description, used for dates and places only.
  2. Thin chart. His documented health history is thin and comes mostly from his own autobiography: near-sightedness, frostbitten fingers in his early twenties, scurvy on the Belgica, the shoulder fracture and polar bear attack on the Maud, the observatory lamp episode with months of palpitations, and a heart specialist's warning in 1922 that he ignored without lasting effect by his account. No illness in 1927 or 1928 was found in a fetched source. The modern episode is built around a defensible, common late consequence of his documented frostbite: cold-induced neuropathic pain and cold sensitivity of the fingers. That condition is synthetic; no source says he had it.
  3. Frostbite. The only documented frostbite of his own is of the fingers on the winter crossing (CLM-0003). He placed it "in my twenty-second year," which would be about 1893 or 1894; other accounts give a different year, which was not checked in a fetched source, so the date is recorded as approximate. The roster's "frostbite (managed)" is not otherwise supported.
  4. Belgica and its physician. His own account says every member of the company had scurvy and that fresh seal and penguin meat brought improvement within a week; the museum of his home says the expedition's physician encouraged the crew to eat it. The physician is not named anywhere in this record, and the commander's refusal of the meat, described in the autobiography, is left out as another man's health and decisions. The beriberi interpretation (Guly) concerns the expedition as a whole and is graded R.
  5. The observatory episode and his heart. His own account is recorded (D). The encyclopedia's "carbon monoxide poisoning" is graded R, because he wrote that he was never sure what happened. The 1922 heart specialist's verdict is recorded as he reported it; no heart diagnosis is adopted, and the modern ECG before the flight discussion is normal and makes no claim about his real heart.
  6. Mood. The encyclopedia's description of him at 55 as tired, lonely and bitter, with money problems, is recorded as a documented claim marked sensitive (CLM-0013). It anchors only the modern PHQ-9 screening; the synthetic PHQ-9 scores (5, then 3) are below 10, no behavioral-health diagnosis is made, and no safety assessment is required.
  7. Bankruptcy year. The encyclopedia places his personal bankruptcy in the context of 1925; the museum of his home says 1924. The claim states the bankruptcy without a year.
  8. Death. The brief's "died in the Barents Sea" is supported by May and Lewis ("into the Barents Sea"); the encyclopedia gives "near Bjørnøya in the Arctic Ocean." Both are recorded, with Bjørnøya placed in the Barents Sea. The historians' view that he took a significant risk is graded R. Neither appears in the modern episode, and the risk discussion at the last visit makes no prediction; it records the decision as his.
  9. Choice of condition and treatment. A late small-fiber neuropathy with cold-induced vasospasm after frostbite is a judgment call; normal nerve conduction studies and normal blood tests are used to teach the exclusion of entrapment, diabetes, B12 deficiency and thyroid disease. Capsaicin cream comes first because he wishes to avoid tablets (NICE 1.1.11), then duloxetine, chosen among the first-line drugs for less sedation in a man who drives and flies; that choice is a clinical judgment, not a NICE ranking. ICD-10-CM M79.2 is used for the neuropathic pain, with T33.531S and T33.532S for the frostbite sequela. The old humerus fracture is coded S42.201S from his documented injury; its range of movement is synthetic. Height, weight and all results are synthetic.
  10. No CMID monograph. The CMID does not cover neuropathic pain or frostbite, so the focused visits follow NICE CG173 and the WMS guidelines, as the brief allows.
  11. Habits. His floor-spring strength training and salt-water morning baths are documented by the museum of his home and anchor the synthetic exercise history. "Never smoked" and "rare alcohol" are synthetic and not claims about his real habits. Whether he lived alone in 1928 and his marital status were not checked in a fetched source and are not recorded.
  12. Family. No family medical history is recorded. Real people who appear in the documented claims (his companions at the Pole, the crewman who set his fracture, the men lost with him) are not named, and none is given a health fact beyond what the account of his own injuries or death requires.
  13. Aliases and names. "Roald Engelbregt Gravning Amundsen" is recorded as the alias; it appears in no other record. The record names no other figure who has a record in the dataset, does not name the Belgica's physician, and refers to no rival expedition. Norwegian place names keep their letters (Østfold, Gjøa, Ny-Ålesund, Bjørnøya).
  14. Sick and preventive visits. A periodic adult preventive visit (age 55) on April 7, 2026 (true frame April 7, 1928) and a same-day sick visit on May 19, 2026 (true frame May 19, 1928) were added. The sick visit is an acute right ankle sprain on the shore below his house, chosen because it interacts with duloxetine (no tramadol; a topical rather than an oral NSAID) and teaches the Ottawa ankle rules; it does not echo a documented illness.
  15. Focused visits. The four chronic-care follow-ups (two weeks, six weeks, twelve weeks, and seventeen weeks) are documented as focused visits on NICE CG173 (pain, adverse effects, function, mood, sleep), duloxetine safety monitoring (blood pressure, sodium and ALT, interactions, no abrupt stopping), and the WMS frostbite and hypothermia guidelines. One protocol deviation is recorded: the sodium and ALT check ordered for two to four weeks after starting duloxetine was missed and drawn at six weeks, with normal results, no harm, and a corrective action.
  16. Consultation. The neurology consultation by the fictional Dr. Abernathy-Dahl on the second visit is synthetic. Its recommendations (capsaicin cream first, then duloxetine with monitoring and no tramadol, cold protection, no calcium-channel blocker for now, protection of the previously frostbitten fingers, and no further tests) are those the later visits carry out.
  17. Amundsen died in 1928, so the recent-death review rule does not apply. Ordinary clinical and provenance reviews remain required.
