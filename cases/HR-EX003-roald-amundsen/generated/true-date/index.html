Teaching objective: Primary care and neurology for a 55-year-old polar explorer with cold-induced burning pain, numbness and color change in the fingers decades after frostbite: separating a late post-frostbite small-fiber neuropathy and cold-induced vasospasm from diabetes, B12 deficiency, thyroid disease and nerve entrapment with blood tests and nerve conduction studies; cold protection and hand care; pharmacological management by NICE CG173, choosing capsaicin cream first because he wishes to avoid tablets and then duloxetine, a first-line drug less sedating than amitriptyline or a gabapentinoid for a man who flies and drives; monitoring of pain, adverse effects, mood, sleep, blood pressure and sodium; avoiding tramadol with duloxetine; preventive care at 55; and a risk discussion about an Arctic air search flight: cold-water immersion, re-injury of previously frostbitten fingers, a stiff old shoulder fracture, and carrying and not stopping duloxetine. Built on NICE CG173, the Wilderness Medical Society frostbite (1926) and hypothermia (1921) guidelines, and the USPSTF and CDC preventive recommendations.

Social, functional, and mental status

  • S Polar explorer and expedition leader, now writing and lecturing; still plans flights and expeditions in the Arctic; often travels by air and sea
  • S Lives in his own house on a fjord outside Oslo; under financial strain after a bankruptcy
  • S Exercise: strength training with floor springs most mornings, a cold salt-water bath each morning, and long walks and skiing; no limitation
  • S Tobacco: never. Alcohol: rarely, a glass of wine at a dinner; no other drugs
  • S Drives a car and flies as a passenger and expedition navigator; wants to stay fit to fly in the Arctic
