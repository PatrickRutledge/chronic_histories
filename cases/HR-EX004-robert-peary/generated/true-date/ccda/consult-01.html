Assessment

  • Pernicious anemia (D51.0). Confirmed. Severe cobalamin deficiency (a low vitamin B12 with a high methylmalonic acid) with megaloblastic pancytopenia and ineffective red cell production, and a positive intrinsic factor antibody, which is highly specific for pernicious anemia; a positive parietal cell antibody and a high gastrin show autoimmune loss of the acid-producing cells of the stomach. The samples were drawn after four injections; the laboratory's guidance on its intrinsic factor assay was followed, and the parietal cell antibody and gastrin agree. Folate is normal, and there is no dietary, drug or surgical cause. [S]
  • Treatment (D51.0). With neurological involvement, intramuscular treatment without delay is right. Complete the seven loading doses, then give 1,000 micrograms weekly while the nerves are improving, then monthly for life, because autoimmune gastritis does not recover. High-dose oral cyanocobalamin (at least 1 mg a day) works because about 1 percent of a large dose is absorbed without intrinsic factor, and is a reasonable maintenance alternative once he has recovered, if he prefers it and takes it reliably; NICE advises intramuscular treatment for life when autoimmune gastritis is the cause. Vitamin B12 levels need not be rechecked once treatment is established. [S]
  • Transfusion and early complications (D51.0). No transfusion: he is stable at rest, the reticulocyte peak shows the marrow responding, and transfusion in severe megaloblastic anemia can overload the circulation. Potassium can fall in the first days of treatment as new cells take it up; the early check was missed, and today's 3.6 mmol/L is acceptable, to be repeated at the next visit. Iron deficiency often appears as the count recovers, because new cells use the stores and an acid-poor stomach absorbs iron poorly; iron studies should be repeated at about eight weeks. [S]
  • Endoscopy and surveillance (D51.0). Upper endoscopy is advised now, with biopsies from the antrum, incisura and body in separate jars (updated Sydney protocol), to confirm autoimmune gastritis and to look for type 1 neuroendocrine tumors and dysplasia. Surveillance afterwards depends on the findings: endoscopy every one to two years if neuroendocrine tumors are found and removed; otherwise an interval decided with him, for example three years with extensive atrophy or metaplasia. A screening colonoscopy, never done, can be done at the same session. [S]
  • Nerves and associated conditions (G32.0). The posterior column and peripheral nerve signs fit subacute combined degeneration. Most recovery happens over three to six months and some numbness may remain, especially at the ends of the feet, so his old toe amputations and the new sensory loss together call for careful foot protection and balance training. Autoimmune thyroid disease is common with autoimmune gastritis: TSH is normal and the thyroid antibody negative; check TSH yearly. HbA1c is normal. [S]
