## Assessment and plan

1. **Acute laryngitis, probably viral** (J04.0). Hoarseness of three days after a cold, without stridor, trouble swallowing, or signs of a bacterial infection. Hoarseness of less than four weeks after a cold does not need laryngoscopy unless it persists or there are warning signs. Antibiotics do not help. A steroid is not used routinely for acute laryngitis and was not given: it would not shorten a viral illness much, can affect mood and sleep during her taper, and should not be given without examining the vocal folds. **S**
   - Voice rest for three days, no singing until the voice returns, then a gradual return to singing; no whispering.
   - Fluids, humidified air and honey for the cough.
   - Acetaminophen 1,000 mg every 6 hours as needed, no more than 3,000 mg a day.
