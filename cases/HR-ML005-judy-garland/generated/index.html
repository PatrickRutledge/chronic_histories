Teaching objective: Iatrogenic dependence and the duty to prescribe controlled drugs safely, especially to minors: nonjudgmental primary care for a 33-year-old singer and actor, three months after childbirth, who has taken prescribed barbiturate sleeping pills and amphetamine since she was a teenager. The episode covers a DSM-5-TR assessment that does not count tolerance and withdrawal from supervised use, the DAST-10, a prescription drug monitoring program review, one prescriber and limited dispensing, a supervised hospital change to phenobarbital because barbiturate withdrawal can cause seizures, a slow outpatient taper that follows the tapering principles of the 2025 Joint Clinical Practice Guideline on Benzodiazepine Tapering (which excludes barbiturates) with addiction medicine guidance, a short stimulant taper, CBT for insomnia as first-line treatment instead of another hypnotic, depression and anxiety care with a structured safety assessment, means safety and the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, contraception that phenobarbital does not weaken, nutrition and weight without stigma, the reason naloxone was not prescribed, and planning around the demands of performing.

Disposition
Outpatient care with close follow-up. Risk factors reviewed without detail: past suicide attempts reported in earlier years, a sedative use disorder, depression, the months after childbirth, and the low mood that can follow stopping a stimulant. Barbiturates are dangerous in overdose, so means safety is part of the plan: she agreed to take the unused capsules from earlier prescriptions to a pharmacy take-back at the next visit, to keep the current supply in a locked box, and to receive no more than seven days at a time. No alcohol with the barbiturate. A written safety plan was made with her, and the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988) was saved in her phone. Safety is reviewed at every visit
