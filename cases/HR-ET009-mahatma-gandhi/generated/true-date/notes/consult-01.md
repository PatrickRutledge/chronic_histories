## Reason for consultation

Please assess his nutritional status, set energy and protein targets within his diet, advise on vitamin B12, vitamin D, calcium, sodium, and potassium, and write a safe plan for the fast and for refeeding that respects his decision to fast. **S**

## Assessment

1. **Nutritional status: low BMI and reduced muscle mass without current malnutrition by GLIM** (R63.6). The phenotypic GLIM criteria are met (BMI below 22 at 70 or older and reduced muscle mass on examination and grip), but no etiologic criterion is met between fasts: intake meets his energy needs and there is no inflammatory disease. He is at risk (MNA 20). During a seven-day fast the reduced-intake criterion will be met, and after it he will be at high risk of refeeding problems by NICE criteria (BMI below 18.5 with little or no intake for more than five days). Nutrition diagnosis: inadequate protein intake related to limited protein foods in his chosen diet, as shown by about 0.9 g/kg a day against a need of 1.0 to 1.2 g/kg and low grip strength. **S**
2. **Vitamin B12 deficiency** (E53.8). Diet supplies well under the 2.4 mcg a day needed; goat's milk is a poor source. Food alone will not correct it, so a supplement is needed long term. **S**
3. **Vitamin D deficiency** (E55.9). Low vitamin D with calcium intake a little below target; relevant to his low bone mass. **S**
4. **Low bone mass** (M85.80). Calcium target 1000 to 1200 mg a day can be met with goat's milk and curd; protein adequacy also supports bone. **S**
5. **Hypertension: sodium and potassium** (I10). Sodium about 1200 mg a day is already below 1500 mg; potassium from fruit and vegetables can be raised. **S**

## Recommendations

1. Energy about 30 kcal/kg a day (about 1400 kcal) and protein 1.0 to 1.2 g/kg a day (about 50 to 55 g), spread over three meals: add a bowl of curd, a daily serving of dal or chickpeas, and a handful of groundnuts or almonds, all within his diet.
2. Vitamin B12 1 mg by mouth daily long term, as Dr. Malhotra has planned; vitamin D3 1000 units daily; calcium 1000 to 1200 mg a day from about 500 mL of goat's milk and two servings of curd, with sesame or leafy greens.
3. Keep sodium below 1500 mg a day as now; raise potassium-rich fruit and vegetables, such as bananas, oranges, and lentils, since his kidney function is normal.
4. During the fast: about 2 L a day of water with a pinch of salt and a little citrus juice, as he plans; rest and limit walking; daily home weight; the nurse's daily call.
5. Refeeding, written plan: day 1 about 10 kcal/kg (about 440 kcal) in five or six small feeds of diluted fruit juice, goat's milk, curd, and rice gruel; increase by about 250 kcal a day to about 30 kcal/kg by days 5 to 7; protein back to 1.2 g/kg by day 7; fluids 30 to 35 mL/kg a day; thiamine before the first feed, a multivitamin and mineral tablet, and electrolyte supplements as prescribed.
6. Electrolytes and weight before the first feed and daily for the first three days, then until stable, with daily dietitian calls to day 7.
7. Recheck weight, Mini Nutritional Assessment, and food record in about three months.
