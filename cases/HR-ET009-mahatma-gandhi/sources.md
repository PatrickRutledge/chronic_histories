## Editorial resolutions

- **Name.** The display name is the one by which he is known; Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi and Mohandas K. Gandhi are recorded as aliases. The notes call him Mr. Gandhi.
- **Index age.** The episode presents him at 70, his age at the 220/110 reading of February 19, 1940, and the year after the 1939 Rajkot fast and the 1939 measurement of his height and weight. The synthetic date of birth is October 2, 1955, so the whole-year shift is 86 years. The index visit is February 23, 2026 (true frame February 23, 1940, four days after that reading); the chronic-care visits follow on March 23, April 13, April 16, and July 13, 2026 (true frame the same days in 1940); the synthetic fast runs from April 6 to 12, 2026 (true frame April 6 to 12, 1940); the sick visit is June 8, 2026 (true frame June 8, 1940) and the annual wellness visit September 14, 2026 (true frame September 14, 1940). He died in 1948, so all fall within his lifetime. The visit type is `annual_wellness_subsequent` because he is 65 or older.
- **Hypertension.** His high blood pressure is documented in his health file, his physicians' statements, and his own letter (**D**). The modern stage 2 hypertension, its readings, the ECG, and the echocardiogram are synthetic; no historical reading is reused. His use of sarpagandha (Rauwolfia serpentina, the source of reserpine) is recorded as history only; the modern plan uses amlodipine, with hold rules chosen because he fasts.
- **Weight and diet.** His height and weight are documented from the 1939 basal metabolic test (**D**), and the synthetic vitals are set close to them. Press reports of a "BMI of 17.1" appear to read the basal metabolic rate printed on the same test; the figures do give a BMI of about 17. His vegetarian diet, his vow about cow's and buffalo milk, his goat's milk, and his teaching against tea, coffee, tobacco, and alcohol are **D**. Vitamin B12 and D deficiency, low bone mass, and the food-record numbers are synthetic; his historical B12 status is not known.
- **Fasting.** His public fasts are documented (**D**), including the March 1939 Rajkot fast and his last fast in January 1948. The seven-day fast in the modern episode is synthetic. It does not echo a dated historical fast, and the record does not claim that he fasted in April 1940. The care plan treats his fasting as his informed choice and an act of conscience: the clinicians state the risks once, offer hospital care, and then build a harm-reduction and refeeding plan with him; no note argues against the fast. His capacity is documented as intact.
- **Refeeding and supplements.** NICE CG32 also advises vitamin B co strong and a balanced multivitamin and mineral tablet. No single RxNorm clinical drug represents a balanced multivitamin, so the notes record an over-the-counter multivitamin and mineral tablet for ten days, and the coded medication list carries thiamine, potassium chloride, phosphate, and magnesium oxide.
- **Indian guideline.** The Indian guidelines on hypertension-IV (2019) are cited as the verifiable Indian guideline (PubMed record of the *J Hum Hypertens* publication); no ICMR hypertension guideline was located for verification.
- **Real people.** No health fact, decision, or statement is attributed to his wife, family, or associates. The physicians named in the sources are not named in the record; his 1941 letter is described as written to one of his physicians. The family history is empty because no relative's health is documented in the cited sources. The assassination is recorded only as a dated historical fact, without detail.
- **Not used.** The 1944 malaria (listed in the source with his earlier malaria), the 1945 influenza, the 1947 eye test, and his refusal of penicillin in 1947 fall after the true-frame index date and are not in the past medical history. The very low 1936 blood sugar of 41 is reported only as a claim, as written. Secondary press stories (Deccan Herald, Tribune) could not be fetched and are not cited. Gandhi died in 1948, so the recent-death review rule does not apply.
- **Sick and wellness visits.** The sick visit is dehydration with orthostatic hypotension and a near-fall in hot weather, chosen because it ties the antihypertensive hold rules, his low body weight, and falls prevention together. It does not echo a documented illness. The wellness visit records a declined statin through shared decision-making and orders a stool test; its advance care planning names no real person.
- **Focused visits.** The four chronic-care follow-ups (four weeks, the morning after the fast, refeeding day 4, and about five months) are documented as focused visits on the CMID hypertension monograph with the 2025 AHA/ACC guideline, NICE CG32 and ASPEN for refeeding, and the CMID osteoporosis monograph with the BHOF guide. Two deviations are recorded as `deferred`, each with its outcome and corrective action, and no result was invented: the daily electrolytes on refeeding days 2 and 3 (no home-visit slot, not escalated), and the repeat phosphate, potassium, and magnesium three to four days after the day 4 result (order placed but no appointment booked). The yearly fundoscopic examination is still due at the end of the episode, within its yearly window.
- **Consultation.** The clinical nutrition consultation is synthetic and is written within a registered dietitian's scope: diet history, nutrition-focused physical examination, GLIM and NICE risk assessment, a nutrition diagnosis, and diet, supplement, fast, and refeeding plans, with laboratory monitoring and prescribing left to the primary clinician. Its recommendations match what followed: vitamin B12 and D started, the fast plan and the refeeding plan followed, protein raised to about 52 g a day, and the weight and Mini Nutritional Assessment rechecked at about three months.
