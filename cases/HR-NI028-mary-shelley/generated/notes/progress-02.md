## Assessment and plan

1. **Left frontoparietal convexity meningioma with vasogenic edema** (D32.0). A symptomatic meningioma with edema, causing seizures and mild right-hand weakness; the presenting concern is resolved into this diagnosis and focal epilepsy. Observation is not advised for a symptomatic tumor with edema. Stereotactic radiosurgery is less suitable for a 3.1 cm tumor with edema next to the motor cortex, and gives no tissue diagnosis. Resection aiming at removal of the tumor with its dural attachment (Simpson grade I) is likely to be curative and to relieve the seizures and weakness. She chose resection after hearing the three options with their benefits and risks. **S**
   - Left craniotomy and resection in three weeks with Dr. Ferreira-Lindgren, with neuronavigation and motor mapping.
   - Dexamethasone 4 mg twice a day from three days before surgery, then tapered after it; not started now, because the weakness is mild.
   - MRI within 48 hours after surgery; pathology with WHO CNS5 grading.
2. **Focal epilepsy due to the meningioma** (G40.109). Recurrent focal aware seizures with a structural cause and an epileptiform EEG meet the definition of epilepsy, with a high risk of recurrence, so an antiseizure medicine is indicated now rather than prophylaxis. **S**

   - Keep a seizure diary; no driving until seizures are controlled for the period her state requires and the neurosurgeon agrees.
