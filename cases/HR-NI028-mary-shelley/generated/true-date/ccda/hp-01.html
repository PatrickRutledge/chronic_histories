  • Her first child, a daughter born prematurely in February 1815, died after a few days. [D]

  • Her daughter Clara died in Italy in 1818, at about one year, and her son William died in Italy in June 1819, at three; she then had no living children. [D]
  • Her son Percy Florence, born in November 1819, was the only one of her four children to survive childhood. [D]
  • In June 1822 she nearly died of hemorrhage after a miscarriage about three months into a pregnancy; her husband stopped the bleeding by placing her in ice baths. [D]
  • In July 1822 her husband, the poet Percy Bysshe Shelley, drowned while sailing in the Gulf of Spezia, leaving her a widow before she was 25, with a son of two; she decided to earn her living by her writing and by publishing his. [D]
  • She published the novels Valperga (1823) and The Last Man (1826), a revised Frankenstein (1831), and Rambles in Germany and Italy (1844). [D]
  • At about fourteen she had a very painful skin condition of one entire arm, treated with poultices; at times she could not move the arm and wore it in a sling. It has been interpreted as eczema, psoriasis, or chickenpox. [D]
  • In her journal in June 1840 she wrote that she had been long oppressed by care, disappointment, and ill health, and that lately ill health and extreme depression had marred what little she could do for others. [D]
  • From 1839 she had recurrent severe headaches of varying severity, site, and duration, with dizziness, pain in the legs, right hand, and right eye, brief episodes of partial paralysis of the right leg and hand, tremors, and convulsive seizures. Her physicians called the illness psychosomatic in 1842, a functional derangement of the nerves or brain in 1845, and neuralgia of the heart in 1846. [D]
  • From 1846 to 1851 she lived at 24 Chester Square, Belgravia, London, and spent time at Field Place in Sussex, the home of her son and his wife after their marriage in 1848. [D]
