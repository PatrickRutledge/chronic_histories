Assessment

  • Left frontoparietal convexity meningioma with vasogenic edema (D32.0). The appearance is typical of a meningioma. Because it causes seizures and weakness and is surrounded by edema, observation is not appropriate. Stereotactic radiosurgery works best for small tumors without mass effect; at 3.1 cm with edema next to the motor cortex it carries a real risk of worsening the swelling, and it gives no tissue for grading. Surgical resection with its dural attachment (Simpson grade I) is the treatment of choice, is likely to be curative, and should relieve the seizures. The main risk is weakness of the right hand, usually temporary, which motor mapping during surgery reduces. She chose resection. [S]
  • Focal epilepsy due to the meningioma (G40.109). Recurrent focal aware seizures with a causative lesion and an epileptiform EEG: an antiseizure medicine is indicated now. Levetiracetam suits her, with few interactions; its main drawback is irritability or low mood, which should be watched given her history. [S]
  • Edema and corticosteroids. The deficit is mild and stable, so dexamethasone is not needed now. A short course from three days before surgery, tapered over about ten days afterwards, reduces brain swelling around the operation; longer use brings more harm than benefit. [S]
  • Pathology and follow-up. Grade decides adjuvant treatment. After complete resection of a CNS WHO grade 1 meningioma, radiation is not needed and follow-up is by MRI; grade 2 or 3 features (mitotic count, brain invasion, TERT promoter mutation, or CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion) would lead to a discussion of radiation.
