Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Index age. The modern episode presents Grant at 62, his age when throat pain began in June 1884. The index visit is July 7, 2026, about four weeks into symptoms, so the true-date frame places it in July 1884. The fourth visit falls on October 22, 2026, which maps to October 22, 1884, the day Grant first saw Dr. Douglas. Past medical history and family history include only events before July 1884.
  2. Modern diagnosis. The documented diagnosis was carcinoma of the right tonsillar pillar (D). The synthetic episode makes it a biopsy-proven, p16-negative squamous cell carcinoma (ICD-10-CM C09.1). The p16 result, imaging, staging, and treatment are S. In 1884–1885 about four months passed between symptoms and specialist evaluation; the synthetic pathway reaches a tissue diagnosis in two weeks.
  3. Scope. The episode stays at primary-care level: urgent referral, imaging, tobacco cessation, nutrition, dental preparation, symptom control, mood screening, and laboratory monitoring. Chemoradiation is mentioned as directed by the head and neck oncology team and is not itemized as primary-care medication.
  4. Cause. The link between his cigars and his cancer is graded R (probable). The NCI fact sheet is general context, not evidence about Grant.
  5. Conflicting dates. The Grant Cottage gives June 16, 1885 for the arrival at Mount McGregor and the NLM finding aid gives June 17, 1885. The timeline uses June 16 and the claim notes both.
  6. Not used. The year and details of the Wall Street loss were not given by the NPS source and are not dated here. A cigar gift after Fort Donelson, a hip injury, the doctors’ later debate over surgery, Grant’s alcohol use, and his tongue involvement appear in popular accounts that were not verified for this case and are not recorded. The ENT Today/AAO-HNS Bulletin article on Grant’s tumor could not be retrieved. The Powers, Pappas, and Powers 2026 editorial (PubMed 42833982) has no abstract and was not reviewed. The 1976 Steckler and Shedd full text was not reviewed.
  7. Unscheduled sick visit. One same-day sick visit (ENC-005) was added on July 28, 2026, which maps to July 28, 1884, in the true-date frame: one week after the synthetic biopsy and two days before the staging PET/CT. No documented event falls in that window; the historical record places Grant at Long Branch with throat pain that summer and gives no intercurrent illness, so a head cold contradicts nothing. The illness, an acute viral bronchitis that resolved without antibiotics (J20.9), is S and does not echo a documented illness. It was chosen because it interacts with the workup and his smoking: aspiration had to be weighed against his dysphagia, the cold could cause reactive uptake on the PET/CT, the acetaminophen limit rules out combination cold products, a numbed throat after lidocaine raises choking risk, and illness is a relapse trigger two weeks after stopping cigars. No visit was placed during chemoradiation, when intercurrent illness is managed by the oncology team, or after October 22, 2026, the documented referral date. No adult preventive visit was added: a routine preventive examination in the middle of a new cancer workup is not sensible, and his immunizations and screening are already handled at the cancer-care visits.
  8. Focused visits. The three scheduled follow-ups (the biopsy day, the post-staging visit, and the visit three weeks after chemoradiation) are documented as focused primary-care visits. Head and neck cancer is not a CMID condition, so each protocol table follows the NCCN head and neck workup and follow-up principles (with the PubMed Insights caveat above), the ACS head and neck survivorship guideline for the post-treatment visit, the USPSTF tobacco recommendation, and the CMID depression and hypothyroidism monographs for the PHQ-2 and TSH. The biopsy-day note is the primary-care visit on the afternoon of the otolaryngology consultation; the consultation itself stays in the consult note. Synthetic findings introduced: a same-day frozen section showing squamous cell carcinoma, with p16 and the final report following within days (the problem is recorded as confirmed that day); nausea when varenicline was taken without food, which kept him on 0.5 mg twice daily for a second week and explains the step-up on the biopsy day; a quit date one week before the biopsy, matching the sick note and the goal outcome; a dietitian assessment between the biopsy and the post-staging visit; the oncology team's chemoradiation recommendation before the post-staging visit; restored teeth and gum recession on examination; a dental evaluation and nightly fluoride trays before radiation; and dry mouth and muted taste after treatment. No feeding tube is recorded, so the note says none was needed. Protocol deviations recorded: the PHQ-2 the care plan calls for at every visit after the diagnosis was not repeated on the biopsy day (recorded as deferred in that note), and the PHQ-2 of 2 at the post-staging visit was not followed by the PHQ-9 that the CMID depression monograph calls for (recorded at the post-treatment visit, where the PHQ-2 was 1, so no harm is evident). No visit is added after October 22, 2026, the documented referral date, so the post-treatment PET/CT, the first TSH after radiation, and the repeat blood counts are recorded as due.
  9. Consultation. The otolaryngology–head and neck surgery referral and consultation (Henrik Dahl, MD, a fictional consultant, on the morning of the biopsy day) are S. The note matches the biopsy-day focused visit: an ulcerated right tonsillar pillar lesion, a clear larynx, one firm right level II node, a same-day frozen section showing squamous cell carcinoma, and p16 and the final report pending. Its recommendations are what the record then shows: p16 reported negative within the week, PET/CT with no distant spread, the multidisciplinary head and neck oncology team (which recommended chemoradiation), dental care with fluoride, a speech-language pathology baseline, the dietitian, and continued abstinence on varenicline. Judgment calls: (1) no TNM stage is given, because the record gives none, and the consultant explains that stage waits for p16 and PET/CT; (2) the consultant names transoral surgery and chemoradiation as options for the team rather than choosing; (3) no tongue involvement or other finding from the unverified popular accounts is recorded; (4) normal findings added to the examination (nasopharynx, hypopharynx, vocal fold movement, mouth opening) and the dental findings first noted at the post-staging visit are consistent with the rest of the record. The consultation cites the NCCN Insights article with the same caveat as the focused visits.
  10. Headaches and fevers. Migraine and malaria are retrospective interpretations (R) and remain in past medical history, not on the active problem list.
  11. Grant died in 1885, so the recent-death review rule does not apply. Ordinary clinical, provenance, and safe-messaging reviews remain required.
