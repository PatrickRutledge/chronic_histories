History of Present Illness

Ms. Fitzgerald, a 29-year-old dancer and writer, is seen at the request of Dr. Ellery four weeks after risperidone was started in hospital. Before the admission she had weeks of very little sleep without tiredness, rapid speech, racing thoughts, grand plans to join a professional company within the year, and training for most of each day; near the end she heard a voice commenting on her dancing and became convinced that her instructors were watching and testing her through signals. She is not sure which came first. Since discharge she has been low and tired but less tearful this week, sleeps nine or ten hours, and has no psychotic or manic symptoms. She has had no previous episodes of depression or mania. She drinks a little at weekends and uses no drugs. Her periods stopped months before the admission. She eats small portions to stay light and felt guilty on rest days, but she does not binge or purge. She has no thoughts of suicide or self-harm. Her fear is that medicines will make her heavy and end her dancing. [S]

Assessment

  • First episode of psychosis with marked mood symptoms, provisional diagnosis (F29). The full manic syndrome before admission and the depressive phase after it make bipolar I disorder with psychotic features the most likely diagnosis. Schizoaffective disorder would require psychotic symptoms for two weeks or more without mood symptoms, and schizophrenia would require psychosis outside mood episodes and usually a decline in function; neither is shown, but the order of symptoms is not yet established. A clearer history, from her own notes or journal and, with her consent, from her roommate, together with the course over the next months, should settle it; the diagnosis should be reviewed at about three months and again if psychosis ever occurs without mood symptoms. Lithium is the first choice for preventing further episodes. Valproate should not be used in a woman who could become pregnant because of its risks of birth defects and effects on a child's development. Lithium carries a small added risk of heart defects with exposure early in pregnancy, which is manageable with reliable contraception and planned pregnancy. [S]
  • Secondary amenorrhea (N91.1). The periods stopped before risperidone, which points to low energy availability from heavy training at a low weight; risperidone's prolactin rise may now be prolonging it. Changing the antipsychotic and adding lithium at the same time would make side effects hard to read, so lithium should be started first and the antipsychotic switched if prolactin stays high and the periods do not return. [S]

Plan of Treatment

  • Teach and write down lithium sick-day rules: stop it during vomiting, diarrhea, or dehydration and call; avoid anti-inflammatory painkillers. [S]
