## Historical basis

- Frederick Augustus Washington Bailey, later Frederick Douglass, was born into slavery in Talbot County, Maryland, in February 1818; the exact day is not known. He later chose February 14 as his birthday, because that was the last time he saw his mother. **D**
- He was separated from his mother as an infant and saw her, to know her as such, no more than four or five times, each time briefly and at night. She died when he was about seven; he was not allowed to be present during her illness, death, or burial. He later wrote that it was "a life-long, standing grief" to him that he knew so little of her. **D**
- As a young child on the plantation where he lived from 1824 to 1826 he "suffered much from hunger, but much more from cold," with no shoes or jacket, and his feet were cracked by the frost. **D**
- As a small child he witnessed the whipping of his aunt, and wrote, "I never shall forget it whilst I remember any thing." **D**
- At about sixteen he was hired out for a year to Edward Covey, a farmer known as a "slave breaker." During the first six months "scarce a week passed without his whipping me"; he wrote that Covey "succeeded in breaking me. I was broken in body, soul, and spirit." The Narrative dates this year to 1833; the National Park Service chronology dates it to 1834. **D**
- On a hot August day that year, while carrying wheat to the fan, he broke down with "a violent aching of the head, attended with extreme dizziness" and trembling, and fell; Covey kicked him and struck him on the head. **D**
- He then resisted Covey in a fight, and afterwards wrote, "From this time I was never again what might be called fairly whipped, though I remained a slave four years afterwards." **D**
- While learning to caulk at a Baltimore shipyard he was attacked by four white apprentices; one kicked him in the left eye with a heavy boot, and he wrote that his "left eye was nearly knocked out" and that the eye was closed and badly swollen. **D**
- On September 3, 1838, he escaped to New York; he married Anna Murray, a free Black woman, and they settled in New Bedford, Massachusetts, where he worked as a caulker and took the name Frederick Douglass. **D**
- In 1841 he was hired as a lecturer by the Massachusetts Anti-Slavery Society, and in May 1845 he published the Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass, an American Slave. **D**
- On September 16, 1843, a pro-slavery mob at Pendleton, Indiana, beat him unconscious and broke his right hand; he continued the lecture tour. He later wrote that "as the bones broken were not properly set, my hand has never recovered its natural strength and dexterity." **D**
- From 1845 to 1847 he lectured in Ireland, Scotland, and England; in 1846 British admirers raised the money to buy his freedom, and he returned to the United States legally free in 1847. **D**
- In December 1847 he began publishing his own weekly newspaper, The North Star, in Rochester, New York, renamed Frederick Douglass' Paper in 1851; from Rochester he helped hundreds of people escaping slavery as a station master of the Underground Railroad. **D**
- On May 5, 1849, he was attacked by a gang while walking along the Battery in New York City with two friends. **D**
- As a boy in Baltimore he was taught his letters and then taught himself to write; in 1831 he underwent a religious conversion and joined Bethel A.M.E. Church. **D**
- He and Anna Murray Douglass had five children, born between 1839 and 1849. **D**
