## Assessment

1. **Primary adrenal insufficiency (autoimmune Addison's disease)** (E27.1). The low cortisol with a markedly raised ACTH before treatment, with low sodium and high potassium, establishes primary adrenal insufficiency, and the positive 21-hydroxylase antibodies establish an autoimmune cause. A cosyntropin test would need treatment to be stopped and adds nothing; adrenal imaging and other causes need not be pursued with positive antibodies. Glucocorticoid replacement is more than he needs at 30 mg a day; mineralocorticoid replacement is too low, shown by salt craving, the postural drop, the electrolytes and the raised renin. **S**
2. **Raised TSH with positive thyroid peroxidase antibodies** (R94.6). Probable autoimmune thyroiditis, which often accompanies autoimmune adrenal insufficiency. A raised TSH can settle once glucocorticoid replacement is established, but he has been on replacement for three months, so this is likely to be true thyroid disease. A newly raised TSH should be confirmed before treatment; levothyroxine is safe to start because glucocorticoid replacement is in place, and it must never be started before it. **S**
3. **Chronic low back pain; bone health** (M96.1). His Z-scores are within the expected range for his age, so there is no sign of bone loss from his earlier corticosteroids. Physiological replacement at 15 to 25 mg of hydrocortisone a day does not need bone-protective medicine; over-replacement is what should be avoided. **S**

## Recommendations

1. No further diagnostic testing for the adrenal insufficiency: the cause is autoimmune.
2. Reduce hydrocortisone to 10 mg on waking, 5 mg at noon and 5 mg at 4 pm (20 mg a day), adjusted by weight, energy and signs of over- or under-replacement rather than by cortisol levels.
3. Increase fludrocortisone to 0.1 mg once daily; check sodium, potassium, renin and lying and standing blood pressure in about six weeks, aiming for renin in the upper normal range.
4. Repeat TSH and free T4 at the six-week visit; if the TSH is still raised, start levothyroxine 50 mcg daily and check TSH six weeks later.
5. No bone-protective medicine; calcium and vitamin D from diet, weight-bearing exercise, and no repeat scan unless his dose rises or he has a fracture.
6. Sick-day rules: double the hydrocortisone for fever above 38 °C and triple it above 39 °C until a day after the fever settles; inject 100 mg into the thigh and call 911 for vomiting, inability to take tablets, or feeling faint. For surgery or dental work under general anesthesia, or a major illness in hospital, hydrocortisone 100 mg intravenously at induction and then 200 mg a day by infusion or divided doses until he is eating, tapering back over one to two days.
7. Carry the injection kit, a steroid emergency card and a medical ID at all times, with spare tablets when traveling.
8. Screen for associated autoimmune disease: TSH at least yearly, and vitamin B12, A1c and celiac serology yearly or with symptoms.
9. Endocrinology review at least yearly.
