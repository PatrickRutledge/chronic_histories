Assessment

  • Primary adrenal insufficiency (autoimmune Addison's disease) (E27.1). The low cortisol with a markedly raised ACTH before treatment, with low sodium and high potassium, establishes primary adrenal insufficiency, and the positive 21-hydroxylase antibodies establish an autoimmune cause. A cosyntropin test would need treatment to be stopped and adds nothing; adrenal imaging and other causes need not be pursued with positive antibodies. Glucocorticoid replacement is more than he needs at 30 mg a day; mineralocorticoid replacement is too low, shown by salt craving, the postural drop, the electrolytes and the raised renin. [S]
  • Raised TSH with positive thyroid peroxidase antibodies (R94.6). Probable autoimmune thyroiditis, which often accompanies autoimmune adrenal insufficiency. A raised TSH can settle once glucocorticoid replacement is established, but he has been on replacement for three months, so this is likely to be true thyroid disease. A newly raised TSH should be confirmed before treatment; levothyroxine is safe to start because glucocorticoid replacement is in place, and it must never be started before it. [S]
  • Chronic low back pain; bone health (M96.1). His Z-scores are within the expected range for his age, so there is no sign of bone loss from his earlier corticosteroids. Physiological replacement at 15 to 25 mg of hydrocortisone a day does not need bone-protective medicine; over-replacement is what should be avoided. [S]

Plan of Treatment

  • No further diagnostic testing for the adrenal insufficiency: the cause is autoimmune. [S]
  • Reduce hydrocortisone to 10 mg on waking, 5 mg at noon and 5 mg at 4 pm (20 mg a day), adjusted by weight, energy and signs of over- or under-replacement rather than by cortisol levels. [S]
  • Increase fludrocortisone to 0.1 mg once daily; check sodium, potassium, renin and lying and standing blood pressure in about six weeks, aiming for renin in the upper normal range. [S]
  • Repeat TSH and free T4 at the six-week visit; if the TSH is still raised, start levothyroxine 50 mcg daily and check TSH six weeks later. [S]
  • No bone-protective medicine; calcium and vitamin D from diet, weight-bearing exercise, and no repeat scan unless his dose rises or he has a fracture. [S]
  • Sick-day rules: double the hydrocortisone for fever above 38 °C and triple it above 39 °C until a day after the fever settles; inject 100 mg into the thigh and call 911 for vomiting, inability to take tablets, or feeling faint. For surgery or dental work under general anesthesia, or a major illness in hospital, hydrocortisone 100 mg intravenously at induction and then 200 mg a day by infusion or divided doses until he is eating, tapering back over one to two days.
