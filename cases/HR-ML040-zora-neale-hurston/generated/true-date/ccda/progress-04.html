History of Present Illness

Ms. Hurston, a 67-year-old woman with hypertensive heart disease and heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, asked for a same-day visit. For two days, during an early heat wave, she has felt lightheaded each time she stands up, worst in the morning after her pills; yesterday she had to hold the kitchen counter for a few seconds until it passed. She did not fall or lose consciousness. Her house has fans but no air conditioning, she has been working in her garden in the afternoons, and she admits she has drunk less than usual and eaten little at midday. She takes chlorthalidone, amlodipine and spironolactone, all started this spring, and dapagliflozin. She has had no weakness or numbness, no trouble speaking or seeing, no imbalance between episodes, no headache, chest pain, palpitations, vomiting or diarrhea. She knew the BE FAST signs and that this did not match them, but came in because she was worried. [S]
