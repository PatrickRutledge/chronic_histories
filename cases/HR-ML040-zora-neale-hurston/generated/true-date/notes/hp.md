## Historical basis

- Zora Neale Hurston was born on January 7, 1891, in Notasulga, Alabama, the fifth of eight children of John Hurston, a carpenter and Baptist preacher, and Lucy Potts Hurston, a former schoolteacher. **D**
- She was born as Zora Lee Hurston, according to the Florida Artists Hall of Fame. **D**
- Her family moved to Eatonville, Florida, the nation's first incorporated Black township, when she was a toddler (at about three, in 1894), and her father later became its mayor; for her, Eatonville was always home. **D**
- Her mother died in 1904, when Zora was 13. **D**
- In 1917, aged 26 and needing to present herself as a teenager to qualify for free public schooling in Baltimore, she gave her age as 16 and her birth year as 1901; from then on she presented herself as at least ten years younger than she was. The headstone placed on her grave in 1973 reads 1901. **D**
- She attended Howard University and Barnard College, where she studied anthropology with Franz Boas and received a bachelor of arts degree in 1928; she did folklore field work in the South and became a writer and anthropologist of the Harlem Renaissance. **D**
- Over more than 30 years she published four novels, including Their Eyes Were Watching God (1937), two books of folklore, an autobiography, and many stories, essays, articles and plays. **D**
- She never received the financial rewards her work deserved; the largest royalty she earned from any of her books was $943.75. **D**
- In 1950 she worked as a maid in Florida while still publishing in national magazines, and she struggled to find work throughout the 1950s. **D**
- She worked as a librarian at Patrick Air Force Base in Florida (from June 1956 by one account, for about a year by another) and then moved to Fort Pierce, where she wrote a column on "Hoodoo and Black Magic" for the Fort Pierce Chronicle from 1957 to 1959; a family friend from Eatonville invited her to live in a small house in Fort Pierce rent-free. **D**
- In 1958 she worked as a substitute teacher at Lincoln Park Academy in Fort Pierce. **D**
- Rarely a drinker, she was remembered by friends for her humor and storytelling and for the gift of walking into hearts. **D**
