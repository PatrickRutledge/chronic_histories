Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Sources. The case relies on PBS American Masters, the Zora Neale Hurston Trust (Valerie Boyd's essay and chronology), the University of Florida Smathers Libraries (which hold her papers), the Encyclopedia of Alabama, the Florida Department of State and a Library of Congress research guide. The Library of Congress collection pages, Florida Memory, the Smithsonian NMAAHC and the New York Times obituary could not be fetched (blocked or not found). Valerie Boyd's Wrapped in Rainbows is used only through her essay and chronology on the Trust's site; Robert Hemenway's biography was not read and is not cited. Wikipedia and popular sites found in searches were not used.
  2. Recent-death rule. She died in 1960, so the rule does not apply. A sick visit is included.
  3. Birth date. The true date is January 7, 1891, in Notasulga, Alabama (Trust, PBS, Encyclopedia of Alabama). From 1917 she gave her birth year as 1901 to qualify for free schooling and presented herself as at least ten years younger from then on (Trust); the 1973 headstone reads 1901 (University of Florida). The 1901 year is kept as a superseded alternative. Eatonville, Florida, which she named as her birthplace in her autobiography, is recorded in the claim's interpretation, not as an alternative date. The synthetic date of birth is 1959-01-07, a shift of 68 years.
  4. Index age and dates. The index visit on March 9, 2026 maps to March 9, 1958, when she was 67 and living in Fort Pierce, working as a substitute teacher at Lincoln Park Academy (Trust, 1958) and writing for the Fort Pierce Chronicle (1957 to 1959). The visits on March 23, April 6, May 18, June 1, September 14 and October 19, 2026 map to the same days of 1958, all before the stroke in early 1959. Her day-to-day whereabouts in 1958 are not documented in the sources read; nothing places her away from Fort Pierce then. No past medical history is documented before 1958, so that section is empty.
  5. Stroke date. The Trust gives “early 1959” and PBS 1959; the Florida Department of State gives 1960. The claim (CLM-0013) uses early 1959 with certainty “probable” and records the 1960 date as disputing. October 1959 for the welfare home is agreed.
  6. Cause of death. The Trust chronology quotes “hypertensive heart disease” and PBS agrees; the Encyclopedia of Alabama says “a heart attack.” The claim (CLM-0014) uses hypertensive heart disease with certainty “probable” and records the conflict. Her death certificate was not seen. The modern diagnosis of hypertensive heart disease with heart failure (I11.0) is linked to this claim (placement both_with_link); nothing in the record says she had heart failure in her lifetime.
  7. Name and alias. The display name Zora Neale Hurston splits simply into given and family names, so identity.name_parts is not used. The alias Zora Lee Hurston comes only from the Florida Department of State (CLM-0002, certainty “probable”); it appears in no other record. The notes call her Ms. Hurston.
  8. Living people and relatives. The writer who found and marked her grave in 1973 is living and is not named; she is called “a younger writer.” Her parents appear only in claims; her mother's death in 1904 is family history with the cause not recorded. No relative, friend or historical person is given a modern statement, decision or health fact. The friend from Eatonville who let her live rent-free appears in a claim without a name; the modern friend who lets her live without rent, her health care surrogate, the community health worker and the SHINE counselor are synthetic and unnamed.
  9. Events not used. Other events in the PBS timeline, including a false accusation that was dismissed in 1949, and the burning of her papers after her death, are not used; they do not bear on the modern episode.
  10. Presenting problems and diagnoses. Very high blood pressure at intake with morning headaches (R03.0) and breathlessness on exertion with mild ankle swelling (R06.09) are the presenting problems, resolved at the second visit, when 24-hour ambulatory monitoring confirmed stage 2 hypertension and the echocardiogram and cardiology established hypertensive heart disease with heart failure (I11.0) and HFpEF (I50.32), both recorded at the second visit. The intake documents show only what was known at intake.
  11. HFpEF decision. HFpEF is diagnosed: NT-proBNP 480 pg/mL, ejection fraction 62%, left atrial volume index 38 mL/m2, E/e' 15 and concentric hypertrophy, with an H2FPEF score of 5. The consultant considered transthyretin amyloidosis (including the hereditary form more common in Black Americans), valve disease and coronary disease and found no red flags; no technetium scan, light-chain tests or stress test were done, and the record says when they would be.
  12. Treatment choices. (1) Treatment started the morning after the 24-hour ambulatory recording, so that confirmation did not delay treatment of stage 2 blood pressure; the recording result is reported in the two-week note. (2) Chlorthalidone 12.5 mg (half of a 25 mg tablet) with amlodipine 5 mg, as the 2025 guideline advises for a Black adult without kidney disease or albuminuria (urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio 22 mg/g); no ACE inhibitor or ARB was needed. (3) Dapagliflozin for HFpEF from the consultation, through the manufacturer's patient assistance program until her drug plan with Extra Help began. (4) Spironolactone 12.5 mg added at the third visit for blood pressure above target and potassium 3.4 mmol/L. (5) Moderate-intensity atorvastatin 20 mg for primary prevention (LDL 132 to 76 mg/dL).
