1. PBS (WNET), *American Masters*. “Zora Neale Hurston's life and career timeline” (April 9, 2008), for *Zora Neale Hurston: Jump at the Sun*. Supports her birth in 1891 “in Notasulga, Alabama,” “the fifth of eight children”; the 1894 move to Eatonville, “the first incorporated black community in America, where her father becomes mayor”; her mother's death when she was 13; work as a maid in 1950; that “after working as a librarian at Patrick Air Force Base in Florida for a year,” she moved to Fort Pierce to write a column for the *Fort Pierce Chronicle* “for the next two years”; that in 1959 she “suffers a debilitating stroke and is forced to enter the St. Lucie County Welfare Home in October”; and that she “dies at age 69 in the St. Lucie County Welfare Home of hypertensive heart disease on January 28 and is buried in an unmarked grave in the segregated Garden of Heavenly Rest, Fort Pierce.” https://www.pbs.org/wnet/americanmasters/zora-neale-hurston/1155

## Editorial resolutions

- **Sources.** The case relies on PBS *American Masters*, the Zora Neale Hurston Trust (Valerie Boyd's essay and chronology), the University of Florida Smathers Libraries (which hold her papers), the *Encyclopedia of Alabama*, the Florida Department of State and a Library of Congress research guide. The Library of Congress collection pages, Florida Memory, the Smithsonian NMAAHC and the *New York Times* obituary could not be fetched (blocked or not found). Valerie Boyd's *Wrapped in Rainbows* is used only through her essay and chronology on the Trust's site; Robert Hemenway's biography was not read and is not cited. Wikipedia and popular sites found in searches were not used.
- **Recent-death rule.** She died in 1960, so the rule does not apply. A sick visit is included.
- **Birth date.** The true date is January 7, 1891, in Notasulga, Alabama (Trust, PBS, *Encyclopedia of Alabama*). From 1917 she gave her birth year as 1901 to qualify for free schooling and presented herself as at least ten years younger from then on (Trust); the 1973 headstone reads 1901 (University of Florida). The 1901 year is kept as a superseded alternative. Eatonville, Florida, which she named as her birthplace in her autobiography, is recorded in the claim's interpretation, not as an alternative date. The synthetic date of birth is 1959-01-07, a shift of 68 years.
- **Index age and dates.** The index visit on March 9, 2026 maps to March 9, 1958, when she was 67 and living in Fort Pierce, working as a substitute teacher at Lincoln Park Academy (Trust, 1958) and writing for the *Fort Pierce Chronicle* (1957 to 1959). The visits on March 23, April 6, May 18, June 1, September 14 and October 19, 2026 map to the same days of 1958, all before the stroke in early 1959. Her day-to-day whereabouts in 1958 are not documented in the sources read; nothing places her away from Fort Pierce then. No past medical history is documented before 1958, so that section is empty.
- **Stroke date.** The Trust gives “early 1959” and PBS 1959; the Florida Department of State gives 1960. The claim (CLM-0013) uses early 1959 with certainty “probable” and records the 1960 date as disputing. October 1959 for the welfare home is agreed.
- **Cause of death.** The Trust chronology quotes “hypertensive heart disease” and PBS agrees; the *Encyclopedia of Alabama* says “a heart attack.” The claim (CLM-0014) uses hypertensive heart disease with certainty “probable” and records the conflict. Her death certificate was not seen. No relative, friend or historical person is given a modern statement, decision or health fact.
