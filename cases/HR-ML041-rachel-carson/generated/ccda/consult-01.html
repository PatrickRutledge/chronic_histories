Assessment

  • Invasive ductal carcinoma of the left breast, clinical T2 N0 M0, hormone receptor positive, HER2 negative (C50.412). Early breast cancer suitable for surgery first. The tumor is single and can be removed with clear margins and a good appearance, so breast-conserving surgery followed by radiation gives the same survival as mastectomy. Both were explained to her with their trade-offs: radiation for several weeks with breast conservation, or no radiation in most cases but loss of the breast, with or without reconstruction, with mastectomy. She chose breast-conserving surgery. MRI is not needed for a single lesion in breasts that are not dense, and staging scans are not indicated at this stage without symptoms. [S]
  • Axillary staging. The axilla is normal on examination and ultrasound, so sentinel lymph node biopsy is the standard. If one or two sentinel nodes are involved, axillary dissection adds arm swelling without improving survival when whole-breast radiation follows; she would then have radiation that may include the regional nodes. [S]
  • Genetic risk. Germline testing is recommended for everyone with breast cancer diagnosed at 65 or younger. A pathogenic variant could change the operation, so the result should be known before surgery. [S]
  • What to expect after surgery. Endocrine therapy will be recommended because the cancer is strongly hormone receptor positive. Radiation follows breast conservation. Whether chemotherapy or a CDK4/6 inhibitor adds benefit depends on the nodal result and a recurrence score on the specimen; she will hear those numbers before any decision. [S]
