D His physicians at Washington University reported his case in 2002: his injury was classified as C-2, ASIA grade A, with no substantial recovery in the first five years. D In September 2002 the Associated Press reported that the first sign of change had been a movement of his left index finger almost two years earlier. He still used a wheelchair and a ventilator all but one hour a day and needed around-the-clock nursing care. He could feel about two-thirds of normal touch, which let him know when to shift his weight, so that he could sit in his wheelchair up to 16 hours a day without getting pressure sores. His program included electrically stimulated cycling for an hour three times a week and weekly pool therapy. Before it, his muscles had withered, his bones had thinned, and he had had repeated infections and life-threatening complications.
