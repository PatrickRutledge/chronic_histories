## Editorial resolutions

- **Recent-death rule.** Reeve died in 2004, so the rule applies. The case uses only what he and his physicians made public: Associated Press, Al Jazeera, CNN and medical news reports of his injury, his recovery program, his diaphragm pacing and his death; his physicians’ published 2002 case report, in which the Associated Press identified him; and reviews of his books. No private medical record, family account, or speculation is used. No sick visit is added. His death is recorded once (CLM-0010) as history; it falls about a year after the modern episode and is not used in it. No claim concerns mental health or substance use; *Still Me* and his interviews about the early months after the injury are not used, and his substance use was not researched. The modern mood screens are synthetic and negative, so no safety assessment is required.
- **Sources that could not be read.** The Christopher & Dana Reeve Foundation pages returned errors, UPI’s 2002 report was blocked, and the University Hospitals of Cleveland release could not be loaded, so none is cited. The books themselves were not read; they are cited through reviews.
- **Dates.** The 1995 article dated June 1 (a Thursday) reports the fall “Saturday,” so the injury is dated May 27, 1995; the claim says “a Saturday at the end of May 1995.” The diaphragm pacing implant is dated 2003 (year precision): CNN reported it on March 13, 2003, and other reports give February 28, 2003, but no fetched source states the day. HospiMedica names the Cleveland Clinic as the site; the University Hospitals release and other reports name University Hospitals of Cleveland and Dr. Raymond Onders, which the claim follows.
- **Index age and dates.** The index visit on April 13, 2026 maps to April 13, 2003, when he was 50, some weeks after the pacing implant. The synthetic date of birth is September 25, 1975 (shift 23 years). The chronic-care visits on April 27, June 22, August 24, and October 26, 2026 and the preventive visit on September 14, 2026 (age 50, so a periodic adult preventive visit) map to the same days of 2003, all after the injury and the implant and about a year before his death. Past medical history includes only the injury, the recovery program and the pacing, all before the true-frame index date.
- **Synthetic clinical picture.** Problems: incomplete C2 tetraplegia coded G82.52, following the published ASIA grade C; ventilator dependence (Z99.11), with the tracheostomy and pacer in the display; neurogenic bladder (N31.9) and neurogenic bowel (K59.2), which are expected at this level of injury but are not described in any public source, so their management (an indwelling catheter, a morning suppository program, senna) is generic and synthetic. The bisacodyl suppository is described in the plan and notes but is not coded as a medication, because the record generator has no rectal route; RxNorm 198509 (bisacodyl 10 MG Rectal Suppository) would apply. No autonomic dysreflexia episode, pressure injury, infection, fracture or hospital stay is recorded in the modern episode, so nothing reads as a claim about his private course; the autonomic dysreflexia plan and nitroglycerin ointment are preventive. The pacer times (two hours twice a day rising to three hours twice a day) start from the publicly reported “more than two hours” and are synthetic. The low hip T-score (-1.6) is synthetic and chosen to be consistent with the published reversal of earlier osteoporosis; insufficient vitamin D, low HDL, the Braden scores, PHQ-9 scores, pain scores, all laboratory values, the ultrasound and DXA results, the power wheelchair with tilt-in-space, the seating review, the nursing team’s routines and the equipment are synthetic. His height (193 cm) is from the 1995 report; weights are synthetic. Qualitative results (kidney and bladder ultrasound, HIV, hepatitis C and stool immunochemical tests) appear only in note text.
- **Language.** Disability is described as part of a working life: he uses a power wheelchair, directs his own care, works and travels. The record avoids pity language and describes his care team and equipment as supports.
- **Real people.** His wife, children, parents, publicist, Dr. John Jane, Dr. John McDonald, Dr. Raymond Onders, and the nurse who co-authored the report have no role in the modern episode; only Dr. Onders is named in a claim (CLM-0009), and the others appear only as authors in source citations, if at all. His family is mentioned in the modern social history only as the people he lives with, with no decision, statement or health fact attributed to them. No family history is recorded.
