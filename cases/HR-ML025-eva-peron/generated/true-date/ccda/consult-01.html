Assessment

  • Squamous cell carcinoma of the cervix (C53.9). Invasive squamous cell carcinoma of the cervix related to HPV 16, about 4.5 cm with clinical spread into the left parametrium: at least FIGO 1943 stage IIB on examination. FIGO 1943 allows imaging to assign stage: a pelvic MRI defines local extent and an FDG PET-CT looks for nodal and distant spread, which would change the radiation fields and the stage. With parametrial spread, radical hysterectomy is not advised; the standard treatment, with intent to cure, is pelvic external beam radiation with weekly cisplatin, followed by image-guided brachytherapy, completed within eight weeks, because a longer overall time lowers the chance of cure. Kidney function allows cisplatin. HIV testing is recommended at diagnosis. Chemoradiation will end her ability to carry a pregnancy and will very likely end ovarian function; egg or embryo freezing needs about two weeks of stimulation and would delay treatment slightly, and laparoscopic ovarian transposition can sometimes preserve hormone function; any later pregnancy would need a gestational carrier. The decision is hers. Bleeding is now light; if it becomes heavy, vaginal packing or a few early radiation fractions control it. Anemia should be corrected before radiation with iron, and hemoglobin watched weekly during it. Acetaminophen is the right analgesic; anti-inflammatory painkillers should be avoided with cisplatin. The diagnosis was given to her first, in her language, and she asked to hear everything; I explained the plan to her directly and answered each of her questions honestly. [S]
  • Iron deficiency anemia (D50.0). From chronic tumor bleeding; oral iron is reasonable now that the bleeding is lighter, with transfusion only if hemoglobin falls sharply or she has symptoms. [S]
