Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Recent-death rule. She died in 1962, so the rule does not apply. Even so, every historical claim is limited to what the fetched sources state, and the claims about mental health, medicines, pregnancies, and her death are marked sensitive and kept short.
  2. Source quality. Much of what is written about her health comes from tabloids, fan sites, auction catalogues, and clinic marketing. Only a public broadcaster (PBS), a physician-historian’s column, Smithsonian Magazine, Biography.com, and two PubMed-indexed articles were used. Auction material quoted in the Smithsonian article was not used.
  3. Not verified, left out. The following were found only in blogs, auction or clinic pages, or secondhand accounts, and are not claimed: repeated gynecologic operations for endometriosis and their dates; a 1952 appendectomy with a note to the surgeon about her ovaries; clauses in her studio contract about working during menstruation; a hysterectomy proposed and refused; the gallbladder operation and its date (Markel and Smithsonian mention only gall bladder disease); a second dated miscarriage; her height; and any detail of her autopsy. The CLM-0006 count of pregnancy losses follows Biography.com; other accounts differ.
  4. Endometriosis. Graded D with certainty “probable” (CLM-0004): it is reported by her biographer, quoted in a medical review, and listed by Markel and by Smithsonian Magazine, but no medical record was fetched. Summers’s causal reading that it drove her use of analgesics, tranquilizers and hypnotics is R, probable (CLM-0005). The modern diagnosis is synthetic.
  5. Mental health. Her 1961 admission (CLM-0007) and the prescribed sedatives (CLM-0009) are D; Markel’s “most likely” bipolar disorder (CLM-0008) and the 2023 psychological autopsy and the roster’s borderline personality disorder (CLM-0011) are R, possible. None is coded. The modern episode codes a moderate depressive episode (F32.1), screens for bipolar disorder (Mood Disorder Questionnaire negative) and anxiety, and treats it with therapy; it does not center the record on mental health.
  6. Birth and death dates. Her birth on June 1, 1926 is from PBS and Biography.com. The fetched sources give her death as August 5, 1962 (Biography.com) or say she was found dead early that day (PBS); the assignment gave August 4, which is recorded as a conflicting alternative because she may have died late on August 4. Her death is recorded in CLM-0010 as history only: the place, her age, and the official ruling, without detail or speculation.
  7. Index age and dates. The index visit on June 15, 2026 maps to June 15, 1956, when she was 30. The synthetic date of birth is June 1, 1996 (shift 70 years). The visits on June 24, September 14, and October 26, 2026, the sick visit on July 20, 2026, and the preventive visit on August 17, 2026 map to the same days of 1956. She married in June 1956 and was filming abroad that summer and autumn; the synthetic visits are not a claim about where she was. The modern episode includes no pregnancy, so it does not contradict the documented loss in September 1956 or reproduce it.
  8. Real people. Her mother appears only in the claims and the family history (D). Arthur Miller, Anthony Summers, and Howard Markel appear only in the claims. No modern decision, statement, or health fact is attributed to any real person: the modern partner (later spouse) is unnamed and has no role, and her health care agent is not named. The aliases “Norma Jeane Mortenson” and “Norma Jeane Baker” appear in no other record.
  9. Presenting problem. Severe painful periods with suspected endometriosis (N94.6) is the presenting problem at intake, resolved at the consultation on June 24, 2026, when the transvaginal ultrasound showed a typical left endometrioma and endometriosis of the left ovary (N80.102, unspecified depth because no surgery was done) was recorded. The intake documents state only what was known then.
  10. Modern management. First-line naproxen and a continuous combined pill were started at intake without waiting for a diagnosis (ACOG); the diagnosis was made from imaging without laparoscopy (ESHRE 2022); surgery was kept in reserve with counseling about ovarian reserve and avoiding repeated operations, a deliberate contrast with the repeated surgery reported in her life; the anti-Mullerian hormone was 2.6 ng/mL; pelvic floor physical therapy was added at three months for persistent deep pain with intercourse (pelvic floor muscle dysfunction, M62.89, recorded that day); and no opioid, benzodiazepine, or sleep medicine was prescribed. The urgent-care hydrocodone and the diphenhydramine in her modern medication history are synthetic and are not a claim about her real use of medicines.
  11. Sick visit. A same-day visit for acute uncomplicated cystitis (N30.00), chosen because it is common, interacts sensibly with the case (it must be told apart from endometriosis pain, and nitrofurantoin does not reduce the effect of her pill), and insinuates no undocumented real diagnosis. Treated with nitrofurantoin for five days; the culture grew a sensitive E. coli.
  12. Preventive visit. A periodic adult preventive visit at age 30 (CPT 99395 and 99497 in plain text): pregnancy-intention counseling (a pregnancy in about a year), the continuous pill continued for pain and contraception, folic acid, documented MMR and varicella immunity, primary HPV cervical screening, HIV and hepatitis C tests (all reported negative at the next visit), chlamydia and gonorrhea up to date from intake, alcohol, tobacco, anxiety and intimate partner violence screens, influenza vaccine deferred to September (given at the three-month visit), COVID-19 vaccine discussed and declined for now at the next visit, and advance care planning with an unnamed agent.
  13. Focused visits.
