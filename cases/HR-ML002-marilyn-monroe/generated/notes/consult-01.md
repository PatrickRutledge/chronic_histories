## Assessment

1. **Endometriosis with a left ovarian endometrioma** (N80.102). Her symptoms, the examination and a typical endometrioma on ultrasound establish endometriosis. The ESHRE guideline no longer requires laparoscopy for diagnosis when imaging shows an endometrioma or deep disease, so no surgery is needed to confirm it. There is no deep disease of the bowel or bladder. A continuous combined pill with an anti-inflammatory is the right first-line treatment and also covers contraception for the coming year; progestin alone or a GnRH antagonist with add-back would be the next steps if it fails. Surgery does not improve her chances of pregnancy at this stage and removing an endometrioma lowers the ovarian reserve, and each repeat operation lowers it further; surgery is kept for pain that does not respond, a growing cyst, or atypical imaging, and should then be a single, complete excision by an experienced surgeon. Her ovarian reserve is typical for her age. Most women with endometriosis conceive; hormonal treatment does not improve fertility and should be stopped when she wants to conceive. I agree with avoiding opioids and sedatives: pain from endometriosis is long-lasting, and these medicines add risk without treating the cause. **S**

## Recommendations

1. Continue ethinyl estradiol 0.02 mg with norethindrone acetate 1 mg daily without a pill-free week, and naproxen 500 mg twice daily with food on pain days, no more than 10 days a month.
2. No laparoscopy now; repeat transvaginal ultrasound in about four months, then yearly or if symptoms change.
3. If pain is not controlled after three to six months, change to norethindrone acetate alone or a GnRH antagonist with add-back therapy before considering surgery.
4. Keep surgery in reserve for pain that does not respond, a growing cyst, or atypical imaging, as a single complete excision by an experienced surgeon, with counseling that ovarian surgery lowers the ovarian reserve and repeated operations should be avoided.
5. Fertility: a preconception visit with folic acid before she stops the pill; try naturally once she is ready; refer to reproductive endocrinology if not pregnant after six months of trying.
6. Pelvic floor physical therapy if deep pain with intercourse persists once the periods are suppressed.
7. No opioids, benzodiazepines or sleep medicines; heat, regular exercise, and continued cognitive behavioral therapy for mood and sleep.
