Assessment

  • Endometriosis with a left ovarian endometrioma (N80.102). Her symptoms, the examination and a typical endometrioma on ultrasound establish endometriosis. The ESHRE guideline no longer requires laparoscopy for diagnosis when imaging shows an endometrioma or deep disease, so no surgery is needed to confirm it. There is no deep disease of the bowel or bladder. A continuous combined pill with an anti-inflammatory is the right first-line treatment and also covers contraception for the coming year; progestin alone or a GnRH antagonist with add-back would be the next steps if it fails. Surgery does not improve her chances of pregnancy at this stage and removing an endometrioma lowers the ovarian reserve, and each repeat operation lowers it further; surgery is kept for pain that does not respond, a growing cyst, or atypical imaging, and should then be a single, complete excision by an experienced surgeon. Her ovarian reserve is typical for her age. Most women with endometriosis conceive; hormonal treatment does not improve fertility and should be stopped when she wants to conceive. I agree with avoiding opioids and sedatives: pain from endometriosis is long-lasting, and these medicines add risk without treating the cause. [S]

Plan of Treatment
