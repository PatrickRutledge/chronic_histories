Teaching objective: Primary care for a 30-year-old actor with severe painful periods and pelvic pain: a careful history and pelvic examination, suspected endometriosis treated first with an anti-inflammatory and continuous hormonal suppression, diagnosis by transvaginal ultrasound without surgery as the ESHRE 1952 guideline allows, a gynecology consultation on when surgery helps and why repeated ovarian surgery should be avoided, fertility counseling with an ovarian reserve test for a woman who wants children, pelvic floor physical therapy for persistent pain with intercourse, a pain plan that avoids opioid and sedative accumulation, and depression and anxiety care with a structured safety assessment, on the ACOG endometriosis and chronic pelvic pain bulletins, the ESHRE 1952 guideline, the CDC opioid guideline, and the CMID depression monograph.

Disposition
Outpatient care. She has no thoughts of suicide or self-harm now and has never had them. Because pain, poor sleep and a moderate depressive episode raise risk, a brief written safety plan was made with her, with warning signs (feeling hopeless, not sleeping for nights at a time, wanting to use medicines to blot out the day) and what to do about each. Lethal-means counseling: no firearm at home; leftover medicines are to be returned to a pharmacy take-back site, and no opioid, benzodiazepine or sleep medicine will be prescribed. The 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline was given and saved in her phone, with the clinic number. Safety reviewed at every visit
