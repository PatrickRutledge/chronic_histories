Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Recent-death rule. Ali died in 2016. The case uses only what his physicians made public (the neurologist who diagnosed him, the physician interviewed on television in 1996, and his later physician at the Barrow Neurological Institute), as reported by news outlets and the institute, together with basic biographical facts and the cause of death given publicly by a family spokesman. No medical records, family accounts, or speculation about undisclosed conditions are used. His stage, examination scores, medicines and doses, screening results, rehabilitation, and preventive care are synthetic. The recent-death review rule applies.
  2. Diagnosis year and label. The diagnosis in 1984 at age 42 is reported by CBS News and the AP; the Barrow press release says only “in the 1980s,” and Al Jazeera gives age 43, which cannot be right for a diagnosis in 1984 with a birthday on January 17, 1942; the case uses 42. The sources use the labels parkinsonism, Parkinson’s syndrome, and Parkinson’s disease. The diagnosing neurologist said some features were too early for classic Parkinson’s disease; his later physicians called it Parkinson’s disease. The modern record codes Parkinson’s disease (G20.A1) with placement both_with_link, and the movement disorders consultation confirms it against the MDS criteria in the synthetic episode only.
  3. Boxing and head trauma. The 1996 physician statement attributed the condition to pugilistic brain syndrome, the diagnosing neurologist thought head blows the most probable cause, and his later physician and the Muhammad Ali Parkinson Center director disagreed. The causal link is graded R with certainty disputed. It is not coded (no secondary parkinsonism, traumatic brain injury, or chronic traumatic encephalopathy code), and the past medical history records the boxing career with the dispute stated. The consultant says only that the link cannot be confirmed or excluded during life and does not change treatment.
  4. Index age. The modern episode presents Ali at 54, twelve years after the diagnosis, in the year his physician said publicly that his cognition had not declined and in which he lit the Olympic cauldron with a visible tremor. The synthetic date of birth is January 17, 1972 (shift 30 years). The index visit on February 10, 2026 maps to February 10, 1996; the chronic-care visits on March 10, June 9, and September 8, 2026 map to the same days of 1996; and the preventive visit on October 6, 2026 maps to October 6, 1996. Past medical history includes only events before the index date; the 1996 interview and the Olympic torch are claims only.
  5. Synthetic clinical picture. The motor features recorded (slowness, rigidity, rest tremor, masked face, reduced blinking, soft speech, short steps) are those his physicians described publicly. Everything else is synthetic and was kept to normal or negative findings so that nothing reads as a claim about undisclosed private health: MoCA 27 (in keeping with his physician’s 1996 statement), PHQ-9 3, EAT-10 2, negative sleep, autonomic, psychosis, and impulse-control screens, no orthostatic hypotension, no falls, and normal laboratory results. The levodopa regimen and its increase, the speech therapy course, and the physical therapy are synthetic; his actual treatment was not made public. Hoehn and Yahr stage, MDS-UPDRS Part III, Timed Up and Go, EAT-10, the REM sleep behavior disorder questionnaire, the Epworth Sleepiness Scale, QUIP-RS, and GAD-2 have no verified LOINC code in the terminology server and are recorded as narrative.
  6. Preventive visit. A periodic adult preventive visit (age 54, so `preventive_adult`) was added on October 6, 2026 (true frame October 6, 1996), four weeks after the six-month review. No sick visit was added: under the recent-death rule the case adds no synthetic illness episode. Judgment calls: (1) the health risk assessment records no tobacco and no alcohol; these are synthetic and no fetched source covers them; (2) hepatitis B, hepatitis C, and HIV screening and the stool test are ordered with no results recorded; (3) advance care planning names an unnamed health care agent and records only general priorities (staying active, his public and charitable work, being told plainly about his health), and deliberately records no treatment preference, so that nothing in it reads as a claim about his real choices; (4) the spouse is unnamed, gives no statement, and is not a source of any history; (5) driving is recorded as short local daytime trips, a synthetic detail, so that the yearly driving review has content.
  7. Focused visits. The three chronic-care follow-ups (four weeks, three months, and six months after the first visit) are documented as focused visits on the CMID Parkinson’s disease monograph, and each lists every item the protocol calls for at that point, with its interval and status.
