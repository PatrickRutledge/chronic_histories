# Teaching Note — Muhammad Ali pilot

> **SYNTHETIC RECORD, NOT A REAL MEDICAL RECORD**

## Why this case matters

This case shows how historical evidence can support a transparent fictional modern episode without presenting interpretation or generated content as documented fact.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish documented (**D**), retrospective (**R**), and synthetic (**S**) content.
2. Keep historical and active problems distinct.
3. Connect measurable goals and interventions to guidelines.
4. Preserve strengths, function, identity, and agency.

## Destigmatization message

A health condition is one part of a person’s life and does not erase achievement, relationships, purpose, or agency.

## Discussion questions

- Which facts support concern without establishing a modern diagnosis?
- Which entries belong in active problems, historical problems, or both?
- What additional evidence would change an evidence grade?
- How should an EHR distinguish synthetic and historical content?

## Evidence-grade summary

- **D:** Muhammad Ali was born Cassius Clay in Louisville, Kentucky, on January 17, 1942. Ali died on June 3, 2016, in the Phoenix area, at age 74. A family spokesman gave the cause as septic shock due to unspecified natural causes. In 1984, at age 42 and three years after he retired from boxing, Ali was diagnosed with a parkinsonian condition by the neurologist Stanley Fahn in New York; it has been publicly described as Parkinson's disease since. The diagnosing neurologist later said publicly that slowness of movement was Ali's main limitation at the time of diagnosis, with a masked face, decreased blinking, a typical parkinsonian tremor, and a shuffling gait, and that some features were too early for classic Parkinson's disease. In a 1996 television interview, Ali's physician Dennis Cope described his condition as Parkinson's syndrome attributed to pugilistic brain syndrome from boxing, and said testing showed his cognitive function had not declined: "His mind is fine." As the disease progressed, Ali developed a tremor and his speech became increasingly difficult. When he lit the Olympic cauldron in Atlanta on July 19, 1996, his hand shook as he held the torch. Ali, his physician Abraham Lieberman, and a Phoenix philanthropist founded the Muhammad Ali Parkinson Center at the Barrow Neurological Institute in 1997. Lieberman, who was among those who diagnosed him in 1984 and later treated him, said Ali stood up to the disease for 34 years and that in his final years he lost the ability to speak and use his hands.
- **R:** Whether boxing caused Ali's Parkinson's disease is disputed. The diagnosing neurologist thought the early symptoms were probably caused by repeated blows to the head but could not be certain; his later physician Abraham Lieberman did not think boxing contributed, though he was not a hundred percent certain; and the director of the Muhammad Ali Parkinson Center said there was no medical evidence that boxing contributed.
- **S:** Shifted identity, fictional care team, modern encounters, results, plans, and outcomes.
