Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Muhammad Ali was born Cassius Clay in Louisville, Kentucky, on January 17, 1942. [D]
  • In 1984, at age 42 and three years after he retired from boxing, Ali was diagnosed with a parkinsonian condition by the neurologist Stanley Fahn in New York; it has been publicly described as Parkinson's disease since. [D]
  • The diagnosing neurologist later said publicly that slowness of movement was Ali's main limitation at the time of diagnosis, with a masked face, decreased blinking, a typical parkinsonian tremor, and a shuffling gait, and that some features were too early for classic Parkinson's disease. [D]
  • In a 1996 television interview, Ali's physician Dennis Cope described his condition as Parkinson's syndrome attributed to pugilistic brain syndrome from boxing, and said testing showed his cognitive function had not declined: "His mind is fine." [D]
  • Whether boxing caused Ali's Parkinson's disease is disputed. The diagnosing neurologist thought the early symptoms were probably caused by repeated blows to the head but could not be certain; his later physician Abraham Lieberman did not think boxing contributed, though he was not a hundred percent certain; and the director of the Muhammad Ali Parkinson Center said there was no medical evidence that boxing contributed.
