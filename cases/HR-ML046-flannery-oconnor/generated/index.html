Teaching objective: Recognize systemic lupus erythematosus in a woman of 25 with inflammatory arthritis, fever, a malar rash, cytopenias, and protein and blood in the urine, rather than calling it rheumatoid arthritis; classify it with the 2019 EULAR/ACR criteria (ANA, anti-dsDNA, anti-Sm, complements, antiphospholipid antibodies) and tell her the diagnosis directly. Measure the urine protein-to-creatinine ratio, biopsy the kidney, and treat class III lupus nephritis as the 2024 ACR and 2023 EULAR guidance advise: hydroxychloroquine for every patient with baseline eye screening, pulse then tapering glucocorticoids kept to 5 mg a day or less, mycophenolate with belimumab, and an angiotensin receptor blocker. Protect bone and watch for avascular necrosis, give vaccinations before immunosuppression, protect her from the sun, manage cardiovascular risk, plan contraception and any future pregnancy with her antiphospholipid status in mind, and protect her writing life. The teaching note contrasts this with the high-dose steroid treatment, the steroid bone damage, and the concealment of her diagnosis that she lived through.
