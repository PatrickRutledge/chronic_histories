Assessment

  • Chronic hepatitis C, genotype 1a, treatment-naive (B18.2). Chronic hepatitis C, almost certainly acquired through transfusions. He meets the AASLD-IDSA criteria for simplified treatment of compensated cirrhosis. I recommend sofosbuvir-velpatasvir, one tablet daily for 12 weeks; glecaprevir-pibrentasvir for 8 weeks would also be suitable but interacts strongly with atorvastatin, which he needs. He must not take a proton pump inhibitor during treatment; famotidine up to 40 mg twice a day is acceptable if needed. Hepatitis B tests are negative, so there is no reactivation risk. On-treatment liver tests are optional; glucose should be watched because control can improve quickly as the virus clears. Cure is confirmed by HCV RNA 12 or more weeks after the last dose. [S]
  • Compensated cirrhosis, Child-Pugh A5, MELD-Na 8 (K74.69). FIB-4 4.94, liver stiffness 26.8 kPa, a nodular liver, splenomegaly and platelets of 118 establish cirrhosis; alcohol has added to the damage. It is compensated: Child-Pugh A5 and MELD-Na 8. He needs liver cancer surveillance by ultrasound with alpha-fetoprotein every six months for life, including after cure. He does not need a transplant evaluation now; I would refer him if he developed ascites, variceal bleeding, encephalopathy or jaundice, or a MELD-Na of 15. Stopping alcohol completely is the most important thing he can do alongside cure. [S]
  • Clinically significant portal hypertension (K76.6). Under Baveno VII, a liver stiffness of 25 kPa or more establishes clinically significant portal hypertension. Carvedilol lowers the risk of decompensation, and a patient taking it does not need screening endoscopy for varices, which would not change treatment. Start 6.25 mg daily and raise it to 12.5 mg if systolic pressure stays above 100. Reassess stiffness and platelets a year after cure. [S]
  • Type 2 diabetes in cirrhosis (E11.65). Metformin is appropriate in compensated cirrhosis with normal kidney function, provided he stops drinking. The A1c can read falsely low in cirrhosis, so a continuous glucose monitor is better for decisions. A moderate-intensity statin is safe and indicated. [S]
  • Chronic pain from old fractures (G89.29). Keep acetaminophen at or below 2 g a day; avoid oral NSAIDs, which can cause kidney injury, bleeding and fluid retention in cirrhosis; topical diclofenac on small areas is reasonable; avoid opioids, which can precipitate encephalopathy and falls; physical and aquatic therapy are the mainstay. His many old fractures and liver disease also put him at risk of osteoporosis, and a bone density scan is worthwhile. [S]
