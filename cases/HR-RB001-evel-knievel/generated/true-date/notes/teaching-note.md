# Teaching Note — Evel Knievel pilot

> **SYNTHETIC RECORD, NOT A REAL MEDICAL RECORD**

## Why this case matters

This case shows how historical evidence can support a transparent fictional modern episode without presenting interpretation or generated content as documented fact.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish documented (**D**), retrospective (**R**), and synthetic (**S**) content.
2. Keep historical and active problems distinct.
3. Connect measurable goals and interventions to guidelines.
4. Preserve strengths, function, identity, and agency.

## Destigmatization message

A health condition is one part of a person’s life and does not erase achievement, relationships, purpose, or agency.

## Discussion questions

- Which facts support concern without establishing a modern diagnosis?
- Which entries belong in active problems, historical problems, or both?
- What additional evidence would change an evidence grade?
- How should an EHR distinguish synthetic and historical content?

## Evidence-grade summary

- **D:** Robert Craig Knievel was born on October 17, 1938, in the copper-mining town of Butte, Montana, and was raised by his grandparents. He began his daredevil career in 1965 with a touring motorcycle show. His crash after jumping the fountains at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas, dated 1968 in the wire obituaries, put him in the hospital, by their account in a coma for a month. In 1974 his rocket-powered cycle failed in an attempt to jump Idaho's Snake River Canyon. In 1975 the crash landing of his jump over 13 buses at Wembley Stadium in London broke his pelvis. In the winter of 1976 an attempt to jump a tank of live sharks in Chicago left him with a concussion and two broken arms, after which he retired from major jumps. Guinness World Records lists him as holding the record for the most broken bones in a lifetime, with 433 bone fractures by the end of 1975. His obituaries instead said he suffered nearly 40 broken bones before he retired in 1980. He had a liver transplant in 1999 after nearly dying of hepatitis C, which his obituaries said he likely contracted through a blood transfusion after one of his crashes. A news agency obituary reported that he did not quit drinking until his liver transplant in 1999. In his last years he was in failing health with diabetes and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, an incurable scarring of the lungs, and had two strokes in the years before his death. His final years were plagued by pain from his accidents. In a newspaper interview published early in 2007 he was described as frail and reliant on an oxygen tank and an implanted drug pump to relieve his pain. He died on November 30, 2007, at 69, at his condominium in Clearwater, Florida, after having trouble breathing; his death was reported as from natural causes.
- **R:** None recorded.
- **S:** Shifted identity, fictional care team, modern encounters, results, plans, and outcomes.
