Teaching objective: Find and treat chronic hepatitis C with cirrhosis in a 57-year-old former stunt performer who received many transfusions before 1962: raised transaminases and low platelets prompt hepatitis C antibody with reflex RNA; staging by FIB-4, elastography and ultrasound, with Child-Pugh and MELD-Na; the AASLD-IDSA simplified pathway for compensated cirrhosis with a pan-genotypic regimen (sofosbuvir-velpatasvir for 12 weeks), hepatitis B testing, a drug interaction check (proton pump inhibitor, statin) and hypoglycemia monitoring; cure confirmed by HCV RNA 12 or more weeks after treatment; Baveno VII use of liver stiffness and platelets to identify clinically significant portal hypertension and treat it with carvedilol instead of screening endoscopy; liver cancer surveillance by ultrasound with alpha-fetoprotein every six months that continues after cure; hepatitis A and B vaccination; transplant referral only if decompensation or a MELD-Na of 15 or more develops; pain care that is safe in cirrhosis (acetaminophen up to 2 g a day, no oral NSAIDs, no opioids, topical treatment, physical and aquatic therapy, sleep and coping skills); diabetes care adapted to cirrhosis, with continuous glucose monitoring because the A1c can read falsely low; alcohol counseling toward abstinence; and fall and fracture prevention, with bone density testing, vitamin D and an intravenous bisphosphonate, on the AASLD-IDSA HCV guidance, the AASLD 1993 HCC guidance, Baveno VII, the AASLD portal hypertension and ascites guidance, the ADA Standards of Care and the CMID diabetes and osteoporosis monographs.
