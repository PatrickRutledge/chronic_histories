Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Ludwig van Beethoven was born in December 1770 in the house at Bonngasse in Bonn. Only his baptism is recorded: he is entered in the register of the parish church of St. Remigius as the first of two children baptized on December 17, 1770. The day of his birth is not recorded; the Beethoven-Haus gives it as probably the 16th or 17th. [D]
  • In November 1792 Beethoven left Bonn for Vienna to study with Haydn; he intended to stay a year and lived there for over 33 years. [D]
  • His father's acute alcoholism forced him to retire in 1789. [D]
  • In a letter to his friend the physician Franz Gerhard Wegeler dated June 29, 1801, Beethoven wrote that his hearing had become weaker and weaker for the last three years; that a stomach complaint was said to be its cause; that Johann Peter Frank had treated him with tonics and his hearing with oil of almonds, and a tepid Danube bath had helped his general health but not his hearing; that his ears were "ringing and singing night and day"; that for two years he had shunned all society because he could not say to people "I am deaf"; that in the theater he had to lean close to the orchestra to understand the actors and could not hear the high notes of instruments or singers at a distance; that he heard the sounds but not the words of people speaking softly, yet could not bear shouting; and he asked Wegeler to tell no one. [D]
  • In a second letter to Wegeler, dated November 16, 1801, he wrote that the ringing in his ears was somewhat less, "especially in my left ear, in which the disease began," but that his hearing was no better; that his physician, Dr. Vering, had applied blistering bark to both his arms; and that he had begun to mix in society again after two deserted and miserable years. [D]
  • Cochlear otosclerosis has been the cause most often proposed by otologists, with Paget disease of bone among the alternatives; a later review notes that the middle ears examined at his autopsy showed no otosclerotic foci. [R]
  • Some authors attribute his hearing loss and abdominal complaints to chronic lead poisoning, citing high lead levels in hair and bone, and in 2024 high lead levels were reported in two authenticated locks of his hair; others favor other causes, and the question is not settled. [R]
  • A 2023 genome study of authenticated locks of his hair found no genetic explanation for his hearing loss or gastrointestinal problems, but found a genetic predisposition to liver disease and evidence of hepatitis B infection in at least the months before his death; with his alcohol consumption, the authors offered these as plausible explanations for the severe liver disease that culminated in his death. [R]
  • His alcohol consumption is described by modern authors as broadly accepted, and his physicians are said to have thought he was dependent on alcohol. [R]
