Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Sources and how little is knowable. Every surviving account of his life was written centuries after it (Plutarch and Arrian in the Roman imperial period), from lost contemporary histories such as those of Ptolemy and Aristobulus and from the court journal, the 'Royal Journals' (source 8). Only the Babylonian astronomical diary is contemporary. Claims are graded D only where Plutarch or Arrian plainly report an event that modern scholars accept (his birth, the wounds, the Malli wound, the drinking parties, Hephaestion's death and his grief, the last illness); every retrospective diagnosis is R. Nothing in the ancient record is a medical record of him, and the chart is thin on purpose. Britannica (access refused), the full text of Hall's article and the full NEJM discussion could not be read; Hall's argument is used only as the University of Otago release states it.
  2. Name. The display name is “Alexander the Great.” “Alexander III of Macedon” is the alias and appears in no other record. “Alexander” alone is not an alias, because the word appears in other records. He had no family name, so `identity.name_parts` has an empty given array and the whole name as the family name (as for other single-name records); the notes call him “Alexander.” No other figure with a record is named.
  3. Dates (rule 28). Historical dates use signed astronomical years: birth -0355-07 (July 356 BC, month precision, calendar unknown, because Plutarch gives an Attic month that his editor equates only roughly with July and the day may have been moved to fit the temple fire); death -0322-06-11 (June 11, 323 BC, Julian, from the Babylonian diary, with Plutarch's editor's June 13 recorded as superseded). The widely repeated conversion to July 20 or 21 was not found in a scholarly source and is not used as a fact. The record has no true-date variant (docs/date-shifting.md).
  4. Index date (correction to the brief). The brief asked for an index in 324 BC after Hephaestion's death. Livius dates his death to October 324 BC, and the modern frame keeps month and day and ends on November 1, so no full episode fits between his death and the cutoff in that year. The index is therefore March 9, 2026, mapping to March 9, 323 BC, at 32, five months after the death, after the winter campaign against the Cossaeans and on the march to Babylon in the spring (CLM-0010); the synthetic date of birth is July 20, 1993 (shift 2,348 years). The visits map to March 9 to May 11, 323 BC, before his last illness. Where he was on any of these days is not recorded; the modern episode has its own synthetic setting (an army resting in the city with field work ahead).
  5. Chest wound (CLM-0003, D; CLM-0004, R, disputed). The Malli wound is documented in both ancient historians and dated 326 or 325 BC (the thoracic surgery review gives 326 BC). The retrospective tension pneumothorax and the opposing view are recorded as R and not used. The modern findings (a healed fifth rib fracture, localized pleural thickening read as an old organized hemothorax, mild restriction, no retained fragment) are synthetic, chosen as the common and teachable late result of a penetrating chest wound with bleeding, and consistent with the documented removal of the arrowhead. They are not a claim about his real chest.
  6. Drinking (CLM-0006, D; CLM-0007, D; CLM-0008, R, disputed; sensitive). That he held long drinking parties is documented, as is the ancient disagreement about how much he drank, and the killing of Clitus at a drinking party. Whether he had a drinking disorder is disputed (Liappas and colleagues found no convincing evidence of abuse or dependence), so the modern diagnosis is a mild alcohol use disorder (three DSM-5-TR criteria, F10.10) with episodic heavy drinking and no withdrawal, managed with a moderation goal, brief counseling and addiction medicine sessions, with naltrexone offered and declined. This differs from the two other alcohol use disorder records of this kind in the dataset (severe daily drinking with withdrawal management, and night drinking with post-traumatic stress disorder). The criteria, amounts, AUDIT, AUDIT-C and PEth values are synthetic. The killing of Clitus is not used in the modern criteria.
  7. Grief (CLM-0009, D; sensitive). His grief for Hephaestion is documented. Hephaestion is named only in the documented history; in the modern episode he is “his closest companion,” and he has no statement, decision or health fact there. At five months the modern assessment is bereavement with depressive symptoms (PHQ-9 12, item 9 scored 0) that do not meet criteria for a major depressive episode; prolonged grief disorder needs at least twelve months in adults and is not diagnosed. The problem is coded Z63.4, the code DSM-5-TR uses for uncomplicated bereavement whatever the relationship, although the ICD-10-CM title reads “family member.” Livius calls Hephaestion his lover; the record uses “closest companion,” the wording of the ancient historians, and makes no claim either way.
  8. Safety assessment. Required by the PHQ-9 of 12 and the alcohol use disorder code. It records no current suicidal ideation, intent or plan; lethal-means counseling for a synthetic service pistol (kept in the unit armory off duty and never carried after drinking); no riding or driving after drinking; a written safety plan and the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. Safety is reviewed at every later visit.
  9. His death is not foreshadowed. The last illness and the disputed causes of death (typhoid, malaria, West Nile virus, poisoning, alcohol, Guillain-Barré syndrome) are recorded once as later history (CLM-0011, CLM-0012), linked to the death event so that the intake documents do not show them. The modern episode never refers to them. The typhoid vaccine at the preventive visit is routine CDC travel advice for Iraq and implies nothing about his death; no fever or infection appears in the modern episode.
  10. Relatives and other real people. His parents appear only as documented history. Hephaestion, Clitus, his officers and his physicians are named only in documented history. The health care agent is unnamed. No relative or other real person has a statement, decision or health fact in the modern episode. No family medical history is recorded.
  11. Clinical judgment calls. (1) A reduced FVC with a normal ratio was treated as possible restriction to be confirmed with lung volumes, as the ERS/ATS standard advises. (2) CT without contrast to look for a retained fragment, fistula, loculated fluid or trapped lung. (3) No decortication for a localized fibrothorax with a TLC of 79 percent and normal exercise capacity. (4) A lidocaine 5% patch and acetaminophen limited to 2,000 mg a day for intercostal neuropathic pain; opioids, tramadol, gabapentinoids and benzodiazepines avoided while he drank heavily; an intercostal nerve block held in reserve and not needed. (5) A moderation goal accepted for a mild alcohol use disorder, with naltrexone offered. (6) Thiamine for three months for heavy episodic drinking with an irregular diet. (7) Grief at five months not medicated and not labeled prolonged grief disorder. (8) Spirometry repeated at nine weeks rather than twelve, because the episode ends before his true-frame last illness.
  12. Synthetic clinical picture. Everything in the modern episode is synthetic: every result, the chest X-ray, CT, spirometry, lung volumes, diffusing capacity and six-minute walk, the HIV and hepatitis results (narrative), the examination findings, the scores (AUDIT, AUDIT-C, PHQ-9, PHQ-2, GAD-7, pain), the medicines and the outcomes. Height (170 cm) and weights are synthetic.
  13. Preventive visit. A periodic adult preventive visit at 32 (CPT 99395 with 99497 in plain text), built around travel and campaign health in Mesopotamia (modern Iraq). CDC's traveler page for Iraq recommends hepatitis A and typhoid vaccines for most travelers and warns of sand fly and tick-borne disease; the current CDC Yellow Book country table (blocked to automated access) was read through a travel-medicine site that reproduces the CDC entry for Iraq as “No malaria transmission” and WHO's note of no indigenous cases since 2009, so malaria chemoprophylaxis is recorded as not indicated, with bite precautions. Judgment calls: hepatitis A and typhoid Vi vaccines; rabies pre-exposure vaccination offered and declined; HIV, hepatitis C and hepatitis B screening drawn; influenza vaccine in the autumn; advance care planning centered on staying fit to lead.
  14. Sick visit. A 4 cm forearm laceration from a practice blade in a training drill, chosen because it fits his life without pointing at any documented illness and tests the plan: simple repair, Tdap more than five years after the last tetanus dose, and pain relief within the acetaminophen limit without opioids or regular anti-inflammatory tablets. He had not been drinking. It is not a historical claim.
  15. Focused visits. The three chronic-care follow-ups (two weeks with the consultation, six and nine weeks) are documented as focused visits on the ATS/ERS 2019 and ERS/ATS 2022 lung function standards, the BTS 2023 pleural guideline, the CDC 2022 opioid guideline, the USPSTF 2018, VA/DoD 2021 and APA 2018 alcohol guidance, the CMID depression monograph and the VA/DoD suicide-risk guideline. One deviation is recorded instead of an invented result: the four-week telephone check on the lidocaine patch was missed while he was away with his army; no harm resulted (no skin irritation, pain 3 of 10 at six weeks); checks now go on the nurse's call list with a rebooking rule. Synthetic findings introduced: one banquet above his limit, PEth falling from 236 to 88 to 31 ng/mL, the PHQ-9 falling from 12 to 5, and a 6 percent rise in FVC as pain and splinting eased.
  16. Consultation. The pulmonology referral and the consultation by Konstantin Abernathy-Sowande, MD (a fictional consultant), on the day of the two-week visit, are S. The consultant answers the five referral questions in line with what the record shows afterwards: mild extraparenchymal restriction from a localized fibrothorax, no retained fragment or trapped lung, no surgery, a lidocaine patch with acetaminophen and no opioid or sedating medicine, a nerve block held in reserve (not needed at six weeks), physical therapy and graded training with full duty, and repeat spirometry in about two months (done at nine weeks). Addiction medicine care is given by the clinic's addiction medicine clinician, recorded in the alcohol plan.

CLM-0004Physicians writing since have read the Malli wound as a tension pneumothorax with injury to the upper lobe of the left lung; others have argued that his recovery and return to the saddle within days make a pneumothorax unlikely. How he recovered is scarcely recorded.disputed
CLM-0008Whether he had what would now be called an alcohol use disorder is disputed. A psychiatric review of the ancient historians concluded that he periodically drank large quantities of undiluted wine to the point of pathological intoxication, but that the data do not give convincing evidence of abuse or dependence by DSM-IV or ICD-10 criteria, and that alcohol was unlikely to have caused his death; a medical historian has questioned retrospective diagnoses of this kind.disputed
CLM-0012The cause of his death is unknown and much argued. Modern proposals include typhoid fever (an NEJM clinicopathological discussion and a review that found the court journal's account most characteristic of typhoid), malaria, West Nile virus encephalitis, poisoning (Veratrum album thought the most plausible poison if he was poisoned), alcohol, and Guillain-Barre syndrome with a false diagnosis of death; each has been disputed. Plutarch thought the story of poisoning a fabrication.disputed
