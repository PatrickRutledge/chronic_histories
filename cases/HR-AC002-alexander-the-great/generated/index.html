Teaching objective: Chest wall pain and reduced lung function after a penetrating chest injury, with heavy episodic drinking and grief, in a young commander on campaign: a 32-year-old who was shot through the left chest some years ago comes in with pain at the scar and breathlessness on long marches, and is found to drink heavily at banquets since the death of his closest companion five months ago. The episode covers the chest X-ray and spirometry, with a reduced FVC and a normal ratio that needs lung volumes (ATS/ERS 2019 and the ERS/ATS 2022 interpretation standard); CT for a retained fragment, pleural thickening and a trapped lung; why a localized fibrothorax with mild restriction is not an indication for decortication (BTS 2023); non-opioid treatment of post-traumatic intercostal pain (acetaminophen with a lower limit, a lidocaine patch, physical therapy, and an intercostal nerve block if needed), avoiding opioids and sedating medicines while he drinks (CDC 2022); the AUDIT, the DSM-5-TR criteria and PEth, a mild alcohol use disorder and a moderation goal chosen with him, with medicine offered (APA 2018, VA/DoD 2021, USPSTF 2018); grief that is not yet prolonged grief disorder and is not major depression, followed with the PHQ-9; the safety assessment and lethal-means counseling; travel and campaign health in Mesopotamia (no malaria chemoprophylaxis where there is no transmission, bite precautions, typhoid and hepatitis A vaccines, and tetanus for a wound); and a plan that keeps him fit to lead.
