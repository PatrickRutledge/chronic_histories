Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Name. She is known by a single name in this record, “Cleopatra”, so `identity.name_parts` has an empty given array and the family “Cleopatra” (the dataset's convention for single names); the C-CDA and FHIR names are “Cleopatra” with no given name. “Cleopatra VII Philopator” and “Cleopatra VII” appear in no other record and are both aliases: the first is her formal name and the second the form most sources use. Her daughter, Cleopatra Selene, is named only in a documented claim.
  2. Dates and calendars. Historical dates use signed astronomical years: -0068 is 69 BC, -0039 is 40 BC, -0029 is 30 BC. Her birth in early 69 BC has no known day or month (precision “approximate”, calendar “unknown”); late 70 BC is recorded as a conflicting alternative. Ptolemaic Egypt used the Egyptian civil calendar, and Greek and Roman writers dated events in their own calendars, so the dates given by modern historians are conversions. Her death on about 10 or 12 August 30 BC is recorded as a Julian date with precision “approximate”, because Roman dates before about 8 BC are uncertain by a day or two. The twins' birth is dated only “by the end of 40 BC”; the timeline uses December 40 BC as that upper bound. Because she was born before 1 CE, the record has no true-date variant (docs/date-shifting.md).
  3. Index age and dates. The episode presents her at 29, in her pregnancy with the twins. The synthetic date of birth is January 15, 1997 (the day and month are synthetic, since hers are unknown), so the whole-year shift is 2065 years and 2026 maps to 40 BC. Her last period is set at December 30, 2025, so she is 9 weeks at the index visit (March 3, 2026) and the estimated due date is October 6, 2026. In the true frame, conception falls about January 40 BC, while Mark Antony was wintering in Alexandria (he left by spring 40 BC), and the visits fall from March to August 40 BC, before the twins' documented birth “by the end of 40 BC”. The source does not give the month of the birth, so the modeled dates contradict nothing documented. She is assumed to have been in Alexandria; no source read places her elsewhere in 40 BC. The assignment suggested 41 to 40 BC and an age of 28 to 29; 40 BC and 29 are used.
  4. Thin chart. Almost nothing about her health is documented. The sources record four children (a son in 47 BC, twins by the end of 40 BC, and a son by the summer of 36 BC) and nothing about any pregnancy or birth. The twins are D: Cassius Dio calls them “twins at a birth”, and modern scholarship accepts it. Chorionicity is a modern concept and cannot be known. No illness, diagnosis, or retrospective diagnosis is recorded, and none is invented. The modern episode is built around a common, defensible condition that fits the documented life: a twin pregnancy found because the uterus is large for dates.
  5. Roster corrections. The roster lists “acute stress reaction” with the manner of her death, “political persecution; probable depression; loss of children to Roman captivity”, that she “spoke nine languages”, and that she “practiced medicine herself”. No source read supports a diagnosis of depression or an acute stress reaction, and none is recorded. Her children were taken into Roman custody only after her death, not before. Plutarch lists seven peoples she spoke with and says she knew many more languages; no count of nine was found. No source read supports her practising medicine; writings on cosmetics and medicine later attributed to her are not used. The roster's age of 39 agrees with Plutarch.
  6. Her death (safe messaging). Her death and its manner are recorded once, plainly, in one claim (CLM-0009, sensitive), following safe-messaging practice. The means is not described anywhere in the record. Ancient writers already disagreed on it, and Plutarch says “the truth of the matter no one knows”; the traditional account is therefore treated as uncertain and is not repeated. The timeline says only that she died in Alexandria. Her death falls 10 years after the modern episode's true frame, appears in no intake or visit document, and is not referred to in the modern episode. EPDS scores are below 10 with item 10 scored 0, so no safety assessment is required.
  7. Presenting problem (judgment call). The index visit records a uterus larger than dates at 9 weeks (O26.841) as its own problem, with the differential of a multiple pregnancy, a dating error, fibroids, or a molar pregnancy; it is resolved at the 11-week visit, when the ultrasound shows twins (O30.041, recorded that day). The intake documents state only what was known at intake. The ultrasound is scheduled two weeks later, at 11 weeks, so that dating, chorionicity, and the nuchal translucency can be done in one scan, because she has no bleeding, no severe vomiting, and an hCG within the normal range; she is told to come in sooner for bleeding, pain, or vomiting that stops her keeping fluids down. A reviewer may prefer a scan the same week.
  8. Chorionicity (judgment call). Dichorionic diamniotic, the most common type, chosen from a twin-peak (lambda) sign and a thick dividing membrane at 11 weeks, when the assessment is most accurate. The dates are kept from her certain last period, because the crown-rump lengths differ by three days or less.
  9. Delivery timing (correction). The assignment suggested 37 to 38 weeks for uncomplicated dichorionic twins. ACOG Committee Opinion 831, as summarized in the sources read, gives 38 0/7 to 38 6/7 weeks, and the record follows it (induction planned at 38 weeks 0 days).
  10. Delivery not modeled (judgment call). The episode ends at 33 weeks, after the delivery plan is agreed, with a planned induction at 38 weeks (September 22, 2026). A modeled birth would fall in September 40 BC in the true frame, which is consistent with but not supported by the sources, and would add nothing the plan does not already teach; it is therefore not recorded.
