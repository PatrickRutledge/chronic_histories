# Teaching Note — Cleopatra pilot

> **SYNTHETIC RECORD, NOT A REAL MEDICAL RECORD**

## Why this case matters

This case shows how historical evidence can support a transparent fictional modern episode without presenting interpretation or generated content as documented fact.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish documented (**D**), retrospective (**R**), and synthetic (**S**) content.
2. Keep historical and active problems distinct.
3. Connect measurable goals and interventions to guidelines.
4. Preserve strengths, function, identity, and agency.

## Destigmatization message

A health condition is one part of a person’s life and does not erase achievement, relationships, purpose, or agency.

## Discussion questions

- Which facts support concern without establishing a modern diagnosis?
- Which entries belong in active problems, historical problems, or both?
- What additional evidence would change an evidence grade?
- How should an EHR distinguish synthetic and historical content?

## Evidence-grade summary

- **D:** Cleopatra VII Philopator was born in early 69 BC (some sources allow late 70 BC), daughter of Ptolemy XII Auletes; her mother is not identified with certainty. She was of Macedonian Greek descent, from the dynasty founded by Ptolemy I Soter; her first language was Koine Greek, and she is the only Ptolemaic ruler known to have learned the Egyptian language. Plutarch writes that her tongue, 'like an instrument of many strings', could turn to whatever language she pleased, and that she seldom needed an interpreter with Ethiopians, Troglodytes, Hebrews, Arabians, Syrians, Medes, or Parthians. She was queen of Egypt from 51 BC to 30 BC. Her first child, a son, Ptolemy Caesar, called Caesarion, was born in 47 BC, possibly on 23 June; Plutarch says he 'was believed to be' the son of the Roman dictator. Mark Antony summoned her to Cilicia in 41 BC; she drew him to Alexandria, where he spent the winter of 41 to 40 BC with her, and he left Egypt by the spring of 40 BC. By the end of 40 BC she had borne twins, a son, Alexander Helios, and a daughter, Cleopatra Selene; Cassius Dio calls them 'twins at a birth', and Mark Antony later acknowledged them as his children. By the summer of 36 BC she had a third child with Mark Antony, a son, Ptolemy Philadelphus. She died by suicide in Alexandria in August 30 BC, on about 10 or 12 August, at 39, after the Roman capture of the city. Ancient writers did not agree on how she died; Plutarch writes that 'the truth of the matter no one knows'.
- **R:** None recorded.
- **S:** Shifted identity, fictional care team, modern encounters, results, plans, and outcomes.
