Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Recent-death rule. Hepburn died in 2003. The case uses only what she said publicly (her 1993 documentary statement as quoted in a TIME obituary, and her remarks as recalled by a magazine editor who interviewed her), the tremor that was visible in her public work, and basic biographical facts and the executor’s public statement on her death. No physician of hers made a public statement about the tremor that was found. No medical records, accounts by relatives, or speculation about undisclosed conditions are used. Her scores, examination findings beyond the visible head tremor, medicines, therapy, and preventive care are synthetic. The recent-death review rule applies.
  2. Diagnosis label. Sources conflict. Her own public account was a shaking head inherited from her grandfather, and she denied having Parkinson’s disease; an encyclopedia article, a public radio report, and a physician’s newspaper column call it essential tremor; an Associated Press obituary described “tremors similar to Parkinson’s disease”; and several sources say it was widely mistaken for Parkinson’s disease. The essential tremor label is therefore graded R (certainty probable). The modern record codes essential tremor (G25.0) with verification provisional and placement both_with_link, as the brief requires for a contested label, and the movement disorders consultation applies the MDS consensus criteria in the synthetic episode only. Parkinson’s disease is not coded.
  3. Voice tremor. The only fetched description of her voice tremor (an obituary calling her voice in her last years “quivery”) describes a later period than the episode. The mild voice tremor at 72, and its coding as R49.8, are synthetic extrapolations anchored to that description, kept mild so they do not read as a claim about her health at that age.
  4. Family history. The paternal grandfather’s shaking head is recorded only as she described it publicly (grade D); no diagnosis is attributed to him, and no other relative is mentioned. A niece’s public comments, reported by secondary sites, were not used.
  5. Index age. The modern episode presents Hepburn at 72, when her head tremor was already visible in her film work. The synthetic date of birth is May 12, 1953 (shift 46 years). The index visit on January 13, 2026 maps to January 13, 1980; the chronic-care visits on February 10, May 5, and August 4, 2026 map to the same days of 1980; and the annual wellness visit on October 6, 2026 maps to October 6, 1980, when she was 73. Past medical history includes only the tremor visible before the index date; the 1981 film, the 1991 autobiography, and the 1993 documentary are claims only.
  6. Synthetic clinical picture. The visible side-to-side head tremor is from the public record. Everything else is synthetic and kept to findings typical of essential tremor and to normal or negative results, so that nothing reads as a claim about undisclosed private health: bilateral postural and kinetic hand tremor, a mild voice tremor, no signs beyond tremor, MoCA 28, PHQ-9 2 and 3, normal TSH and chemistry, no falls, and no asthma or heart disease. The propranolol treatment and its benefit, the voice therapy, the occupational therapy, and the preventive care are synthetic; her actual treatment, if any, was not made public. TETRAS, spiral drawing, Timed Up and Go, and seated and standing blood pressure have no verified LOINC code and are recorded as narrative.

No sick visit was added: under the recent-death rule the case adds no synthetic illness episode. Judgment calls: (1) the health risk assessment records no tobacco and an occasional drink (AUDIT-C 1); these are synthetic, and no fetched source about her drinking habits was used; (2) the mammogram, DXA, stool test, and hepatitis C test are ordered with no results recorded; (3) advance care planning names an unnamed health care agent and records only general priorities (independence, continuing to work, being told plainly), with no treatment preference, so nothing reads as a claim about her real choices; (4) supports are a household assistant and friends, and no relative is named or given a statement.
