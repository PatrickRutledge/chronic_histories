## Assessment

1. **Essential tremor** (G25.0). Essential tremor by the MDS consensus classification: an isolated action tremor of both upper limbs for more than three years, with head and voice tremor and no other neurological signs. There is no rest tremor, bradykinesia, or rigidity to suggest Parkinson's disease; no abnormal posture, head turning, or null point to suggest dystonic tremor; and no tandem-gait, memory, or other soft signs that would make it essential tremor plus. The reported family history fits. A dopamine transporter scan or brain MRI would add nothing. **S**
2. **Choice of treatment**. Propranolol and primidone are both established first-line treatments for limb tremor. At 72 she has no asthma, slow pulse, heart block, or diabetes, so propranolol is suitable and avoids the sedation, unsteadiness, and fall risk primidone carries at her age. The extended-release form allows one morning dose. **S**
3. **Head and voice tremor** (R49.8). Head and voice tremor respond less to medicines than hand tremor. Propranolol may help the head tremor. Botulinum toxin injection is an option for a disabling head or voice tremor, but neither is disabling now, and voice therapy techniques can steady her sustained notes. **S**
4. **Advanced treatments**. Focused ultrasound thalamotomy and deep brain stimulation are for disabling limb tremor that persists after adequate trials of propranolol and primidone. They are not indicated now. **S**

## Recommendations

1. Start propranolol extended-release 60 mg each morning; do not stop it suddenly.
2. Check resting pulse and seated and 3-minute standing blood pressure at each visit on propranolol, and ask about wheeze, fatigue, low mood, and lightheadedness; repeat the PHQ-9 after three months.
3. If the hand tremor still limits her after a fair trial, raise propranolol as the pulse allows, or add primidone at 12.5 to 25 mg at bedtime, increased slowly, with checks for sedation and unsteadiness.
4. Speech-language therapy for the voice tremor.
5. Occupational therapy for adaptive aids: weighted utensils, a lidded cup, and a thick-barreled pen.
6. Botulinum toxin injection for the head or voice tremor if either becomes disabling.
7. No brain imaging or dopamine transporter scan.
8. Keep caffeine modest, and review any new medicine for tremor-worsening effects.
9. Refer for focused ultrasound thalamotomy or deep brain stimulation only if disabling limb tremor persists after adequate trials of propranolol and primidone.
10. Primary care follows her every three to six months with TETRAS, spiral drawing, pulse, standing blood pressure, side effects, and falls.
