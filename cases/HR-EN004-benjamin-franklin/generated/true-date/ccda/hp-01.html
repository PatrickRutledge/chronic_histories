Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Benjamin Franklin was born in Boston on January 17, 1706 (New Style); an editor's note to his autobiography gives the date as Sunday, January 6, old style, 1706, in a house on Milk Street. [D]
  • In a letter of July 31, 1786, Franklin recalled that in 1724, as a compositor at Palmer's printing house in London, he sometimes heated his case of type before the fire to warm his hands, until an old workman warned him he might lose the use of his hands, and that he had felt a kind of obscure pain in the bones of his hand when working over very hot type. [D]
  • A 2008 review of his medical history by Finger and Hagemann concluded that he suffered repeated attacks of gout and a large bladder stone, which caused considerable pain and markedly decreased his mobility. [D]
  • The same review argues that several factors that raise uric acid converged in him, including sex, genetics, diet, drinking, advanced age, psoriasis, and possible lead poisoning from lead exposure. [R]
  • Franklin wrote the Dialogue between Franklin and the Gout, headed Midnight, October 22, 1780, in which the Gout tells him he has eaten and drunk too freely and indulged his indolence; a 2023 rheumatology review states that he woke with a gout attack that night. [D]
  • In a letter from Passy dated March 12, 1781, to Samuel Huntington, President of Congress, Franklin wrote that he had passed his seventy-fifth year and that the long and severe fit of the gout he had the last winter had shaken him exceedingly, leaving him far from his former bodily strength. [D]
  • The 1806 edition of his works states that he had been little subject to disease, except an attack of the gout occasionally, until about the year 1781, when he was first attacked with symptoms of the calculous complaint, which continued during his life. [D]
  • In his autobiography Franklin wrote that at the beginning of February 1726/7 he was taken ill with a pleurisy that very nearly carried him off. [D]
  • The 1806 edition of his works adds that in 1735 he had a severe pleurisy that ended in an abscess of the left lobe of his lungs, and that a second similar attack some years later left no lasting effect on his breathing. [D]
  • His autobiography records that he never knew his father or mother to have any sickness but that of which they died, his father Josiah at 89 in 1744 and his mother Abiah at 85 in 1752. [D]
  • Franklin wrote that in 1736 he lost a son, a boy of four, to smallpox taken in the common way, and regretted that he had not had him inoculated. [D]
  • A 2023 review describes him as overweight and as probably having had consequences of the metabolic syndrome, including gout. [R]
