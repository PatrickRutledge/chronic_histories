# Teaching Note — Theodore Roosevelt pilot

> **SYNTHETIC RECORD, NOT A REAL MEDICAL RECORD**

## Why this case matters

This case shows how historical evidence can support a transparent fictional modern episode without presenting interpretation or generated content as documented fact.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish documented (**D**), retrospective (**R**), and synthetic (**S**) content.
2. Keep historical and active problems distinct.
3. Connect measurable goals and interventions to guidelines.
4. Preserve strengths, function, identity, and agency.

## Destigmatization message

A health condition is one part of a person’s life and does not erase achievement, relationships, purpose, or agency.

## Discussion questions

- Which facts support concern without establishing a modern diagnosis?
- Which entries belong in active problems, historical problems, or both?
- What additional evidence would change an evidence grade?
- How should an EHR distinguish synthetic and historical content?

## Evidence-grade summary

- **D:** Theodore Roosevelt was born at 28 East 20th Street, New York City, on October 27, 1858. In his autobiography Roosevelt wrote that he was a sickly, delicate boy who suffered much from asthma and frequently had to be taken away on trips to find a place where he could breathe; he remembered sitting up in bed gasping at night as a very small child. When he was nearly fourteen, during an attack of asthma, he was sent alone to Moosehead Lake; after two other boys on the stage-coach easily overpowered him, he began boxing lessons and a long program of physical training, with his father's approval. Roosevelt wrote that he was very near-sighted as a boy without knowing it, and that at about thirteen, after he could not read a distant billboard, he got his first pair of spectacles. Roosevelt received his Bachelor of Arts degree from Harvard in June 1880 and was elected to the New York State Assembly in 1881. His father, Theodore Roosevelt Sr., died in 1878 shortly after being diagnosed with stomach cancer. On a 1914 expedition in Brazil he contracted what was called jungle fever, injured his leg, and lost sixty pounds. Roosevelt died on January 6, 1919, at Sagamore Hill, Oyster Bay, New York, at 60; the National Park Service attributes his death to a blood clot that had lodged in his heart.
- **R:** A 2015 review of the historical record in the Journal of Allergy and Clinical Immunology: In Practice concluded that his respiratory problems began in early childhood, that the record gives strong evidence for poorly controlled persistent asthma, that the asthma became relatively quiet in adolescence when his exercise program began at about 12, and that he continued to have serious exacerbations in adolescence and adulthood; it found little if any support for the claim that his asthma was psychosomatic.
- **S:** Shifted identity, fictional care team, modern encounters, results, plans, and outcomes.
