Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Index age. The episode presents him at 23, when he was a young man and a first-term legislator, the period in which the retrospective review describes continuing adult exacerbations of a lifelong asthma. The synthetic date of birth is October 27, 2002, so the whole-year shift is 144 years. The index visit is March 9, 2026 (true frame March 9, 1882); the chronic-care visits follow on April 6, June 8, and September 14, 2026 (true frame April 6, June 8, and September 14, 1882); the sick visit is August 3, 2026 (true frame August 3, 1882) and the preventive visit October 19, 2026 (true frame October 19, 1882), all before his 24th birthday and long before his death at 60.
  2. Modern diagnosis. His childhood asthma is documented in his own words (D). That it was persistent and continued into adulthood is a modern retrospective reading of the record (R, probable); the dates of individual adult exacerbations were not verified and are not recorded. The synthetic diagnosis is moderate persistent asthma with type 2 inflammation. His myopia is documented (D) and appears as a linked active problem.
  3. Family history. Only his father's death in 1878 is recorded, as the NPS states it. The deaths of his mother and his first wife on the same day in 1884 fall after the true-frame index date and are not used anywhere in the episode.
  4. Not used. The roster lists depression, grief, a boxing brain injury, and the loss of sight in one eye. Depression and brain injury were not verified in a fetched reliable source and are not recorded; no behavioral-health problem appears, so no safety assessment is required. The autobiography does describe the later loss of sight in one eye, but that injury came long after the index age and is not used. The remedies his doctors are said to have tried (black coffee, cigars, ipecac) appear only on a secondary page and are not used. Remarks attributed to his father about building his body are not used, because the brief forbids attributing statements to real relatives.
  5. Cause of death. The NPS wording, a blood clot that had lodged in his heart, is reported as written; it is not reinterpreted.
  6. Spirometry category. Spirometry, peak flow, and FeNO are measurements rather than laboratory specimens, so they use the `imaging` category, as eye pressure and other device measurements do in other records. The predicted values (FEV1 4.45 L, FVC 5.30 L, FEV1/FVC lower limit 0.72) are a synthetic reference set for a 23-year-old man 175 cm tall.
  7. Sick and preventive visits. The sick visit on August 3 is a moderate exacerbation triggered by a viral cold, chosen because viral infection is the commonest trigger and because it teaches exacerbation management in primary care on MART (a five-day prednisone course, no antibiotics or chest radiograph). It does not echo a dated historical episode. The preventive visit is `preventive_adult` because he is 23; it adds a synthetic outdated glasses prescription and sun-protection counseling, and its advance care planning names no real person as his health care agent.
  8. Focused visits. The three chronic-care follow-ups (four weeks, three months, and six months) are documented as focused visits on the CMID asthma monograph, with GINA 2025 and NAEPP 2020. Each lists every item the protocol calls for at that point, with its interval and status. The inhaler-technique errors at the four-week visit and the missed evening doses are synthetic. One deviation is recorded: the CMID calls for a clinic review within one week of any exacerbation, and the schedule has none after the August exacerbation (only a nurse telephone check at day three), so the six-month note records it as `deferred`, with no harm judged from later control and peak flow and a booking fix as corrective action. No result was invented for the missed review.
  9. Consultation. The pulmonology referral and consultation are synthetic. The consultant's phenotyping results (FeNO, eosinophils, total and dust mite IgE) are the results recorded at the four-week visit. Its recommendations match what followed: spirometry at three months, step-down only after control without exacerbation (deferred at six months after the August exacerbation), dust-mite covers, a pre-exercise reliever dose, PCV20 that day, and no immunotherapy or biologic.
  10. Roosevelt died in 1919, so the recent-death review rule does not apply. Ordinary clinical, provenance, and safe-messaging reviews remain required.
