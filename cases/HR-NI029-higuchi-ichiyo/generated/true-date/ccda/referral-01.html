Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Higuchi Ichiyō, whose given name was Natsu (also written Natsuko), was born in Tokyo on the 25th day of the 3rd month of Meiji 5 in the Japanese lunisolar calendar, which is 2 May 1872 in the Gregorian calendar, in official housing within the grounds of the Tokyo prefectural office in Uchisaiwai-chō (now in Chiyoda). [D]
  • Japan replaced its lunisolar calendar with the Gregorian calendar at the end of Meiji 5: the 3rd day of the 12th month of Meiji 5 became 1 January 1873 (Meiji 6). Dates before then, including her birth, were recorded in the old calendar. [D]
  • From 1886 she studied classical poetry (waka) at the Haginoya academy of Nakajima Utako. [D]
  • Her father died of illness in the summer of 1889, when she was 17, leaving debts from a failed business; she became head of the household and took on the support of her mother and younger sister, who did washing and needlework, and the family lived in poverty. [D]
  • In 1891 she became a pupil of the novelist Nakarai Tōsui and began to write fiction, choosing to earn her living by writing at a time when this was very hard for a woman; her story Umoregi (1892) brought her into the literary world. [D]
  • From July 1893 to April 1894 she ran a small shop selling household goods and cheap sweets in Shitaya Ryūsenji-chō, beside the Yoshiwara pleasure quarter (now in Taitō); the shop closed after about ten months, and her life there became material for Takekurabe. [D]
  • In May 1894 she moved to Hongō Maruyama-Fukuyama-chō (now Nishikata, Bunkyō). [D]
  • At Maruyama-Fukuyama-chō she wrote the works that made her name, among them Ōtsugomori (1894), Nigorie (1895), Takekurabe (1895 to 1896) and Jūsan'ya; Nippon.com calls the period 'the miraculous 14 months', and the Nipponica encyclopedia calls the time up to January 1896 'the miraculous year'. [D]
  • From the beginning of 1896 her health declined rapidly. [D]
