Teaching objective: Recognize and treat pulmonary tuberculosis in a 23-year-old writer in Tokyo with a low income, inside Japan's public health system: seven weeks of cough, fevers, night sweats and weight loss resolved into a diagnosis by chest X-ray, three sputum specimens for acid-fast smear, Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra with rifampin resistance, liquid culture and drug susceptibility testing; HIV, hepatitis and pregnancy testing, baseline liver tests, vision testing before ethambutol, and diabetes screening; airborne precautions and immediate notification to the public health center under the Infectious Diseases Control Law; outpatient care with public funding for a smear-negative patient; the choice of the standard six-month regimen (isoniazid, rifampin, pyrazinamide and ethambutol for eight weeks, then isoniazid and rifampin for eighteen weeks) because her weight is below the 40 kg needed for the four-month rifapentine-moxifloxacin regimen; daily video-observed therapy with pyridoxine; monitoring for liver injury and optic neuropathy; a contact investigation of her household led by the public health center; treatment of moderate malnutrition and help with income; contraception counseling for rifampin interactions; and a paced plan that lets her keep writing. The modern episode is a teaching case, not a claim about her real illness or care.
