Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Friedrich Nietzsche was born on October 15, 1844, in Röcken, near Leipzig, where his father was a Lutheran minister. [D]
  • His father, a Lutheran pastor, died in 1849, when his son was four, after about a year of seizures and progressive neurological deficits; an autopsy described "softening" of the brain. Nietzsche later wrote that his father died "in his six-and-thirtieth year"; medical accounts give his age as 36. The cause was not established. [D]
  • He had severe myopia, worse in the right eye, and a right pupil larger than the left, both noted in early childhood; at about five an eye examination found that the right eye needed a very strong (six-dioptre) lens. By about 30 the right eye was functionally blind, and in 1878 an ophthalmologist found fluid in the right retina and diagnosed retinitis (central chorioretinitis). [D]
  • Severe headaches began in his school years; at nine he was already missing school because of them. They were mainly right-sided, frontal and above the eye, came with sensitivity to light and with nausea and vomiting, and grew more severe in adult life. [D]
  • In 1869, at 24, he was appointed to the chair of classical philology at Basel. Ill health led to sick leave in 1871 and again in 1876 to 1877, and in 1879 he resigned his professorship and received a small pension; he never held another post. [D]
  • Looking back in 1888, he wrote that in 1879, in his "six-and-thirtieth year," he reached "the lowest point in my vitality," when "my eyes could distinguish nothing that lay three paces away from me"; he resigned at Basel, and the next winter, his "first winter in Genoa," he wrote Dawn "in the midst of the agony of a headache which lasted three days, accompanied by violent nausea." [D]
  • After resigning he settled into a pattern of spending winters near the Mediterranean, usually in Italy, and summers in Sils Maria. After an unsuccessful spring taking the waters at Recoaro, he went to the Upper Engadine at the beginning of July 1881 and rented a modest room in Sils Maria, hoping the dry, sunny climate would relieve his headaches; he kept a strict daily schedule with five to seven hours of walking, filling notebooks as he went. [D]
  • By his own admission he had gonorrhea twice in his twenties; whether either infection was in fact syphilis could not have been told apart reliably at the time. [D]
