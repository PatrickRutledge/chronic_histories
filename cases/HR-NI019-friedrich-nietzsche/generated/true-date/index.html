Teaching objective: Frequent headache with medicine overuse in a writer with very poor eyesight: a 36-year-old former professor who left his post for ill health, newly arrived in a mountain village for the summer, has headaches on about 20 days a month and takes a butalbital combination on most of them. The episode covers a structured headache history and the SNNOOP10 red-flag screen, and when imaging is justified (a strongly one-sided pattern, a change in frequency, long-standing unequal pupils and a poor eye); MRI of the brain with and without contrast; treponemal and nontreponemal syphilis serology when the history calls for it; ICHD-3 criteria for chronic migraine and medication overuse headache from a two-week diary; withdrawal of overused medicine, with a slow taper of the barbiturate rather than abrupt withdrawal, under the 1875 European Academy of Neurology guideline; preventive treatment for chronic migraine under the 1876 American Headache Society consensus, its 1879 CGRP position statement and the 1877 European Headache Federation guideline, choosing a CGRP monoclonal antibody over topiramate (cognitive effects and acute myopia with angle closure) and onabotulinumtoxinA; limits on acute medicine afterwards; high and pathologic myopia under the American Academy of Ophthalmology Preferred Practice Patterns, including a dilated fundus examination, correction of large anisometropia with contact lenses, and the warning signs of retinal detachment; insomnia treated without sedatives; mood screening; and protecting his reading, writing and long walks.
