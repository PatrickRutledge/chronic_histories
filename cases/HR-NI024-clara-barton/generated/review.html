  1. Index age. The episode presents her at 52, after her documented return home in October 1873 from war relief abroad, when she broke down with what she called prostration of the nervous system. The synthetic date of birth is December 25, 1973, so the whole-year shift is 152 years. The index visit is January 20, 2026 (true frame January 20, 1874). The chronic-care visits follow on February 3, March 3, April 14, and June 16, 2026; the sick visit is July 27, 2026; the preventive visit is October 13, 2026 (true frame February 3, March 3, April 14, June 16, July 27, and October 13, 1874). Past medical history and family history include only events before January 1874; her sister's death in May 1874 stays in the claims and timeline and is not in the family history.
  2. Diagnosis. Her periods of depression and nervous prostration are documented in her own diary and letters (D). Calling them major depressive disorder is a retrospective interpretation (R, possible); no diagnosis was made in her lifetime. The synthetic diagnosis, recurrent major depressive disorder (F33.1), is placed both_with_link to those claims. Burnout (Z73.0) is added as a synthetic contributing factor linked to her documented exhaustion after wartime relief; it is described as an occupational phenomenon, not a mental disorder. The F33 code requires the safety assessment, which is present.
  3. Rest cures. Her stays abroad on medical advice and at the Dansville sanitarium, which she called a water and rest cure, are recorded as history only (D). Claim CLM-0011 states that her recovery cannot be credited to the rest cure and that rest cures are not an effective treatment for depression. In the modern episode she asks whether she simply needs a long rest; the clinician and the consultant explain that prolonged rest or withdrawal from activity does not treat depression and that structured activity, psychotherapy, an antidepressant, sleep regularity, and social contact do. The two weeks of medical leave are a work-load measure inside an active treatment plan, not a rest cure.
  4. Safe messaging. The safety assessment is synthetic and records no current ideation, intent, or plan. PHQ-9 item 9 is scored 0 at every visit, and the safety plan and the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline are reviewed at every later visit, including the sick visit and the preventive visit. Diary passages in which she wrote of wishing for death, and a 2012 newspaper report that her diaries show she considered suicide, are not quoted, are not used as claims, and are not echoed in the synthetic episode. The 1852 diary episode is recorded only as “a brief period of depression.” Her death is stated without detail.
