Teaching objective: Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction from ischemic cardiomyopathy in a 66-year-old Native Hawaiian man, sheriff of Honolulu and a lifelong Olympic swimmer and surfer, five years after a heart attack. The episode covers breathlessness, ankle swelling and tiredness diagnosed by NT-proBNP and echocardiography (ejection fraction 30%); invasive coronary angiography for the ischemic cause; the four pillars of guideline-directed medical therapy under the 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA guideline and the 2024 ACC decision pathway: changing lisinopril to sacubitril-valsartan with a 36-hour washout, changing metoprolol tartrate to carvedilol, empagliflozin and spironolactone, titrated every two weeks with potassium, magnesium and kidney checks; a loop diuretic adjusted to euvolemia; intravenous iron for iron deficiency; sick-day rules when vomiting and diarrhea raise potassium and creatinine; a missed laboratory check recorded honestly; the decision against a primary-prevention defibrillator when the ejection fraction rose from 30% to 38% after more than 90 days of therapy; sodium guidance without routine fluid restriction; cardiac rehabilitation adapted to an ocean athlete, with swimming and surfing safety; vaccinations; and culturally safe care for a Native Hawaiian patient, with his ʻohana involved as he chooses, his own healing practices respected, and attention to the higher and earlier burden of heart failure hospitalization among Native Hawaiians.
