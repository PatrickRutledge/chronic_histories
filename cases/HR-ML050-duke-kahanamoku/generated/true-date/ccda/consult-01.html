Assessment

  • Diagnosis and cause (I50.22). Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, stage C, NYHA class III, from ischemic cardiomyopathy: an ejection fraction of 30% with a thinned, akinetic anterior wall and apex in the territory of the old anterior infarction, and Q waves to match. The mitral regurgitation is functional, from the dilated ventricle. TSH and blood count exclude thyroid disease and anemia as contributors; he does not drink alcohol. [S]
  • Ischemic evaluation (I25.2). Invasive coronary angiography is reasonable in new HFrEF with a likely coronary cause, to define the anatomy and look for disease that revascularization could help, especially in vessels other than the infarct artery. A thinned, akinetic wall of 5 mm or less is unlikely to recover even if its artery is opened. [S]
  • Guideline-directed medical therapy (I50.22). Start all four classes within weeks rather than months. Stop lisinopril and begin sacubitril-valsartan 24/26 mg twice daily at least 36 hours after the last lisinopril dose, to avoid angioedema. Replace metoprolol tartrate, which is not one of the beta-blockers proven in heart failure, with carvedilol 3.125 mg twice daily. Start empagliflozin 10 mg now. Add spironolactone 12.5 mg at the next visit if potassium is below 5.0 mmol/L and eGFR above 30. Then raise one medicine every two weeks toward target doses (sacubitril-valsartan 97/103 mg and carvedilol 25 mg twice daily) as blood pressure, heart rate, potassium and kidney function allow, with a metabolic panel one to two weeks after each change. Keep furosemide at the lowest dose that keeps him dry. Teach sick-day rules. [S]
  • Iron deficiency (E61.1). Ferritin below 100 ng/mL is iron deficiency in heart failure even without anemia. Intravenous iron improves symptoms, exercise capacity and quality of life; oral iron does not. Give ferric carboxymaltose 750 mg today and again in two weeks, and check iron studies at about six months. [S]
  • Defibrillator and resynchronization. Not now. Repeat the echocardiogram after at least 90 days of optimized therapy; a primary-prevention ICD is indicated if the ejection fraction is still 35% or below with NYHA class II to III and a good expected survival. His QRS of 104 ms does not meet criteria for resynchronization. [S]
  • Exercise and the ocean. Exercise training is safe and helps in stable HFrEF. Cardiac rehabilitation for heart failure is covered once he has had six weeks of stable therapy and no procedure in the previous six weeks. Until then, walking. After rehabilitation, swimming is reasonable in calm water with a companion; breath-hold diving and big surf should wait until he is clearly stable, because a sudden loss of consciousness in the water is the main danger. Drink water through the morning on the diuretic and empagliflozin. [S]
