  • Duke Kahanamoku was born on August 24, 1890, in Honolulu, at Haleʻākala, the Honolulu home of Princess Bernice Pauahi Bishop; he was named after his father, who had been named Duke for a visit of the Duke of Edinburgh to Hawaiʻi. [D]
  • Olympedia gives his full name as Duke Paoa Kahino Makoe Hulikohoa Kahanamoku. [D]
  • He won the Olympic 100-meter freestyle in 1912 at Stockholm (with silver in the 4 x 200-meter relay) and again in 1920 at Antwerp (with gold in the relay), and silver in the 100-meter freestyle in 1924 at Paris: three gold and two silver medals. [D]
  • He co-founded the Hui Nalu surf club in 1911, introduced surfing to the Atlantic coast of the United States in 1914 and gave a surfing exhibition at Freshwater Beach in Sydney on December 24, 1914, widely regarded as a seminal event for surfing in Australia; he was inducted into the International Swimming Hall of Fame in 1965 and was the first inductee of the International Surfing Hall of Fame in 1966. [D]
  • From 1922 he lived in Los Angeles for years and found small roles in many Hollywood films. [D]
  • He was elected sheriff of Honolulu for 13 consecutive terms, from 1934 until the post was eliminated in 1960, after which he was named Hawaiʻi's "Ambassador of Aloha"; other sources date his first election to 1932 and his departure to 1961. [D]
  • He married Nadine Alexander on August 2, 1940, in Kailua-Kona. [D]
  • He never drank alcohol or smoked and trained consistently. [D]
  • He had a heart attack in 1952 and suffered several heart attacks in his later years, continuing to swim and sail almost to the end of his life. [D]
  • He had no great personal wealth. [D]
  • He was the official United States representative at the 1956 Melbourne Olympic Games. [D]
