Assessment

  • Urothelial carcinoma of the bladder, pT2a pN0, margins negative, after radical cystoprostatectomy (Z85.51). Organ-confined, node-negative disease. Upstaging from non-muscle-invasive disease at resection to pT2a at cystectomy is common, which is one reason early cystectomy is offered for high-risk disease that recurs after intravesical therapy, especially in an irradiated bladder. Adjuvant cisplatin-based chemotherapy or immunotherapy is offered for pT3 to pT4 or node-positive disease; for pT2a N0 with negative margins, the benefit does not justify the toxicity, and none is recommended. The main risks are recurrence in the pelvis or at distant sites and a new tumor in the upper tracts or the urethra. [S]
  • Ileal conduit urostomy with appliance leaks and moisture dermatitis (Z93.6). The leaks come from a skin crease below the stoma when he sits, which a flat wafer cannot seal. The skin change is irritant dermatitis from urine, not candida. The stoma itself is healthy, with good spout height. Later problems to watch for are parastomal hernia, stomal stenosis or retraction, and peristomal skin overgrowth from chronic urine contact. [S]
  • Kidney and metabolic effects of the ileal conduit (Z90.6). Reabsorption of chloride and ammonium from urine in the ileal segment causes hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis in some patients; his bicarbonate is at the lower limit. Kidney function can decline from infection, obstruction at the ureteric anastomoses, or reflux. Vitamin B12 deficiency is uncommon with the short segment used for a conduit, but yearly checks are advised from two to three years after surgery. [S]
  • Urine findings with a conduit. Conduit urine is almost always colonized, and the dipstick and culture from the bag are usually abnormal. Bacteriuria without symptoms should not be treated. Fever, flank pain, rigors, or a rise in creatinine suggest upper-tract infection, which needs a culture taken by sterile catheter through the stoma and prompt treatment. [S]
  • Retained urethra. The urethral margin was negative and the prostatic urethra was not involved, so the risk of urethral recurrence is low and routine urethral wash cytology is not needed; bloody discharge from the urethra should be reported. [S]
