  • Hubert Horatio Humphrey Jr. was born on 27 May 1911 in Wallace, South Dakota. [D]
  • Humphrey served in the U.S. Senate from Minnesota from 1949 to 1964, as vice president from 1965 to 1969, and again in the Senate from 1971 until his death. [D]
  • Humphrey sought medical advice in 1967 for blood in his urine; some doctors suspected bladder cancer, others disagreed, and he was not treated. In 1968 doctors found and removed several pinhead-size nonmalignant growths from his bladder. In 1994 researchers reported a p53 gene mutation in bladder cells from a 1967 urine sample of his. [D]
  • In 1973 doctors found possibly cancerous tissue in his bladder and treated it with thiotepa and X-ray therapy, given five minutes a day for five weeks. [D]
  • After three years free of cancer, an examination at the National Naval Medical Center in Bethesda for symptoms of a urinary tract infection found a low-grade malignancy, and in October 1976 a six-hour radical cystectomy at Memorial Sloan-Kettering Cancer Center removed his bladder, prostate, lymph nodes, the fatty tissue around the bladder, and part of the urethra; the ureters were joined to a conduit made from a segment of small intestine, brought out to an opening on the skin to the right of the navel and drained into a bag. The nodes were to be examined for spread, and an experimental two-drug chemotherapy might be offered. [D]
  • He resumed his Senate duties in January 1977, serving as deputy president pro tempore from January 5, 1977. [D]
