  • He was born Narendranath (Narendra Nath) Datta in an affluent family in Calcutta (Kolkata) on 12 January 1863. His father, Vishwanath Datta, was a successful attorney; his mother was Bhuvaneshwari Devi. [D]

  • He left Bombay for America on 31 May 1893 and spoke at the World's Parliament of Religions in Chicago in September 1893, which made him famous; he then spent nearly three and a half years teaching Vedanta, mostly in the eastern United States and London, and returned to India in January 1897. [D]
  • On 1 May 1897, at a meeting of the monastic and lay devotees of Sri Ramakrishna in Calcutta, he founded the Ramakrishna Mission Association; in February 1898 the monastery moved to a garden house at Belur on the west bank of the Ganga, and land for the Belur Math was bought in early 1898. [D]
  • In March and April 1897, soon after his return to India, he wrote that he had diabetes. On 16 March he wrote: "The work has broken me down completely, and I have got 'diabetes', an incurable disease"; on 26 March: "The result of this steady work in the West and the tremendous work of a month in India upon the Bengalee constitution is 'diabetes'. It is a hereditary foe," adding that "Eating only meat and drinking no water seems to be the only way to prolong life"; and on 7 April that he had been "attacked by 'Diabetes', a fell disease" and had gone to Darjeeling, cool and "very good for the disease," where the doctors did not want him to move about. A biography says he went to Darjeeling on the advice of physicians. [D]
  • On 29 May 1897, from Almora, he wrote to a physician disciple that he was taking the medicines sent to him, riding morning and evening and feeling very strong, but that "I never in my life could sleep as soon as I got into bed. I must toss for at least two hours," that he felt hot after the evening meal, that "There has not been any thirst even though the days are fearfully hot," and that he was afraid of "getting fat on a too much milk diet."
