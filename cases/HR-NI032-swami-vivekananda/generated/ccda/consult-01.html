Assessment

  • Type 2 diabetes (E11.65). Question 1: diabetes is confirmed by two abnormal results (A1c 9.4 percent, fasting glucose 172 mg/dL). GAD65 antibodies are negative and his fasting C-peptide is preserved, so this is type 2 diabetes; slowly progressive autoimmune diabetes is unlikely, though antibodies should be rechecked if he loses weight again or needs rapidly escalating treatment. Monogenic diabetes is unlikely without a clear three-generation pattern. In South Asians type 2 diabetes appears earlier and at lower BMI, with more insulin resistance for a given weight, which is why ADA screens Asian Americans from a BMI of 23. Question 2: insulin is not needed now. He has no ketosis, his own insulin secretion is good and the A1c is below 10 percent; his weight loss reflects glucose spilling into the urine and should stop with treatment. [S]

Avoid sulfonylureas, which cause hypoglycemia during fasts. A GLP-1 receptor agonist is not the first choice for a man who has just lost weight unintentionally and prefers tablets. [S]
