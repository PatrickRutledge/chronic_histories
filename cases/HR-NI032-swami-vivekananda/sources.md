## Historical evidence

## Editorial resolutions

- **Recent-death rule.** He died in 1902, so the rule does not apply. The claims are still limited to what the fetched sources state.
- **Name.** “Swami” is a monastic title, not a given name, and Vivekananda is a monastic name with no family name. The display name is “Swami Vivekananda,” as he is known; `identity.name_parts` is `{given: [], family: "Vivekananda"}`, as for other single names, so exchange documents carry “Vivekananda” as the name and the title is not treated as a given name. His birth name, Narendranath Datta (also written Narendra Nath Datta), is recorded as an alias; neither form appears in any other record.
- **Another figure.** The brief noted that a Bengali contemporary has a record in the dataset (HR-ET021); no claim needs him and he is not named, so `historical_mentions` is not used. His disciples, brother monks and Western friends appear only as recipients of the letters cited; none is given a role in the modern episode.
- **Sources not read.** The Advaita Ashrama edition of the *Complete Works* was read through Wikisource transcriptions; *The Life of Swami Vivekananda by His Eastern and Western Disciples* and Sankar’s *The Monk as Man* (said in secondary sources to list 31 ailments) were not read and are not cited. No peer-reviewed article on his illnesses was found and read; none is cited. The Vedanta Society pages were not reached.
- **Diabetes (D).** His diabetes is documented in his own letters from March and April 1897, at 34, with his physicians’ advice to go to Darjeeling (CLM-0005). How it was diagnosed is not recorded. He called it “a hereditary foe”; no relative is named, so the family history records only “family member” with his words, graded D. His classification as type 2 is retrospective (CLM-0014, R, possible).
- **Corrections to the brief.** The brief asked for an index age of 35 to 37 with diabetes first diagnosed in the modern episode. His diabetes was documented at 34, so the modern episode presents him at 35 as a man told of diabetes a year earlier and never tested, whose thirst and weight loss resolve into a confirmed and classified diagnosis at the second visit. The brief described a “vegetarian” diet; his letters show he also ate meat and fish (in 1897 he wrote of “eating only meat,” and in 1901 his doctors forbade meat), so the modern diet is mostly vegetarian with fish at some meals, by his choice. The brief said he traveled to the West in 1899 to 1900; the Belur Math biography confirms June 1899 to December 1900, and the modern visits avoid it.
- **Kidney disease.** His own letters report albuminuria “the last three years” before December 1901 (that is, from about 1898) and a diagnosis of Bright’s disease (CLM-0011, D). His biographer describes dropsy in his last illness (CLM-0012, D, probable). That this was diabetic kidney disease is retrospective (CLM-0015, R, possible). The modern albuminuria and the diabetic kidney disease (E11.21) are synthetic and linked to these claims.
- **Death.** He died at the Belur Math on July 4, 1902, at 39 (D). The physicians’ cause, as his biographer records it, was “apoplexy or sudden failure of the heart”; the frequently repeated “rupture of a blood vessel in the brain” was not found in a fetched primary or authoritative source and is recorded only as a retrospective interpretation (CLM-0016, R, disputed). The monks’ understanding of his death as samadhi is recorded alongside it, with respect.
- **Index age and dates.** The synthetic date of birth is January 12, 1991 (shift 128 years). The index visit on February 9, 2026 maps to February 9, 1898, when he was 35, at Calcutta and Belur, where the monastery moved that month. The visits map to February 9 and 16, March 9 and March 23, 1898 (before he left for Darjeeling on March 30), and October 20 and 27, 1898 (after his return to Belur on October 18). The long gap matches his documented travels in the north from May to October 1898. The visits are not a claim about his real care.
