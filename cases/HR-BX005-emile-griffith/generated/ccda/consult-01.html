Assessment

  • Mild cognitive impairment (CDR 0.5) of frontal-subcortical pattern (R41.3). Slowed processing, poor set-shifting and retrieval that improves with cues, with preserved independence in daily life, fit mild cognitive impairment (mild neurocognitive disorder), not dementia. [S]
  • Traumatic brain injury and traumatic encephalopathy syndrome (Z87.820). Repetitive head impacts over a long career and the severe assault are the most likely causes; the MRI shows an old right frontal injury, frontal microbleeds and a cavum septi pellucidi, and spares the hippocampi. He probably meets the NINDS 2021 criteria for traumatic encephalopathy syndrome (substantial exposure, a core cognitive impairment, a progressive course, not fully accounted for by another condition). These are research criteria: they cannot confirm chronic traumatic encephalopathy in life, they are not a clinical diagnosis, and they do not change treatment. The single severe injury makes it impossible to separate the two exposures. [S]
  • Alzheimer's disease and vascular disease. Alzheimer's disease is less likely given the retrieval pattern and normal hippocampi, but co-pathology at 67 cannot be excluded. Vascular change is slight (Fazekas 1, no infarct). [S]
  • Chronic PTSD, depressive symptoms and short sleep (F43.12). These can impair attention and retrieval and are treatable; treating them is the best chance of improving his day-to-day thinking. Sertraline is a reasonable choice: it has no anticholinergic burden; sodium and falls need watching. Benzodiazepines, sedating sleep aids and anticholinergic drugs should be avoided. [S]
