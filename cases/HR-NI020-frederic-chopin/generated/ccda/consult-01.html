Assessment

  • Pulmonary tuberculosis, cavitary and smear-positive (A15.0). Confirmed by a positive amplification test with three positive smears, in keeping with the cavity and the symptoms. No rifampin resistance. A positive interferon-gamma release assay supports the diagnosis but would not have excluded it if negative. Nontuberculous mycobacteria do not give a positive amplification test for the tuberculosis complex. Cultures, molecular tests for isoniazid and fluoroquinolone resistance, and phenotypic susceptibility are pending. [S]
  • Choice of regimen. He is eligible for the four-month regimen: age over 12, weight 48.4 kg, pulmonary disease only, HIV negative, no prolonged QT history or QT-prolonging medicines, normal potassium and magnesium, normal liver tests and creatinine, no earlier treatment, and specimens sent for susceptibility before the first dose. A cavity does not exclude it. Compared with the six-month regimen it shortens treatment by two months, which matters to him as a teacher and performer, at the cost of more tablets taken with food and the moxifloxacin precautions. He chose it after hearing both options. [S]
  • Monitoring. An electrocardiogram is not routinely needed, because he has no QT risk factors. His low weight and albumin justify one early liver check even without symptoms. Moxifloxacin can cause tendon injury and, with isoniazid, nerve damage, which would matter greatly to a pianist; both should be asked about at every visit and every observed dose. [S]
  • Isolation and return to teaching. He remains infectious until he has had at least two weeks of treatment, his symptoms are improving, and three sputum smears collected at least eight hours apart are negative. After that, with the health department's agreement, he can teach in person and needs no mask. [S]
  • Cystic fibrosis and alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency. Neither is likely: the CT shows no bronchiectasis or emphysema, he has no sinus or pancreatic symptoms, and tuberculosis fully explains his illness. Because his lifelong chest illness raises the question and he wants it answered, a sweat chloride test and a serum alpha-1 antitrypsin level are reasonable and simple; a sweat chloride below 30 mmol/L makes cystic fibrosis unlikely. [S]
  • Moderate protein-calorie malnutrition (E44.0). Undernutrition raises the risk of poor outcomes and drug toxicity; pyridoxine is needed with isoniazid. [S]
