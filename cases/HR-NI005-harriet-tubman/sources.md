## Historical evidence

1. U.S. National Park Service, Harriet Tubman Underground Railroad National Historical Park. “The Life of Harriet Tubman” (Harriet Tubman Underground Railroad Byway fact sheet). Supports her birth into slavery in early 1822 in Dorchester County, Maryland; her name Araminta (“Minty”) and her parents Ben and Rit Ross; her being hired out at six; the near-fatal blow to the head from an iron weight thrown by an overseer at the Bucktown Village Store, which affected her for the rest of her life; her 1844 marriage, 1849 escape, rescues, Civil War service, 1869 marriage to Nelson Davis; and her death on March 10, 1913. https://www.nps.gov/hatu/planyourvisit/upload/MD_TubmanFactSheet_Biography_2.pdf
2. Maryland State Archives. “Harriet Ross Tubman Davis (b. circa 1822 – d. 1913),” Biographical Series. Supports the birth year of about 1822, the mid-1830s injury from a two-pound weight that broke her skull, periodic loss of consciousness throughout her life, and the proposal that she had temporal lobe epilepsy. https://msa.maryland.gov/megafile/msa/speccol/sc5400/sc5496/013500/013562/html/013562biog.html
3. Thomas O. “Ross, Araminta (Harriet Tubman).” *Dictionary of Canadian Biography*, vol. 14. Gives her birth as 1820 or 1821, the injury at about 13 causing “seizures and bouts of somnolence for the rest of her life,” and her death of pneumonia in Auburn, New York, on March 10, 1913. https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/ross_harriet_14E.html
4. Balmuth EA, Scammell TE. “Harriet Tubman's Hypersomnia: Insights from Historical and Medical Perspectives.” *J Gen Intern Med* 2023;38(16):3621–3627. Open-access review quoting primary accounts (Bradford 1869 and 1901, Telford c. 1905, the 1865 *Freedmen's Record*, Siebert). Supports the injury at age 13, the headaches, the sudden sleep spells during work or conversation that ended abruptly after a few minutes, the visions, her account of surgery in Boston for headaches (placed by historians in the late 1890s, undocumented), and the authors' argument against temporal lobe epilepsy and for post-traumatic narcolepsy. Graded **R** for the diagnostic proposals. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10713879/
5. HISTMED Master Roster v3. Internal project source. Its clinical claims were provisional and were not treated as independent corroboration.

## Editorial resolutions

- **Birth date and synthetic date of birth.** No birth record survives. The NPS and the Maryland State Archives give about 1822 (NPS: “early 1822”); the Dictionary of Canadian Biography gives 1820 or 1821. The preferred birth is recorded as 1822 with precision “approximate,” and 1820 or 1821 as a conflicting alternative. Because the month and day are unknown, the policy rule to keep them does not apply; the synthetic date of birth uses March 1, a placeholder within “early 1822,” and is 1951-03-01. The shift is 129 years, so the true-date variant places the birth on March 1, 1822, which is a modeling artifact, not a claim.
- **Index age.** The modern episode presents Tubman at 75. The 2023 review cites a mid-1890s account of spells “at frequent intervals,” and she sought surgery in Boston for debilitating headaches, placed by historians in the late 1890s, when she was in her mid-70s by the 1822 birth year. The index visit is April 6, 2026 (true frame April 6, 1897) and the last visit is August 17, 2026 (true frame August 17, 1897).
- **Contested diagnoses.** Temporal lobe epilepsy and post-traumatic narcolepsy are both retrospective proposals (**R**, possible). Neither is placed on the problem list as historical fact. In the synthetic episode, captured spells are sleep onset rather than seizures and the MSLT meets narcolepsy criteria; the resulting problem, narcolepsy without cataplexy due to a medical condition (G47.429), is marked provisional and linked to both R claims, so the synthetic finding echoes but does not settle the historical debate.
- **Seizures.** The Dictionary of Canadian Biography uses the word “seizures” for her spells. Because the 2023 review notes that no convulsions, auras, automatisms, or post-ictal confusion are described, the case records the documented behavior (sudden sleep, visions) rather than a seizure diagnosis.
- **Surgery.** Her own account of an operation in Boston is graded **D** with certainty probable. Its date is unknown, so it is kept in the timeline without a date and left out of the past medical history.
- **Not used.** Larson's 2004 biography and the primary accounts by Bradford and Telford were not reviewed directly; they are known through the 2023 review. Family medical history is not recorded because no relative's cause or date of death was found in a fetched source. The death certificate year of 1815 and her own reported year of 1825 appeared only in search summaries and are not used.
- **Trauma-informed care.** Her childhood under slavery is recorded factually and without graphic detail. The PC-PTSD-5 screen is synthetic and negative; no behavioral-health diagnosis is made, so no safety assessment is required.
- Tubman died in 1913, so the recent-death review rule does not apply. Ordinary clinical, provenance, and safe-messaging reviews remain required.
