## Clinical guidance

1. Chaikof EL et al. The Society for Vascular Surgery practice guidelines on the care of patients with an abdominal aortic aneurysm. *Journal of Vascular Surgery* 2018;67:2-77.e2 (PMID 29268916). Surveillance by diameter, repair at 5.5 cm in men or with symptoms or rapid growth, endovascular repair, smoking cessation, and medical therapy. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29268916/
2. Isselbacher EM et al. 2022 ACC/AHA Guideline for the Diagnosis and Management of Aortic Disease. *Circulation* 2022;146:e334-e482 (PMID 36322642; also *J Am Coll Cardiol* 2022, PMID 36334952). AAA recommendations read in the open-access full text: ultrasound every 3 years at 3.0 to 3.9 cm, yearly at 4.0 to 4.9 cm in men, and every 6 months at 5.0 cm or more in men; CT when ultrasound is inadequate and for repair planning; repair at 5.5 cm or more in men, for symptoms, and possibly for growth of 0.5 cm or more in 6 months; antihypertensive treatment when the average blood pressure is 130/80 or more; a moderate- or high-intensity statin with aortic atherosclerosis; low-dose aspirin may be considered with atheroma; smoking cessation; shared decision-making with a multidisciplinary team; and the association of fluoroquinolones with aneurysm and dissection. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36322642/

## Editorial resolutions

- **The aneurysm and the 1948 operation.** The abdominal pain of 1948, the exploratory laparotomy of December 31, 1948, the aneurysm found and wrapped in cellophane, and his discharge about three weeks later are documented (**D**) and appear as past medical history. Source 3 dates his seeking advice to the fall of 1948 without giving the operation date; source 2’s date is used. The documented size (“the size of a grapefruit”) is not used: the modern episode is a synthetic aneurysm below the repair threshold (5.0 cm by ultrasound, 5.2 cm by CT, 5.1 cm at six months), as the teaching objective requires. Modern notes refer to the operation without a date and record that the wrapping does not change the thresholds or surveillance. The CT finding of thickening along the front of the aneurysm is a synthetic depiction of the documented wrapping.
- **Index age and dates.** The modern episode presents Einstein at 70, three months after the operation. The synthetic date of birth is March 14, 1956 (shift 77 years). The index visit on March 24, 2026 maps to March 24, 1949; the chronic-care visits on April 21, July 14, and September 22, 2026, the sick visit on June 2, 2026, and the annual wellness visit on August 25, 2026 map to the same days of 1949. Every true-frame visit falls after the operation, so no note denies prior abdominal surgery; the notes record a healed midline scar. Source 2 says he left the hospital for a long holiday in Florida; it does not give its length, so the visits are not checked against it.
- **Death and his refusal of surgery.** The rupture in April 1955, the surgeon’s advice, his refusal of operation, and his death are history only (**D**), recorded as claims dated after the episode and absent from intake documents. Source 2’s introduction gives April 15, 1955 as the date of death, which conflicts with its own narrative and with source 1; April 18 is preferred and April 15 is recorded as a conflicting alternative. His reported words are paraphrased, not quoted. The modern episode does not stage his death: it records synthetic shared decision-making in which he chooses surveillance now, would consider endovascular repair at the threshold, wants each decision made with him when the time comes, and has set no treatment limits.
- **Pipe smoking.** His long pipe smoking (source 4; source 2 says he smoked constantly from adolescence) anchors the synthetic nicotine dependence (F17.290), which is coded only in the modern frame and marked sensitive in the claims. The quantity, readiness to quit, quit date, nicotine replacement, and abstinence are synthetic. The habit of holding an empty, unlit pipe after quitting echoes the documented later habit (source 4) without claiming it happened in 1949. Lung cancer screening is recorded as not indicated because the USPSTF criteria count cigarette pack-years.
- **Synthetic risk factors.** Hypertension (I10) on amlodipine, the lipid values, aortic atherosclerosis on CT angiography (I70.0), and all laboratory and imaging results are synthetic; no fetched source documents his blood pressure or cholesterol. His height (172 cm) and weight are synthetic.
- **Retrospective labels.** The suggestion that he had features of the autism spectrum is graded **R** (certainty disputed), marked sensitive, and is not a modern diagnosis or used in the episode. The roster’s “dyslexia,” “language delay,” and “difficult persona” were not verified in a fetched source and are left out.
- **Real people.** No statement, decision, or health fact in the modern episode is attributed to his relatives, friends, or the surgeons named in the history. The household and the health care agent are not named.
- **Sick visit.** A same-day sick visit on June 2, 2026 records an acute low back strain after lifting boxes of books (S39.012A). It was chosen to teach how new back pain is weighed in a patient with a known aneurysm: a non-tender aneurysm, stable vital signs, and a same-day duplex ultrasound showing no change and no periaortic fluid, before treating it as mechanical, with acetaminophen rather than anti-inflammatory drugs (aspirin and losartan) and no muscle relaxants. It does not echo a documented illness, and it is resolved within ten days.
- **Annual wellness visit.** At 70 he receives `annual_wellness_subsequent` on August 25, 2026. Judgment calls: (1) AAA screening is not indicated because the aneurysm is known; (2) PCV20 and the first recombinant zoster dose are given, with influenza and COVID-19 vaccines due next month; (3) colorectal (stool test) and hepatitis C screening are ordered with no results recorded; (4) advance care planning records a health care agent, not named, and general values, with no treatment limits.
- **Focused visits.** The three chronic-care follow-ups (four weeks, about four months, and six months) are documented as focused visits on the SVS 2018 and ACC/AHA 2022 aneurysm guidelines and the CMID hypertension and hyperlipidemia monographs. Each lists the aneurysm items (duplex diameter, CT angiography, femoral and popliteal arteries, symptoms and emergency plan, vascular surgery and the shared decision) and the risk-factor items (blood pressure, home readings, metabolic panel, lipids, ALT, HbA1c, urine albumin, statin and aspirin safety, a medication review for fluoroquinolones and anti-inflammatory drugs, tobacco, ECG, ankle-brachial index, eye examination, weight). One protocol deviation is recorded instead of an invented result: the lipid panel was not drawn within 4 to 12 weeks of starting atorvastatin, because no standing order was placed and no visit fell in the window; it was drawn at 16 weeks (LDL 62 mg/dL, down 56 percent), so no harm resulted, and standing orders are now placed. The ECG at the four-week visit, the popliteal duplex, and the ankle-brachial index at six months are recorded in narrative only.
- **Consultation.** The vascular surgery referral and the consultation by Kofi Mensah, MD (a fictional consultant), on the day of the four-week visit, are **S**. The consultant answers the six referral questions with what the record shows happened afterwards: an aneurysm below threshold whose wrapping does not change the plan; duplex ultrasound every six months; repair at 5.5 cm, growth of 0.5 cm or more in six months, or symptoms, with endovascular repair favored; a blood pressure target below 130/80 with losartan increased, atorvastatin continued, and aspirin started; a popliteal duplex added to the six-month scan; and the shared decision with his preferences. The aspirin and the shared-decision steps carry the date of that visit, as does the aortic atherosclerosis found on CT.
- **Recent-death rule.** Not applicable; he died in 1955.
