Assessment

  • Infrarenal abdominal aortic aneurysm (I71.43). Fusiform infrarenal aneurysm of 5.2 cm by CT outer-wall measurement and 5.0 cm by ultrasound, asymptomatic and below the 5.5 cm threshold for elective repair in men. The difference between the two tests is the usual one, so ultrasound should be used consistently to follow it. The wrapping produced the thickening seen along the front wall; it does not reliably prevent growth or rupture, and it does not change the thresholds or the surveillance interval. In trials of aneurysms below 5.5 cm, early repair did not improve survival over surveillance. [S]
  • Surveillance. At 5.0 to 5.4 cm in a man, duplex ultrasound every six months is recommended, with CT if the views are poor or repair is being planned. [S]
  • Thresholds and repair options. Elective repair is advised at 5.5 cm, at growth of 0.5 cm or more in six months, or with pain or tenderness attributable to the aneurysm. His anatomy suits endovascular repair: a neck 2.2 cm wide and 2.0 cm long with 30 degrees of angulation and iliac arteries of 1.3 cm. Open repair would be harder after the previous laparotomy and the scarring around the wrapped aorta. Endovascular repair carries a lower early risk but needs lifelong imaging, and some patients need a further procedure. [S]
  • Atherosclerosis of the abdominal aorta (I70.0). Calcified atheroma of the aorta and iliac arteries on CT angiography. This is atherosclerotic disease, so a high-intensity statin is recommended and low-dose aspirin is reasonable; he has no history of ulcer or bleeding. [S]
  • Essential hypertension (I10). Above the 130/80 target, which is recommended for patients with an aneurysm; no blood pressure medicine has been shown to slow aneurysm growth, so the aim is overall cardiovascular risk. [S]
  • Other arteries. No femoral or popliteal aneurysm on examination. A popliteal duplex added to the six-month scan will complete the check, as the examination can miss a small popliteal aneurysm. [S]
  • Shared decision. The options were explained with the figures: continued surveillance now, or repair now, which is not advised at this size; and, at the threshold, endovascular or open repair. He chose surveillance. He said he wants plain numbers at every scan and wants to make each decision himself when the time comes, rather than settling it in advance. He values being able to keep working and walking. If the aneurysm reaches the threshold, he would consider endovascular repair and would want to discuss it again then. He has not chosen to decline repair, and no treatment limits were set. [S]
