Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Recent-death rule. Holiday died in 1959, so the rule does not apply. Every historical claim is still limited to what the six fetched sources state.
  2. Index age. The index visit on April 20, 2026 maps to April 20, 1948, when she was 33, five weeks after her early release from federal custody on March 16, 1948. The synthetic date of birth is April 7, 1993 (shift 78 years). The chronic-care visits on April 27, May 26, July 20, and October 19, 2026, the preventive visit on August 31, 2026, and the sick visit on September 21, 2026 map to the same days of 1948, before her next documented arrest. Past medical history and family history include only events before the index date. The index was chosen because the weeks after release from custody carry the highest overdose risk, which makes medication for opioid use disorder and naloxone the central teaching point.
  3. Liver disease. Cirrhosis is documented only in her final year, eleven years after the episode. Liver disease is therefore kept out of the problem list. The episode screens: liver tests, PEth, FIB-4 (1.18 at intake, 0.78 at six months), hepatitis C, hepatitis B, and HIV tests, and hepatitis A and B vaccines. The AST, GGT, and MCV elevations at intake are synthetic findings of heavy drinking, not a claim about her real liver.
  4. Arrests and targeting. The arrests, the guilty plea, the sentence, and the loss of her cabaret card are documented (D) and are recorded as history, without judgment and never as evidence of her character. The account that the narcotics bureau’s pursuit of her was racially motivated comes from later writers and is graded R with certainty probable; the record states no person’s motives as fact and names no official. In the visit notes it appears only as her experience of being singled out by law enforcement, which shapes a trauma-informed, confidential approach. The custody facility is named Alderson, West Virginia, as in Clio; the Jazz.com entry’s “Alderston, Virginia” is treated as an error.
  5. Death date. PBS American Masters, Biography.com, and Clio give July 17, 1959; the Jazz.com entry gives July 15. July 17 is preferred and July 15 recorded as a conflicting alternative. The cause is given as cirrhosis with pulmonary edema (The Globe and Mail) or pulmonary edema and heart failure (Clio); both are recorded in CLM-0009 without detail.
  6. Problems. Opioid use disorder is coded F11.20 (opioid dependence, uncomplicated), with the display recording early remission in a controlled environment at intake; the code does not change with the return to use. Alcohol use disorder is coded F10.20. Both are both_with_link to the documented history and trigger the structured safety assessment, which records no suicidal ideation, gives the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, adds overdose prevention, and is reviewed at every later visit. A periapical abscess (K04.7) is added at the sick visit and resolved.
  7. Non-linear recovery. Following the integrated recovery approach of the project’s alcohol-and-trauma pilot record, the three-month visit follows a brief return to heroin use caused by a gap in her medication (a lapsed prior authorization on tour). It is managed without blame, discharge, or a change in her place in treatment: buprenorphine continues, support increases, and the cause is fixed. Her own documented wish to recover (CLM-0005) is the anchor for her engagement.
  8. Person-first language. The record says “opioid use disorder,” “return to use,” “person who uses drugs,” and “urine drug test,” not “addict,” “relapse” as a moral failure, “clean,” or “dirty.” Urine testing is described as part of care, never as a test of trust or a condition of treatment.
  9. Quotations. Her autobiography and her own published words were not fetched, so nothing is quoted from her or attributed to her. No statement, decision, or health fact is attributed to any other real person; William Dufty is named only as co-author of the autobiography in CLM-0008, and her mother appears only in the family history and the claims.
  10. Synthetic content. All visits, laboratory values, vital signs, PEth, PHQ-9, AUDIT-C, and DAST-10 scores, the urine drug test results, the medicines and doses, her tobacco use, her friend and peer recovery coach, the tour details, the dental abscess, and her height (165 cm) are synthetic. Qualitative results (hepatitis B surface antigen and core antibody, HIV antigen-antibody, pregnancy test, urine drug tests, and the sexual-health and cervical screening ordered at the preventive visit) appear only in the note text, because the schema stores numeric results; the cervical and sexual-health results are not reported.
  11. Preventive visit. A periodic adult preventive visit (CPT 99395 with 99497 in plain text) was added at age 33. Judgment calls: cervical, chlamydia, gonorrhea, and syphilis tests are ordered with no results recorded; tobacco cessation and the HPV vaccine are recorded as declined; advance care planning names an unnamed health care agent and records only general priorities.
  12. Sick visit. A same-day visit for a periapical abscess was added three weeks after the preventive visit found untreated decay. The infection is synthetic and implies no real diagnosis.
  13. Focused visits. The four chronic-care follow-ups (one week, five weeks, three months, and six months) are documented as focused visits on the ASAM and SAMHSA guidance for opioid use disorder, the VA/DoD guideline for alcohol use disorder, the AASLD liver guidance, the CMID depression monograph, and the VA/DoD suicide-risk guideline.
