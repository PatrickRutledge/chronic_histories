  • Her conviction cost her the New York City cabaret card she needed to perform in clubs that served alcohol. [D]
  • The Federal Bureau of Narcotics pursued Holiday for years, and later writers have described that pursuit as driven by racism and linked it to her performance of "Strange Fruit," a song against lynching. [R]

Assessment

  • Opioid use disorder, severe, early in buprenorphine treatment (F11.20). Buprenorphine is a good choice: it lowers the risk of overdose death, can be prescribed from primary care, and fits her touring better than daily methadone clinic attendance, which remains an option if buprenorphine does not control her cravings. Extended-release naltrexone would need a long opioid-free period and does not protect her if she loses tolerance and uses again. Starting at a low dose was right after enforced abstinence. Evening cravings at 8 mg mean the dose is too low; most people do best at 16 mg or more. [S]
  • Alcohol use disorder, moderate (F10.20). Naltrexone, the usual first choice, cannot be used with buprenorphine. Acamprosate is safe with buprenorphine and with her normal kidney function; topiramate is a second choice. Her withdrawal risk is low, so she does not need a medically managed withdrawal. [S]
  • Liver. AST higher than ALT with a raised GGT and a high MCV points to alcohol. Albumin, bilirubin, and platelets are normal, and FIB-4 is 1.18, below 1.3, so advanced fibrosis is unlikely and elastography is not needed now. Repeating liver tests as drinking changes, and FIB-4 within a year, is enough. She is not immune to hepatitis B and should complete the series. [S]
  • Return to use. A return to use is common and is a reason to strengthen treatment, not to stop it. Her greatest risk is a gap in her medication while touring. [S]
