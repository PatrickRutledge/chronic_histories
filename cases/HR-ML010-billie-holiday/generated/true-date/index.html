Social, functional, and mental status

Social history

  • S Professional singer; returned to concert work and touring after her release; can no longer work in clubs in her home city because of a licensing rule tied to her conviction
  • S Returned home five weeks ago after about ten months in federal custody on a narcotics possession charge; no medication for opioid use disorder was offered there, and she has used no opioids since release
  • S Alcohol: four or five drinks on most performance nights since release (AUDIT-C 8); drinks to calm her nerves before and after shows
  • S Tobacco: about five cigarettes a day; not ready to stop now and knows help is available
  • S Supports: musicians she has worked with for years, a close friend who has agreed to carry naloxone, and a peer recovery coach; her music is her strongest source of purpose
  • S Has repeatedly been singled out by law enforcement and feels watched; wary of clinics and of anything written about her. Confidentiality under the federal substance use disorder privacy rule explained, and she decides what is shared

Functional status

  • S Independent in all activities; performing several nights a week; sleep broken since release
