Plan of Treatment

  • Radioactive iodine as definitive treatment: it is effective, quick, and avoids long-term antithyroid drug risks. Long-term methimazole is less suitable because relapse after stopping is common, and surgery is not needed without a large goiter, compressive symptoms, or eye disease. Iodine-131, 15 mCi, booked for next week. [S]
  • Stop methimazole three days before treatment; restarting it afterwards is not needed while free T4 is only mildly raised and he is on a beta-blocker. [S]
  • Continue metoprolol until he is no longer thyrotoxic, then taper. [S]
  • No steroid cover; report any eye symptoms promptly. [S]
  • Free T4 and total T3 within one to two months of treatment, then every four to six weeks until stable. [S]
  • Hypothyroidism is expected within two to three months. Start levothyroxine once free T4 falls below normal, at a dose below full replacement because of his age and recent atrial fibrillation, and titrate by TSH every four to eight weeks to 0.4 to 4.0 mIU/L. [S]
  • Reassess anticoagulation once he is no longer thyrotoxic and sinus rhythm has been confirmed on ambulatory monitoring. [S]
  • Written radiation-safety instructions for the first week after treatment. [S]
  • Discussed with Dr. Whitford the same day; the patient agrees with radioactive iodine and the follow-up plan. [S]
  • Follow-up: Endocrinology will give the radioactive iodine next week. Primary care will monitor thyroid levels and levothyroxine dosing on the CMID hypothyroidism schedule.
