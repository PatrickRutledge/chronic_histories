# Teaching Note — George H. W. Bush pilot

> **SYNTHETIC RECORD, NOT A REAL MEDICAL RECORD**

## Why this case matters

This case shows how historical evidence can support a transparent fictional modern episode without presenting interpretation or generated content as documented fact.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish documented (**D**), retrospective (**R**), and synthetic (**S**) content.
2. Keep historical and active problems distinct.
3. Connect measurable goals and interventions to guidelines.
4. Preserve strengths, function, identity, and agency.

## Destigmatization message

A health condition is one part of a person’s life and does not erase achievement, relationships, purpose, or agency.

## Discussion questions

- Which facts support concern without establishing a modern diagnosis?
- Which entries belong in active problems, historical problems, or both?
- What additional evidence would change an evidence grade?
- How should an EHR distinguish synthetic and historical content?

## Evidence-grade summary

- **D:** George Herbert Walker Bush was born in Milton, Massachusetts, on June 12, 1924. On May 4, 1991, he developed atrial fibrillation while running at Camp David; it presented as unusual fatigue during the run. He was flown to Bethesda Naval Hospital, where an electrocardiogram showed no abnormality except the irregular rhythm and a cardiac ultrasound showed no structural abnormality and normal heart function. Treatment began with digoxin. On May 5, 1991, the White House said he still had atrial fibrillation, with no sign of heart damage or heart attack, and that procainamide had been added to digoxin; electrical cardioversion would be considered if the rhythm did not return to normal. His normal rhythm returned late on May 5, atrial fibrillation recurred early on May 6, and cardioversion was judged unnecessary because his response to medication was encouraging. He was discharged on May 6 and was in normal sinus rhythm later that day, on digoxin and procainamide. Later in the same week he was diagnosed with Graves' disease. A physician's account published in 2016 reports a ventricular rate of 150 beats per minute on arrival at the hospital, an overactive thyroid as the explanation for the atrial fibrillation, treatment with an oral dose of radioactive iodine, a thyroid hormone level that became too low within a few months, and synthetic thyroid hormone thereafter for life. Newsweek reported that he had regained eight of the 13 pounds he lost after doctors used radioactive iodine to control his overactive thyroid. After his physical examination on March 26, 1992, the White House reported that his thyroid function remained completely normal on Synthroid 0.15 milligrams a day, that his electrocardiogram was normal and there was no evidence of heart disease, that hip and neck x-rays showed mild degenerative osteoarthritis, and that raised pressure in his left eye was stable without signs of glaucoma. He died on November 30, 2018, at the age of 94.
- **R:** None recorded.
- **S:** Shifted identity, fictional care team, modern encounters, results, plans, and outcomes.
