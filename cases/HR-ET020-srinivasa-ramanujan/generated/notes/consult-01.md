## Assessment

1. **Amebic liver abscess** (A06.4). A young man from an endemic area with fever, a tender liver and a single right-lobe abscess, positive Entamoeba histolytica serology and dysentery years ago: an amebic liver abscess. A pyogenic abscess is unlikely with negative blood cultures and no biliary or abdominal source. A negative stool antigen is common with liver abscess and does not argue against the diagnosis. Serology can stay positive for years after an old infection, but in this setting it supports the diagnosis. The abscess is under 10 cm, in the right lobe, without signs of imminent rupture, so medicine alone is the right treatment; aspiration is not needed now. **S**
2. **Positive interferon-gamma release assay** (R76.12). The IGRA shows tuberculosis infection, common in people who grew up in South India. Active pulmonary tuberculosis is very unlikely: the radiograph shows only the changes of the abscess at the right base, and three smears and the NAAT are negative. His illness is explained by the abscess. Metronidazole and paromomycin have no activity against tuberculosis, so treating the abscess will not hide it. Cultures must be final before latent infection is diagnosed and treated. **S**

## Recommendations

1. Metronidazole 750 mg three times a day with food for 10 days; expect a metallic taste and nausea; stop and call for numbness or tingling.
2. Then paromomycin 500 mg three times a day for 7 days to clear intestinal cysts, started after metronidazole and not at the same time, so that its diarrhea does not confuse the response.
3. Aspiration only if fever persists after 72 hours of metronidazole, the abscess enlarges, or it is in a position to rupture; a phone check on the third day.
4. No repeat imaging if he recovers clinically; abscess cavities can take months to resolve on ultrasound.
5. End airborne precautions now; await the mycobacterial cultures at six to eight weeks.
6. When the cultures are negative and the liver tests are normal, treat latent tuberculosis infection with rifampin 600 mg daily for 4 months, with monthly clinical checks and liver tests at baseline and two months.
7. No tuberculosis notification is needed, since active disease has not been found; report amebiasis to the health department where the jurisdiction requires it. No contact screening is needed for amebiasis acquired years ago.
8. Treat the vitamin B12 and vitamin D deficiency, as Dr. Ashworth plans.
