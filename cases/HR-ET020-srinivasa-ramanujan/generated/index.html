Teaching objective: The evaluation of fever, weight loss and right upper quadrant pain in a 29-year-old mathematician from Tamil Nadu, a strict vegetarian, who came to a research fellowship far from home. The episode works through the differential that divided his historical physicians and later historians, tuberculosis or amebiasis, with modern tests: a chest radiograph, induced sputum for smears, NAAT and culture, an interferon-gamma release assay, stool tests and Entamoeba histolytica serology, and an abdominal ultrasound. The single modern diagnosis is an amebic liver abscess, treated by CDC guidance with metronidazole and then the luminal agent paromomycin; the positive IGRA with negative cultures is latent tuberculosis infection, treated after the abscess with four months of rifampin under the 2020 NTCA/CDC guideline, with the ATS/CDC/IDSA guidelines for diagnosis and treatment. The episode adds a nutrition assessment with vitamin B12 and vitamin D repletion suited to a lacto-vegetarian, a dietary plan built on his own food and rules, PHQ-9 and GAD-7 screening with a structured safety assessment, counseling, and practical support for a student far from home. The modern diagnosis is synthetic and does not settle the historical question.
