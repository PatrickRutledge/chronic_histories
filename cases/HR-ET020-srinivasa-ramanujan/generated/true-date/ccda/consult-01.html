Assessment

  • Amebic liver abscess (A06.4). A young man from an endemic area with fever, a tender liver and a single right-lobe abscess, positive Entamoeba histolytica serology and dysentery years ago: an amebic liver abscess. A pyogenic abscess is unlikely with negative blood cultures and no biliary or abdominal source. A negative stool antigen is common with liver abscess and does not argue against the diagnosis. Serology can stay positive for years after an old infection, but in this setting it supports the diagnosis. The abscess is under 10 cm, in the right lobe, without signs of imminent rupture, so medicine alone is the right treatment; aspiration is not needed now. [S]
  • Positive interferon-gamma release assay (R76.12). The IGRA shows tuberculosis infection, common in people who grew up in South India. Active pulmonary tuberculosis is very unlikely: the radiograph shows only the changes of the abscess at the right base, and three smears and the NAAT are negative. His illness is explained by the abscess. Metronidazole and paromomycin have no activity against tuberculosis, so treating the abscess will not hide it. Cultures must be final before latent infection is diagnosed and treated. [S]

Plan of Treatment

  • Metronidazole 750 mg three times a day with food for 10 days; expect a metallic taste and nausea; stop and call for numbness or tingling. [S]
