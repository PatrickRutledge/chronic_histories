## History

Since her late teens she has restricted her eating, with single-food meals, a fixed upper weight of 50 kg that she has kept below for decades, tight lacing, and four to six hours of fast walking a day with gymnastics each morning; she feels anxious and guilty if she cannot exercise. ## Assessment

1. **Anorexia nervosa, restricting type, moderate (DSM-5-TR)** (F50.011). Restriction leading to a significantly low weight, intense fear of weight gain with persistent behavior that prevents it, and a disturbed experience of her body meet the DSM-5-TR criteria for anorexia nervosa; with no binge eating or purging in the last three months it is the restricting type, and a BMI of 16.0 at intake is moderate. OSFED does not apply because all criteria are met. Most anorexia nervosa in later life, as here, began in youth and became long-standing. The normal TSH, CRP, celiac antibody and electrolytes, with low rather than high glucose, make thyroid disease, inflammation, celiac disease, diabetes and adrenal insufficiency unlikely; no further tests are needed. Compulsive exercise is part of the disorder. **S**
2. **Level of care and refeeding**. She does not meet the thresholds for admission: her pulse is above 40 awake, systolic pressure above 90, temperature above 35.6 C, potassium above 3.0 mmol/L, and she is eating. Admission would be advised for any of those, for fainting, chest pain or arrhythmia, phosphate below 2.0 mg/dL, a fall in weight despite treatment, or food refusal. Her phosphate of 2.3 mg/dL is an early refeeding effect that needs replacement and weekly checks. The slightly worse ankle edema is refeeding edema; a diuretic would worsen it later. **S**
3. **Recurrent depression with persistent grief** (F33.1). A recurrent moderate depressive episode with persistent grief, bereavement by suicide, and passive thoughts of death without intent or plan. Antidepressants work poorly at very low weight and starvation mimics depression, so nutrition comes first; an SSRI with low QT risk, such as sertraline, is reasonable once nutrition is under way and the ECG is repeated. **S**
4. **Bones and exercise**. Decades of low weight and her age put her at high risk of low bone mass; DXA is indicated. Weight restoration is the main treatment. Exercise should be limited while her pulse and blood pressure are abnormal. **S**

## Recommendations

1. Outpatient treatment: weekly weights at therapy, weekly blood tests for three weeks, and medical review by Dr. Marchetti-Osei in three weeks and at least every two months while underweight; admission for any of the thresholds above.
2. Phosphorus 250 mg twice daily for three weeks; finish 14 days of thiamine; electrolytes, phosphate and magnesium weekly for three weeks, then at each visit while underweight.
3. The dietitian to advance the meal plan by about 200 to 300 kcal every few days toward weight gain of 0.5 to 1 kg a week, with three meals and two to three snacks.
4. Weekly enhanced cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT-E) with open weighing in the session, and an end to weighing at home; video sessions when she travels; psychiatry review by video at about four weeks and then as needed.
5. No gymnastics and walks of no more than an hour a day for now, with a graded return agreed by the team once vital signs are normal and weight is rising.
6. Sertraline 25 mg, then 50 mg, at about four weeks if the ECG is normal, with grief-focused work in therapy; safety review at every visit and the safety plan kept current, including crisis numbers abroad.
7. DXA of the spine and hip at about four weeks, with calcium to about 1,200 mg a day and vitamin D.
8. Privacy: a restricted chart, a scheduling alias, and secure video; information shared only with her consent.
