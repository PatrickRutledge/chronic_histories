## Editorial resolutions

- **Index age and whereabouts.** The modern episode presents her at 56. The synthetic date of birth is 1969-12-24 (the true month and day), so the shift is 132 years. The index visit is February 3, 2026 (true frame February 3, 1894) and the last visit is June 30, 2026 (true frame June 30, 1894). 1894 was chosen because it is the year of her lowest recorded weight (43.5 kg) and falls in the brief's range of 1893 to 1896, five years after her son's death. Her day-to-day whereabouts in 1894 were not verified in a fetched source; the sources say only that after 1889 she travelled widely, often incognito, and retreated to Corfu, where the Achilleion was built. The modern episode therefore places her in the clinic between journeys, with care by video while she travels.
- **Name.** She had no surname in the ordinary sense: she was born a Duchess in Bavaria of the house of Wittelsbach and became, by marriage, a member of the house of Habsburg-Lorraine, the form the City of Vienna wiki uses. `identity.name_parts` gives "Elisabeth" as the given name and "of Austria" as the family element, so that the C-CDA and FHIR names reproduce the display name "Elisabeth of Austria" by which she is known, rather than a dynastic house name nobody uses for her. The aliases are "Elisabeth Amalie Eugenie," "Empress Elisabeth" and "Sisi"; a whole-word, case-sensitive search found none of them in any other record ("Sisi" was checked separately because it is short). The modern notes call her "Empress Elisabeth," keeping her title as other records of rulers do.
- **Weights and the synthetic BMI.** Her documented lowest weight, 43.5 kg in 1894 (BMI 14.7), would be extreme anorexia nervosa and would call for inpatient care today. The synthetic intake weight is 47.4 kg (BMI 16.0, moderate) so that the episode can teach outpatient medical stabilization with stated admission thresholds; this is a judgment call. Her height of 172 cm is documented and used. All weights after intake, every result and every vital sign are synthetic.
- **Diagnosis chosen.** DSM-5-TR anorexia nervosa, restricting type, moderate (F50.011), rather than OSFED, because the synthetic presentation meets every criterion and has no binge eating or purging. It is anchored to her documented restriction, weighing, lacing and exercise and to the retrospective diagnosis argued by Skårderud, Vandereycken and Abatzi, and Túry and colleagues (**R**). She was never so diagnosed. The episode differs from the record built on bulimia: the teaching is restriction, refeeding risk, compulsive exercise and bone health in an older woman. The presenting weight loss with fatigue, ankle swelling and dizziness is a separate problem that resolves at the consultation, when the diagnosis is made.
- **Hunger edema and sciatica.** The essay documents fluid accumulation, bodily pains and chlorosis in her last years (**D**); Túry and colleagues interpret the swelling as edema from starvation (**R**). The brief's sciatica and hunger edema at the ankles rest on a newspaper report of archival letters kept by her spa physician (**D**, possible); the letters' dates are mostly not given, so the brief's "1870s or later" is recorded only as "in recent years" before the index date, and the historian quoted is not named. Sciatica is not used in the modern episode. Chlorosis is the period diagnosis and is not adopted.
- **Her son's death.** The deaths at Mayerling are recorded once, in a single claim marked sensitive, with the City of Vienna wiki's wording that he killed his companion and then himself; no method is recorded, and the companion is not named. The modern episode refers to "the death of her son five years ago" as a bereavement, and the consultant's history names it once as a bereavement by suicide because it is a recognized risk factor that a safety assessment must consider. No decision, statement or health fact of her son, husband, daughters or any other real person is used in the modern episode.
- **Depression, grief and safety.** Her documented mourning and "ever-deepening depressions," with a "death-wish" in her last years, are marked sensitive (CLM-0013). The modern diagnosis is recurrent major depressive disorder, moderate (F33.1), with persistent grief coded as Z63.4; prolonged grief disorder (F43.81) was considered and not coded, so that the grief is treated within the depression and therapy plan. The intake safety assessment records passive thoughts of death without intent, plan or past attempts (PHQ-9 16, item 9 scored 1), outpatient care with a collaborative safety plan, the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline and local emergency numbers abroad, and medicines kept by her companion; safety is reviewed at every later visit and the passive thoughts end by twelve weeks.
- **Antidepressant timing.** Sertraline is started at four weeks, not at intake, because antidepressants work poorly at very low weight and starvation mimics depression, and because the ECG was repeated first; sertraline is chosen for its low QT risk. This follows the consultant's advice and is a clinical judgment.
- **Adult guidance.** The SAHM 2022 paper addresses adolescents and young adults; it is applied with care for the monitoring and refeeding approach, alongside the APA 2023 guideline, the AED medical care standards and the ASPEN refeeding consensus, which cover adults. The admission thresholds in the consultation are stated as clinical judgment drawn from these sources, not as a quotation of any one of them.
- **Bone health.** DXA shows low bone mass (synthetic T-scores -2.1 and -1.8) with FRAX probabilities below drug-treatment thresholds, so no bone drug is used; weight restoration, calcium and vitamin D are the plan, under the CMID osteoporosis monograph and the USPSTF 2025 recommendation for postmenopausal women under 65 at increased risk.
- **Privacy of a public figure.** The modern plan restricts and audits the chart, books her under a scheduling alias (anchored to her documented travel incognito), keeps weights to the consulting room, and uses secure video. Nothing in the record suggests her real illness was public, and no modern statement is attributed to her household.
- **Habits.** Never smoking, no alcohol, and no laxatives, diuretics, diet pills or vomiting are synthetic and not claims about her real habits.
- **Family.** No family medical history is recorded. Her parents, husband and son are named only in documented claims, without health facts beyond her son's death. The eating-disorder article's remark about her son's mood is left out.
- **Sick and preventive visits.** A periodic adult preventive visit (age 56) on March 24, 2026 (true frame March 24, 1894) and a same-day sick visit on May 26, 2026 (true frame May 26, 1894) were added. The sick visit is a viral gastroenteritis with mild hypokalemia, chosen because it interacts with anorexia nervosa and sertraline (potassium, glucose reserve, QTc, no ondansetron) and teaches a non-judgmental screen for purging; it does not echo a documented illness. Lipid screening at the preventive visit is deferred until weight is restored.
- **Focused visits.** The four chronic-care follow-ups (one week, four weeks, twelve weeks and twenty-one weeks) are documented as focused visits on the APA, SAHM, AED and ASPEN guidance for medical monitoring of anorexia nervosa (weight, orthostatic vital signs, temperature, ECG, electrolytes with phosphate and magnesium, glucose, blood count, liver enzymes, refeeding edema, exercise, treatment attendance), the CMID depression monograph with the VA/DoD guidelines (PHQ-9, suicide-risk reassessment, SSRI tolerability and sodium), and the CMID osteoporosis monograph (DXA, FRAX, calcium and vitamin D). Two protocol deviations are recorded: the electrolyte check due three to four days after nutrition started was missed while she was at a spa, and the phosphate had fallen to 2.3 mg/dL by day seven, without symptoms, before replacement began; and the electrolyte recheck planned two to three days after the gastroenteritis was missed because she travelled, with normal results five weeks later and no harm. Each has a corrective action. The weekly tests between the first visits are described as reported normal in the notes but are not recorded as observations, because results are stored only on visit dates.
- **Consultation.** The eating disorders psychiatry consultation by the fictional Dr. Haddad-Lindström on the second visit is synthetic. Its recommendations (outpatient care with admission thresholds, phosphate and thiamine, a graded meal plan, weekly CBT-E with open weighing, limits on exercise, sertraline at about four weeks after a repeat ECG, DXA with calcium and vitamin D, and privacy measures) are those the later visits carry out.
- Elisabeth died in 1898, so the recent-death review rule does not apply. Ordinary clinical and provenance reviews remain required, with safe-messaging review of the sensitive claims.
