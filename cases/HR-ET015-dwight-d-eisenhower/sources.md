## Editorial resolutions

- **Index age and date shift.** The heart attack occurred on September 24, 1955, when Eisenhower was 64, and he was back on a full schedule by early 1956. Every visit must fall in 2026, so the episode starts at the earliest point the shift allows: age 65, about sixteen weeks after the infarction, when he is establishing primary care and planning a return to full-time work. Synthetic date of birth October 14, 1960; shift 70 years. The index visit is January 12, 2026 (true frame January 12, 1956). Chronic-care visits follow on January 26, March 16, May 11, and September 28, 2026 (true frame the same days in 1956).
- **Modern version of the documented infarction.** The anterolateral ST-elevation infarction, the late presentation, and the golf on the day before are **D**. In the modern episode the same event is treated with a stent, an echocardiogram, and cardiac rehabilitation (all **S**); the 1955 treatment is not described beyond the documented hospital stay. The synthetic infarction date (September 24, 2025) keeps the true month and day, so it maps to the documented date in the true frame. The hospital ejection fraction of 45 percent, the recovery to 52 percent, the single-vessel disease, the lipid values, and the hypertension are synthetic. Records describing a possible cardiac aneurysm appear only as a folder title in the Army medical records finding aid and are not used.
- **Tobacco.** The four packs a day during the war years and the quit around 1949 are **D** from the NPS. In the modern frame he quit about seven years before intake, which keeps the same interval in the true frame. His pack-year total is not documented; the record says only that it is well over 20 pack-years, the threshold that matters for lung cancer screening.
- **Return to work.** His documented return to a full schedule by early 1956 and his reelection announcement are the anchor for the synthetic goal of returning to full-time work. The record does not describe his office, and no decision or statement is attributed to his physicians or relatives.
- **Later illnesses.** The June 1956 ileitis surgery, the November 1957 cerebrovascular event, and the later heart attacks were verified and fall after the index date, so they appear only in the historical timeline. Because the June 1956 operation falls inside the true-frame window, no visit is scheduled from June to August 2026, and no note states that he had no illness or hospital stay during the year. The exact date, deficits, and type of the 1957 event (widely reported as a minor stroke affecting speech) were seen only in search summaries and secondary pages, so the claim uses the archival wording. The cause of death is not stated in the fetched sources and is not recorded.
- **Not used.** The parents' dates and causes of death were not found in a fetched reliable source, so the family history is empty. Reports of his cholesterol values, of a 1954 Fitzsimons admission (a folder title only, with no stated reason), and of anxiety and depression during his convalescence appeared only in search summaries or secondary pages and are not recorded. The roster's notes on heart failure late in life were not confirmed and are not used.
- **Sick and wellness visits.** An unscheduled sick visit on April 13, 2026 (true frame April 13, 1956) treats an anterior nosebleed on dual antiplatelet therapy, chosen because bleeding is the main hazard of the first year after a stent and because the teaching point is to control the bleed and continue the antiplatelet drugs; it does not echo a documented illness. The wellness visit on October 19, 2026 (true frame October 19, 1956) is coded as an **initial** annual wellness visit, not a subsequent one: with a synthetic 65th birthday in October 2025, he has had Medicare Part B for just over twelve months, so he cannot have had an earlier annual wellness visit. It orders a one-time abdominal aortic aneurysm ultrasound and lung cancer screening because of his smoking history; no results are invented. Its advance care planning names no real person as his health care agent.
- **Focused visits.** The four chronic-care follow-ups (two weeks, about two months, four months, and eight and a half months after intake) are documented as focused visits on the CMID acute myocardial infarction monograph for the first year, with the ischemic heart disease, hyperlipidemia, and hypertension monographs. Two protocol deviations are recorded as deferred instead of inventing results: the echocardiogram due about 40 days after the infarction was not done before he transferred care and was done at about four months (LVEF 52 percent, no harm); and the basic metabolic panel due six months after the lisinopril increase was ordered but not drawn, and was drawn at the one-year visit (normal, no harm). The lipid recheck at nine weeks is noted as one week past the AMI monograph's 4-to-8-week window but within the 4-to-12-week window of the other two monographs.
- **Consultation.** The cardiology consultation on January 26, 2026, is synthetic. It answers the five referral questions (ventricular recovery and defibrillator need, return to work and flying, antiplatelet duration, beta-blocker duration, and the LDL goal), and its recommendations match what the record shows afterwards: full-time work from the following week, ticagrelor stopped at one year, metoprolol continued for hypertension, ezetimibe kept with LDL cholesterol at goal, and no surveillance testing.
- Eisenhower died in 1969, so the recent-death review rule does not apply. Ordinary clinical, provenance, and safe-messaging reviews remain required.
