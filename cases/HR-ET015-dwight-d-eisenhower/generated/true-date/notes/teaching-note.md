# Teaching Note — Dwight D. Eisenhower pilot

> **SYNTHETIC RECORD, NOT A REAL MEDICAL RECORD**

## Why this case matters

This case shows how historical evidence can support a transparent fictional modern episode without presenting interpretation or generated content as documented fact.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish documented (**D**), retrospective (**R**), and synthetic (**S**) content.
2. Keep historical and active problems distinct.
3. Connect measurable goals and interventions to guidelines.
4. Preserve strengths, function, identity, and agency.

## Destigmatization message

A health condition is one part of a person’s life and does not erase achievement, relationships, purpose, or agency.

## Discussion questions

- Which facts support concern without establishing a modern diagnosis?
- Which entries belong in active problems, historical problems, or both?
- What additional evidence would change an evidence grade?
- How should an EHR distinguish synthetic and historical content?

## Evidence-grade summary

- **D:** Dwight D. Eisenhower was born on October 14, 1890, in Denison, Texas, to David Jacob and Ida Elizabeth (Stover) Eisenhower. During his war years Eisenhower smoked about four packs of cigarettes a day; he quit, essentially cold turkey, around 1949 at a doctor's suggestion. On September 23, 1955, during a working vacation in Denver, Eisenhower played golf at Cherry Hills; chest pain woke him early on September 24, an electrocardiogram taken by a cardiac specialist from Fitzsimons Army Hospital confirmed a heart attack, and he was admitted to Fitzsimons that day. The electrocardiogram recorded an anterolateral acute myocardial infarction with ST-segment elevation. During the golf game he had complained of what he took to be indigestion, and he was hospitalized more than 24 hours after his symptoms began. Eisenhower remained at Fitzsimons Army Hospital from September 24 until his discharge on November 11, 1955. By the beginning of 1956 Eisenhower had resumed a full schedule, and on February 29, 1956, he announced that he would seek reelection. In June 1956 Eisenhower needed emergency abdominal surgery for bowel obstruction caused by ileitis (Crohn's disease); on June 9 the surgeons bypassed the diseased segment of terminal ileum rather than resecting it. In November 1957 Eisenhower had a cerebrovascular event that his Army medical records file as a vascular "accident" and that an Eisenhower Library oral-history summary calls a minor cerebral artery spasm. Eisenhower had another heart attack in 1965, had his gallbladder removed in 1966, and had further heart attacks in 1968; he spent his last nine months at Walter Reed Army Hospital and died there on March 28, 1969, aged 78.
- **R:** None recorded.
- **S:** Shifted identity, fictional care team, modern encounters, results, plans, and outcomes.
