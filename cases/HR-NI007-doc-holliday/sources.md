## Historical evidence

1. Macdonald JD. “John Henry ‘Doc’ Holliday (1851–1887).” *New Georgia Encyclopedia* (Georgia Humanities), originally published August 28, 2009, last edited January 13, 2022. Supports his birth in Griffin on August 14, 1851; his mother's death of tuberculosis in 1866; his enrollment at the Pennsylvania College of Dental Surgery in 1870 and graduation in 1872; his practice in Atlanta and then his own practice in Griffin; that he soon developed chronic pulmonary tuberculosis; that he headed west in 1873 to find a drier climate and arrived in Dallas in September 1873; and his death in Glenwood Springs, Colorado, on November 8, 1887, at 36, when his tuberculosis had reached its final stages. https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-henry-doc-holliday-1851-1887/
2. Ward DS. “Holliday, Doc (1852–1887).” *Encyclopedia of the Great Plains* (University of Nebraska–Lincoln). Supports that he was diagnosed with tuberculosis in 1873 and moved west for a healthier climate, and his death of tuberculosis in Glenwood Springs on November 8, 1887. Gives his birth as August 14, 1852. https://plainshumanities.unl.edu/encyclopedia/doc/egp.ii.035.html
3. HISTORY.com Editors. “Doc Holliday dies of tuberculosis.” HISTORY.com, published November 16, 2009, last updated May 27, 2025. Supports his graduation from the Pennsylvania College of Dental Surgery in 1872; that shortly after starting as a dentist in Atlanta he developed a bad cough, and doctors diagnosed tuberculosis and advised a more arid climate; that he made his living at card tables, aggravating his tuberculosis with heavy drinking and late nights; and that he died in bed at 36. https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/november-8/doc-holliday-dies-of-tuberculosis

## Editorial resolutions

- **Name.** The display name is John Henry Holliday; the alias is “Doc Holliday” (not “Doc” alone, which would match ordinary words in other records).
- **Birth date.** The New Georgia Encyclopedia gives August 14, 1851, which is preferred and agrees with the roster; the Encyclopedia of the Great Plains gives 1852, recorded as a conflicting alternative.
- **Time of diagnosis.** The roster says he was diagnosed at 22. The fetched sources say he was diagnosed in 1873, shortly after starting to practice in Atlanta, and that he moved west in 1873, arriving in Dallas in September. Born in August 1851, he was 21 for most of 1873 before his birthday in August, so the episode presents him at 21, at the time of the diagnosis and before the move. The roster's age is not used.
- **Index age and dates.** The synthetic date of birth is August 14, 2004 (shift 153 years). The index visit on February 10, 2026 maps to February 10, 1873; the chronic-care visits on February 24, March 17, April 14, and August 18, 2026 and the sick visit on May 19, 2026 map to the same days of 1873; the preventive visit on September 22, 2026 (age 22) maps to September 22, 1873. All fall within his lifetime. The modern episode ends in cure; that is a synthetic outcome of modern treatment and does not contradict his documented death from tuberculosis fourteen years later in the true frame.
- **Tuberculosis.** The documented diagnosis, the advice to move to a drier climate, and the chronic pulmonary disease are graded **D**. The modern problem is coded A15.0 (tuberculosis of lung) with placement both_with_link. The cavity, smear grades, molecular and culture results, susceptibility, and all monitoring are synthetic. Qualitative microbiology (smears, nucleic acid amplification, cultures, susceptibility, HIV, and hepatitis serology) is recorded in the note text and monitoring tables, because the canonical schema carries only numeric results.
- **Alcohol.** That he aggravated his tuberculosis with heavy drinking and late nights is graded **D** and marked sensitive; the source does not date the drinking. Placing heavy drinking at the time of diagnosis, the AUDIT scores, the three criteria met, and the diagnosis of a mild alcohol use disorder (F10.10) are synthetic. The roster's “alcoholism” is not used as a documented diagnosis. The F10 code triggers the structured safety assessment, which records no current risk, gives the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, and is reviewed at every visit.
- **Real people.** His mother's death of tuberculosis in 1866 is recorded only in the family history and the historical-basis sections, as the source states it; no relative appears in the visit notes, and no decision or statement is attributed to any real person. Contacts in the contact investigation (a roommate, coworkers, and patients) are synthetic, unnamed, and their results are not part of his record. The card games and late nights in the modern social history are anchored to the documented card playing; the record makes no reference to any other episode of his life.
- **Occupation.** The modern frame keeps his documented early qualification as a dentist, even though qualifying at 20 or 21 is unusual today; he is an associate in a group dental office, which shapes the infection-control and return-to-work decisions.
- **Synthetic content.** All laboratory values, vision results, chest X-rays, weights, the dosing history, the household, the counselor, the party lapse, the sick visit, and the preventive care are synthetic. Height (178 cm) is synthetic.
- **Sick and preventive visits.** The sick visit is a viral gastroenteritis during the continuation phase that teaches how to assess nausea on isoniazid and rifampin: doses held by the nurse, same-day liver tests, and the definition of drug-induced liver injury. The preventive visit uses the adult preventive service (age 22) with health-care personnel vaccination (influenza, first hepatitis B dose with a later antibody check, and documented measles, mumps, rubella, and varicella immunity) and a yearly symptom screen instead of tuberculosis testing. Advance care planning is a brief discussion with no agent named and no preference recorded.
- **Focused visits.** The four chronic-care follow-ups (two weeks, five weeks, two months, and the end of treatment) are documented as focused visits with protocol monitoring tables built from the ATS/CDC/IDSA guideline and the ATS hepatotoxicity statement (symptoms, weight, hepatitis symptoms, liver tests, sputum smear and culture, the two-month culture, susceptibility, vision on ethambutol, HIV and hepatitis serology, diabetes screen, chest X-ray, observed doses, neuropathy and pyridoxine, rifampin interactions, isolation, contact investigation, reporting, and infectious diseases review) and the alcohol guidance (AUDIT-C, brief counseling, counseling sessions, medication offered, and suicide-risk review). Protocol deviation recorded instead of invented results: the liver tests planned two weeks after the sick visit and the monthly tests in the fourth and fifth months were not drawn; no symptoms were reported and the end-of-treatment tests were normal, so no harm is evident.
- **Consultation.** The infectious diseases referral and the consultation by Leticia Navarro, MD (a fictional consultant), on the day of the two-week visit, are **S**. The consultant confirms the regimen and doses, sets monthly liver tests and the thresholds for stopping, the release and return-to-work criteria, stopping ethambutol on susceptibility results, the two-month culture rule for cavitary disease, and acamprosate as the preferred medicine if one is wanted, and explains why the four-month rifapentine and moxifloxacin regimen is not started midway. The record follows these recommendations, apart from the liver-test gap recorded as a deviation.
- **Not used.** His later life, his gambling career beyond the card playing named above, gunfights, and accounts of his final illness are not used. Find a Grave, Wikipedia, and other secondary pages found in searches were not used.
