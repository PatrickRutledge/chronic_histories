Assessment

  • Cavitary pulmonary tuberculosis, probably drug-susceptible (A15.0). Smear-positive, culture-positive cavitary disease with an early clinical response and full adherence. No rifampin resistance on the molecular test; isoniazid susceptibility is pending. The regimen and the weight-band doses are correct: isoniazid about 5 mg/kg, rifampin about 10 mg/kg, pyrazinamide 1500 mg and ethambutol 1200 mg for 56 to 75 kg. HIV negative. [S]
  • Liver risk. Heavy drinking, low albumin, and the three hepatotoxic drugs together raise his risk of drug-induced liver injury. Today's ALT 41, AST 39, and bilirubin 0.7 are normal. Injury is defined as ALT at least three times the upper limit with symptoms, or at least five times without symptoms, or a bilirubin rise; small rises below this are common and do not need the drugs stopped. [S]
  • Infectiousness and return to work. He remains infectious until he has three negative sputum smears, a clinical response, and at least two weeks of effective treatment. Because he works close to patients' faces, return should wait for all three. [S]
  • Length of treatment. Six months in total if the two-month culture is negative. A cavity on the first X-ray together with a positive two-month culture would extend the continuation phase to seven months. The four-month rifapentine and moxifloxacin regimen is an alternative only at the start of treatment; switching now offers no benefit. [S]
  • Alcohol use disorder, mild (F10.10). Stopping alcohol is the most useful thing he can do for his liver during treatment. If medication is wanted, acamprosate is preferred, because it is not processed by the liver; naltrexone and disulfiram are best avoided while he is on hepatotoxic drugs. [S]
