## Historical evidence

1. Regional Oral History Office, The Bancroft Library, University of California, Berkeley. *Dorothea Lange: The Making of a Documentary Photographer.* Oral history interview, October 1960 to August 1961 (transcript issued 1968), with appended memorial material. Her own words: "I was physically disabled, and no one who hasn't lived the life of a semi-cripple knows how much that means. I think it perhaps was the most important thing that happened to me, and formed me, guided me, instructed me, helped me, and humiliated me"; that her "lameness as a child and my acceptance, finally, of my lameness truly opened gates for me"; and that working among strangers as a disabled person "gives an immense advantage. People are kinder." The appended material records that she traveled widely abroad with her husband from 1958, her death of cancer on October 11, 1965, at 70, and that she spent her last months preparing the Museum of Modern Art retrospective that opened in January 1966. https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/record/217204/files/lange_dorothea__w.pdf
2. PBS, *American Masters*. "Dorothea Lange: biography and timeline" (July 31, 2014). Supports her birth in Hoboken (May 26, 1895 in the summary; May 25 in the timeline); "Dorothea is disabled by polio. She walks with a limp the rest of her life" (1902); her hiring by the Resettlement Administration in June 1935, later the Farm Security Administration; "Migrant Mother" in Nipomo, California, in March 1936; homes in Berkeley from December 1935; illness setting in from 1945 to 1948, with pain and discomfort for the rest of her days; diagnosis of cancer of the esophagus in July and August 1964; and her death on October 11, 1965, at 70. https://www.pbs.org/wnet/americanmasters/dorothea-lange-biography-with-photo-gallery/3097

## Clinical guidance

1. European Federation of Neurological Societies. "EFNS guideline on diagnosis and management of post-polio syndrome." *Eur J Neurol* 2006;13(8):795–801. PMID 16879288 (abstract read). Recommends Halstead's criteria for diagnosis; supervised aerobic and strength training as safe and effective (level B), with warm-climate and non-swimming water exercise; respiratory muscle training and early non-invasive ventilation for respiratory impairment (level C); group training, follow-up, and education; weight control and properly fitted assistive devices (good practice); no proven effect of pyridostigmine, corticosteroids, or amantadine. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16879288/
2. March of Dimes. *Post-Polio Syndrome: Identifying Best Practices in Diagnosis & Care* (conference of May 2000, report 2001), listed by Polio Place; the PDF could not be opened. The five criteria were read as reproduced in an open-access study (Open Neurol J 2016;10:77–82, PMID 27651845): a history of paralytic polio confirmed by history, neurological examination, and EMG; partial or complete functional recovery followed by a stable period of at least 15 years; gradual or sudden onset of progressive and persistent new weakness or decreased endurance, with or without fatigue, atrophy, or muscle and joint pain; symptoms persisting for at least a year; and exclusion of other causes. https://polioplace.org/medical-articles/post-polio-syndrome-identifying-best-practices-diagnosis-care

## Editorial resolutions

- **Index age and dates.** Index age 61. The index visit on February 9, 2026 maps to February 9, 1957, when she lived and worked in Berkeley, 55 years after her polio and about a decade after her ulcer surgery. The synthetic date of birth is May 26, 1964 (shift 69 years), keeping her preferred birthday. The other visits (February 25, April 20, April 22, May 18, August 17, and September 28, 2026) map to the same days of 1957, all within her lifetime, before the documented travels abroad of 1958 onward and long before the 1964 cancer diagnosis.
- **Birth date.** Sources give May 26 (New Jersey Hall of Fame and the PBS summary), May 25 (the PBS timeline), and May 15 (a Gale reference biography). May 26 is preferred and the others are recorded as conflicting alternatives.
- **Polio and the side affected.** Three sources say the right leg (a weakened right leg, a withered right leg, a twisted right foot); the brief's right side is confirmed. The modern foot drop, knee hyperextension, and thin right leg are synthetic findings built on that documented fact.
- **The oral history.** The brief described a 1960 to 1961 oral history at the Smithsonian Archives of American Art. The 1960 to 1961 interview is in fact the Bancroft Library's Regional Oral History Office interview, which was read and quoted; a separate Archives of American Art interview of 1964 exists but was not read. The correction is recorded here, following the sources.
- **Post-polio syndrome in her life.** Only a review of her standard biography (source 5) says she had post-polio symptoms and fatigue in late middle age; the condition was not described until after her death, and the biography itself was not read. The claim is graded **R**, possible, and linked to a later, undated timeline event so that the intake documents do not present it. The modern diagnosis (G14) is **S**, placed both_with_link to the documented polio and the retrospective claim.
- **Ulcers, the esophagus, and the 1964 cancer.** The late-1940s ulcers and "radical surgery" are documented; the operation type is not, so the record says only "surgery for stomach ulcers" and "healed changes of the earlier ulcer operation." The synthetic reflux episode was designed not to foreshadow her documented esophageal cancer: the endoscopy finds grade B esophagitis and a small hiatal hernia, with no Barrett's esophagus and no other lesion, and she does not meet the ACG criteria for Barrett's screening or surveillance. Her later bleeding ulcers, esophageal problems, and cancer are recorded only as claims and are not used. Judgment call: endoscopy was done because symptoms returned within days of stopping an eight-week PPI trial, before committing her to long-term acid suppression and with an earlier ulcer operation; the CCJM summary of the ACG guideline frames off-PPI endoscopy for refractory symptoms, and the clinical summary for symptoms that return.
- **Diagnosis of exclusion.** The work-up excludes thyroid disease, anemia, diabetes, B12 deficiency, kidney and liver disease, inflammation, neuropathy (normal nerve conduction), myopathy and a neuromuscular junction disorder (EMG), depression as a cause (PHQ-9 6, secondary), and sleep-disordered breathing (normal overnight oximetry). The symptoms have lasted about 18 months, meeting the one-year criterion. The mildly raised creatine kinase (228 U/L) is a synthetic finding consistent with overuse.
- **Osteoporosis and zoledronic acid.** Synthetic: osteoporosis of the right femoral neck (T-score -2.7) on the polio side, coded M81.8 (other osteoporosis) for disuse; the FRAX values are teaching approximations. Intravenous zoledronic acid was chosen over an oral bisphosphonate because of active reflux symptoms. The CMID secondary-causes protocol calls for a 24-hour urine calcium; it was ordered but missed before the infusion, recorded as a **deferred** deviation at the third visit with the reasoning and corrective action, and remains due; no result is invented.
- **Focused visits.** The four chronic-care follow-ups (diagnosis with the consultation, eight weeks with the first infusion, endoscopy results, and six months) are documented as focused visits on the EFNS guideline and March of Dimes criteria for post-polio syndrome, the ACG reflux and Barrett's guidelines, the CMID osteoporosis monograph, and the zoledronic acid label.
