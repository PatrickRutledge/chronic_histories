Assessment and Plan

  • Acute-phase reaction after the first zoledronic acid infusion (T50.995A). Fever, muscle and joint aching, and headache starting within a day of a first intravenous bisphosphonate dose, with no focal signs of infection, normal white cell count, and normal calcium and creatinine, is the expected acute-phase reaction. It usually settles within three days and is much less likely with later infusions. It is not a worsening of post-polio syndrome; her strength is unchanged. [S]
    • Acetaminophen 1000 mg up to three times a day for three days, no more than 3000 mg a day; no ibuprofen or naproxen after her ulcer surgery.
    • Fluids and rest; resume her exercise program when the fever settles.
    • Call if fever lasts beyond three days, rises above 39 °C, or if eye pain, cramps, or tingling develop.
    • For next year's infusion, acetaminophen before and for two days after to lessen the reaction.
  • Post-polio syndrome (G14). Aching from the infusion reaction, not new weakness. She was told plainly that a field day next week is reasonable if the fever has gone, with her usual pacing. [S]
    • Keep next week's field day with rest breaks; skip the following exercise session if still tired.
