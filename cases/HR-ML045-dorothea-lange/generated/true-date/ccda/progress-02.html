Assessment and Plan

  • Post-polio syndrome (G14). She meets the modified March of Dimes criteria: paralytic polio in childhood confirmed by history, examination, and EMG; partial recovery followed by five decades of stable function; gradual new weakness and fatigue for more than a year; and other causes excluded by normal nerve conduction, no myopathy on EMG, and normal thyroid, blood count, glucose, B12, and inflammatory markers. The mildly raised creatine kinase fits muscle overuse. Mild depressive symptoms are a response to the fatigue, not its cause. She was told that post-polio syndrome usually progresses slowly, that pacing, the right exercise, and an orthosis often stabilize or improve function, and that field work can continue with changes. [S]
    • Twelve weeks of supervised non-fatiguing exercise with rest days, then a home program.
    • Pacing and energy conservation for field days: a lighter camera on a harness, a seat stick, an assistant to carry the tripod, and a rest day after each field day.
