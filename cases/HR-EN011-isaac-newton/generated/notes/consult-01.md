## Assessment

1. **Raised urine mercury from occupational exposure** (R82.6). Real occupational absorption of elemental mercury vapor, confirmed by the vapor measurement and a urine level about twice the occupational biological exposure index. At 46 ug/L the urine level lies in the band that ATSDR associates with subtle changes on nerve-conduction and psychomotor testing, not with the irritability, depression, tremor, and kidney effects seen above 100 ug/L. There is no tremor, gingivitis, erethism beyond his mood disorder, neuropathy, or kidney injury, so this is exposure without clinical mercury poisoning. Mercury may have added to his insomnia and irritability but does not explain a year of depressive illness. Lead absorption is negligible. Arsenic and antimony exposure was small and intermittent, and a broad metal screen is not useful without a specific exposure and signs. **S**
2. **Major depressive disorder, single episode, severe, without psychotic features** (F32.2). I agree that his depressive and sleep symptoms need treatment now, in their own right, alongside removal from exposure. **S**

## Recommendations

1. No chelation: it is not indicated for urine mercury below 100 ug/L without signs of toxicity, and removal from exposure is the treatment. No provoked (chelator-challenge) urine testing, which is not valid.
2. Keep him out of the laboratory and its adjoining rooms until it is professionally decontaminated, mercury vapor is re-measured below the NIOSH limit of 0.05 mg/m3 averaged over a work shift (OSHA 0.1 mg/m3), and a ducted fume hood is fitted.
3. The hazard assessment should cover vapor at the furnace and bench, the floor and drains, his clothing and his rooms, storage of chemicals, a mercury spill kit, and no food or drink in the laboratory.
4. Repeat 24-hour urine mercury with creatinine and blood mercury four to six weeks after removal, and again at three months with creatinine and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio; nerve conduction studies only if numbness or weakness appear.
5. Return to laboratory work when the conditions above are met and the urine mercury is below 20 ug/g creatinine, with urine mercury checked every six months while he works with mercury.
6. Treat the depression and insomnia now as planned, with sertraline and cognitive behavioral therapy; please ask me to see him again if tremor, gum disease, numbness, or proteinuria appear, or if the urine mercury fails to fall.
