Teaching objective: Dignified, recovery-oriented primary care for a 35-year-old painter twelve days after discharge from a first psychiatric admission for psychosis with mood features: keeping the diagnosis provisional and stating the differential (bipolar I disorder with psychotic features, brief psychotic or schizophreniform disorder, alcohol withdrawal or alcohol-induced psychosis, and focal epilepsy), a first-episode workup with brain MRI and EEG, antipsychotic monitoring to the APA and ADA/APA standard (weight at every visit, blood pressure, fasting glucose, A1c and lipids at baseline and 12 weeks, akathisia and movement checks with the AIMS), recognizing and treating akathisia, AUDIT-C, PEth and naltrexone for alcohol use disorder, sleep as treatment and as an early warning sign, coordinated specialty care, and a structured safety assessment with lethal-means counseling and the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline reviewed at every visit, on the APA 2020 schizophrenia guideline, the CANMAT and ISBD bipolar guidelines, and the CMID depression and bipolar monograph.

Social, functional, and mental status

Social history

  • S Painter; works long days in his own studio and sells some work; lives alone in a rented house that is also his studio. A fellow painter who had been sharing the house left after a heated argument just before the episode
  • S Alcohol: before the episode, five or six drinks most days, wine with meals and anise spirits in the evening (AUDIT-C 9). Was treated for alcohol withdrawal in hospital; since discharge two or three drinks most evenings
  • S Tobacco: smokes a pipe daily; not ready to stop now
  • S Sleep: for several weeks before the episode he slept three or four hours a night while painting, without feeling tired; since discharge about six hours with broken sleep
  • S Meals irregular when he is working, often bread and coffee; drinks several cups of coffee a day
  • S Supports: a friend he paints with who lives nearby, and the coordinated specialty care team; his work is his strongest reason to get well. He decides who is told about his health

Functional status

  • S Independent in all activities; not yet painting since discharge because he cannot concentrate for long; manages his money and medicines

Mental status

  • S Alert and oriented; quiet and cooperative; speech normal in rate; no current hallucinations by report, though he sometimes hears his name called as he falls asleep; mild suspiciousness at night; thought process organized; insight growing
  • S Mood low and flat, ashamed of the episode, which he cannot remember well; PHQ-9 score 13 with item 9 scored 1 for passive thoughts that it would be easier not to wake up; no intent, plan or preparation; no thoughts of harming others

Disposition
Outpatient care with close follow-up. He has passive thoughts that it would be easier not to wake up, without intent, plan or preparation; the self-injury during the episode happened in confusion and without suicidal intent. His risk is higher in the months after a first episode of psychosis, while he is depressed, and while he is drinking. Lethal-means counseling: he has no firearm and no access to one and agreed not to acquire one; medicines are dispensed two weeks at a time; he will keep alcohol out of the house while he is low.
