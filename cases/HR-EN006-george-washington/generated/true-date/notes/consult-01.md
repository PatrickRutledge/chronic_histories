## Assessment

1. **Denture-related traumatic ulcers** (K12.1). The ulcers healed within two weeks once the denture was out between meals, which confirms trauma from the overextended, unstable lower flange. No lesion needs a biopsy; any ulcer that persists for two weeks after the cause is removed would. **S**
2. **Complete edentulism, ACP diagnostic index class III** (K08.103). Complete edentulism of both jaws, class III by the American College of Prosthodontists diagnostic index because of the mandibular bone height of about 14 mm and the unfavorable lower ridge and muscle attachment. The worn dentures with a closed bite account for the pain, the instability and the change in the support of his lips. Relining the old dentures would not correct the worn teeth or the bite, so replacement is advised. **S**
3. **Implant candidacy** (K08.103). The McGill and York consensus statements make a two-implant overdenture the first-choice standard for the edentulous lower jaw, with better chewing, comfort and satisfaction than a conventional denture. He is healthy, does not smoke, has no diabetes and takes no bone-modifying drug, and the panoramic radiograph suggests enough bone in the canine regions; a cone beam CT will confirm it. The upper denture is expected to do well without implants on his well-formed upper ridge. **S**
4. **Nutrition and speech** (R63.4). Poor chewing explains his narrowed diet and weight loss. New dentures need a few weeks of adaptation for chewing and speech; the lower denture will stay less stable than the upper until the attachments are connected. **S**

## Recommendations

1. Relieve the lower flange and reline the old lower denture with a tissue conditioner today, and keep it out overnight.
2. New upper and lower complete dentures over the next four weeks, the lower designed for conversion to an implant overdenture, with an adjustment visit 24 hours to one week after delivery.
3. Cone beam CT of the mandible, then two implants in the lower canine regions after the new dentures are delivered, with amoxicillin 2 g one hour before surgery, chlorhexidine rinses for two weeks and a short course of ibuprofen; the denture relieved and soft-lined over the implants; sutures out at about ten days.
4. Connect stud attachments to the implants after about three months of healing, with a check within a week.
5. Read aloud daily with the new dentures; a speech-language assessment if speech is not clear by six weeks.
6. Continue the soft high-protein diet with the supplement until chewing is restored, and widen the diet as the new dentures settle.
7. Clean the dentures daily with a soft brush and non-abrasive cleanser, keep them out overnight in water, and attend a yearly prosthodontic recall; replace the attachment inserts when retention falls.
