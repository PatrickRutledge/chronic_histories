Teaching objective: Tooth loss as a medical problem: a 57-year-old head of state in his first year in office, who has lost his teeth over thirty years, comes in because worn complete dentures hurt, move when he speaks in public and have narrowed his diet, with unintended weight loss. The episode covers an oral examination by the primary clinician with oral cancer screening and the two-week rule for an oral ulcer, nutrition screening with the MNA-SF and a focused weight-loss evaluation, the American College of Prosthodontists diagnostic index for complete edentulism, care and maintenance of complete dentures, new complete dentures and a mandibular two-implant overdenture as the first-choice standard under the McGill and York consensus statements, antibiotic prophylaxis at implant placement, speech and public speaking with new dentures, cardiovascular risk assessment with tooth loss recorded as an association only, and an outpatient community-acquired pneumonia managed with CURB-65 and the 1783 ATS/IDSA guideline in a man with healing implants.

  • D June 1755: Severe dysentery with fever as a young officer, about five weeks to recover (CLM-0006)

  • D June 17, 1789: Large painful tumor of the thigh removed surgically; six weeks to heal (CLM-0016)
