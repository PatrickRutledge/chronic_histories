## Historical evidence

1. Patrick J. “George Washington and Health,” George Washington's Mount Vernon. His habits (exercise, moderate eating and drinking, sleep, no tobacco); smallpox in Barbados in 1751 (“strongly attacked with the small Pox,” three weeks in bed); malarial fevers treated with bark; dysentery on the Braddock expedition in June 1755; his tooth extraction at 24 by a “Doctr Watson”; the thigh tumor of June 1789 (“a very large and painful tumor on the protuberance of my thigh”), removed on June 17, 1789, six weeks to heal, with a milder recurrence two years later; influenza and pneumonia in the spring of 1790, which affected his hearing and eyesight; his letter on “the remains of the violent affection of my lungs”; and his final illness. https://www.mountvernon.org/george-washington/health
2. Etter WM. “False Teeth,” *George Washington's Mount Vernon Digital Encyclopedia*. ## Editorial resolutions

- **Sources.** The case relies on eight George Washington's Mount Vernon pages, the National Library of Medicine exhibition, *Encyclopedia Virginia*, and volume 6 of the Papers of George Washington edition of his diaries, read in the Library of Congress digitization. Founders Online (National Archives) returned empty pages to automated requests, so no letter was read there; letters are quoted only as Mount Vernon or the diary editors quote them. For the four medical articles only the PubMed abstracts were read. Popular health sites found in searches were not used.
- **Birth date and calendar.** He was born on February 11 in the Old Style (Julian) calendar then used in Virginia; because the legal year then began on March 25, the year was written 1731 (or 1731/32). The same day is February 22, 1732, in the Gregorian calendar. The preferred date is February 22, 1732; the Old Style date is a superseded alternative recorded with its Julian date (February 11, 1732, counting the year from January 1). The synthetic date of birth keeps February 22. Every other date in the case is after 1752 and is Gregorian.
- **Index age and dates.** The index visit on January 19, 2026 maps to January 19, 1790, the day after his diary records “an Aching tooth, and swelled and inflamed Gum,” when he was 57 and nine months into his presidency in New York. The synthetic date of birth is February 22, 1968 (shift 236 years); he turns 58 before the preventive visit. The visits on February 2, March 16, April 13, May 11, May 25 and July 13, 2026 map to the same days of 1790. His diary places him in New York throughout (with a short Long Island trip from April 20) until the diary stops on July 14, 1790, so the last visit is set before then. The synthetic procedures (March 2, March 23 and June 22) fall in the same months. Past medical history includes only events up to January 18, 1790; the 1790 pneumonia and all later events are linked to later timeline events.
- **Presenting problem.** Pain and difficulty chewing and speaking with loose dentures (K08.9) and the two ulcers under the lower denture (K12.1) are the intake problems. Both end at the two-week visit: the prosthodontist diagnoses complete edentulism, ACP class III (K08.103), recorded on that date, and the ulcers have healed, which confirms denture trauma. The weight-loss problem (R63.4) is recorded at intake and resolved at six months. Intake documents name no diagnosis class, implant plan or treatment choice.
- **Complete edentulism in the modern episode.** He kept one natural tooth from 1789 until 1796, and his diary records an aching tooth on January 18, 1790. The synthetic patient is completely edentulous, as the assignment asks; the remaining tooth is not modeled, and the aching tooth is used only as the documented reason for the index date. The cause of tooth loss is coded as unspecified because the sources do not establish it.
- **Ulcers, not denture stomatitis.** Denture-related traumatic ulcers were chosen over candidal denture stomatitis because the documented complaint is of loose, painful dentures; the palate is recorded as normal. ICD-10-CM K12.1 (other forms of stomatitis) is used for the traumatic ulcers, which the index places there. The two-week re-examination after removing the irritant follows the ADA 2017 guideline; because the ulcers healed, no biopsy was done.
- **Dentures and enslaved people.** The assignment's lead was that his dentures were made with human teeth bought from enslaved people. Mount Vernon documents something more specific, and the case records exactly that: in May 1784 his accounts record a payment to enslaved people at Mount Vernon for nine teeth, on the account of the visiting dentist Jean Pierre Le Mayeur (CLM-0009, D); two of his four surviving dentures contain human teeth whose source is unknown, and Mount Vernon says teeth from enslaved people may be in them but no record shows it (CLM-0013, R, possible). Mount Vernon's statement that enslaved people could have felt compelled to sell teeth is included. These are stated plainly, have no clinical role, name no enslaved person and record no health fact about any enslaved person; they appear in the historical timeline and are not used as color in the modern episode.
- **Slavery.** At least 577 enslaved people lived and worked at Mount Vernon over his life, and 317 were held there at his death (CLM-0021, D). This is recorded plainly and linked to the timeline at his death, so it is not shown in the intake documents; the New York household's enslaved workers are recorded in CLM-0015. The modern episode is a present-day synthetic record and contains no enslaved people.
- **The 1789 thigh tumor.** Mount Vernon documents a large, painful tumor of the thigh removed on June 17, 1789 (CLM-0016, D). The roster's word carbuncle could not be confirmed from a reputable source read; the NLM exhibition lists anthrax among his serious illnesses without dating it. What the tumor was is recorded as unsettled (CLM-0017, R, possible), and no modern diagnosis is assigned.
- **Final illness and bloodletting.** His death of a throat infection after four bleedings is D (CLM-0002). Acute epiglottitis is the usual modern interpretation and is R, probable (CLM-0003); Mackowiak (2021) re-examines the diagnosis, and only its abstract was read. The roster's figure of 40% of blood volume in 12 hours is not used; the case gives the NLM exhibition's three quarts, half his blood volume, and Cheatham's over 2500 mL. The death is history only.
- **1790 pneumonia and the sick visit.** His diary and its editors document a cold on May 9, 1790 that became a severe pneumonia from May 10, with his life despaired of on May 15 (CLM-0019, D). The synthetic sick visit on May 11, 2026 is deliberately much milder, an outpatient community-acquired pneumonia with CURB-65 0, so that it teaches outpatient management; it is not a claim about the cause or severity of his real illness. Amoxicillin was chosen for an adult without comorbidities; the single prophylactic dose at implant surgery seven weeks earlier was judged not to change the choice. Rapid SARS-CoV-2 and influenza tests are recorded as negative in narrative.
- **Roster leads not used.** Tuberculosis: no source read says he had it; his half-brother's tuberculosis appears only as the reason for the Barbados journey. Infertility: Encyclopedia Virginia states that the claim that smallpox made him sterile is not supported, so it is not a claim or a diagnosis. Smallpox inoculation of the army is documented but is not part of this episode.
- **ACP guidance.** The assignment's “ACP guidance” is taken to mean the American College of Prosthodontists (the diagnostic index and the complete-denture care guideline), the society relevant to dentures; the American College of Physicians has no guideline on dentures. The ADA guidance used is the 2017 oral mucosal lesion guideline.
- **Instruments.** The MNA-SF has no LOINC code for the 14-point screening score (LOINC 107114-1 is the full MNA total), so it is recorded as narrative with its six items. Chewing ability (out of 10) and CURB-65 are narrative.
- **Cardiovascular risk.** Tooth loss is recorded only as associated with cardiovascular disease in cohort studies (Cheng 2018) and in the AHA 2012 statement on periodontal disease, which found no proof of a causal link; it does not change his risk estimate or treatment.
- **Relatives.** No relative is named in the modern episode or given a decision, statement or health fact. The social history says only that he is married and raising two grandchildren. His half-brother appears in one historical claim, by relationship. The advance care planning agent is an unnamed long-time friend. No family history is recorded.
- **Synthetic clinical picture.** Everything in the modern episode is synthetic: the oral findings, the ulcers, the diagnostic class, the radiographs and scan, the dentures and implants, every laboratory value and score (PHQ-2, AUDIT-C, MNA-SF, chewing ratings), the vital signs, the medicines, the pneumonia and the outcomes. Height (188 cm) and weights are synthetic.
- **Clinical judgment calls.** (1) Oral examination and oral cancer screening by the primary clinician, with the two-week rule, before the prosthodontic visit. (2) A focused weight-loss evaluation (blood count, metabolic panel, albumin, hemoglobin A1c, TSH, vitamin B12) even though poor chewing was the likely cause. (3) New complete dentures first, then two implants with conventional loading at about three months, rather than immediate loading. (4) Single-dose amoxicillin prophylaxis at implant placement, per the 2026 Cochrane review. (5) No implants for the upper jaw. (6) Outpatient pneumonia treatment at CURB-65 0 during implant healing, with no follow-up chest radiograph. (7) PCV20, recombinant zoster and Tdap at the preventive visit at 58; no statin at a 10-year risk of about 7%; PSA deferred by his choice after discussion.
- **Preventive visit.** A periodic adult preventive visit at 58 (CPT 99396 with 99497 in plain text). Lipid panel with no statin; diabetes screening up to date from intake; a FIT kit (negative, reported at twelve weeks); one-time HIV, hepatitis C and hepatitis B screening (nonreactive; immune to hepatitis B from past vaccination); Tdap, PCV20 and the first recombinant zoster dose, the second at six months; COVID-19 vaccine deferred under shared decision-making; advance care planning records an unnamed agent.
- **Focused visits.** The four chronic-care follow-ups (two weeks with the consultation, twelve weeks, the two-week pneumonia resolution review, six months) are documented as focused visits on the ACP diagnostic index and complete-denture guideline, the McGill and York statements, the ADA 2017 guideline, the MNA-SF, the 2026 Cochrane review and, for the pneumonia, the CMID pneumonia monograph with the ATS/IDSA 2019 guideline. One deviation is recorded instead of an invented result: the adjustment visit planned 24 hours to one week after delivery of the new dentures was not kept because of his schedule; a sore spot developed and healed after relief on day 12, with no lasting harm, and the corrective action was a protected appointment within a week of any new delivery or attachment pick-up, which was kept after the attachments were connected. Synthetic findings introduced: a lisp for a few days after delivery, mild swelling after implant surgery, and 1.3 kg of weight lost during the pneumonia.
- **Consultation.** The prosthodontic referral and the consultation by Julian Okoro-Lindqvist, DDS (a fictional consultant), on the day of the two-week visit, are **S**. The consultant answers the five referral questions in line with what the record shows afterwards: denture-trauma ulcers with no biopsy, replacement rather than relining, new dentures followed by two lower implants with a cone beam CT, amoxicillin, chlorhexidine and conventional loading, interim relief and a tissue-conditioning reline, speech practice, and yearly recall.
