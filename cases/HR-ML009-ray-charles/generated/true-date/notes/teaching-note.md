# Teaching Note — Ray Charles pilot

> **SYNTHETIC RECORD, NOT A REAL MEDICAL RECORD**

## Why this case matters

This case shows how historical evidence can support a transparent fictional modern episode without presenting interpretation or generated content as documented fact.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish documented (**D**), retrospective (**R**), and synthetic (**S**) content.
2. Keep historical and active problems distinct.
3. Connect measurable goals and interventions to guidelines.
4. Preserve strengths, function, identity, and agency.

## Destigmatization message

A health condition is one part of a person’s life and does not erase achievement, relationships, purpose, or agency.

## Discussion questions

- Which facts support concern without establishing a modern diagnosis?
- Which entries belong in active problems, historical problems, or both?
- What additional evidence would change an evidence grade?
- How should an EHR distinguish synthetic and historical content?

## Evidence-grade summary

- **D:** Ray Charles Robinson was born in Albany, Georgia, on September 23, 1930; his mother moved him as an infant to Greenville, a small town in north Florida. Ray Charles died at his home in Beverly Hills, California, on June 10, 2004, of acute liver disease, according to his spokesman. Charles began to lose his sight at about age five and was blind by age seven; after he was declared legally blind, he was enrolled at the Florida School for the Deaf and Blind in St. Augustine, where he remained for nine years. In 1964 Charles was arrested for possession of heroin, and he later admitted that he had been addicted since the late 1940s. After a period of rehabilitation at a California sanitarium he stopped using drugs and received a five-year suspended sentence. Charles struggled with heroin addiction for nearly 20 years and quit "cold turkey" in 1965, after an arrest at the Boston airport. He later became reluctant to discuss his drug use, fearing it would taint how people viewed his work. In the year Charles began to lose his sight, his younger brother drowned. His mother died in 1945.
- **R:** The cause of his blindness is uncertain. It is described as probably congenital juvenile glaucoma, and glaucoma is often mentioned as the cause, but Charles said that nothing was ever diagnosed.
- **S:** Shifted identity, fictional care team, modern encounters, results, plans, and outcomes.
