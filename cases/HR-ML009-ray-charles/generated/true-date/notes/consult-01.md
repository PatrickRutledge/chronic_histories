## Assessment

1. **Blindness of both eyes since childhood, no light perception** (H54.0X55). Comfortable, quiet eyes with no active disease. The cause of the childhood blindness cannot be settled from today's examination; congenital or juvenile glaucoma is often suggested, but no diagnosis was ever made and none is recorded now. There is no inflammation, exposure, or sign of painful raised pressure. **S**
2. **Eye pressure in blind, painless eyes**. Lowering eye pressure protects sight that remains; with no light perception there is no sight to protect. In a painless eye, pressure checks and drops would add burden without benefit. Pressure matters again only if the eye becomes painful or red, when it may need treatment for comfort. **S**
3. **Surveillance**. A yearly comfort-focused eye examination is enough, with earlier review for symptoms. Optic nerve imaging, visual fields, gonioscopy, and corneal thickness would not change his care. **S**
4. **Vision rehabilitation**. He has long-established nonvisual skills and the technology he needs. Services remain open to him if his needs change. **S**

## Recommendations

1. No eye drops or other eye treatment now.
2. No routine eye pressure checks, optic nerve imaging, or visual fields.
3. Yearly ophthalmology review focused on comfort and the ocular surface.
4. Same-day review for eye pain, redness, discharge, swelling, light sensitivity, or headache around the eye, so that a painful blind eye can be treated promptly.
5. Primary care to ask about eye comfort at each visit.
6. Vision rehabilitation services (orientation and mobility, assistive technology) offered today; he does not want them now and can ask at any time.
