Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Recent-death rule. He died in 1981, so the rule does not apply. Even so, the historical claims are limited to what the fetched sources state.
  2. His reasons for declining amputation. The reliable sources agree that he was advised amputation and declined it (CLM-0006), but they put his reasons in different words: Hektoen International reports that he said it would be against his Rastafari faith, Biography.com that his Rastafarian beliefs prohibited it, and Wikipedia that he cited religious beliefs and that amputation would have hindered his performing career. CLM-0007 records all three, with certainty “probable,” without choosing between them. Other reasons found only on low-quality sites (for example, fear of appearing weak) are not claimed. The record makes no general statement about what the Rastafari faith permits or forbids, and it never presents his faith as the cause of his death. Whether amputation would have cured him cannot be known; Hektoen’s “might have been curative” is not repeated as a claim.
  3. The football injury. That he first thought the lesion was a football injury, and that the first physician agreed, is D (CLM-0004). Whether the injury caused it is R, disputed (CLM-0008): Wikipedia says it did not, and clinicians say only that many patients recall trauma. The modern episode uses the injury only as the reason the band was first called a bruise, and teaches that a bruise under the nail grows out while a melanoma band does not.
  4. Missed follow-up. Biography.com states that he did not attend routine follow-up appointments after the diagnosis. This single-source statement is not claimed, because it adds nothing to the teaching and could be read as blame for his outcome.
  5. Cannabis. No fetched reliable source about his illness documents his cannabis use in relation to his health. The encyclopedia mentions a conviction for possession of a small quantity and his support for legalization, but these are not health facts and are not used. No cannabis use is recorded, no substance-use disorder is coded, and the preventive visit records only a negative single-question screen for unhealthy drug use.
  6. Faith in the modern episode. His Rastafari faith appears as he and the sources describe it (CLM-0003, CLM-0007): in the modern episode he says that his faith, his music and his football matter to him in the decision, and asks to be understood before he decides.
