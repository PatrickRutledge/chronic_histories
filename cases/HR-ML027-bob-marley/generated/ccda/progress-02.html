A thin acral melanoma of the nail unit, diagnosed nine days after he first came in, because a band that had not grown out and pigment on the nail fold were biopsied rather than called a bruise. Acral melanoma is the most common melanoma in people with darker skin and is not caused by the sun. At 0.8 mm it is T1b: no scans or blood tests are needed, and a sentinel lymph node biopsy is offered because the chance of a positive node is about 5 to 10 percent. Dr. Mbeki-Rowe set out the two surgical choices: amputation of the toe at the interphalangeal joint, or digit-sparing excision of the whole nail unit down to the bone with a skin graft. For a melanoma this thin, digit-sparing excision is an accepted choice with similar survival in published series, at the cost of a higher chance of a local recurrence that would then need amputation. With his values in view, he chose digit-sparing excision with a sentinel node biopsy, and agreed in advance that amputation would be the next step if the margins were involved or the melanoma proved thicker.
