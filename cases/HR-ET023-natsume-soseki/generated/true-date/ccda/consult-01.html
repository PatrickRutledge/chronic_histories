Assessment

  • Gastric ulcer with recent bleeding (K25.4). The source is a 15 mm gastric ulcer at the angulus with a clean base, which carries a low risk of rebleeding and needs no endoscopic treatment. The likely causes are Helicobacter pylori and the ibuprofen. With a stable hemoglobin and normal pulse and blood pressure, he can be treated at home. The ulcer looks benign, but a gastric ulcer must be biopsied and followed to healing because an early gastric cancer can look like one. [S]
  • Helicobacter pylori infection (B96.81). The rapid urease test is positive. Clarithromycin resistance is common in Japan, so the regimen should follow the PCR result: vonoprazan, amoxicillin and clarithromycin for 7 days if the strain is susceptible, or vonoprazan 20 mg twice daily with amoxicillin 1 g three times daily for 14 days if it is resistant. Bismuth quadruple therapy, preferred elsewhere when susceptibility is unknown, is not available for this use in Japan. [S]
  • Iron-deficiency anemia (D50.0). Explained by the bleeding ulcer. In an adult man with iron-deficiency anemia a colonoscopy is usually advised too, but with an upper source found and no bowel symptoms it can wait to see whether the anemia corrects once the ulcer heals; if it does not, colonoscopy is needed. [S]
  • Gastric cancer risk. The open-type atrophy at endoscopy suggests long-standing infection; the mapping biopsies will stage atrophy and intestinal metaplasia. Eradication lowers the risk of gastric cancer but does not remove it when atrophy is extensive, so surveillance endoscopy is likely to be needed. [S]
