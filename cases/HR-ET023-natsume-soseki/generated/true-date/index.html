Teaching objective: Recognize a bleeding peptic ulcer in a 43-year-old novelist in Tokyo with epigastric pain, black stools and iron-deficiency anemia, and care for it as Japanese and international guidance advise: a Glasgow-Blatchford score and clear instructions on when to call 119; stopping NSAIDs; urgent upper endoscopy with biopsy of the ulcer margin to exclude gastric cancer, which is common in Japan, and mapping biopsies for Helicobacter pylori and atrophy; acid suppression with vonoprazan; H. pylori eradication chosen by local resistance and the patient's own susceptibility result (vonoprazan-amoxicillin dual therapy for a clarithromycin-resistant strain, because bismuth quadruple therapy is not available for this use in Japan); confirmation of eradication by urea breath test off acid suppression; repeat endoscopy to confirm healing of a gastric ulcer; oral iron with monitoring of the blood count (CMID anemia monograph); advice on alcohol and smoking; stress and depression screening; and endoscopic surveillance for gastric cancer after eradication because of atrophy and intestinal metaplasia. The modern episode is a teaching case, not a claim about the cause of his real illness.
