## Editorial resolutions

- **Recent-death rule.** He died in 1916, so the rule does not apply. Claims are limited to what the fetched sources state. Claims about his nervous distress are marked sensitive.
- **Name (judgment call).** Japanese order puts the family name first: Natsume is the family name, Sōseki a pen name (gō), and Kinnosuke his given name. The record uses the name he is known by, with identity.name_parts given “Sōseki”, family “Natsume” and family_first true, as other family-name-first records do; the notes call him Mr. Natsume. The macron in Sōseki is kept throughout. The alias “Natsume Kinnosuke” appears in no other record.
- **Birth date and calendar.** Japan used a lunisolar calendar until 1873. The National Diet Library gives his birth as Keiō 3, 1st month, 5th day, equal to February 9, 1867 (Gregorian), which Shinjuku City also gives; the record uses the Gregorian date and notes the original. His death in 1916 is in the Gregorian calendar.
- **The London illness (judgment call).** The documented fact is severe nervous distress in Britain that continued after his return (CLM-0005, D, probable). The labels used for it, shinkei suijaku (nervous breakdown, neurasthenia) and “nervous depression”, are recorded separately as **R** (CLM-0006), because they are period terms or translations, not diagnoses. No modern psychiatric diagnosis is made; the modern episode screens for stress and depression (PHQ-9 7 and GAD-7 8 at intake, 3 and 4 at the end) and records the history as past. A 2023 *Brain and Nerve* abstract mentioning an attempted overdose of sleeping pills was found while searching; it is a single uncorroborated statement on a sensitive matter, outside the brief, and was left out. No safety assessment is required (no F-code problem, PHQ-9 below 10, item 9 scored 0).
- **The ulcer and Helicobacter pylori.** His gastric ulcer is documented (CLM-0008, D), so the modern diagnosis (K25.4) is linked to it with placement both_with_link. Helicobacter pylori was not known in his lifetime; its role in his illness is unknown, and the modern infection, its resistance and its treatment are synthetic. Diabetes in his later years (CLM-0011) is history only; the prediabetes found at the modern preventive visit is a synthetic finding and is not linked to that claim.
- **Presenting problem.** The epigastric pain with black stools and anemia at intake is its own problem (R10.13), resolved at the endoscopy three days later, when K25.4 and B96.81 are recorded. The intake documents show only what was known then. Iron-deficiency anemia is diagnosed at intake from the blood tests.
- **Index age and dates.** The index visit on May 18, 2026 maps to May 18, 1910, when he was 43 and had had stomach illness since about May 1910, before the Nagayo hospital stay. The synthetic date of birth is February 9, 1983 (shift 116 years). The visits on May 21, June 4, June 11 and June 16, 2026 map to dates before his admission on June 18, 1910, and the last visit, August 3, 2026, maps to August 3, 1910, between his discharge on July 31 and his departure for Shuzenji on August 6; no visit falls during the hospital stay or the Shuzenji period. - **Regimen choice (judgment call).** Clarithromycin resistance is common in Japan, so the eradication regimen follows the patient’s own biopsy PCR result. With a resistance mutation, the record uses vonoprazan 20 mg twice daily with amoxicillin 1 g three times daily for 14 days, an ACG 2024 regimen and an option in Japanese practice. Bismuth quadruple therapy, preferred by Maastricht VI and ACG when susceptibility is unknown, is described as not available for this use in Japan; this rests on general knowledge of Japanese practice rather than a fetched source and should be checked. Vonoprazan 20 mg daily for eight weeks heals the gastric ulcer (JSGE 2026), and the urea breath test is timed at least four weeks after the antibiotics and two weeks after vonoprazan.
