Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Leroy Robert Page was born in Mobile, Alabama, and was delivered at home by a midwife; his mother was a washerwoman and his father a gardener. The family later changed the spelling of the name to Paige. His accepted date of birth is July 7, 1906. [D]
  • His age was disputed for most of his career. He told stories that kept it uncertain, among them that his birth certificate had been kept in a Bible that the family goat ate; later he did not argue with the evidence for July 7, 1906. One historian suggests he was probably born earlier, with the date altered to make him more marketable. [R]
  • In 1918, at twelve, he was convicted of shoplifting and sentenced to five years in the Alabama Reform School for Juvenile Negro Law-Breakers, a segregated reform school; he was released at the end of 1923 and began his professional career soon after. [D]
  • Because organized baseball had excluded Black players since 1889, he spent his prime in the Negro leagues and on barnstorming tours from 1927 to 1947, pitching for clubs in Chattanooga, Birmingham, Baltimore, Cleveland, Pittsburgh and Kansas City. Black players on tour were barred from the hotels and restaurants used by white people, and he refused to play in towns where he could not get a room or a meal. [D]
  • In the late 1930s he developed arm trouble for the first time; after an arm injury in 1938 the Kansas City Monarchs signed him, and his arm recovered the following year. [D]
  • In July 1948, at 42, he made his major league debut for the Cleveland Indians, the oldest rookie in the major leagues, and helped them win the 1948 World Series. He pitched his last major league game for the Kansas City Athletics in 1965 at 59, and in 1968 joined the Atlanta Braves as a coach so that he could qualify for a major league pension. [D]
  • In 1971 he was the first Negro leagues player elected to the National Baseball Hall of Fame, as the first choice of its Committee on Negro Baseball Leagues. [D]
  • He married Janet Howard in 1934. He later married Lahoma Brown, and together they raised seven children in Kansas City, where he lived for most of his adult life. [D]
