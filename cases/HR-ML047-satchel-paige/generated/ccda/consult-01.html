Following the 2023 ATS statement, the results were interpreted with the race-neutral GLI Global equations: FEV1 61% of predicted, GOLD grade 2 (moderate). Race-specific equations for African American men predict lower normal values and would have put his FEV1 near 70%, making his disease look milder than it is. The normal alpha-1 antitrypsin excludes deficiency. [S]
  • Maintenance treatment (J44.9). With a CAT of 18, mMRC 2 and no exacerbations in the past year he is in GOLD group B, for which GOLD 2025 recommends a LAMA with a LABA in one inhaler. An inhaled corticosteroid is not indicated: his eosinophils are 0.08 x 10^3/uL and he has had no exacerbations, so it would add the risk of pneumonia without benefit. A once-daily dry powder inhaler suits him; his inspiratory effort is strong, and he learned the technique on a placebo device. Treatment should be reviewed for symptoms and exacerbations at each visit; if exacerbations recur despite good technique, the eosinophil count guides the next step. [S]
  • Oxygen (J44.9). Pulse oximetry can overestimate arterial saturation in people with darker skin, so a room-air arterial blood gas was done. His PaO2 of 70 mm Hg is well above the long-term oxygen threshold of 55 mm Hg (60 with cor pulmonale or polycythemia), and his lowest walking saturation was 90 percent. Long-term oxygen did not help people with moderate desaturation in the LOTT trial, and he does not have the severe exertional hypoxemia for which the ATS suggests ambulatory oxygen. He needs no oxygen now; this will be reassessed if his saturations fall. [S]
