## Historical evidence

1. Tye L. “Satchel Paige,” SABR Biography Project, Society for American Baseball Research, drawn from his book *Satchel: The Life and Times of an American Legend*. “Satchel entered the world as Leroy Robert Page”; his mother Lula “was a washerwoman”; “Two weeks before his 12th birthday Satchel was sentenced to the Alabama Reform School for Juvenile Negro Law-Breakers”; his clubs in Chattanooga, Birmingham, Baltimore, Cleveland, Pittsburgh and Kansas City; his last major league game at “59 years, two months and eight days” in 1965; with his wife Lahoma he “raised seven children”; his last public appearance on June 5, 1982, with “an oxygen tube strapped to his face”; on June 8 “his heart gave out for good in the ambulance and he was pronounced dead at 1:15 p.m. at Truman Medical Center.” https://sabr.org/bioproj/person/satchel-paige/
2. Durso J. “Satchel Paige Is Dead at 75,” *The New York Times*, June 9, 1982, as reproduced by the Negro League Baseball Players Association. He “died yesterday at his home in Kansas City, Mo., after a long siege of heart trouble and emphysema”; “He was believed to be 75 years old”; the story that his birth certificate “had been placed between the pages of a Bible that was eaten by the family's goat,” and that “later he did not argue with evidence that he had been born on July 7, 1906”; his last public appearance, in a wheelchair, at a Kansas City park named for him. https://www.nlbpa.com/news/june-8-1982
3. The State Historical Society of Missouri. “Satchel Paige,” Historic Missourians. Born July 7, 1906, in Mobile; sentenced in 1918, at twelve, to five years in the Alabama Reform School and paroled at the end of 1923; “After baseball became segregated in 1889, blacks were prohibited from playing in the major leagues”; barred from hotels and restaurants on tour, he “refused to play in towns where he could not get a hotel room or a meal”; the 1938 arm injury and the Kansas City Monarchs; the oldest rookie at 42; the 1948 World Series; the Kansas City Athletics in 1965; the Atlanta Braves in 1968, to qualify for a pension; first Negro leagues player elected to the Hall of Fame in 1971; married Janet Howard on October 26, 1934, and later Lahoma Brown, with seven children; “died of a heart attack on June 8, 1982, in Kansas City”; buried in Forest Hill Memorial Park Cemetery. https://historicmissourians.shsmo.org/satchel-paige/
4. National Baseball Hall of Fame and Museum. “Satchel Paige,” Hall of Famers. Born July 7, 1906, in Mobile; “In the late 1930s, Paige developed arm problems for the first time”; “At the age of 42, Paige made his American League debut”; elected in 1971 “as the first electee of the Committee on Negro Baseball Leagues.” https://baseballhall.org/hall-of-famers/paige-satchel
5. Powell L. “Satchel Paige,” Encyclopedia of Alabama. “Officially, Paige reported he was born on July 7, 1906, in Mobile”; “He was probably born earlier than that, with the date altered to make him more marketable on the baseball field”; the family's change of spelling; “He suffered from emphysema in his later years and died on June 8, 1982, at his home in Kansas City.” https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/satchel-paige/
6. HISTMED Master Roster v3. Internal project source; not treated as independent corroboration.

## Editorial resolutions

- **Sources.** The case relies on Larry Tye's SABR biography (drawn from his book, which was not read directly), the *New York Times* obituary by Joseph Durso as reproduced by the Negro League Baseball Players Association, the State Historical Society of Missouri, the National Baseball Hall of Fame and the Encyclopedia of Alabama. The Negro Leagues Baseball Museum's page could not be found (not found at the address tried), and the Hall of Fame's archive catalog record of a 1981 interview, which a search summary said mentions smoking, could not be opened; neither is used.
- **Name.** The display name is “Satchel Paige”; the alias “Leroy Robert Paige” appears in no other record (his birth name was spelled Page, as the claims record). The first Black player in the modern major leagues has his own record and is not named here; the State Historical Society page that mentions him is cited only for other facts. No other figure with a record in the dataset is named; “Cleveland” and “Kansas City” appear only as places and team names.
- **Age (CLM-0001, D; CLM-0002, R, disputed).** His age was disputed for decades. The Hall of Fame, the State Historical Society of Missouri and SABR give July 7, 1906, which he later accepted; the Encyclopedia of Alabama suggests he was probably born earlier. The record uses July 7, 1906, records the dispute as an R claim and a disputed alternative birth date, and notes that an earlier birth would make him older at the index date.
- **Index age and dates.** The index visit on July 14, 2026 maps to July 14, 1979, when he was 73 by the accepted birth date and living in Kansas City, in the later years when he had emphysema. The synthetic date of birth is July 7, 1953 (shift 147 years). The visits on July 28, September 8, September 22, October 6 and October 27, 2026, and the CT, rehabilitation and ultrasound dates, map to the same days of 1979, all at 73 and before his death in 1982. Past medical history includes the arm trouble of 1938 and the emphysema and heart trouble of his later years, whose date is not recorded; his last public appearance and his death are on the timeline only.
- **Emphysema and heart trouble (CLM-0009, D).** The *New York Times* and the Encyclopedia of Alabama document emphysema and heart trouble in his later years; when they began, how they were diagnosed and how they were treated are not recorded in the sources read, so the timeline event and past medical history carry no date. The modern COPD diagnosis is linked to this claim. The oxygen tube at his last appearance (CLM-0010) is after the index date and is not used in the intake documents; the modern episode finds that he does not need oxygen yet, which is not a claim about his real needs in 1979. The modern episode adds no heart disease beyond cardiovascular risk factors; the hyperlipidemia and coronary calcification are synthetic.
- **Death (CLM-0011, D).** The State Historical Society of Missouri gives a heart attack as the cause. The place differs between sources: at home (the *New York Times*, the Encyclopedia of Alabama) or in the ambulance and pronounced dead at Truman Medical Center (SABR). Both are recorded; the details of his last morning in the SABR account, which involve his family, are not used.
- **Smoking (PRB-003, SOC-003).** Whether he smoked is not documented in the sources read. As the brief allows, the modern episode gives him a synthetic history of about 30 pack-years, stopped eleven years before the index visit, labeled synthetic in the problem, the social history and here. It makes him eligible for lung cancer screening and is not a claim about his real habits.
- **Equity and dignity.** His exclusion from the major leagues until 42, the segregated reform school of his boyhood, the segregation of Black players on tour, and his late coaching job to qualify for a pension are recorded plainly as documented history (CLM-0003, CLM-0004, CLM-0006), without stereotype. The modern episode applies equity in practice: race-neutral GLI Global spirometry equations (ATS 2023), with the note that race-specific equations would have made his impairment look milder; the race-free CKD-EPI 2021 eGFR; an arterial blood gas because pulse oximetry can overestimate saturation in darker skin (Sjoding 2020); results shown and explained to him at his request; attention to inhaler cost and transport to rehabilitation.
- **Relatives.** His marriages and seven children are recorded only as documented history (CLM-0008). His wife appears in the modern social history only as “his wife”; no relative is named in the modern episode or given any statement, decision or health fact there. The advance care planning agent is unnamed and no relationship is given. No family history is recorded, because the sources read give no health facts about his parents or siblings.
- **Roster leads.** The roster's emphysema and heart disease are documented (CLM-0009). No diagnosis of heart failure or coronary disease is made in the modern episode; NT-proBNP and the ECG are normal.
- **Clinical judgment calls.** (1) Spirometry at a pulmonology consultation two weeks after intake, with a reliever and a statin started at intake. (2) GOLD group B: umeclidinium-vilanterol once daily, a dry powder inhaler chosen for his strong inspiratory effort; no inhaled corticosteroid with eosinophils of 0.08 x 10^3/uL. (3) An arterial blood gas at a saturation of 94 percent, above the CMID threshold of 92 percent, because of pulse-oximetry bias; no long-term or ambulatory oxygen (PaO2 70 mm Hg; lowest walking saturation 90 percent; LOTT and ATS 2020). (4) Lung cancer screening at 73 with a synthetic 30 pack-years, quit eleven years ago (USPSTF 2021). (5) Atorvastatin 20 mg by shared decision for an estimated 10-year risk of about 18 percent with LDL 148 mg/dL, supported later by coronary calcification on the screening CT. (6) After one moderate exacerbation: prednisone 40 mg for five days and doxycycline for five days for purulent sputum; treated at home; no escalation to an inhaled corticosteroid, azithromycin or roflumilast after a single event; still group B. (7) RSV vaccine at 73 because COPD raises his risk; PCV20 and Tdap at intake; influenza, COVID-19 and zoster at the wellness visit. (8) One-time aortic ultrasound as a man of 65 to 75 who has smoked.
- **Synthetic clinical picture.** Everything in the modern episode is synthetic: every result, the spirometry, blood gas, walk tests, CT, X-ray, ECGs and ultrasound, the scores (CAT, mMRC, PHQ-9, PHQ-2, Mini-Cog, AUDIT-C, Timed Up and Go), the medicines, the exacerbation and the outcomes. Height (191 cm) and weights are synthetic; his real height is not taken from a source.
- **Wellness visit.** A subsequent annual wellness visit at 73 (HCPCS G0439 with CPT 99497 in plain text), two weeks after the exacerbation. High-dose influenza, RSV, COVID-19 and the first recombinant zoster vaccines given; aortic ultrasound ordered (normal at 2.4 cm a fortnight later); hepatitis C and B screening (negative); PSA not indicated at 73; osteoporosis screening not indicated after one short steroid course; advance care planning recorded as his own general wishes.
- **Sick visit.** A moderate COPD exacerbation after a viral cold, chosen because it tests the plan: he used his action plan to call the same day, the chest X-ray excluded pneumonia, he was treated at home, and the decision not to escalate maintenance therapy afterwards follows GOLD 2025. It is not a historical claim.
- **Focused visits.** The three chronic-care follow-ups (two weeks with the consultation, eight weeks, fifteen weeks) are documented as focused visits on the CMID COPD and hyperlipidemia monographs, GOLD 2025, the 2023 ATS race-neutral statement, the 2023 ATS rehabilitation guideline, the 2020 ATS oxygen guideline with LOTT, Sjoding 2020, the USPSTF lung cancer and statin recommendations and the CDC schedule. One deviation is recorded instead of an invented result: the ECG that the CMID protocol calls for after an exacerbation was not done at the sick visit; no harm was evident (no chest pain or palpitations, and the ECG at fifteen weeks was unchanged), and the corrective action was the ECG at fifteen weeks and adding it to the clinic's exacerbation visit template. Synthetic findings introduced: centrilobular emphysema, a benign 4 mm nodule and coronary calcification on the screening CT, and the exacerbation.
- **Consultation.** The pulmonology referral and the consultation by Marcus Lindqvist-Bello, MD (a fictional consultant), on the day of the two-week visit, are **S**. The consultant answers the five referral questions in line with what the record shows afterwards: COPD, GOLD grade 2 by race-neutral equations; umeclidinium-vilanterol without an inhaled corticosteroid; a blood gas and no oxygen; pulmonary rehabilitation and a screening CT; and the action plan, vaccines and yearly spirometry.
