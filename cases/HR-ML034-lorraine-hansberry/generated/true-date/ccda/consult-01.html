Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Lorraine Vivian Hansberry was born in Chicago on May 19, 1930, the youngest of four children of Carl Augustus Hansberry, a successful real estate broker who founded one of the city's first Black banks, and Nannie Louise (Perry) Hansberry, a schoolteacher.   • She enrolled at the University of Wisconsin in 1948, left in 1950 for New York, and wrote for the newspaper Freedom. In June 1953 she married Robert Nemiroff. Sources differ on when they divorced (1962 or 1964); they continued to work together. [D]
  • A Raisin in the Sun opened at the Ethel Barrymore Theatre on Broadway on March 11, 1959, the first play by a Black woman to be produced on Broadway. It won the New York Drama Critics' Circle Award; she was the first Black playwright and, at 29, the youngest American to win it. In 1962 she moved to Croton-on-Hudson, New York. [D]

Assessment

  • Adenocarcinoma of the head of the pancreas, borderline resectable (C25.0). Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma of the head of the pancreas invading the duodenum, which explains the bleeding, the anemia, the obstructed bile and pancreatic ducts, the pain and the new diabetes. By NCCN criteria it is borderline resectable, because the tumor contacts more than half the circumference of the superior mesenteric vein, although the vein can be reconstructed and no artery is involved; there is no distant spread on CT. For borderline resectable disease, chemotherapy first is preferred over going straight to surgery: it treats cancer that may already have spread unseen, can shrink the tumor away from the vein, and shows whether the cancer is controlled before a major operation. She is young and fit, with normal kidney function and bilirubin now normal, so modified FOLFIRINOX is the regimen of choice. The aim is cure, and surgery after chemotherapy offers the best chance of it; she was also told honestly that pancreatic cancer often returns even after successful surgery. [S]
  • Diabetes due to the pancreatic cancer (E08.9). New diabetes with weight loss at her age is caused by the cancer (type 3c), not type 2. It may worsen after removal of the head of the pancreas, and insulin is the treatment if glucose-lowering is needed; dexamethasone should be limited. [S]
  • Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (K86.81). Pale floating stools and weight loss with a blocked pancreatic duct: start enzyme replacement now on clinical grounds, without waiting for a stool test. [S]
  • Cancer-related pain (G89.3). Moderate tumor pain; low-dose opioid with a bowel regimen first, and EUS-guided celiac plexus neurolysis if it becomes severe. [S]
