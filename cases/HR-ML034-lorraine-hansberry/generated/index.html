Teaching objective: Recognize pancreatic cancer in a young Black woman whose epigastric and back pain, weight loss, new diabetes and iron deficiency anemia could be put down to an ulcer or to stress, and care for it on the NCCN pancreatic adenocarcinoma guideline and the ASCO guidelines on potentially curable pancreatic cancer, palliative care and supportive care: pancreas-protocol CT and endoscopy at once; tissue by endoscopic ultrasound and biliary drainage before CA 19-9 is read; resectability staging and a multidisciplinary tumor board; germline genetic testing for every patient with pancreatic cancer and molecular profiling of the tumor; modified FOLFIRINOX before surgery for borderline resectable disease, with dose and support matched to her fitness; pancreaticoduodenectomy and chemotherapy after it; palliative care from the day of diagnosis; pain, nutrition and pancreatic enzyme replacement; diabetes caused by the pancreatic disease; venous thromboembolism risk; fertility and contraception; plain disclosure of the diagnosis and its prognosis; and a plan built around her wish to keep writing. Black Americans with pancreatic cancer are less often offered surgery and have worse survival, so each step is checked for delay.
