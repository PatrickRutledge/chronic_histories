Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Index age. The modern episode presents Curie at 66, her age when she fell ill in May 1934. The index visit is May 11, 2026; the true-date frame places all four visits in May and June 1934, before her death on July 4, 1934. Past medical history and family history include only events before May 1934.
  2. Modern diagnosis. The contemporary diagnosis of aplastic pernicious anemia is documented (D). The synthetic episode makes it severe aplastic anemia confirmed by a hypocellular marrow, coded ICD-10-CM D61.2 (aplastic anemia due to other external agents). The radiation cause is an interpretation (R, probable) and the occupational exposure is coded separately (Z57.1). The marrow findings, counts, and treatment are S. The specialist chooses standard immunosuppressive therapy because an immune mechanism cannot be excluded.
  3. Cataracts. The 1920 double cataract and four operations from 1923 to 1930 are D; the radiation interpretation is R. Because the operations precede the index age, the modern eye problem is posterior capsule opacification after earlier surgery (H26.491), treated with YAG laser capsulotomy. Modern lens implants did not exist in the 1920s; the modern eye history is synthetic.
  4. Finger burns. The documented burns are linked to a synthetic chronic radiodermatitis problem (L58.1).
  5. Nationality. Recorded as “Polish / French,” matching the roster.
  6. Not used. The Musée Curie site was not reviewed. The name of the sanatorium (Sancellemoz, Passy) appears in secondary summaries that were not verified from a logged source; the Nobel biography says only that she died in Savoy. Her surgeon’s name and the dates of the individual cataract operations were not found. Her father’s death and her daughter Irène’s later leukemia were not used. Her recollection of profound low mood after her mother’s death was not recorded as a diagnosis.
  7. Focused visits. The three follow-ups (the hematology consultation and the three- and six-week primary care reviews) are documented as focused visits on the BSH aplastic anaemia guideline, with the CMID anemia monograph (symptom assessment, restrictive transfusion threshold) and cataract monograph (comprehensive eye examination and functional vision assessment) and the AAO cataract Preferred Practice Pattern for the capsulotomy. No visit was added, and nothing anticipates her death in the true frame. Synthetic findings introduced: bruises and lower-leg petechiae, a flow murmur, the hazy and then clear right red reflex, completion of the four-day antithymocyte globulin course, and full adherence. Protocol deviations recorded instead of invented results: no pre-treatment ALT before eltrombopag; no pre-treatment viral serology in the record; no cyclosporine trough level in the record at either review, with the creatinine rise from 0.78 to 1.02 mg/dL prompting a dose review; weekly blood counts missed between visits, and kidney function, potassium, and ALT checked at three weeks rather than every two; no antibacterial or antifungal prophylaxis while neutrophils were at or below 0.5, which the guideline leaves to local policy; and no post-laser pressure reading after the capsulotomy. Later results showed no harm from any of them by the six-week visit.
  8. Consultation. The hematology referral and consultation are synthetic and written for this case. Dr. Brenner's examination repeats the focused note of the consultation day, and his recommendations agree with the record: the four-day horse antithymocyte globulin course, cyclosporine, eltrombopag, and acyclovir from that day; irradiated blood products; inactivated vaccines only; and weekly counts with kidney function, potassium, and ALT every two weeks. Where the focused visits record deviations, the consultant recommends the step and the later notes show it was late or never done: ALT and viral serology with the next blood draw (ALT first appears three weeks later; no serology ever appears), a first cyclosporine trough within the first weeks (none appears), and weekly counts through the hematology day unit (none appear between visits). No result is stated for any of them. Judgment calls: the consultant records that treatment began without ALT or viral serology because neutrophils were 0.4 and platelets 15; antibacterial and antifungal prophylaxis are not started and are left to local policy, matching the recorded gap; and platelet support is described only as following the hematology unit's protocol during the antithymocyte globulin course and given for bleeding afterward, so no platelet transfusion or threshold is invented. Nothing in the consultation speaks of prognosis or anticipates her death in the true frame.
  9. Curie died in 1934, so the recent-death review rule does not apply. Ordinary clinical, provenance, and safe-messaging reviews remain required.
