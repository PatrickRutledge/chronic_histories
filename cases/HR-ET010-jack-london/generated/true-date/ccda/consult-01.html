Assessment

  • Chronic kidney disease G3b A2 (N18.32). Chronic kidney disease, stage G3b A2 by KDIGO 1913: an eGFR of 42 to 43 with albumin 240 mg/g, and a creatinine already 1.7 mg/dL fourteen months ago, so it has lasted more than three months; high risk on the KDIGO heat map. Normal-sized echogenic kidneys, a bland sediment without blood, moderate rather than heavy albuminuria and long-standing untreated hypertension point to hypertensive kidney disease, with two years of daily naproxen adding harm. There are no stones or obstruction, and no gout attacks; the uric acid of 8.4 mg/dL does not need treatment without gout. Alcohol raises blood pressure but is not itself a usual cause of this picture. Nothing suggests a glomerulonephritis or another disease that needs a biopsy now; hepatitis B, hepatitis C and HIV serology are drawn today, and a biopsy would be reconsidered for blood in the urine, rising albumin or a fall in eGFR without explanation. A cystatin C estimate is not needed now, since no decision hinges on it. 25-hydroxyvitamin D was not drawn and should be. [S]
  • Blood pressure (I12.9). Aim for a systolic below 120 by standardized office measurement if he tolerates it, as KDIGO 1910 advises, and at least below 130/80 as the 1914 AHA/ACC guideline does. Losartan should go to the maximum tolerated dose, 100 mg, because it lowers albuminuria as well as pressure; potassium 4.9 and a 3 percent rise in creatinine are acceptable. Check potassium and creatinine two to four weeks after each change; accept a creatinine rise of up to 30 percent. Less than 2 g of sodium a day; amlodipine would be the next step. [S]
  • SGLT2 inhibitor (N18.32). He meets the KDIGO 1913 recommendation for an SGLT2 inhibitor (eGFR 20 or more with albumin 200 mg/g or more), which slows kidney decline whether or not there is diabetes. But heavy drinking, missed meals and vomiting raise the risk of ketoacidosis on these drugs, even without diabetes. I advise waiting until he has been abstinent for some weeks and is eating regularly, then dapagliflozin 10 mg daily with written sick-day rules, and potassium and creatinine two to four weeks after starting, expecting an early dip in eGFR. [S]
  • Medicine for alcohol use disorder (F10.20). Naltrexone is the better choice. It is cleared mainly by the liver, needs no dose change at his kidney function, and his liver tests are falling and under three times normal; he takes no opioids. Acamprosate is cleared by the kidneys: at a creatinine clearance of 30 to 50 mL/min the dose must be cut to 333 mg three times a day and below 30 it should not be used, and with his clearance near 58 and likely to change, and a three-times-a-day schedule he says he would not keep, it is a second choice. The outpatient chlordiazepoxide taper was reasonable for his kidneys, since it is cleared by the liver, and safe for a liver without cirrhosis; in a man with cirrhosis or worse liver function a shorter-acting benzodiazepine would be preferred. Disulfiram is not advised. [S]
  • Pain relief (Z79.1). No naproxen, ibuprofen, high-dose aspirin or other oral anti-inflammatory medicines, which cut kidney blood flow and caused part of this damage. Acetaminophen is the first choice, kept to 2,000 mg a day after heavy drinking. A short course of a topical anti-inflammatory gel on a small area is acceptable, because blood levels are low. No opioids or tramadol: naltrexone blocks opioids, morphine's active products build up when the kidneys are weak, and opioids add risk in early recovery. Any new medicine should be checked for kidney dosing, and iodinated contrast used only with precautions. [S]
