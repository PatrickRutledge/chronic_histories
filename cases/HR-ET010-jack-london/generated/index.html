Teaching objective: Alcohol use disorder with chronic kidney disease and hypertension in a working writer: a 39-year-old novelist who writes a thousand words every morning on his ranch comes in tired, with swollen ankles, and is found to have a blood pressure of 158/98, an eGFR of 43 and albumin in the urine, and to drink heavily. The episode covers the AUDIT, the DSM-5-TR criteria and PEth; liver tests, FIB-4 and elastography; outpatient withdrawal management with a withdrawal-risk score and thiamine; medicine for alcohol use disorder chosen with kidney function in mind (naltrexone, with acamprosate needing a lower dose at his creatinine clearance and avoided below 30 mL/min); CKD staging by KDIGO 2024 with eGFR and urine albumin, the cause (hypertension and two years of daily naproxen, without stones or gout), ultrasound, the kidney failure risk equation and mineral tests; blood pressure under KDIGO 2021 and the 2025 AHA/ACC guideline with an angiotensin receptor blocker at the maximum dose and potassium and creatinine checks; an SGLT2 inhibitor once he is abstinent, because heavy drinking raises the risk of ketoacidosis; analgesic and opioid safety, with no NSAIDs, a lower acetaminophen limit and naltrexone's opioid blockade; the safety assessment and lethal-means counseling; tobacco; vaccinations in kidney and liver disease; and a plan that protects his morning writing hours.
