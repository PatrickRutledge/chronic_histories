Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Fannie Lou Hamer was born on October 6, 1917, in Montgomery County, Mississippi, the twentieth child of Lou Ella and James Lee Townsend, sharecroppers. She joined her family in the cotton fields at six and left school after the sixth grade, at 12, to work on the cotton plantations of the Delta. [D]
  • She worked for eighteen years as a sharecropper and timekeeper on a Delta plantation, made timekeeper because she could read and write. In 1964 she gave her home as Ruleville, in Sunflower County, Mississippi. [D]
  • In 1961, hospitalized for abdominal pain, she was found to have a uterine tumor; during the operation to remove it, a white surgeon performed a hysterectomy without her knowledge or informed consent. She later called this a "Mississippi appendectomy," a name for the sterilizations done without consent to Black women in the South. [D]
  • On August 31, 1962, she tried to register to vote in Indianola; when she refused to withdraw her application, the plantation owner ordered her off his land and she had to leave that same night. [D]
  • On June 9, 1963, returning from a voter-registration workshop, she was arrested with other activists in Winona, Mississippi, and in the jail two Black prisoners were ordered by officers to beat her with a blackjack, as she testified the next year. [D]
  • The beating left her with lifelong injuries to her eyes, legs and kidneys: permanent kidney damage, and a blood clot in her left eye with loss of vision in one eye. [D]
  • On August 22, 1964, she testified before the Credentials Committee of the Democratic National Convention in Atlantic City about her eviction and the Winona beating. [D]

Assessment

  • Chronic kidney disease G2 A2 (N18.2). The small, scarred right kidney is a structural abnormality consistent with her old injury and is in itself chronic kidney disease; with an eGFR of 73 and albuminuria confirmed on two samples, she is G2 A2. Diabetes and high blood pressure probably add to the albumin loss. Nothing on ultrasound suggests blockage, stones or another cause, and no further imaging is needed. [S]
  • Hypertension with chronic kidney disease (I12.9). Stage 2 hypertension is confirmed by home readings. With albuminuria an ACE inhibitor is the foundation, and at this level two medicines together are recommended; a single combined capsule keeps cost and pill count down. Target below 130/80. [S]
  • Type 2 diabetes with chronic kidney disease (E11.22). An SGLT2 inhibitor protects the kidneys and heart at her eGFR and albuminuria, independent of its glucose effect. Metformin is safe at this eGFR at full dose. Finerenone is for albuminuria that persists despite an ACE inhibitor at the dose she tolerates and an SGLT2 inhibitor, and can wait. [S]
