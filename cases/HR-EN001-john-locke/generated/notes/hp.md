- From about 1667 he was a close friend of the physician Thomas Sydenham, accompanied him on visits to patients, helped him with his writings, and was praised by him; Sydenham embraced careful observation of disease. **D**
- His biographer H. R. Fox Bourne wrote that about 1670 Locke began to have a cough that seriously troubled him through the remainder of his life, and that an undated fragment in his hand from those years, titled 'Tussis', was the start of a paper on coughs. His health was already weak by the early 1670s; a letter from Sydenham, probably at the end of 1674, advised him to go to bed early and be very temperate and cautious, and from late 1675 to 1677 he lived in France, mostly at Montpellier, then much visited by invalids. **D**
- Lady Masham later wrote that although his travels in France had done his health some good, after his return he was still less able than before to stay long in London without retreating to a neighbouring village 'to recover the prejudice his health received from the smoke of that vast quantity of sea-coal which is burnt in this great city'. From then on he left London as often as he could, and always in the winter months. **D**
- Among his medical papers, an account of consumption that Fox Bourne describes as drawn from Sydenham's observation and confirmed by Locke attributes the frequency of consumptions in London to 'a perpetual mist' mixed with the fumes of the city's trades, 'but especially the sulphur and fumes of sea coals, with which the air is repleted; and these being sucked into our lungs ... give an occasion to a cough'. **D**
- He left England in the autumn of 1683, when he was under suspicion for his association with Shaftesbury, lived in Holland, and landed at Greenwich on 12 February 1688/9 (Julian). He soon declined a diplomatic mission on the ground of insufficient health, and wrote in April 1689 that he hoped to breathe more easily and be less troubled by his cough. **D**
- He found the smoke of London unfavourable to his health, and from the spring of 1691 lived at Oates, in the parish of High Laver, Essex, the home of Sir Francis and Lady Masham (Damaris Cudworth). **D**
- On 29 February 1691/2 (Julian; 10 March 1692, Gregorian) he wrote to Philipp van Limborch that since visiting Robert Boyle on his deathbed in December, 'my health has kept me in the country, for I found my lungs could not bear the smoke of the city'. **D**
