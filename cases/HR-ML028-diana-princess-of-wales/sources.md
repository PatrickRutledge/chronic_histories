## Editorial resolutions

- **Recent-death rule.** She died in 1997, so the rule applies. Every health claim is her own public statement in the 1995 *Panorama* interview, taken from the published transcript and a contemporary wire report; no biography, medical record, account by anyone else, or speculation is used. Claims about bulimia, depression, and self-injury (CLM-0005 to CLM-0008) are marked sensitive and kept short. No sick visit is added. Her death is recorded once (CLM-0010) in one plain sentence, without detail.
- **The interview as a source.** In May 2021 an inquiry commissioned by the BBC found that the interview had been obtained by deceit, and the BBC accepted the findings (CLM-0009). The finding concerns how the interview was obtained; the record relies only on the words she spoke in it, as broadcast and transcribed. The BBC’s own pages could not be fetched, so the transcript is the FRONTLINE copy and the broadcast date (20 November 1995) comes from the UPI report dated that day.
- **Living people.** Her former husband appears only by title in the marriage and divorce claims (CLM-0002, CLM-0004); her children are not named; her words about her marriage are recorded in CLM-0006 as her own and are not used in the modern episode. No decision, statement, or health fact is attributed to any living person. The interviewer and the family member named in the inquiry reports are not named in the record. No family history is recorded.
- **Name.** The display name is “Diana, Princess of Wales.” For C-CDA and FHIR the coded name is given “Diana,” family “Spencer,” her birth surname from the official biography (CLM-0001). The notes call her “Ms. Spencer.” The aliases “Lady Diana Spencer” and “Diana Frances Spencer” appear in no other record; “Diana” alone was not used as an alias.
- **Index age and dates.** The index visit on February 9, 2026 maps to February 9, 1990, when she was 28, within the years of bulimia she later described (a little more than two or three years, during her marriage, and over by the time of the interview). The synthetic date of birth is July 1, 1997 (shift 36 years); she turns 29 before the last visit. The visits on February 16, March 16, April 13, May 11, and July 13, 2026 map to the same days of 1990. Because she spoke publicly only in 1995, the interview claims are linked to the 1995 timeline event and do not appear in the intake documents; the intake shows only her birth, marriage, and charity work. No past medical history is recorded, because no fetched source dates any illness before the index date.
- **Diagnoses.** Bulimia nervosa, severe (F50.23), and major depressive disorder, recurrent, moderate (F33.1), are synthetic modern diagnoses linked to her own descriptions of bulimia and of post-natal depression; the earlier episode of depression after childbirth in the modern history stands for the one she described. The frequency of about ten episodes a week, the duration of about three years, the findings, and every result are synthetic; the frequency is not taken from her words. Hypokalemia (E87.6) is a presenting laboratory finding resolved at the five-week visit. Bulimia nervosa stays coded as severe at the end of treatment, with early remission described in the note, because six weeks without symptoms is not yet sustained remission.
- **Self-injury.** Her own account of hurting her arms and legs is recorded once as history (CLM-0008, sensitive). The modern safety assessment refers to past self-injury only as part of that history and records no current self-injury; the intake examination notes no scars of recent self-injury.
- **Modern setting.** The modern episode is set in a United States clinic (St. Posthumous Health Center), so the safety plan uses the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline and the preventive visit follows the USPSTF and CDC. Her work for charities in the modern social history is anchored to her documented charity work (CLM-0003); she lives alone and is not in a relationship, so no real person has a role.
- **Preventive visit.** A periodic adult preventive visit at 28 (CPT 99395 and 99497 in plain text): cervical cytology current (every three years to 29), hepatitis C screening ordered, folic acid counseled (she chose an over-the-counter tablet, which is not entered as a prescription), immunizations up to date, intimate partner violence and alcohol screens negative, the dental examination still due, and advance care planning with an unnamed agent.
- **Focused visits.** The four chronic-care follow-ups (one week with the consultation, five weeks, three months, and five months at the end of CBT-E) are documented as focused visits on the APA 2023 guideline, NICE NG69, the CMID depression monograph, and the VA/DoD suicide-risk guideline.
