Assessment

  • Bulimia nervosa, severe (F50.23). She meets the DSM-5-TR criteria: recurrent binge eating with a sense of loss of control, recurrent self-induced vomiting to prevent weight gain, both at least weekly for more than three months, and self-evaluation unduly shaped by weight and shape, without the low weight of anorexia nervosa. With about seven to ten compensatory episodes a week it is severe. Outpatient care is safe: potassium has risen to 3.6 mmol/L on oral replacement, the QTc is below 460 ms, she has not fainted, and the pulse rise on standing is modest. Because the illness is severe and has lasted years, eating-disorder-focused CBT (CBT-E) is a better choice than guided self-help. Fluoxetine 60 mg daily has the most evidence in bulimia nervosa and also treats her depression, so it should start now rather than after six weeks of psychotherapy alone. Bupropion should be avoided because of the risk of seizures in bulimia nervosa. [S]
  • Major depressive disorder, recurrent, moderate (F33.1). A moderate depressive episode, recurrent given the earlier depression after childbirth. It often improves as bingeing and vomiting settle; fluoxetine treats both. Her risk of self-harm is higher with both conditions, so the safety plan should be reviewed at every visit, especially in the first weeks of fluoxetine. [S]
  • Hypokalemia from vomiting (E87.6). Mild and improving on oral replacement. It should be checked weekly until normal twice and then at each visit while she vomits; magnesium should be checked again, because low magnesium keeps potassium low. [S]

Plan of Treatment

  • Outpatient care. Arrange urgent medical care, or a higher level of eating-disorder care, for potassium below 3.0 mmol/L despite replacement, QTc above 500 ms or new arrhythmia, fainting, a resting pulse below 50, vomiting she cannot control, blood in the vomit, or suicidal thoughts. [S]
  • Eating-disorder-focused CBT (CBT-E): 20 sessions over 20 weeks with an eating disorders therapist, twice weekly for the first four weeks, starting this week, with regular eating, weighing in session, and a relapse-prevention plan at the end. [S]
  • Start fluoxetine 20 mg each morning, increase to 40 mg after one week and 60 mg after two weeks; a nurse phone check one to two weeks after starting for side effects, agitation, and suicidal thoughts; continue for at least a year after remission. Avoid bupropion. [S]
