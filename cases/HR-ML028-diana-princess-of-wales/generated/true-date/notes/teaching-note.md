# Teaching Note — Diana, Princess of Wales pilot

> **SYNTHETIC RECORD, NOT A REAL MEDICAL RECORD**

## Why this case matters

This case shows how historical evidence can support a transparent fictional modern episode without presenting interpretation or generated content as documented fact.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish documented (**D**), retrospective (**R**), and synthetic (**S**) content.
2. Keep historical and active problems distinct.
3. Connect measurable goals and interventions to guidelines.
4. Preserve strengths, function, identity, and agency.

## Destigmatization message

A health condition is one part of a person’s life and does not erase achievement, relationships, purpose, or agency.

## Discussion questions

- Which facts support concern without establishing a modern diagnosis?
- Which entries belong in active problems, historical problems, or both?
- What additional evidence would change an evidence grade?
- How should an EHR distinguish synthetic and historical content?

## Evidence-grade summary

- **D:** She was born The Honourable Diana Frances Spencer on 1 July 1961 at Park House, near Sandringham in Norfolk, England. On 29 July 1981 she married the Prince of Wales at St Paul's Cathedral in London. During her marriage she was president or patron of more than 100 charities, and she drew attention to homelessness, disability, children's causes, and HIV/AIDS. She and the Prince of Wales separated in December 1992 and divorced on 28 August 1996. In an interview broadcast on the BBC's Panorama programme on 20 November 1995, she said that she had had bulimia for a number of years, calling it "a secret disease" that you inflict upon yourself "because your self-esteem is at a low ebb"; asked how long it went on, she said "a long time", a little more than two or three years, and "I'm free of it now". In the same interview she said that with bulimia "you fill your stomach up four or five times a day" and that "it gives you a feeling of comfort"; that it "was a symptom of what was going on in my marriage"; and that "when you have bulimia you're very ashamed of yourself", so you do not discuss it with people. In the same interview she said that she had been unwell with post-natal depression, "which no one ever discusses". In the same interview she said that at a time of great distress she had hurt her arms and legs: "You have so much pain inside yourself that you try and hurt yourself on the outside". In May 2021 an independent inquiry commissioned by the BBC and led by Lord Dyson found that the 1995 interview had been obtained by deceit, in serious breach of the BBC's producer guidelines; the BBC accepted the findings in full and apologized. She died in Paris on 31 August 1997 after a car crash.
- **R:** None recorded.
- **S:** Shifted identity, fictional care team, modern encounters, results, plans, and outcomes.
