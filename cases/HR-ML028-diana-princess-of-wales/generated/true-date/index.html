Teaching objective: Respectful, unhurried primary care for a 28-year-old woman who asks for help with bulimia nervosa she has hidden for years: a structured eating-disorder assessment (SCOFF and EDE-Q) and DSM-5-TR severity grading; a medical risk assessment for purging (orthostatic vital signs, potassium, magnesium, phosphate and bicarbonate, an ECG with the QTc, and the parotid, dental and skin findings of vomiting), with oral potassium replacement and clear thresholds for a higher level of care; eating-disorder-focused cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT-E) as first-line treatment with fluoxetine 60 mg daily, the medicine with the best evidence; nutrition built on regular eating; dental protection; measurement-based care for co-occurring depression; and a structured safety assessment with the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline reviewed at every visit, on the APA 1987 eating disorders guideline, NICE NG69, and the CMID depression monograph.

Disposition
Outpatient care with close follow-up. She has no current thoughts of suicide or self-harm and no self-injury now; past self-injury at a time of great distress is part of her history and was discussed with her gently. Eating disorders and depression both raise the risk of suicide and self-harm, so a written safety plan was made with her, with her own warning signs (a run of bad nights of bingeing, feeling hopeless, or wanting to hurt herself) and what to do about each, including whom to call. Lethal-means counseling: no firearm and no access to one; medicines are kept in small amounts. The 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988) was given and saved in her phone, with the clinic number. Safety reviewed at every visit
