Teaching objective: Men get breast cancer. Care for a man of 83 with node-positive, hormone receptor-positive breast cancer after bilateral mastectomy, on the ASCO male breast cancer guideline and the NCCN breast guideline: tamoxifen as the preferred adjuvant endocrine therapy for men (not an aromatase inhibitor alone), with venous thromboembolism, mood, hot flash, sexual side effect, and adherence checks at every visit; germline genetic testing and genetic counseling offered to every man with breast cancer, with information for relatives; bone health on hormone therapy at his age; and survivorship care with no routine imaging or tumor markers. His own public account, that he dismissed his symptoms and had never had his chest examined, frames the teaching on why men present late.

Procedures

  • S Diagnostic mammography of both breasts: 1.9 cm irregular, spiculated retroareolar mass in the right breast (BI-RADS 5); left breast with no mass or gynecomastia (BI-RADS 1)
