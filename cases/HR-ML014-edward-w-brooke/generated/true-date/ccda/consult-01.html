Assessment

  • Right breast cancer, pT1c pN1a M0, stage IIA, ER and PR positive, HER2 negative, grade 2 (C50.121). Breast cancer in men is less common than in women but is biologically similar, and most cases are hormone receptor-positive, as his is. With one small nodal metastasis, a strongly ER-positive grade 2 tumor, and his age of 83, endocrine therapy gives most of the benefit. The added gain from chemotherapy would be small and its harms greater at his age. Radiation after mastectomy for one involved node is a choice with a small gain in local control; with clear margins and no extranodal spread, omitting it is reasonable, and I agree with the decision made after surgery. No staging scans are needed without symptoms. [S]
  • Adjuvant tamoxifen (Z79.810). Tamoxifen is the preferred adjuvant endocrine therapy for men. An aromatase inhibitor alone does not suppress estrogen adequately in men and is not recommended; it would be used only with a GnRH agonist if tamoxifen could not be taken. His mild hot flashes are common in men on tamoxifen. The main serious risk at his age is venous thromboembolism; he has no history of clots. [S]
  • Germline genetic testing. Genetic counseling and germline testing should be offered to every man with breast cancer, whatever his age or family history. BRCA2 is the gene most often involved; a pathogenic variant would raise his risk of prostate and pancreatic cancer and would mean his relatives should be offered testing. [S]
  • Bone health. Low bone mass at the femoral neck with mild vitamin D insufficiency. His fracture risk does not call for a bone drug. Bone-modifying drugs should not be given to prevent recurrence of early breast cancer in men, but can be used if osteoporosis develops. [S]

Data reviewed: Outside imaging reports (bilateral diagnostic mammography and bilateral breast and axillary ultrasound), biopsy and surgical pathology with ER, PR, HER2, and Ki-67; the first-visit blood count, chemistry, and 25-hydroxyvitamin D; and the DXA scan from this morning (femoral neck T-score -1.2, lumbar spine -0.4). Plan of Treatment

  • Continue tamoxifen 20 mg daily for an initial five years; at five years, if he tolerates it, discuss five more years given the involved node. Do not switch to an aromatase inhibitor alone. [S]
  • No chemotherapy and no radiation now. [S]
  • Genetic counseling and a germline multigene panel (BRCA2, BRCA1, PALB2, CHEK2, ATM, and others), sent today; if a variant is found, a letter for relatives offering them testing, and prostate and pancreatic screening for him. [S]
  • Tamoxifen safety at each visit: symptoms of leg or lung clots, mood, hot flashes, sexual side effects such as lower libido or erectile problems, and adherence; avoid paroxetine, fluoxetine, and bupropion, which block its activation; report any change in vision. [S]
  • Surveillance: history and examination of both sides of the chest wall and both axillae every 3 to 6 months for 3 years, then every 6 to 12 months to 5 years, then yearly. No mammography, because both breasts were removed, and no routine blood tests, tumor markers, or scans without symptoms. [S]
  • Bone health: vitamin D 1000 units daily with a recheck in three months, calcium from food, weight-bearing exercise, and DXA every two years. [S]
  • Lymphedema risk reduction for the right arm and a written survivorship care plan within the first year. [S]
