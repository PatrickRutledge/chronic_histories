## Editorial resolutions

- **Recent-death rule.** Brooke died in 2015. The case uses only what he made public himself (a live CNN interview in June 2003 and the Associated Press reports of the same week), basic biographical facts from the House of Representatives and the Congressional Record, and the cause of death given publicly by his former chief counsel. No physician of his made a public statement that was found. No medical records, accounts by relatives, or speculation about undisclosed conditions are used. His tumor size, grade, receptor status, node count, stage, examination findings, results, medicines, germline result, and preventive care are synthetic. The recent-death review rule applies.
- **Men get breast cancer.** The record states this plainly and treats his cancer as an ordinary diagnosis. His own public points (that he dismissed the symptom, that no doctor had ever examined his chest, that he had not known men could get it, and that men should not be “macho” about it) frame the teaching. Nothing in the record treats the diagnosis as embarrassing or unmanly.
- **Relatives.** The interview and the AP report give a relative’s role in his seeking care and a relative’s own health history. Neither is used, so that no decision, statement, or health fact is attributed to a real relative. The record has no family history entries. The spouse in the social history and in the advance care planning is not named. The letter for relatives after the germline result names no one.
- **Operation.** The AP report, citing his *New York Times* interview, says he had a double mastectomy. In the CNN interview he said he “had to be operated on” and that all the lymph nodes on his right side were removed. The two accounts do not conflict. The record codes a bilateral mastectomy with a right axillary dissection. The reason the left breast was also removed was not made public, so none is given. The pathology of the left breast is synthetic and benign.
- **Index age.** The modern episode presents Brooke at 83, about three and a half months after his operation. That is the earliest point the 2026 calendar allows without contradicting the documented September 2002 diagnosis. His public interview fixed his age as 83 at that time. The synthetic date of birth is October 26, 1942 (shift 23 years). The diagnostic imaging, biopsy, operation, and start of tamoxifen are dated September and October 2025 (true frame September and October 2002). The index visit on January 13, 2026 maps to January 13, 2003. The chronic-care visits on February 10, May 12, and August 11, 2026 map to the same days of 2003. The annual wellness visit on October 13, 2026 maps to October 13, 2003, when he was still 83. His public disclosure in June 2003 falls between the third and fourth visits, so the fourth visit and the wellness visit mention that he has begun speaking about breast cancer in men. - **Synthetic clinical picture.** The right-sided cancer, the imaging and needle biopsy, the operation, and the nodal spread are from his public account. Everything else is synthetic and chosen to be typical of breast cancer in men (retroareolar, invasive ductal, strongly hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative): a 1.8 cm grade 2 tumor with one of fourteen nodes involved (pT1c pN1a, stage IIA).
