History of Present Illness

Ms. Tudor is seen at the request of Dr. Ashcombe-Nwosu, two weeks after her first visit, at the end of the clinic day at her request. She describes three months of a heavy, unremitting low mood that does not lift with good news, loss of pleasure in music, riding, and company, waking at 2 or 3 in the morning, poor appetite with weight loss, slowed thinking, and a sense that she has outlived her circle. She dwells on a former protégé and confidant who died two years ago after a bitter breach, and on her own part in it, with guilt that comes in waves. She has had no period of elevated or irritable mood, reduced need for sleep, or racing thoughts, and no hallucinations or fixed false beliefs. The passive wish not to wake has not recurred since the first visit; she has never harmed herself. She has no memory complaints and continues to manage her papers. She stopped diphenhydramine two weeks ago and sleeps about four hours. She drinks a small glass of wine some evenings. She prefers to be treated privately and individually and is willing to take a medicine if it is explained to her. [S]
