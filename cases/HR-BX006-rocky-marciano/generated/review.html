Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Sources. The International Boxing Hall of Fame page for Marciano could not be reached (connection refused), the New York Times archive could not be fetched, and Russell Sullivan's biography Rocky Marciano: The Rock of His Times was not read, directly or through a fetched quotation; no Brockton Historical Society page on his health was found. The record therefore rests on reference encyclopedias (Encyclopedia.com), HISTORY.com, Biography.com, two sports history features (VICE, ESPN Classic), Boxing News and the Iowa History Journal. Claims supported by a single feature article are graded D with certainty “probable” (CLM-0002, CLM-0006, CLM-0010).
  2. Thin documented health history. The documented health facts are few: pneumonia as a small child, broken hands, back injuries and cuts during his career, by one account a ruptured disc from 1949 and an arthritic right elbow from 1951, and a hospital diagnosis of a ruptured disc in July 1956. Nothing else about his health is documented in the sources read, and nothing is added to his history. The modern episode is built around common, defensible problems for a retired boxer: chronic low back pain with a recent disc herniation, thumb-base osteoarthritis, the metabolic change after heavy training stops, and a brain health baseline.
  3. Name. The display name is “Rocky Marciano”; the alias “Rocco Francis Marchegiano,” his birth name, appears in no other record. “Newton” appears only as the place of the crash. No other figure with a record in the dataset is named; his opponents other than Jersey Joe Walcott are not named.
  4. Index age and dates. The index visit on August 4, 2026 maps to August 4, 1956, when he was 32 and living in Brockton, three months after he announced his retirement and a few weeks after the July 1956 hospital diagnosis of a ruptured disc. The synthetic date of birth is September 1, 1993 (shift 70 years). The visits on August 18, September 8, September 22, September 29 and October 27, 2026 map to the same days of 1956, at ages 32 and 33, thirteen years before his death. The index setting is Brockton rather than Fort Lauderdale: the sources read place him in Brockton in 1956 and do not date a move to Florida.
  5. Corrections to the brief. (1) The brief gives his retirement at 32; HISTORY.com and Boxing News agree, while VICE and the UXL and World Encyclopedia entries say 31. Born September 1, 1923, he was 32 on April 27, 1956, and the record says 32. (2) The brief names “back problems and hand injuries” as documented; the sources support broken hands and back injuries in general terms only, not which hand or which bones, so the left thumb in the modern episode is a synthetic choice. A broken left thumb in his amateur days is reported in secondary summaries citing Sullivan, but no source that states it was read, so it is not a claim.
  6. Disc dates (CLM-0006, CLM-0010). ESPN Classic says he knew from August 1949 that he was fighting with a ruptured disc; VICE says a hospital diagnosed a ruptured disc in July 1956.   7. Roster leads not used. No relative has a role, statement, decision or health fact in the modern episode; the health care agent is unnamed.
