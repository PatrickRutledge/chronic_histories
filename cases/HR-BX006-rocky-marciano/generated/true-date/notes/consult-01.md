## Assessment

1. **Disc herniation: imaging and surgery** (M51.16). An improving left L5 radiculopathy with no motor deficit and no red flags. Most such herniations improve within six to twelve weeks without surgery, and repeat imaging would not change the plan. A surgical opinion would be needed for any new weakness, saddle numbness or bladder or bowel change (the same day), or for disabling leg pain that has not improved after six to twelve weeks of good conservative care. **S**
2. **Exercise therapy and return to training** (M54.59). Exercise therapy is the main treatment for both the radiculopathy and the chronic back pain: directional preference (extension) exercise, which centralizes his pain today, then motor control and progressive trunk and hip strengthening, twice a week for six weeks, then a home program. Naproxen with food is reasonable for a short course but should be needed less as therapy works; if he needs it most days he should say so rather than keep taking it. No opioids or muscle relaxants. Walking and swimming now; slow jogging on the flat once the leg pain has been gone for a few weeks; no heavy lifting from the floor for three months. **S**
3. **Thumb-base osteoarthritis** (M18.32). Post-traumatic osteoarthritis of the left first carpometacarpal joint. A hand-based thumb orthosis has the strongest support and was fitted today, to wear for gripping work and at night, with hand therapy exercises and continued topical diclofenac. A corticosteroid injection is an option if pain persists after six to twelve weeks of this; it is not needed now. **S**
4. **Brain health baseline** (Z87.828). A normal baseline: no cognitive, mood, behavioral, sleep or balance complaints, a normal neurological screen, MoCA 29, PHQ-9 3, GAD-7 4 and a PROMIS sleep T-score of 55 with back pain at night. Traumatic encephalopathy syndrome is a research diagnosis under the 1951 NINDS consensus criteria, which require progressive core clinical features; he has none, and the diagnosis is not made on a history of exposure alone. Brain imaging is not indicated without new symptoms or signs. The baseline should be repeated yearly, with earlier review for any change in memory, mood, behavior, speech or movement; he should avoid sparring and other head impacts. **S**
5. **Exercise and weight after heavy training** (E66.3). His energy needs have fallen sharply while his eating has not. A target of 5% to 7% weight loss over six months is realistic: 150 to 300 minutes a week of moderate aerobic activity and strength training twice a week, smaller portions with less salt, and a week of home blood pressure readings before each review. His current home average of 126/78 mmHg is in the elevated range and should improve with this. **S**

## Recommendations

1. No repeat imaging and no surgical opinion now; a surgical opinion for any new weakness, saddle numbness or bladder or bowel change, or for disabling leg pain beyond six to twelve weeks.
2. Physical therapy twice a week for six weeks (extension-based exercise, motor control and strengthening), then a home program.
3. Naproxen with food only as long as needed, and tell Dr. Fairweather-Obi if he needs it most days; no opioids or muscle relaxants.
4. Walking and swimming now; slow jogging once the leg pain has been gone for a few weeks; no heavy lifting from the floor for three months.
5. Thumb orthosis (fitted today) for gripping work and at night, hand exercises and topical diclofenac; an injection only if pain persists after six to twelve weeks.
6. Brain health baseline repeated yearly; no brain imaging without new symptoms or signs; no sparring or other head impacts.
7. 150 to 300 minutes a week of moderate aerobic activity, strength training twice a week, and a 5% to 7% weight loss goal over six months.
