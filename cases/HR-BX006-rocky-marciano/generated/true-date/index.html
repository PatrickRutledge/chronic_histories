Teaching objective: Primary care for a retired professional boxer: a 32-year-old former heavyweight champion, four months retired, comes in a few weeks after a lumbar disc herniation with sciatica, on a background of years of back pain, with an aching thumb base from an old fracture. The episode covers the history and neurological examination of low back pain with radiculopathy and its red flags; when imaging and surgical referral are and are not needed; exercise therapy, motor control training and a short NSAID course without opioids under the 1947 American College of Physicians guideline; post-traumatic osteoarthritis of the thumb base with a splint, hand therapy and topical diclofenac under the 1949 American College of Rheumatology/Arthritis Foundation guideline; NSAID safety, with dyspepsia that follows unmonitored daily naproxen and its test-and-treat management; a brain health baseline after repetitive head impacts (symptom inventory, MoCA, mood, anxiety and sleep, no imaging without an indication, and traumatic encephalopathy syndrome as a research diagnosis); the metabolic change after a sudden end to heavy training, with weight, blood pressure confirmed by home readings, lipids and an exercise prescription; and anxiety screening that is negative.

Social history

  • S Retired professional heavyweight boxer, unbeaten and a former world champion; retired four months ago, saying he wanted more time with his family and was tired of the training; now works on personal appearances and business interests
  • S Married, with a young daughter; lives in his home town south of Boston
  • S Trained six days a week for years, with long roadwork, heavy sparring and a strict diet; since retiring he walks most days but has stopped running and bag work, and eats as he did in camp
  • S Never used tobacco; an occasional glass of wine with dinner (AUDIT-C 1); no drug use; no anabolic steroids or other performance drugs
