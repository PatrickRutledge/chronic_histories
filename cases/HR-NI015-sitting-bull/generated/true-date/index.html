Teaching objective: Post-traumatic osteoarthritis of the hindfoot decades after a gunshot wound, in a 55-year-old Hunkpapa Lakota leader and holy man seen at a clinic serving the Standing Rock Reservation. The episode covers culturally safe, trauma-informed care that asks his preferences, offers a Lakota interpreter and traditional healing alongside Western care without probing his beliefs, as the Indian Health Service policy asks; weight-bearing X-rays and CT; retained bullet fragments, when they raise blood lead and why these do not; pain treatment led by a topical NSAID, a short oral NSAID course and no opioids; a rigid ankle-foot orthosis, physical therapy and an ultrasound-guided subtalar injection under the ACR/Arthritis Foundation guideline and the CMID osteoarthritis monograph; surgical options (arthrodesis) explained with shared decision-making and his decision to wait respected; an NSAID-related dyspepsia; screening for diabetes, high blood pressure and depression in a man who has lived through forced displacement, exile, hunger and imprisonment, without turning grief for real losses into a diagnosis; prediabetes; and food insecurity on a commodity diet, with the Food Distribution Program on Indian Reservations, traditional foods and the Community Health Representative program.

Social, functional, and mental status

Social history

  • S Hunkpapa Lakota; a respected leader and holy man of his people; lives with his family in a log house on the Grand River near where he was born, on the Standing Rock Reservation, about an hour's drive from the clinic; keeps horses and a garden
  • S Speaks Lakota as his first language and some English; chose to have the clinic's Lakota-speaking interpreter at his visits; asked to be called Sitting Bull
  • S His people's way of life was taken from them within his lifetime: the buffalo are gone, the bands were pursued and exiled, he was held as a prisoner, and his people now farm and depend on government food; he spoke of these losses plainly and chose not to discuss them further at the first visit
  • S Diet: commodity foods (flour, canned meat, lard, sugar, coffee), garden vegetables in season and game when it can be found; food runs short at the end of each month
  • S Does not drink alcohol; does not use commercial tobacco (traditional and ceremonial use was not asked about and is not recorded)
  • S Traditional healing: he has his own spiritual practices and healers and will decide whether and how they are part of his care; the clinic offered access through its traditional medicine program and asked nothing further
  • S Strengths and supports: his standing and responsibilities among his people, a deep spiritual life, decades of endurance, family around him, horsemanship, and a clear sense of what he wants from care

Functional status

  • S Independent in daily activities; walks with a limp, about half a mile before he must stop; mounts and rides a horse with difficulty; avoids uneven ground

Mental status

  • S Alert, composed and dignified; watchful at first, then warm; speech measured, through the interpreter and in English; mood steady, tired from broken sleep because of pain; grief and anger about the losses of his people expressed as understandable responses to real events; no thoughts of suicide or of harming anyone

Allergies and medications

Allergies

  • S No known allergy

Medications

Procedures and immunizations

Procedures

  • S Weight-bearing X-rays of the left foot and ankle: narrowing and bone spurs of the posterior subtalar and talonavicular joints, an old healed irregularity of the plantar heel, a normal ankle (tibiotalar) joint, and several small metallic densities in the soft tissue of the heel

  • S Ultrasound-guided injection of the left posterior subtalar joint with triamcinolone 40 mg and lidocaine 1% 2 mL, after consent; pain fell from 6 to 1 of 10 on walking within 15 minutes
  • S Advance care planning conversation, about 20 minutes, with the interpreter
