## Assessment

1. **Post-traumatic osteoarthritis of the left subtalar and talonavicular joints** (M19.172). The pattern on X-ray and CT, with the old wound track through the heel, is post-traumatic arthritis of the posterior subtalar and talonavicular joints; the ankle joint itself is spared. A healed, non-draining scar, normal inflammatory markers and no bone changes of infection on CT make chronic osteomyelitis very unlikely. **S**
2. **Retained metal fragments** (Z18.12). Lead from retained bullet fragments is absorbed mainly when fragments sit in a joint, bone or other fluid-filled space; his lie in soft tissue, and his blood lead is low. Removal would mean surgery through scarred heel skin for no benefit, so they should stay. **S**
3. **Non-operative treatment** (M19.172). The ACR/Arthritis Foundation guideline covers the hand, hip and knee, but its core advice applies here: exercise and therapy, an orthosis that limits painful motion, topical then oral NSAIDs for limited periods, and intra-articular corticosteroid injection. A rigid ankle-foot orthosis and a rocker-sole shoe reduce subtalar motion. A short course of naproxen is reasonable with normal kidney function and blood pressure, watching his stomach. An ultrasound-guided subtalar injection is both a test and a treatment. Opioids have no place here. **S**
4. **Surgery** (M19.172). If pain stops him walking or riding despite these measures, a subtalar arthrodesis, or a double arthrodesis that includes the talonavicular joint, reliably relieves pain but removes side-to-side motion of the hindfoot, needs about three months without weight on the foot and a year to recover fully, and would change how he rides. It is his decision, and there is no harm in waiting. **S**
5. **Care close to home**. He lives an hour from the clinic. Therapy by video, rides through the Community Health Representative program and visits at the outreach session keep his care close to home; the interpreter at each visit and his own choices, including traditional healing if he wishes, should shape the plan. **S**

## Recommendations

1. A rigid ankle-foot orthosis molded by the orthotist, with a rocker-sole shoe.
2. Physical therapy weekly for six weeks with a home program for hindfoot mobility, strength and balance.
3. Continue diclofenac gel; naproxen 500 mg twice daily with food for up to six weeks; stop for stomach pain or black stools.
4. If pain stays above 4 of 10 after four to six weeks, an ultrasound-guided subtalar corticosteroid injection, no more than three or four a year.
5. Leave the metal fragments in place; repeat blood lead only for symptoms of lead toxicity or if a fragment moves into a joint.
6. No opioids.
7. Arthrodesis only if he chooses it after the non-operative plan; I will see him again at any outreach session if he wishes to discuss it.
8. If travel to therapy becomes hard, video sessions and rides through the Community Health Representative program.
