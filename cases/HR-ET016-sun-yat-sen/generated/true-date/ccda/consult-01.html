Assessment

  • Adenocarcinoma of the gallbladder invading the liver (C23). This is not a primary liver cancer. In a liver without cirrhosis the imaging criteria for hepatocellular carcinoma do not apply and tissue is needed; the biopsy shows an adenocarcinoma with a pancreatobiliary pattern, the imaging shows it arising from the gallbladder wall, and alpha-fetoprotein is normal. This is a primary gallbladder adenocarcinoma invading the liver, clinical T3 N1 M0 (stage IIIB) on imaging. Gallbladder cancer often has peritoneal spread that CT does not show, so a staging laparoscopy should come before any attempt at resection. If there is no metastatic disease, an extended cholecystectomy with resection of segments IVb and V and portal lymphadenectomy could be curative in intent; if there is, treatment is systemic: gemcitabine and cisplatin with durvalumab is the preferred first-line regimen. Genomic profiling of the biopsy now will save time if a targeted treatment becomes relevant. [S]
  • Resolved hepatitis B infection (Z86.19). Resolved past infection with undetectable HBV DNA and preserved liver function (Child-Pugh A6, ALBI grade 2). It does not limit surgery or chemotherapy. The reactivation risk with gemcitabine, cisplatin and durvalumab is low to moderate; monitoring HBV DNA and ALT every 3 months, with entecavir if HBV DNA becomes detectable, is appropriate. Hepatitis C is excluded. [S]
