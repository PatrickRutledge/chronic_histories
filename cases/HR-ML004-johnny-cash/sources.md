## Historical evidence

1. Lensing E. “Johnny Cash (1932–2003).” CALS Encyclopedia of Arkansas. Supports his birth on February 26, 1932, in Kingsland, Cleveland County, to Ray and Carrie Cash; his brother Jack’s death in a 1944 sawmill accident; amphetamine use from 1957 and an addiction lasting about a decade; the 1983 hospitalization for internal bleeding and treatment at the Betty Ford Clinic; frequent admissions for pneumonia; the autonomic neuropathy; and his death at Baptist Hospital in Nashville on September 12, 2003, of respiratory failure brought on by complications of diabetes. https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/johnny-cash-9/
2. ABC News. “Johnny Cash Battles Recurring Pneumonia,” February 13, 2001. Supports pneumonia treated at Baptist Hospital in Nashville in February 2001, the October 1997 diagnosis of Shy-Drager syndrome, and Cash’s statement in a 2000 interview that it was a misdiagnosis. https://abcnews.com/Entertainment/story?id=109597
3. CNN. *Larry King Live*, “Interview With Johnny Cash,” November 26, 2002 (rush transcript). Cash’s own public statements: autonomic neuropathy described as a deadening of the nerve endings in his lower extremities that left him able to walk, but not very well; pneumonia four times in three years; about 60 percent of his vision gone, which he attributed to the neuropathy and the diabetes; his hope that the glaucoma could be reversed; and amphetamines and barbiturates in the 1960s. https://transcripts.cnn.com/TRANSCRIPTS/0211/26/lkl.00.html
4. Pollstar. “Johnny Cash Dies,” September 12, 2003. Quotes the statement by his manager, Lou Robin, issued by Baptist Hospital: he died of complications from diabetes, which resulted in respiratory failure. Also reports autonomic neuropathy and pneumonia in his last years. https://news.pollstar.com/2003/09/12/johnny-cash-dies/
5. HISTMED Master Roster v3. Internal project source. Its clinical claims were provisional and were not treated as independent corroboration.

## Editorial resolutions

- **Recent-death rule.** Cash died in 2003. The case uses only what Cash himself, his manager, and published reference works made public: his own televised statements, the hospital-issued statement at his death, a news report, and an encyclopedia entry. No medical records, family accounts beyond those sources, or speculation about undisclosed conditions are used. The recent-death review rule applies.
- **Index age.** The modern episode presents Cash at 71, a few months after the November 2002 interview in which he described his diabetes, neuropathy, glaucoma, and vision loss, so the clinical notes can cite what he made public. Age 70, his age at the interview, would have put the true-date visits before the interview (the visit window ends on November 1, 2026). The synthetic date of birth is February 26, 1955 (shift 23 years); the index visit on February 27, 2026 maps to February 27, 2003, and the last visit on August 14, 2026 maps to August 14, 2003, before his final hospitalization and death. Past medical history and family history include only events before the index date. The episode does not mention June Carter Cash, who died on May 15, 2003, during the true-date span of the visits; the supports are described generically.
- **Diabetes.** His diabetes is documented by his own statement and by his manager’s statement at his death. When it was diagnosed and how it was treated were not found in the sources and are not recorded. The modern diagnosis, HbA1c values, and medicines are synthetic.
- **Shy-Drager syndrome.** The October 1997 diagnosis and Cash’s later statement that it was wrong are both documented (**D**). The teaching episode treats his autonomic symptoms as diabetic autonomic neuropathy, which is consistent with what he made public, and does not put Shy-Drager syndrome or multiple system atrophy on the problem list. The CNN rush transcript dates his undiagnosed illness to 1993 and orders the diagnoses differently; the case uses the October 1997 date reported by ABC News and does not record the transcript’s sequence.
- **Glaucoma.** Cash mentioned “the glaucoma” on air but did not give its type or stage, so the claim is graded **D** with certainty probable. Primary open-angle glaucoma, severe stage, and the mild diabetic retinopathy are synthetic choices.
- **Substance use.** His amphetamine and barbiturate use and his treatment at the Betty Ford Clinic are well documented and were made public by Cash. They are recorded as history, marked sensitive, and kept off the problem list. The modern social history records them only to guide prescribing (avoiding controlled stimulants and sedatives). No behavioral-health diagnosis is coded, so no safety assessment is required.
- **Not used.** The family’s and biographers’ accounts of his final weeks, the Parkinson’s disease announcement at the October 1997 Flint concert, reports of asthma, and the dates of his parents’ deaths were not verified in a fetched source and are not recorded. The 1983 internal bleeding is recorded without a cause because the source gives none.
