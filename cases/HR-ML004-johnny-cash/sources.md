## Editorial resolutions

- **Recent-death rule.** Cash died in 2003. The case uses only what Cash himself, his manager, and published reference works made public: his own televised statements, the hospital-issued statement at his death, a news report, and an encyclopedia entry. No medical records, family accounts beyond those sources, or speculation about undisclosed conditions are used. The recent-death review rule applies.
- **Index age.** The modern episode presents Cash at 71, a few months after the November 2002 interview in which he described his diabetes, neuropathy, glaucoma, and vision loss, so the clinical notes can cite what he made public. Age 70, his age at the interview, would have put the true-date visits before the interview (the visit window ends on November 1, 2026). The synthetic date of birth is February 26, 1955 (shift 23 years); the index visit on February 27, 2026 maps to February 27, 2003, and the last visit on August 14, 2026 maps to August 14, 2003, before his final hospitalization and death. The annual wellness visit on April 24, 2026 (true frame April 24, 2003) sits between the existing visits. Past medical history and family history include only events before the index date. The episode does not mention June Carter Cash, who died on May 15, 2003, during the true-date span of the visits; the supports are described generically.
- **Diabetes.** His diabetes is documented by his own statement and by his manager’s statement at his death. When it was diagnosed and how it was treated were not found in the sources and are not recorded. The modern diagnosis, HbA1c values, and medicines are synthetic.
- **Shy-Drager syndrome.** The October 1997 diagnosis and Cash’s later statement that it was wrong are both documented (**D**). The teaching episode treats his autonomic symptoms as diabetic autonomic neuropathy, which is consistent with what he made public, and does not put Shy-Drager syndrome or multiple system atrophy on the problem list. The CNN rush transcript dates his undiagnosed illness to 1993 and orders the diagnoses differently; the case uses the October 1997 date reported by ABC News and does not record the transcript’s sequence.
- **Glaucoma.** Cash mentioned “the glaucoma” on air but did not give its type or stage, so the claim is graded **D** with certainty probable. Primary open-angle glaucoma, severe stage, and the mild diabetic retinopathy are synthetic choices.
- **Substance use.** His amphetamine and barbiturate use and his treatment at the Betty Ford Clinic are well documented and were made public by Cash. They are recorded as history, marked sensitive, and kept off the problem list. The modern social history records them only to guide prescribing (avoiding controlled stimulants and sedatives). No behavioral-health diagnosis is coded, so no safety assessment is required.
- **Annual wellness visit.** A subsequent Medicare annual wellness visit was added on April 24, 2026 (true frame April 24, 2003), four weeks after the endocrinology consultation and five weeks before the three-month review. The true-frame visits fall in his last months, so the visit was placed between existing visits rather than after the schedule, and well before his documented final hospitalization. No sick visit was added: under the recent-death rule the case adds no synthetic illness episode, and his documented pneumonia admissions are not echoed with a synthetic infection. Judgment calls: (1) the visit records no new diagnosis, fall, or hospital stay, and the safety-events history covers only the time since his first visit, not the preceding year, so it does not contradict his documented admissions; (2) the eye examination is recorded as current because the dilated examination at the first visit also served as diabetic retinopathy screening, with glaucoma care left with the eye clinic; (3) the AUDIT-C (score 0) is administered as a routine screen like any other, the past substance use is not restated in the note, and the prescribing precautions stay in the social history only; hepatitis C screening is offered as the universal one-time screen for adults through 79, not because of his history; (4) cognition is assessed by direct observation, and the Mini-Cog from two months earlier is not repeated; (5) lung cancer and aneurysm screening are marked not indicated because the record's social history records no tobacco use; the case makes no further claim about his smoking history; (6) advance care planning records his stated priorities, drawn from the existing health concerns (keeping his sight, staying at home, continuing to write), and an unnamed health care agent, but deliberately records no preference about life support or resuscitation, because the true-frame date is months before his documented final hospitalization and such a preference could read as a claim about his real end-of-life choices; (7) the family member who helps at home stays generic, consistent with the decision to omit June Carter Cash; (8) the FIT kit and hepatitis C test are ordered but no results are recorded, and the first zoster dose is planned for the three-month review, where it is already recorded.
