Assessment

  • Neurogenic orthostatic hypotension from diabetic autonomic neuropathy (I95.1). Systolic pressure falls 32 mmHg from sitting to standing while heart rate rises only 4 beats per minute, the same pattern as at intake: a neurogenic cause. With long-standing diabetes and length-dependent sensory loss, diabetic autonomic neuropathy explains it, and the earlier Shy-Drager label is not carried forward. Formal autonomic testing would not change treatment. He is still symptomatic on non-drug measures, and lying pressure of 150/84 mmHg allows a low dose of a pressor. [S]
  • Type 2 diabetes with diabetic autonomic neuropathy (E11.43). HbA1c 8.6 percent, above an individualized goal below 7.5 percent that suits his age, neuropathy, falls, and low vision. Autonomic neuropathy can blunt the warning signs of low glucose, so insulin and sulfonylureas are avoided. An SGLT2 inhibitor would add fluid loss to his orthostatic hypotension, and a GLP-1 receptor agonist's nausea, slowed stomach emptying, and weight loss are poorly tolerated with autonomic neuropathy; he has no atherosclerotic disease, heart failure, or kidney disease that would make either essential. Sitagliptin carries little risk of low glucose and does not lower blood pressure, and eGFR 72 allows the full dose. [S]
  • Diabetic peripheral neuropathy with loss of protective sensation (E11.42). No ulcer or pre-ulcerative lesion. Vitamin B12 286 pg/mL is low-normal on metformin and should be watched. [S]

Data reviewed: The intake HbA1c, kidney function, urine albumin-creatinine ratio, vitamin B12, TSH, and hemoglobin; today's basic metabolic panel; the intake and today's lying, seated, and standing blood pressures and heart rates; and his home glucose log. [S]

Plan of Treatment

  • Start midodrine 2.5 mg on waking, at midday, and mid-afternoon, with the last dose at least four hours before bed. If he is still symptomatic and lying pressure allows, increase to 5 mg three times daily. [S]
