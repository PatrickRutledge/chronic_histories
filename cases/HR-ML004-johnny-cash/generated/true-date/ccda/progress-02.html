Chief Complaint

Endocrinology consultation and four-week diabetes check: lightheadedness on standing and choice of add-on glucose-lowering therapy. [S]

History of Present Illness

Mr. Cash is a 71-year-old man with type 2 diabetes, autonomic and peripheral neuropathy, and severe primary open-angle glaucoma, seen four weeks after his first visit for an endocrinology consultation on the same day as his diabetes check. Metformin was raised to 1000 mg twice daily, atorvastatin was started, and dorzolamide was added to latanoprost four weeks ago. He takes all of them, and metformin causes no stomach upset. His fasting home glucose readings run 140 to 170 mg/dL, with none below 70. He now stands up in stages, has raised the head of his bed, wears waist-high compression, and drinks about 2 liters a day. He is still lightheaded on standing most mornings and after meals, but he has had no further falls since the first visit. He asks again what he really has, since he was once told it was Shy-Drager syndrome. [S]
