Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Recent-death rule. She died about 1458 BC, so the rule does not apply. Claims are limited to what the fetched sources state.
  2. How little is knowable (thin chart). Nothing about her health was written down in her lifetime in any source fetched for this record. Everything medical comes from CT scans of a mummy, KV60A, announced as hers in 2007, and from a lotion flask bearing her name. The identification is debated (CLM-0008), the findings were reported by a learned society's magazine and a documentary fact sheet rather than in a peer-reviewed paper, and even an accepted identification would show only the state of a body at death, read more than three thousand years later. Every finding on the mummy (obesity, the dental abscess and missing teeth, osteoarthritis, osteoporosis, the bone lesion read as a metastasis, the proposed diabetes) and the lotion hypothesis are therefore graded R. Her past medical history and family history are empty because nothing is documented. The history is not padded.
  3. Dates (correction to the brief). The brief gives her reign as about 1479 to 1458 BC. The sources support a regency from about 1479 BC, when Thutmose III came to the throne, and kingship from about his year 7: the Metropolitan Museum of Art dates objects of the joint reign to about 1473 to 1458 BCE, and the British Museum gives 1479 to 1457 BC. The record treats 1479 BC as the start of the regency, about 1473 BC as the start of her kingship, and her death as about 1458 BC (Thutmose III's year 22), all approximate. Historical dates use signed astronomical years (-1506 for 1507 BC, -1457 for 1458 BC), with calendar "unknown" and precision "approximate".
  4. Birth year (judgment call). No fetched source records her birth year. The roster's 1507 BC is kept as a very uncertain estimate (-1506, approximate), back-calculated from an age of about 50 suggested for KV60A, and graded R, possible (CLM-0005).
  5. Index age and dates; no true-date variant. The synthetic date of birth is February 10, 1978 (her birth month and day are unknown), shift 3484 years. The index visit on April 14, 2026 falls at age 48, which maps to about 1459 BC, late in her reign at Thebes and about a year before her death. Because she was born before 1 CE, the record has no true-date variant (docs/date-shifting.md); the build skips it and the review page says why.
  6. Claims after the index date. The 2007 identification, the CT findings, the lotion flask, her death and the later effacing of her monuments are linked only to timeline events after the index date, so the intake documents do not show them. No modern problem is linked to a claim about the mummy: the presenting jaw swelling, the diabetes, the obesity and the knee osteoarthritis echo the reported findings without claiming them.
  7. Name (judgment call). She is known by a single name. The record uses identity.name_parts with an empty given array and "Hatshepsut" as the family element (family_first false), as other single-name records do, so that generated documents carry the name she is known by. No alias is recorded; her throne name and the title "king" are not used as aliases. The notes call her Ms. Hatshepsut.
  8. Culture. Ancient Egyptian kingship, religion and the divine-birth claim are described plainly from museum and university sources. The modern frame makes her the director of a large public authority for building works and trade missions, the modern counterpart of her documented rule, without costume or exotic detail.
  9. Antibiotics withheld (judgment call). The abscess was localized, with a temperature of 37.6 °C, no trismus and no fascial-space spread, and drainage with extraction was available within 24 hours, so no antibiotic was given, as the ADA 2019 guideline advises. Her new diabetes raised the risk of spread, which the record handles with explicit warning signs and review the next day rather than with an antibiotic.
  10. Diabetes diagnosis and treatment (judgment call). An A1c of 7.6% with a glucose of 212 mg/dL from the same sample confirms diabetes (ADA 2026, two abnormal results). Metformin is started at intake; semaglutide is added at six weeks, after the infection, chosen with her for glucose and weight; an SGLT2 inhibitor is not indicated without kidney disease, heart failure or cardiovascular disease; a moderate-intensity statin is started as the Standards advise for adults 40 to 75 with diabetes. Endocrinology is kept in the plan only if the A1c is not at goal by six months; it was at goal at fourteen weeks.
  11. Weight without stigma. Weight is recorded and discussed only with her permission, in person-first language; her goal is strength and easier stairs, not a number.
  12. No bone cancer workup (judgment call). The proposed metastatic lesion on the mummy is history only. Nothing in the modern episode (no bone pain beyond the knees, no bone tenderness, normal calcium and alkaline phosphatase, only intended weight loss) gives a reason to look for bone cancer, so none is sought. Bone density testing is not indicated at 48 without a risk factor; bone health is addressed through exercise, calcium and vitamin D, with attention to bone and muscle during weight loss on semaglutide.
  13. Lotion and carcinogen. The Bonn hypothesis (CLM-0012) is recorded as R; the modern episode does not invent a skin disease or a carcinogen exposure.
