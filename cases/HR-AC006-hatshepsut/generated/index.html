Teaching objective: Care for a 48-year-old woman with a painful swollen jaw from a dental abscess, and type 2 diabetes found on screening, as current guidance advises and without weight stigma: recognize the warning signs of spreading odontogenic infection; give an NSAID with acetaminophen for dental pain and no opioid; withhold antibiotics when the abscess is localized, there is no systemic involvement and definitive treatment is available the next day (ADA 2019); arrange urgent drainage and extraction; diagnose diabetes from two abnormal results in one sample; start metformin, then add a GLP-1 receptor agonist chosen for weight and glucose (ADA Standards of Care 2026), with an SGLT2 inhibitor not indicated without kidney or heart disease; start a statin as the Standards advise for adults 40 to 75 with diabetes; screen for eye, foot and kidney complications; explain the two-way link between periodontitis and diabetes and arrange periodontal therapy; manage knee osteoarthritis with exercise and a topical NSAID; and keep up cancer screening by age (mammography, colorectal and cervical) and bone health, without a search for bone cancer when nothing in the modern episode points to one. The modern episode is a teaching case, not a claim about her real illnesses, which cannot be known from ancient remains whose identity is debated.
