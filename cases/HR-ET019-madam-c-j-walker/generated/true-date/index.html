Teaching objective: Hypertension with chronic kidney disease in a 49-year-old Black woman who founded and runs a national hair-care company and travels most weeks to train her sales agents. The episode covers accurate office measurement and 24-hour ambulatory and home monitoring under the 1916 AHA/ACC guideline; kidney function by the race-free CKD-EPI 1912 equation, stated explicitly, with the urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, chronicity and KDIGO 1915 staging and risk; an angiotensin receptor blocker titrated to the maximum dose, an SGLT2 inhibitor for CKD with albuminuria, and a calcium channel blocker to reach goal; a statin chosen by shared decision; potassium and creatinine after each start or increase; sick-day rules tested by a real gastroenteritis; sodium counseling built on the food she likes; nephrology consultation; and a work and rest plan she sets herself. The teaching contrast is the historical advice to rest and slow down: here the risks are stated plainly, the treatment does the work, and she decides how she lives. Race and medicine: the eGFR uses no race term, the first medicine is chosen for the albuminuria rather than for her race, and APOL1 genetic testing is offered with counseling and left to her choice.
