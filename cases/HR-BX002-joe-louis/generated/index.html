Teaching objective: Cocaine use disorder with a cocaine-induced psychotic disorder in a 55-year-old Black man, a retired heavyweight champion and Army veteran, who comes in two weeks after collapsing in the street, with persecutory fears and little sleep. The episode covers a nonjudgmental assessment with DSM-5-TR criteria, the DAST-10 and urine drug testing with consent; the differential between a substance-induced psychosis, a primary psychotic disorder and a neurocognitive disorder after a long boxing career, using the time course after the last use, the MoCA, laboratory tests and brain MRI, and the plain fact that traumatic encephalopathy syndrome is a research diagnosis; a structured assessment that guards against the documented bias toward diagnosing schizophrenia in Black men; a structured safety assessment with the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline; aripiprazole when psychosis persists, with metabolic and movement monitoring under the APA guideline and a taper after remission; contingency management and cognitive behavioral therapy as the ASAM/AAAP guideline advises, since no medicine is approved by the FDA for cocaine use disorder; fentanyl in the stimulant supply, naloxone and fentanyl test strips; the cardiovascular risks of cocaine with an ECG, creatine kinase, home blood pressure, a diagnosis of hypertension and an echocardiogram that measures the aortic root; a one-day return to use met without blame; involving family only with his consent; and help with financial stress through social work and free taxpayer services.
