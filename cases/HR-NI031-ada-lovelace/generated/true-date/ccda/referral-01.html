  • Augusta Ada Byron was born on December 10, 1815, in Piccadilly, London, the daughter of the poet Lord Byron and Anne Isabella Byron. [D]
  • She was often ill as a child. In 1829, at 13, she caught measles, from which she took a long while to recover; she was bedridden for a whole year. [D]

  • Her doctors gave her painkillers such as laudanum and opium; she reportedly had mood swings and hallucinations. [D]
  • Between 1842 and 1843 she translated L. F. Menabrea's article on Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine and added her own extensive Notes, published in 1843 under the initials A.A.L. The Notes, which include a method for computing Bernoulli numbers on the engine, are often described as the first published computer program. [D]
  • Her health, always fragile, declined sharply after 1843. By the end of that year she was taking several medicines for different health problems; in 1844 she wrote that a recent illness had left her 'utterly unable to think even of my studies', and she thought of writing a scientific study of the effects of opium and wine based on her own experience. In the 1840s she had what she called heart or rheumatic attacks. [D]
  • Gambling on horses was a passion of her later years, and she pawned some of her jewels to finance it. [D]
  • By 1851 she was extremely sick with uterine cancer. [D]
