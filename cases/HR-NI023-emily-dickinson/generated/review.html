Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Index age. The episode presents her at 54, in the period of ill health that the museum dates from late 1883 to her death, when her letters mention severe headache and nausea. The synthetic date of birth is December 10, 1971, so the whole-year shift is 141 years. The index home visit is January 12, 2026 (true frame January 12, 1885). The chronic-care visits follow on February 2, March 2, June 1, and September 28, 2026; the sick visit is April 20, 2026; the preventive visit is October 19, 2026 (true frame February 2, March 2, April 20, June 1, September 28, and October 19, 1885). She died in May 1886, so all fall within her lifetime. The visit type is `preventive_adult` because she is under 65. The visits are set at home, by video, or on two combined clinic trips, which does not conflict with her documented life at the Homestead.
  2. Bright's disease and the kidney focus. That her physician entered Bright's disease on her death certificate is documented (D). What that broad nineteenth-century label meant in her case is retrospective: the museum reports the conclusion that she died of heart failure caused by severe hypertension and notes the absence of documented signs of nephritis (R, possible). No blood pressure or kidney test from her lifetime exists. The stage G3a A2 chronic kidney disease and the hypertension are synthetic; both problems are placed both_with_link to these claims, and the record does not claim that she had chronic kidney disease. Heart failure is not added to the synthetic episode.
  3. Eyes. Her eye treatment in Boston and Cambridgeport in 1864 and 1865 is documented (D). The diagnosis is not recorded; iritis, exotropia, a psychiatric cause, and hysterical hyperaesthesia of the retina are retrospective proposals (R, disputed), so the past medical history entry is graded R and no eye diagnosis is coded. The modern episode adds only a yearly hypertensive eye examination and a negative eye review.
  4. Withdrawal, anxiety, and mood. Her withdrawal from social life is documented (D). Whether it was a choice or had a medical cause such as an anxiety condition is disputed (R, sensitive), and agoraphobia and social anxiety are not used as labels. The synthetic PHQ-9 (6, then 3) and GAD-7 (5, then 3) stay below the thresholds for a diagnosis, so no anxiety or depressive disorder is coded, no F code is used, and no safety assessment is required. The care plan respects her wish to stay at home and does not treat it as a disorder. The epilepsy hypothesis (R, disputed) is recorded only as a rejected interpretation.

The index visit, the sick visit, and the three-month review are home visits; the four-week and six-month reviews are video visits with vital signs from her own validated upper-arm cuff (checked against the clinic device at the first clinic trip), scale, thermometer, and pulse oximeter, the respiratory rate counted on video, and blood drawn at home by a mobile phlebotomy service; the height is the measured clinic value. Cardiac auscultation is recorded as deferred at the two video visits. The clinic is used only twice, each time for several tasks on one quiet early appointment. This echoes the documented difficulty her physician had in examining her (D) without attributing any statement to him.
