- By her own account an eye affliction began in the fall of 1863; in February 1864 she consulted the Boston ophthalmologist Henry Willard Williams, lived in Cambridgeport from April to December 1864 to be near him for treatment, and repeated the treatment from April to October 1865, after which she was apparently cured. **D**
- The treatment required confinement in dim light, no reading, and writing only in pencil; she called her first stay in Cambridge eight months of Siberia. **D**
- No record of the eye diagnosis survives. Her main symptoms were sensitivity to light, aching eyes, and a restricted ability to do close work; iritis is considered the most likely explanation, and exotropia, a psychiatric cause, and hysterical hyperaesthesia of the retina have also been proposed. **R**
