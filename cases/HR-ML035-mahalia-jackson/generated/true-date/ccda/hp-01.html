  • Mahalia Jackson was born in New Orleans on October 26, 1911, the third of six children of a man who was a longshoreman by day, a barber by night and a clergyman on Sunday. Her mother died when she was six, and she was raised by an aunt in a poor but respectable home; she left school in the eighth grade to work as a cook and washerwoman. [D]
  • In 1928 she moved to Chicago, worked as a hotel maid and laundress, and joined the Greater Salem Baptist Church, where she became a soloist. She never learned to read music. After her Carnegie Hall debut on October 4, 1950, she began touring abroad in 1952, and toured widely for the rest of her life. [D]
  • She was closely associated with the civil rights movement and sang at the March on Washington in 1963 before Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.'s speech. When she moved into an all-white neighborhood on the South Side of Chicago, a bullet was fired through a window of her home. [D]
  • Touring the segregated South for as many as 200 dates a year, she and her company had trouble finding safe places to sleep, eat and buy gas, sometimes slept in her car, lived on bags of fruit and drove half the night, and she arrived so exhausted she was almost dizzy when she sang; she insisted on being paid in cash because checks were often refused. [D]
  • Before and during her first European tour in 1952 she had difficulty breathing during and after performances and severe abdominal cramping, sang many shows in pain, lost a great deal of weight and finally cancelled the tour. On her return she had a hysterectomy, at which numerous granulomas were found in her abdomen, and she was diagnosed with sarcoidosis. From then on she had occasional bouts of fatigue and shortness of breath that became more frequent as her schedule filled. [D]
