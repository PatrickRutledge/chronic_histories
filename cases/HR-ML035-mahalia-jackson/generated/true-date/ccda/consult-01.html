Assessment

  • Pulmonary sarcoidosis with lymph node involvement (D86.2). Sarcoidosis is confirmed to the standard the ATS guideline asks: a compatible clinical and radiologic picture, non-necrotizing granulomas in lymph nodes obtained by EBUS rather than mediastinoscopy, and no evidence so far of tuberculosis, fungal infection or lymphoma, with final cultures to follow. The lavage lymphocytosis with a high CD4 to CD8 ratio supports it. It is Scadding stage II, with mild restriction and a reduced DLCO, and she is symptomatic. The granulomas reported at her abdominal operation are consistent with long-standing disease. Sarcoidosis is more common in Black women in the United States and carries a higher risk of severe and fatal disease, so a complete organ screen matters. Serum ACE adds nothing to the diagnosis and is not needed. [S]
  • Hypercalcemia (E83.52). Granulomas convert vitamin D to its active form, as the high 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D with a low 25-hydroxyvitamin D shows. The supplement made it worse. No calcium or vitamin D supplements until the calcium is normal on treatment; then vitamin D may be replaced cautiously with calcium checks. Bone protection on glucocorticoids should use a bisphosphonate, not calcium tablets. [S]
  • Palpitations with first-degree atrioventricular block and ventricular ectopy (R00.2). Unexplained conduction disease and ventricular ectopy in a patient with proven extracardiac sarcoidosis means cardiac sarcoidosis must be excluded. The HRS consensus and the AHA statement advise Holter monitoring, an echocardiogram, cardiac MRI with late gadolinium enhancement and cardiac FDG-PET. The PET must be done before any glucocorticoid, which would mask active inflammation, and needs a 72-hour high-fat, very-low-carbohydrate preparation. [S]

Data reviewed: Dr. Fontenot's intake note and results. Chest radiograph: bilateral hilar adenopathy with upper-zone reticulonodular opacities (Scadding stage II). High-resolution CT: hilar, subcarinal and right paratracheal nodes up to 2.4 cm with fine calcification; perilymphatic micronodules in both upper lobes; no fibrosis. EBUS-guided aspiration of stations 4R, 7 and 11L: non-necrotizing granulomas; acid-fast and fungal stains negative; cultures pending; lavage lymphocytes 32 percent with a CD4 to CD8 ratio of 5.2. Interferon-gamma release assay and HIV negative. ECG: sinus rhythm, PR 240 ms, frequent unifocal ventricular ectopy. Pulmonary function today: FVC 74 percent, FEV1 76 percent, FEV1/FVC 80 percent, total lung capacity 78 percent, DLCO 62 percent of predicted. Calcium 10.7 mg/dL today off the supplement (10.8 at the first visit); creatinine 1.04 mg/dL; 25-hydroxyvitamin D 22 ng/mL; 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D 84 pg/mL; alkaline phosphatase 128 U/L; lymphocytes 0.9; NT-proBNP 210 pg/mL. Plan of Treatment

  • Complete the screen for other organ involvement: ophthalmology examination now and yearly; calcium, creatinine and liver tests at each visit; the ECG is done. [S]
  • Cardiac evaluation within two weeks and before any treatment: 24-hour Holter, echocardiogram, cardiac MRI with late gadolinium enhancement, and cardiac FDG-PET with dietary preparation; joint review with cardiology. [S]
  • If cardiac sarcoidosis is confirmed: prednisone 40 mg daily for about two months, then a slow taper over about six months toward 5 to 10 mg, with methotrexate 15 mg weekly rising to 20 mg and folic acid, continued for at least a year; if the heart is not involved, the same steroid-sparing approach for symptomatic stage II disease with impaired lung function, at a lower prednisone dose. [S]
  • If cardiac sarcoidosis is confirmed with an ejection fraction above 35 percent, an electrophysiology study, and a dual-chamber defibrillator if sustained ventricular tachycardia is induced, given the conduction disease; a beta-blocker for the ventricular ectopy. [S]
  • Before immunosuppression: hepatitis B and C serology (drawn today), tuberculosis already excluded by the interferon-gamma release assay, and PCV20, recombinant zoster and, if non-immune, hepatitis B vaccines; no live vaccines on treatment. [S]
  • On treatment: pneumocystis prophylaxis with atovaquone while prednisone is 20 mg or more with methotrexate, avoiding trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole; a bone density scan and a bisphosphonate; glucose, blood pressure, weight, sleep and mood checks; methotrexate blood tests every two to four weeks for three months, then every eight to twelve weeks; no alcohol. [S]
  • Response assessment: lung function about two months after starting and then every three to six months; repeat cardiac FDG-PET three to six months into treatment. [S]
  • Performing and travel: postpone concerts until the cardiac tests and any device implant are done, then resume a lighter schedule with rest days; voice care; carry a medicine list and any device card when travelling. [S]
