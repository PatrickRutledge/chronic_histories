Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Recent-death rule. She died in 1972, so the rule does not apply. The claims are still limited to what the fetched sources state. The Library of Congress, the Historic New Orleans Collection and the Mahalia Jackson Foundation pages, and the biographies by Laurraine Goreau and by Darden, were not read directly; the health history from Goreau is taken from Wikipedia’s summary, with TheWrap as a second source where it agrees, and is graded D with certainty “probable.”
  2. When sarcoidosis was diagnosed. The assignment described a diagnosis “reportedly in 1964.” The sources put the first diagnosis earlier: after the 1952 European tour, when a hysterectomy found granulomas (CLM-0005; Wikipedia and TheWrap). The 1964 admission is when, by Goreau’s account, her doctors told her privately of a heart attack and that “sarcoidosis was now in her heart” (CLM-0006). The modern episode therefore makes the first tissue diagnosis in a patient told years before that she had sarcoidosis, with no records, and finds cardiac involvement; this reconciles the two accounts.
  3. Index age and dates. The synthetic date of birth is October 26, 1973 (shift 62 years). The index visit on February 9, 2026 maps to February 9, 1964, when she was 52, in the year of the documented cardiac admission. All visits map to February to October 1964, within her lifetime; they are not a claim about where she was. The 1964 admission has no month in the sources, so its timeline event is dated “1964 to 1965” (it began a year of recovery) and its claims do not appear in the intake documents, which show only the 1952 history and earlier events.
  4. Cause of death and cardiac sarcoidosis (R). The sources disagree on the cause of death: a heart seizure (The New York Times), heart failure (PBS), a heart attack (Biography.com), heart complications at home (New Orleans Historical, which also conflicts on the place), and death in recovery after surgery for a bowel obstruction that TheWrap attributes to sarcoidosis (CLM-0011, disputed). That her heart disease was cardiac sarcoidosis rests on her biographer’s account of what her doctors told her in private and is recorded as a retrospective interpretation (CLM-0012, possible). The modern cardiac diagnosis is linked to both and resolves neither.
  5. Later symptoms. The eye inflammation and the cramps in her legs and hands by the late 1960s (CLM-0008) are recorded as stated; that the eye inflammation was sarcoid uveitis is not claimed. Her later high blood pressure and the diabetes that some sources mention are not claimed: the high blood pressure postdates the index date, and no fetched source documents diabetes.
  6. Weight and diet. Wikipedia records that she gained weight on tour and lost about 50 pounds during the year after 1964; TheWrap records the 50-pound loss. The modern record measures her weight and BMI (about 32) at each visit as any record would, without a weight-related diagnosis or comment on her body, and discusses diet only for steroid-related glucose and blood pressure. This is a deliberate choice to avoid stigma.
  7. Presenting problems. Exertional breathlessness and cough with hilar adenopathy (R06.09) is resolved at the consultation on February 23, 2026, when pulmonary sarcoidosis with lymph node involvement (D86.2, both_with_link) is recorded after EBUS. Palpitations with conduction disease (R00.2) is resolved on March 9, 2026, when cardiac sarcoidosis (D86.85, both_with_link) is recorded. Hypercalcemia (E83.52) resolves on treatment at the May visit. The intake documents state only what was known then.
  8. Synthetic clinical picture. All visits, results, imaging, pathology, the cardiac findings, the electrophysiology study and defibrillator, the regimen (prednisone 40 mg tapered to 7.5 mg; methotrexate 15 then 20 mg weekly), her height (170 cm) and weights are synthetic. Qualitative results (stains, cultures, lavage, interferon-gamma release assay, HIV and hepatitis tests, Holter, echocardiogram, MRI, PET, device checks, rapid viral tests) are in note and procedure text only.
  9. Defibrillator. An ICD was chosen for an ejection fraction of 47 percent with scar, nonsustained ventricular tachycardia and inducible sustained ventricular tachycardia, a Class IIa indication in the HRS consensus; dual chamber because of the conduction disease. This is a clinical judgment call for review, and the advance care planning records her wishes about deactivation.
  10. Methotrexate and pneumocystis. Atovaquone was chosen for pneumocystis prophylaxis instead of trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole because of the interaction with methotrexate; prophylaxis stopped when prednisone fell below 20 mg. Judgment call for review.
  11. Calcium and vitamin D. Calcium supplements were stopped and avoided because of granuloma-driven hypercalcemia, bone protection used alendronate, and vitamin D was replaced at 1000 units daily only after calcium was normal, with calcium rechecked.
  12. Sick visit. Acute viral laryngitis on prednisone and methotrexate (J04.0), chosen because it is common, matters greatly to a singer, interacts with immunosuppression (no thrush, no fever, viral tests negative, treatment continued), and insinuates no undocumented real diagnosis. A concert was postponed with a promoter letter that gives no medical details.
  13. Preventive visit. A periodic adult preventive visit at 52 (CPT 99396 and 99497 in plain text): influenza and COVID-19 vaccines; PCV20, two recombinant zoster doses and the two-dose hepatitis B series already complete; mammogram ordered; colonoscopy up to date; cervical screening not indicated because examination showed no cervix after the documented hysterectomy (a synthetic finding; the extent of her real operation is not known); prediabetes with a diabetes prevention program offered; lipids without a statin; and advance care planning with an unnamed agent, including defibrillator deactivation.
  14. Focused visits. The four chronic-care follow-ups (the consultation, the cardiac diagnosis and start of treatment, nine weeks, and about six months) are documented as focused visits on the ATS 2020, ERS 2021, HRS 2014, AHA 2024, ACR glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis and ACR methotrexate guidance, the CMID osteoporosis monograph and the CDC schedule.
