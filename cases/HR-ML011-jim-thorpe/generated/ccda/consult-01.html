Assessment

  • Squamous cell carcinoma of the left lower lip (C00.1). Invasive squamous cell carcinoma of the lower lip, 1.2 cm, with no palpable nodes: clinical T1 N0 (AJCC 8th edition, lip and oral cavity). Surgery is the preferred treatment. A V-excision with 5 to 10 mm clinical margins and frozen-section control of the margins, closed directly, removes up to a third of the lower lip with good function and appearance. Lower lip cancers of this size rarely spread to the neck; a contrast CT of the neck is reasonable before surgery to stage it, and elective neck dissection is not needed if the final depth of invasion is small, generally under 4 mm, with clear margins. Radiation is an alternative but is not preferred for a small, resectable lesion. [S]
  • Chronic coronary disease with stable angina (I25.119). An excision under local anesthesia is a low-risk operation. With stable angina, an ejection fraction of 55%, and no heart failure, no further cardiac testing is needed. Aspirin should be continued because bleeding from a lip excision is easily controlled, and metoprolol should not be stopped. [S]
