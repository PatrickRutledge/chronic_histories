  • James Francis Thorpe was born a twin on the Sac and Fox reservation near present Prague, Oklahoma, to Hiram and Charlotte Vieux Thorpe. His Sac and Fox name, Wa-tho-huck, means "Bright Path." He is usually considered Sac and Fox and was brought up as a member of the Sac and Fox tribe; he also had Potawatomi, Kickapoo, Menominee, Irish and French ancestry. The Oklahoma Historical Society gives his birth as May 28, 1888, noting that his estate cites 1887; another reference work gives May 22, 1887. [D]
  • His twin brother, Charles, died of pneumonia just short of their eighth birthday; his mother died when he was twelve and his father when he was sixteen. [D]
  • He attended federal boarding schools, including the Sac and Fox Indian Agency School, Haskell Institute, and from 1903 the Carlisle Indian School in Pennsylvania, where he became a track and football star. [D]
  • At the 1912 Olympic Games in Stockholm he won gold medals in the pentathlon and decathlon. In January 1913 the Amateur Athletic Union stripped him of his medals because he had played semiprofessional baseball; in 1982 the International Olympic Committee recognized him as co-winner, and on July 15, 2022 it restored him as sole winner of both events. [D]
  • He played major-league baseball from 1913 to 1919 and professional football into 1928, and was the first president of the American Professional Football Association, the forerunner of the National Football League. Afterwards he worked as a film extra and in construction, as a security guard and ditch digger, at the Ford plant in Dearborn during the Second World War, and in the Merchant Marine in 1945; he became a critic of the Bureau of Indian Affairs, lectured widely, and worked to inspire American Indians. [D]
