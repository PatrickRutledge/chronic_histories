## Editorial resolutions

- **Recent-death rule.** He died in 1953, so the rule does not apply. Every historical claim is limited to what the fetched sources state.
- **Respect and identity.** He is the panel’s first Indigenous patient. His identity is described in the sources’ terms (born on the Sac and Fox reservation, usually considered Sac and Fox, brought up as a member of the Sac and Fox tribe, with mixed ancestry), and his name Wa-tho-huck is given as the Oklahoma Historical Society spells it. The nationality field records “Sac and Fox Nation” as assigned; his enrollment was not confirmed in a fetched source, and the modern social history states his enrollment as part of the synthetic episode. The modern episode asks his preferences rather than assuming them: he chose to keep medical care at the clinic, took information about Sac and Fox Nation and Indian Health Service programs, and later chose, himself, to use an urban Indian health program for dental care. Traditional or spiritual practices are offered only as room for what he chooses, and are not described.
- **Birth date.** Three options were found: May 28, 1888 (Oklahoma Historical Society, day precision), 1887 (his estate, as reported by the Oklahoma Historical Society, year precision), and May 22, 1887 (Scribner Encyclopedia of American Lives, day precision). May 28, 1888 is used because it comes from the state historical society of his home state and agrees with his age of 64 at death in the 1953 and 2012 reports; the others are recorded as conflicting alternatives. Secondary search results attribute May 22, 1887 to a baptismal certificate and May 28, 1888 to his own statement; neither document was fetched. The synthetic date of birth keeps May 28.
- **Death.** March 28, 1953, in Lomita, California, is agreed by the sources; the cause is given as a heart attack (Scribner, TIME) or heart failure (Smithsonian). It is recorded in CLM-0009 as history only.
- **Lip cancer.** Two sources date his operation for lip cancer to 1951 (TIME, 1953; Smithsonian, 2012) as a charity patient in a Philadelphia hospital. A date of 1950 appears only in a stamp-dealer page and an auction listing and was not used; the hospital’s name was not found. The type of cancer is not recorded, so squamous cell carcinoma, its size, stage, margins and pathology in the modern episode are synthetic. Anchor (a) of the assignment was therefore used, with coronary disease as the secondary problem.
- **Heart disease.** TIME records heart attacks in 1943 and 1952. The 1943 attack is in the past medical history (D) and is the anchor for the modern “heart attack eight years ago”; the 1952 attack is after the index date and is recorded as history only (CLM-0011). The modern stable angina, ECG, echocardiogram and lipids are synthetic.

CLM-0006 is **D** with certainty “probable” and is marked sensitive. The 1953 TIME article’s wording is not reproduced.
