  • Audre Lorde was born Audrey Geraldine Lorde on February 18, 1934, in New York City; her parents were immigrants from Grenada. [D]
  • Lorde was a poet, essayist, and novelist. In her poetry and prose she wrote about matters important to her as a woman of color, as a lesbian, as a mother, and as a feminist; a reference biography describes her as an African American lesbian feminist and poet. [D]
  • In 1977 a doctor found a tumor in Lorde's breast that, after about three weeks of uncertainty, proved benign. In a talk given that year to the Modern Language Association she said: "Your silence will not protect you." [D]
  • On Labor Day 1978, during a routine self-examination, Lorde found a lump in her right breast; a diagnosis of malignancy and a mastectomy followed. She described the experience in The Cancer Journals. [D]
  • After her mastectomy Lorde chose never to hide the fact of her missing breast. She refused to wear the "wad of lambswool pressed into a pale pink breast-shaped pad" pushed on her by nurses and medical counselors, and she was repeatedly scolded for posing a threat to cancer patient "morale."
