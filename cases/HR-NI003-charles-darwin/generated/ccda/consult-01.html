History of Present Illness

Mr. Darwin, a 40-year-old writer and naturalist, is seen at the request of Dr. Ashworth for more than a year of stereotyped vomiting episodes. His diary shows three episodes in the six weeks since his first visit, each 18 to 30 hours, after a prodrome of nausea, sweating, trembling, palpitations, and a swimming head lasting half an hour to an hour. Vomiting is frequent at the peak, then he sleeps. Between episodes he does not vomit, though he has wind and tires by the afternoon. Episodes follow social evenings, travel, short nights, and missed meals. He has no headaches with or between episodes, no dizziness on standing, and no weight loss, nocturnal pain, or blood. He has never used cannabis, by any route, and does not bathe compulsively. He drinks little alcohol and takes no medicines. He worries about being taken ill in company and has begun to avoid gatherings. [S]

Assessment

  • Cyclic vomiting syndrome, provisional (R11.15). He meets the Rome IV criteria: stereotyped episodes of acute-onset vomiting lasting less than a week, well over three in the past year and two in the past six months, at least a week apart, with no vomiting between episodes, and onset more than three months ago. The prodrome, the triggers, and relief with sleep in a dark room are typical. Cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome is excluded by his history. With episodes every one to three weeks that stop his work, it is moderate to severe. The diagnosis is clinical, with no confirmatory test, so it is recorded as provisional and should be revisited if the pattern changes. [S]
  • Work-up. Complete. The blood and urine tests, CT enterography, and upper endoscopy exclude obstruction, malrotation, mucosal disease, pancreatitis, celiac disease, and thyroid, adrenal, and metabolic causes. A gastric emptying study is not needed, as he has no symptoms of gastroparesis between episodes, and brain imaging is not needed without headache or neurological signs. Repeat testing is not advised unless an episode is atypical. [S]
  • Prophylaxis. A tricyclic is first line for moderate-to-severe disease. Amitriptyline at about 1 to 1.5 mg/kg is the usual effective dose, which for him is 75 to 100 mg at bedtime. His baseline QTc of 412 ms allows it. Coenzyme Q10 and riboflavin are conditional options that can be added later if the response is partial; topiramate or aprepitant are alternatives if amitriptyline fails or is not tolerated. [S]
  • Abortive treatment. Nasal sumatriptan with an ondansetron dissolving tablet, taken together within the first 30 to 45 minutes of the prodrome, works best. He has no heart disease, uncontrolled hypertension, or stroke that would rule out a triptan. The serotonin syndrome risk with amitriptyline is low at these doses, and he was told its warning signs. [S]
  • Anxiety and triggers. Mild anxiety (GAD-7 8) centered on episodes, without low mood. Anxiety and stress both trigger episodes and follow from them, so relaxation training and cognitive behavioral therapy are worthwhile alongside medicine. No anxiety disorder is diagnosed. [S]
