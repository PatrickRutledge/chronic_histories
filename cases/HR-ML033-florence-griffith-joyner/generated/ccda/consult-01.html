Assessment

  • Focal epilepsy with structural cause (G40.209). The seizure was focal in onset, from the left orbitofrontal cortex, and spread to both hemispheres (focal to bilateral tonic-clonic). The EEG discharges match the site of the cavernous malformation. Under the ILAE 2014 practical definition, one unprovoked seizure with an epileptogenic lesion and matching discharges is epilepsy, because the risk of another seizure exceeds 60%; her earlier seizures add to that. I recommend starting an antiseizure medication now. For a woman who could become pregnant, levetiracetam or lamotrigine carries the lowest risk of birth defects; levetiracetam reaches an effective dose at once and does not interact with hormonal contraception, while lamotrigine needs about six weeks of titration and its level falls with estrogen. Valproate should be avoided. Levetiracetam can cause irritability or low mood, which should be asked about at each visit. She should take folic acid 0.4 mg daily and plan any pregnancy with the epilepsy team. SUDEP should be discussed with her at diagnosis; today's visit did not allow a full discussion, so I have asked that it be the first item at her next primary care visit: SUDEP is uncommon but real, tonic-clonic seizures are the main risk, and freedom from them is the best protection. [S]
  • Solitary cerebral cavernous malformation, left orbitofrontal (Q28.3). A single cavernous malformation with a developmental venous anomaly is the sporadic form, so familial genetic testing is not needed. Because it is the source of her epilepsy and lies in accessible cortex, neurosurgical evaluation is recommended: complete removal with the surrounding hemosiderin rim gives a high chance of freedom from seizures, and early surgery is associated with better seizure outcomes than surgery after years of seizures. [S]
