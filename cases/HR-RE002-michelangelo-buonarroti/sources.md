## Historical evidence

1. Symonds JA. *The Life of Michelangelo Buonarroti* (1893). Project Gutenberg eBook 11242. Supports his father's notebook entry recording his birth at Caprese on March 6, 1474, Florentine style (1475 by the Roman reckoning); his father Lodovico's birth on June 11, 1444, and death in 1533 or 1534, at ninety by Michelangelo's poem; two acute illnesses between the summer of 1544 and the close of 1545; the 1548 letter on dysuria ("I have lately been in great difficulty from dysuria"); the 1549 letters on calculus and the mineral waters; the 1557 letter on the stone and stiff loins and back; the postscript "Writing is the greatest annoyance to my hand, my sight, my brains"; the letters of February 14 to 18, 1564, describing his last illness; and his death a little before five in the afternoon of February 18, 1564, with two physicians attending. https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11242
2. Carden RW, translator and editor. *Michelangelo: A Record of His Life as Told in His Own Letters and Papers*. London: Constable; 1913. Internet Archive. Supports his letters to his nephew of March 15, 1549 (dysuria, "groaning in pain night and day without sleep," the doctors think "I am suffering from calculus," advised "to try the Baths at Viterbo"); March 23, 1549 (after drinking a water he passed "a large quantity of thick white matter together with portions of the shell of the stone"); April 5, 1549 ("quite certain that I am suffering from urinary calculus, but it is not very advanced"); a letter the editor dates June 8, 1549 (two months of a water "which breaks up the stone" from a spring about forty miles from Rome, to be used for all drinking and cooking); June 16, 1557 ("the stone, so that I cannot pass water; pains in my side and back, so that oftentimes I cannot go up or downstairs"); and the 1559 postscript "Writing is very troublesome to my hand, my eyes and my memory. Such is the effect of age!" https://archive.org/details/michelangeloreco1913mich
3. Lazzeri D, Castello MF, Matucci-Cerinic M, Lippi D, Weisz GM. "Osteoarthritis in the hands of Michelangelo Buonarroti." *J R Soc Med* 2016;109(5):180–183. PMID 26837259; PMC4872204. From portraits of about 1535, 1544, and about 1595, the authors propose degenerative, non-inflammatory arthritis of the left thumb carpometacarpal, metacarpophalangeal, and interphalangeal joints and the index metacarpophalangeal and proximal interphalangeal joints; earlier portraits (1509 to 1512) show no deformity; onset around 1547 to 1553 with worsening in his last fifteen years; letters to his nephew speak of "gout" and a cruel pain in one foot; gouty arthritis of the hands is judged unlikely; and he was seen hammering up to six days before his death. Full text read. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26837259/
4. HISTMED Master Roster v3. Internal project source. Its clinical claims were provisional and were not treated as independent corroboration.

## Editorial resolutions

- **Birth date.** The preferred date is March 6, 1475. His father's notebook gives March 6, 1474, because the Florentine year began on March 25; it is recorded as a superseded alternative. The synthetic date of birth keeps March 6.
- **Index age.** The episode presents him at 85, after his 1559 postscript that writing was very troublesome to his hand, and within the last fifteen years of life in which the 2016 study places the worsening of his hand disease. The synthetic date of birth is March 6, 1941, so the whole-year shift is 466 years. The index visit is March 16, 2026 (true frame March 16, 1560); the chronic-care visits follow on April 13, July 13, and October 19, 2026 (true frame April 13, July 13, and October 19, 1560); the sick visit is June 1, 2026 (true frame June 1, 1560) and the annual wellness visit September 14, 2026 (true frame September 14, 1560). He died in 1564, so all fall within his lifetime. The visit type is `annual_wellness_subsequent` because he is 65 or older.
- **Hand osteoarthritis.** The diagnosis is a retrospective interpretation of portraits (**R**, possible) and is offered by its authors as a plausible reading, not a proof. It is the primary teaching condition. The modern problem is a synthetic diagnosis confirmed by examination and radiographs, placed on the active list with a provenance link to the claim but not as a historical diagnosis; its joint pattern follows the 2016 study. His difficulty writing is documented in his own letters (**D**). The right-hand disease is synthetic, because the portraits show only the left hand.
- **Gout.** The 2016 study states that his letters to his nephew speak of gout and of a cruel pain in one foot. Neither passage was found in Carden's 1913 translation or in Symonds, so gout is graded **R**, possible, as a reading of a period term. The modern gout, crystal-proven in the synthetic past and in remission on allopurinol, is synthetic and secondary, and it is kept quiet because another record in the dataset teaches gout and stones.
- **Stone.** His urinary calculus, the mineral-water treatment, the passed fragments, and the later trouble passing water are documented in his own letters (**D**). Their composition and site are not known. The modern record holds only a history of urinary stones, stone-free on fluids and allopurinol; no stone event is added.
- **No measured values.** No measured height, weight, blood pressure, or laboratory value from his lifetime exists; every modern value is synthetic.
- **Hand function measure.** The CMID and the 2019 guideline name AUSCAN or FIHOA for hand osteoarthritis. Neither has a LOINC code, so the record uses the 0 to 10 pain rating (LOINC 72514-3), the PROMIS upper extremity v2.0 T-score (91612-2), and dynamometer grip strength (83173-5, 83174-3), and says so in the notes.
- **Working with his hands.** The 2016 study reports that he was seen hammering up to six days before his death and suggests that continued work helped him keep the use of his hands. The care plan keeps him carving, with a splint, exercises, tool changes, and rest breaks, rather than advising him to stop.
- **Family history.** Only his father's age at death is recorded; the cause is not in the source. His nephew appears in the sources as the recipient of his letters; no health fact or decision is attributed to him or to any other relative. The modern social history mentions "a nephew he writes to often" as a support only.
- **Not used.** His other illnesses in Symonds are recorded only as the two acute illnesses of 1544 and 1545, with their nature not recorded. Symonds calls the illness of February 14, 1564, "a kind of seizure"; this is recorded as the friend's description, and no stroke or other cause is asserted. The physicians and friends named in the letters are not named in the record. The 1552 letter on writing quoted in the 2016 study was not located in Carden's translation and is not used. Michelangelo died in 1564, so the recent-death review rule does not apply.
- **Sick and wellness visits.** The sick visit is a simple chisel laceration of the left index finger, chosen because weak pinch grip from thumb-base osteoarthritis makes tool work less safe and because it teaches wound repair, tetanus boosting, and the role of the splint. It does not echo a documented injury. The wellness visit orders a DXA; no result is recorded within the episode. Its advance care planning names no real person.
- **Focused visits.** The three chronic-care follow-ups (four weeks, four months, and seven months) are documented as focused visits on the 2019 ACR/Arthritis Foundation guideline and the CMID osteoarthritis monograph, with the ACR gout and AUA stone guidelines for the secondary problems. Two deviations are recorded as `deferred`, each with its outcome and corrective action, and no result was invented: occupational hand therapy, requested at intake, had not started four weeks later because of a waiting list (it began nine weeks after the request); and the pain and function reassessment four to six weeks after the thumb-base injection was not done (only a nurse telephone call at five weeks).
- **Consultation.** The rheumatology referral and consultation are synthetic. The consultant's thumb-base injection is the record's procedure on the consultation day, and its findings agree with the same-day primary care note. Its recommendations match what followed: no repeat injection, topical diclofenac and acetaminophen without oral NSAIDs, the splint and hand therapy, no disease-modifying drugs or repeat radiographs, and allopurinol unchanged. The recommended four-to-six-week reassessment did not happen and is recorded as a deviation at the July visit.
