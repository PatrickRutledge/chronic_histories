## Assessment

1. **Primary osteoarthritis of the left hand, nodal and non-erosive, worst at the thumb base** (M19.042). Confirmed. The bony swelling without synovitis, normal inflammatory markers, negative rheumatoid factor and anti-CCP, and the radiographs fit osteoarthritis. Metacarpophalangeal disease here follows a lifetime of heavy hand use; there is no chondrocalcinosis or hook-like osteophyte to suggest calcium pyrophosphate disease, and normal iron studies make hemochromatosis arthropathy unlikely. **S**
2. **Mild nodal osteoarthritis of the right hand** (M19.041). Typical Heberden nodes; little pain. **S**
3. **Gout in remission** (M1A.9XX0). Not contributing: no tophi, no warmth, and urate at target. No change to allopurinol is needed. **S**
4. **Analgesic safety**. Regular oral NSAIDs are best avoided at his age because of the kidney and blood pressure changes already seen; creatinine is back to 1.02 mg/dL off ibuprofen. Topical diclofenac, acetaminophen up to 3 g a day, and injections are the safer options. **S**

## Recommendations

1. Left thumb-base injection with triamcinolone 10 mg given today; no repeat sooner than three months, and no more than three or four a year.
2. Continue diclofenac gel to the painful hand joints and acetaminophen up to 3 g a day; no regular oral NSAIDs; if a short oral course is ever needed, use the lowest dose for the shortest time with a creatinine and blood pressure check.
3. Wear the hand-based thumb splint for carving and at night; start occupational hand therapy for thumb-stabilizing exercises, joint protection, and tool adaptation, such as padded handles and a lighter mallet.
4. Reassess pain and function four to six weeks after the injection.
5. No hydroxychloroquine, other disease-modifying drugs, or oral glucocorticoids, which do not help hand osteoarthritis; duloxetine only if pain stays high despite these measures.
6. No routine repeat radiographs; consider thumb-base surgery only if pain and function fail to respond.
7. Continue allopurinol 200 mg with six-monthly urate checks; no change because of the hand symptoms.
