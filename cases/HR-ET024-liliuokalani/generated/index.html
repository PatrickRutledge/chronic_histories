Teaching objective: A minor ischemic stroke presenting as a transient ischemic attack in a 78-year-old Native Hawaiian elder, composer and community leader in Honolulu. The episode covers same-day evaluation of transient weakness and speech difficulty (ABCD2 score, CT and CT angiography of the head and neck, then diffusion MRI the next morning, which turns a suspected TIA into a minor stroke); a 21-day course of aspirin and clopidogrel started within 24 hours, as in POINT and CHANCE; ECG, echocardiogram and 30-day rhythm monitoring that find paroxysmal atrial fibrillation, and the change from antiplatelets to apixaban with correct dosing; a high-intensity statin to an LDL below 70 mg/dL; a blood pressure target below 130/80 mmHg; swallow, cognition, mood and falls screening; a nosebleed on apixaban; all under the 2021 AHA/ASA secondary stroke prevention guideline and the CMID stroke and atrial fibrillation monographs. It also teaches culturally safe care: asking how she wishes to be addressed, involving her ʻohana as she chooses, offering Native Hawaiian healing such as lomilomi and hoʻoponopono alongside Western care without probing or describing practices, and acknowledging the historical trauma of the overthrow of the Hawaiian Kingdom, which the United States formally apologized for in 1993.
