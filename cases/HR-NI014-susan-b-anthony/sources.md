## Historical evidence

1. U.S. National Park Service. “Susan B. Anthony.” Supports her birth on February 15, 1820, in Adams, Massachusetts; her 1872 vote, arrest, trial at Canandaigua, and unpaid $100 fine; and her death on March 13, 1906, “of heart failure and pneumonia at her Rochester home.” https://home.nps.gov/people/susan-b-anthony.htm
2. Harper IH. *The Life and Work of Susan B. Anthony*, Volume 1. Indianapolis: Bowen-Merrill; 1898. Project Gutenberg eBook 15220. Authorized biography. Supports her birth date and parents, and her father's sudden illness, called acute neuralgia of the stomach, and death on November 25, 1862. https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/15220
3. Harper IH. *The Life and Work of Susan B. Anthony*, Volume 2. Indianapolis: Bowen-Merrill; 1898. Project Gutenberg eBook 31125. Supports the January 1891 collapse with la grippe at the Garrison home in Brookline (“pneumonia threatened”); the July 26, 1895 faint at Lakeside, Ohio, and the month in bed with “nervous prostration,” her first serious illness in seventy-five years; her daily habits; and the family longevity (father at sixty-nine, mother at eighty-six, paternal grandfather past ninety-seven, maternal grandparents in their eighty-fourth year). https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/31125
4. National Susan B. Anthony Museum & House. “Remember Susan B. Anthony on March 13.” Supports her death at forty minutes past midnight on March 13, 1906, aged 86, in her own bed at her Madison Street home. https://susanb.org/?p=2763
5. National Susan B. Anthony Museum & House. “The nurses who attended Susan B. Anthony in her last illness.” Supports her travel to Washington, D.C., in failing health in February 1906, her care at home by Dr. Ricker and two nurses, and her death about three weeks after returning. https://susanb.org/?p=3482

## Editorial resolutions

- **Index age.** Anthony died at 86, so an episode at that age would have to fit between her birthday on February 15 and her death on March 13. The modern episode presents her at 85, one year before her final illness, so that a chronic heart failure course can unfold. The index visit is March 16, 2026 (true frame March 16, 1905) and the last visit is September 28, 2026 (true frame September 28, 1905). Her final illness and death fall after the index date and remain only in the historical timeline.
- **Modern diagnosis.** The NPS records heart failure and pneumonia as the causes of death (**D**). The type of heart failure is not documented. The synthetic episode makes it heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, the most common form in women of her age, and adds synthetic hypertension; no blood pressure from her lifetime appears in the cited sources.
- **Earlier illnesses.** The 1891 la grippe and the 1895 faint with a month of “nervous prostration” are recorded in past medical history with their contemporary labels. Neither is reinterpreted as heart disease.
- **Family history.** The 1898 biography gives the ages at death of her parents and grandparents but no causes except her father's “acute neuralgia of the stomach,” which is reported as written.
- **Not used.** Reports that she had long-standing valvular heart disease, and a press account attributing her death to “heart failure induced by pneumonia of both lungs,” appeared in search summaries and secondary pages but could not be confirmed in a fetched primary or reliable source, so they are not recorded. Volume 3 of the Harper biography, which covers her last years, was not reviewed. The exact place of her “Failure is impossible” remarks in February 1906 is reported inconsistently and is not used.
- Anthony died in 1906, so the recent-death review rule does not apply. Ordinary clinical, provenance, and safe-messaging reviews remain required.
