Assessment

  • Exertional breathlessness and chest tightness (R06.09). Not ischemic. An exercise stress echocardiogram was chosen because it tests for ischemia, measures his working capacity and shows the blood pressure response to effort; it showed no inducible ischemia at about 10.5 METs. The symptoms are best explained by a steep rise in blood pressure on exertion (to 226/98 mmHg) in a thickened, stiff ventricle, made worse by heat; they should settle as blood pressure is controlled. [S]
  • Type of hypertrophy (I11.9). Hypertensive heart disease. The hypertrophy is concentric and modest (septum 13 mm, septal-to-posterior ratio 1.1) with no systolic anterior motion, no outflow gradient, a normal-sized cavity and diastolic dysfunction, in a man with stage 2 hypertension; this fits hypertension and not hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, and an athlete's heart is unlikely at 50 years and years after competitive training. If the hypertrophy does not regress after six to twelve months of good control, or a family history emerges, cardiac MRI would be the next test. [S]
  • Sudden cardiac death risk (I11.9). Left ventricular hypertrophy raises the risk of sudden cardiac death, and controlling blood pressure lowers it as the hypertrophy regresses. He has no fainting, palpitations or arrhythmia on exercise, a normal ejection fraction and a normal QTc, so ambulatory ECG monitoring and an implantable defibrillator are not indicated; monitoring would be needed for palpitations or fainting. Potassium should be kept in the normal range on the diuretic. He should know the warning symptoms, and the venues where he works should have a defibrillator at ringside and staff trained in CPR. [S]
