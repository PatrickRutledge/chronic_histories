Assessment

  • Proliferative diabetic retinopathy with center-involving diabetic macular edema, both eyes (E11.3513). High-risk proliferative retinopathy in both eyes with center-involving macular edema and reduced vision in both. Anti-VEGF injections treat both the edema and the new vessels and are the first treatment; panretinal photocoagulation adds durable control of the proliferative disease and lessens the burden of frequent injections, and is done when the view allows. [S]
  • Vitreous hemorrhage, right eye (H43.11). From the new vessels; the retina is attached and there is no traction, so vitrectomy is not needed now. Most such hemorrhages clear over weeks with anti-VEGF treatment. [S]
  • Type 2 diabetes (E11.65). Better glucose control will protect his eyes over years, but a very rapid fall in A1c can worsen proliferative retinopathy for a time, and semaglutide was associated with more retinopathy complications in a cardiovascular outcomes trial, mainly in people with existing retinopathy and large early A1c falls. A steady fall of about one point every three months is reasonable, and a GLP-1 receptor agonist can be started once the proliferative disease is controlled, with monthly retina review. An SGLT2 inhibitor raises no retinal concern. [S]
