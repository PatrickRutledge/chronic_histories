# Teaching Note — Jackie Robinson pilot

> **SYNTHETIC RECORD, NOT A REAL MEDICAL RECORD**

## Why this case matters

This case shows how historical evidence can support a transparent fictional modern episode without presenting interpretation or generated content as documented fact.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish documented (**D**), retrospective (**R**), and synthetic (**S**) content.
2. Keep historical and active problems distinct.
3. Connect measurable goals and interventions to guidelines.
4. Preserve strengths, function, identity, and agency.

## Destigmatization message

A health condition is one part of a person’s life and does not erase achievement, relationships, purpose, or agency.

## Discussion questions

- Which facts support concern without establishing a modern diagnosis?
- Which entries belong in active problems, historical problems, or both?
- What additional evidence would change an evidence grade?
- How should an EHR distinguish synthetic and historical content?

## Evidence-grade summary

- **D:** Jackie Robinson was born on January 31, 1919, in Cairo, Georgia, to a family of sharecroppers. On April 15, 1947, he played his first major league game for the Brooklyn Dodgers, ending more than sixty years of segregation in major league baseball. His retirement from baseball was announced in January 1957; he then worked in business and as a spokesman and fundraiser for civil rights causes, including the NAACP. He had diabetes and high blood pressure; his death is attributed to heart problems and complications of diabetes. He was diagnosed with diabetes at about 37, around the time he retired from baseball, and at first kept it private; two of his brothers also had diabetes. By 1969, at 50, he had nerve and artery damage in his legs. In 1970 he had two mild strokes. He lost the sight of one eye and had limited vision in the other; by 1972 he had nearly lost his sight and needed a cane to walk. He had several heart attacks; one account counts three, the third of which was fatal. The dates of the earlier ones are not given. His eldest son, Jackie Robinson Jr., was killed in an automobile accident on June 17, 1971, at 24. In his last year he grieved the deaths of his mentor, his mother and his first son. He died of a heart attack at his home in Stamford, Connecticut, on October 24, 1972, at 53.
- **R:** His loss of vision was most likely caused by diabetic retinopathy; the sources attribute his near-blindness to diabetes and its complications without naming the eye disease.
- **S:** Shifted identity, fictional care team, modern encounters, results, plans, and outcomes.
