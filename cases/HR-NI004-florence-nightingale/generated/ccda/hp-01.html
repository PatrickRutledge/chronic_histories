Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Florence Nightingale was born on May 12, 1820, in Florence, Italy, where her parents were on a European tour; her elder sister, Frances Parthenope, was born in 1819. [D]
  • In October 1854 she led a party of thirty-eight nurses from London to the British military hospitals at Scutari, arriving in November 1854. [D]
  • In May 1855, a few days after crossing to Balaclava to inspect the hospitals in the Crimea, she fell ill with what the army doctors called Crimean fever. She was carried to the Castle Hospital, was dangerously ill and, by her own later account, very near to death; she was reported out of danger on May 24 and made a slow and painful recovery before returning to work at Scutari. [D]
  • Modern writers have interpreted her Crimean fever as brucellosis and her long later illness as chronic brucellosis. [R]
  • She returned to England in the summer of 1856, emaciated and tense, and pressed for a royal commission on the health of the army, which was established in 1857. [D]
  • In 1857, while working on her report for the commission, she was overworking and in failing health; she sought rest at Malvern in August and September and again in December, but would not stop working. She collapsed with cardiac symptoms so severe that it was thought she would die, and afterward suffered from nausea, insomnia, and palpitations. On her doctors' orders she took to her bed or couch but continued to work, the beginning of about twenty years of invalidism. [D]
  • Her own 830-page report, Notes on Matters Affecting the Health, Efficiency, and Hospital Administration of the British Army (1858), used statistical evidence to show how much army mortality was due to conditions in the hospitals. She was elected to the Statistical Society, the first woman member. [D]
  • Her prolonged illness has also been read retrospectively as what is now called myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome, while other writers have proposed neurasthenia, a psychosomatic illness, or illness used to secure time for work. [R]
