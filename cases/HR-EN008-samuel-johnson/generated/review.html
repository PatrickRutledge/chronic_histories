Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Birth date and synthetic date of birth. Boswell gives September 18, 1709, New Style; the same day was September 7 in the Julian calendar then used in England, recorded as an alternative. The synthetic date of birth keeps September 18 and is 1970-09-18, a shift of 261 years.
  2. Index age. The modern episode presents him at 55. Boswell places "a very severe return of the hypochondriack disorder" in 1764, the year he turned 55, and says he was never perfectly relieved of the malady. The index visit is April 6, 2026 (true frame April 6, 1765) and the last chronic-care visit is September 28, 2026 (true frame September 28, 1765), so the episode follows the documented 1764 episode. Choosing 1765 rather than 1764 keeps the whole episode after the documented return, whose month Boswell does not give.
  3. Modern diagnoses. The recurrent major depression is synthetic, anchored to the documented episodes of 1729 and 1764, and makes no claim about his real mental health. The Tourette syndrome is a synthetic modern diagnosis anchored to Boswell's descriptions; the retrospective diagnosis (Murray 1979, Pearce 1994) is graded R, probable, because it is widely accepted in the medical literature yet unprovable, and historians have examined how it was built. The modern problem is coded F95.2 with verification "confirmed" and placement "both_with_link", because the modern neurologist confirms it by present-day criteria; the presenting tic concern (F95.9) resolves into it at the consultation. The blindness of one eye is documented; Boswell does not say which eye, so the left eye is a synthetic choice.
  4. Not verified, not used. Hearing loss from the childhood scrofula could not be confirmed in a fetched source and is not recorded. The roster's labels of obesity and obsessive-compulsive disorder, and its statement that he coined a phrase for his depression, were not confirmed and are not used; the modern episode records mild tic-related obsessive-compulsive symptoms without a separate diagnosis. The year of the royal touch is not given in the abridged Boswell and is recorded only as early childhood. Accounts of a breakdown in 1766 and of his care by friends at that time were not fetched and are not recorded.
  5. People in his life. Contemporaries appear only in documented claims: Boswell, Reynolds, Pope, Dr. Adams, Dr. Swinfen, Sir John Floyer, and his wife and father. The modern episode attributes no decision, statement, or health fact to any real person; his household and friends in the modern frame are unnamed and synthetic, and the advance care planning names no real person as his agent. No other figure with a record in the dataset is named.
  6. Stigma. The ridicule he faced is recorded only through Boswell's documented accounts. The modern social history notes that strangers have stared at and ridiculed his tics, linked to that claim.
  7. Safety assessment. The depression and tic disorder codes and the PHQ-9 of 21 require a structured safety assessment; it records no current suicidal ideation, and safety is reviewed at every visit.
  8. Sick and preventive visits. A periodic adult preventive visit (age 55) on July 27, 2026 (true frame July 27, 1765) and a same-day sick visit on August 31, 2026 (true frame August 31, 1765) were added. The sick visit is an acute low back strain, chosen because treating pain in a patient on sertraline teaches the bleeding risk of NSAIDs with an SSRI and the serotonergic risk of tramadol and cyclobenzaprine; it does not echo any documented illness.
  9. Focused visits. The four chronic-care follow-ups (two weeks, six weeks, eleven weeks, and six months) are documented as focused visits on the CMID depression monograph and the AAN tic guideline. Yale Global Tic Severity Scale and Yale-Brown scores are recorded in the notes only, without a LOINC code, because none was found. One protocol deviation is recorded: there was no reassessment two weeks after the sertraline dose increase, with no harm and a corrective action.
  10. Consultation. The neurology consultation by the fictional Dr. Hassan on the second visit is synthetic. Its recommendations (CBIT as initial treatment, no tic medication now, continued sertraline, tic severity scales at three and six months, and psychoeducation) are those the later visits carry out.
  11. Johnson died in 1784, so the recent-death review rule does not apply. Ordinary clinical, provenance, and safe-messaging reviews remain required.
