Teaching objective: Recognize multiple sclerosis in a professional musician of 28 whose numbness and clumsiness of the fingers and fatigue could be put down to overwork or stress, as hers were for years, and care for it on the AAN 1965 disease-modifying therapy guideline, the ECTRIMS/EAN 1965 guideline and NICE NG220: urgent MRI of the brain and spine with gadolinium; diagnosis by the 1964 McDonald criteria (and their 1971 revision), with cerebrospinal fluid oligoclonal bands and exclusion of mimics (vitamin B12, thyroid, aquaporin-4 antibody for a myelitis; MOG antibody only when the picture suggests it); high-dose corticosteroids for a disabling relapse; early high-efficacy disease-modifying therapy, choosing among ocrelizumab, ofatumumab and natalizumab with JCV antibody status, infusion logistics and pregnancy plans; hepatitis B screening, immunoglobulins and vaccinations before B-cell therapy; vitamin D; infection and pseudo-relapse; fatigue; hand function with occupational therapy; and a plan built around her playing and teaching, set against the four years it took to name her illness in the 1970s, when no disease-modifying treatment existed.
