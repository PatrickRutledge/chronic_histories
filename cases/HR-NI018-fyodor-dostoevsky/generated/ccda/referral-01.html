Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • Fyodor Mikhailovich Dostoevsky was born in Moscow on 11 November 1821 and died in St. Petersburg on 9 February 1881. [D]
  • Dostoevsky had epilepsy throughout his literary career; in 1863 he went abroad to seek expert advice from the neurologists Romberg and Trousseau. [D]
  • Accounts of when his epilepsy began and was diagnosed differ. One account places seizures in his student years (1838 to 1843), a generalized tonic-clonic seizure in 1844, and a diagnosis of epilepsy in 1849, shortly before he was taken to the prison at Omsk; another says the first symptoms appeared in early adulthood and the diagnosis came about a decade later; a third calls the onset controversial. [R]
  • He was sentenced to four years of forced labor at the Siberian prison in Omsk; his seizures worsened during his imprisonment, and by 1853 he was quite debilitated by epilepsy. [D]
  • He described lack of sleep, alcohol, and overwork as triggers of his seizures, and he felt that the illness had damaged his memory. [D]
  • The type of his epilepsy is disputed: it has been interpreted as temporolimbic epilepsy, as an idiopathic generalized epilepsy with minor temporal involvement, as a cryptogenic focal epilepsy of frontomedial or temporal origin, and, for the ecstatic onset he described, as insular in origin. [R]
  • Whether the ecstatic experience he described before some seizures, and gave to characters in his fiction, was an epileptic aura is disputed. [R]
  • He portrayed up to six characters with epilepsy in his fiction, from Murin in The Landlady (1847) to Smerdyakov in The Brothers Karamazov (1880). [D]
  • His passion for roulette began on a tour of Europe in 1863. He fell into a pattern of chasing his losses and often pawned belongings so that he could gamble further. He gambled at Wiesbaden, Baden-Baden, and Bad Homburg, and the compulsion caused repeated financial trouble for about ten years. [D]
  • After returning to Russia he wrote the novella The Gambler, set in a fictional spa town called Roulettenburg, in 26 days. [D]
  • One retrospective review links his gambling to his epilepsy: the stress of gambling, together with sleep deprivation, is described as aggravating his seizures. [R]
