Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Sources. The case relies on Dodge (1904) and Alter (1925) read in full on the Internet Archive; Alter reproduces Samuel Parker's 1838 account of the arrowhead operation, an army officer's account, a neighbor's recollection and his daughter's letter. These are supported by the Wyoming State Historical Society (WyoHistory.org), three National Park Service pages (Bighorn Canyon, the Whitman Mission handbook and the Fort Bridger State Historic Site place page), Larsell (1936) quoting Whitman's journal, the Wyoming State Historic Preservation Office, the Wyoming Game and Fish Department, Encyclopedia.com and HISTORY.com. The Wyoming State Parks page for the Fort Bridger State Historic Site could not be fetched, so the National Park Service place page that describes the site is used. No State Historical Society of Missouri biography of Bridger was found (its Historic Missourians page returned not found). Alter's 1925 book is the J. Cecil Alter biography named in the brief; it was read directly rather than through quotations.
  2. Name. The display name is “Jim Bridger”; the alias is “James Bridger,” the name Whitman, Dodge and Alter use, which appears in no other record. The brief's “James Felix Bridger” is not used: none of the sources read gives him a middle name Felix (Felix was the name of his son), and the Wyoming Game and Fish Department page gives “James H.” instead. No other figure with a record in the dataset is named. “Washington” appears only in the name of the cemetery.
  3. Index age and dates. The index visit on April 7, 2026 maps to April 7, 1874, when he was 70 and living on his farm south of Westport, near Kansas City, in the year his eyesight was failing fastest. The synthetic date of birth is March 17, 1956 (shift 152 years). The visits on April 21, May 26, June 2, July 21, August 18 and September 29, 2026, and the operations on May 5 and June 16, 2026, map to the same days of 1874, all at age 70 and years before his death in 1881. Past medical history includes only events up to 1873; his near-total blindness by late 1874 or early 1875 (CLM-0012), his death and his reburial are on the timeline only and are left out of the intake documents.
  4. Corrections to the brief. (1) The brief names his wives Cora (Flathead) and Mary (Shoshone). The sources read do not name any of his wives; the Flathead, Ute and Shoshone wives are recorded without names, and Mary is recorded in Alter as the name of a daughter born in 1853, not of a wife. (2) The brief gives his death “near Kansas City”; the sources place it on his farm near Westport (WyoHistory) or a mile north of Dallas, not far south of Westport, in what is now Kansas City (Dodge, Alter). The record says “his farm near Westport, near Kansas City.” (3) Alter says his body was removed to Kansas City in 1904; the Wyoming State Historic Preservation Office names Mount Washington Cemetery in Independence, Missouri, which the claim uses.
  5. The arrowhead (CLM-0003, CLM-0004, D). Whitman's journal says the arrow was shot in nearly three years earlier; Alter and Parker say three years; the NPS handbook gives a three-inch iron arrowhead and the place, Horse Creek on the Green River. The sources do not say whether any anesthetic was given, so the record does not say; the roster's “without anesthesia” is left out. The sources do not say where in his back the arrowhead lay; the modern record puts an old scar on the right lower back as a synthetic choice so that the examination can describe it.
  6. His blindness (CLM-0010, CLM-0012, D). His failing sight from the late 1860s and near-blindness by 1874 or early 1875 are documented, but no source read records a cause, and none proposes one, so no retrospective (R) diagnosis is recorded. The modern episode's bilateral cataract is a synthetic choice suited to the teaching objective, linked to the documented loss of sight; it is not a claim that cataract caused his blindness, which in his lifetime would have had other possible causes. The modern episode shows a treatable cause of the same complaint.
  7. Goiter and rupture (CLM-0009, D). Alter records a goiter and a rupture. The type of goiter and its cause, and the side of the rupture, are not recorded. The modern multinodular goiter is a synthetic classification; the rupture appears only in the past medical history.
  8. Rheumatism (CLM-0008, D). The documented rheumatism and arthritis are anchors for the synthetic knee osteoarthritis; the old term does not name a modern diagnosis. The roster's “occupational polytrauma” and “arthritis severe” are not used as diagnoses.
  9. Reading (CLM-0007, D). An army officer's account says he could not read. The modern episode makes this a health-literacy point (instructions given aloud with teach-back, voice reminders for drops, consent read aloud), not a cognitive finding; his Mini-Cog is normal.
  10. Wives and children (CLM-0006, D). His three Native wives and his children are recorded plainly and with respect as documented history. The circumstances of his wives' deaths and of his children's lives are left out, beyond the years of two deaths needed for the order of the marriages, so that no health fact about any wife or child is recorded. No relative is named in the modern episode or given any statement, decision or health fact there; his daughter's letter is used only in a historical claim about his own sight. The advance care planning agent is unnamed and no relationship is given. The family history records only that his mother, a brother and his father died when he was young, “cause not recorded in the cited source.”
  11. Marcus Whitman. Whitman appears only in documented history claims (CLM-0004) and the past medical history, never in the modern episode.
  12. Clinical judgment calls. (1) Surgery indicated by functional impairment rather than an acuity threshold (AAO PPP, CMID). (2) Sequential surgery, worse (right) eye first and the left six weeks later; immediately sequential bilateral surgery discussed and declined by the patient. (3) Monofocal distance lenses; no toric lens with regular corneas. (4) Moxifloxacin for one week and a four-week prednisolone taper for each eye, with intracameral antibiotic at surgery; no NSAID drops. (5) No routine preoperative laboratory tests. (6) A lumbar X-ray at intake despite no red flags, for the specific question of a retained metal fragment at the old wound (relevant also to MRI safety); otherwise the ACP 2017 exercise-first plan, with no opioids or muscle relaxants and no oral NSAID. (7) Topical diclofenac for the knees rather than an oral NSAID at 70. (8) Thyroid ultrasound for a long-standing goiter; no biopsy of a 1.6 cm spongiform nodule; repeat ultrasound at about 18 months (ATA 2015). (9) No statin at the wellness visit: an intermediate risk but none of the USPSTF risk factors, by shared decision. (10) RSV vaccine not given at 70 without a risk condition.
  13. Synthetic clinical picture. Everything in the modern episode is synthetic: every result, the eye examination, OCT, biometry, the operations and their outcomes, the X-rays and ultrasound, the scores (PHQ-9, PHQ-2, Mini-Cog, AUDIT-C, pain scores, VF-14, Timed Up and Go), the medicines, the hearing loss and skin findings at the wellness visit, and the fall. Height (183 cm, from an account of him as “about six feet high” quoted by Alter) and weights are synthetic.
  14. Wellness visit. A subsequent annual wellness visit at 70 (HCPCS G0439 with CPT 99497 in plain text), with the health risk assessment read aloud. PCV20 and the first recombinant zoster dose given; influenza and COVID-19 vaccines due in the autumn; hepatitis C and B screening (negative at the next visit); PSA, AAA, lung cancer and osteoporosis screening not indicated; audiology and dermatology arranged.
  15. Sick visit. A right wrist sprain from a fall on the porch steps three weeks after the first cataract operation, chosen because it tests the plan: the mismatch between a newly clear eye and a still-clouded one upsets depth judgment, a known risk between operations. The X-ray with scaphoid views showed no fracture, the operated eye was unharmed, and the falls plan was tightened (a temporary spectacle change, a second handrail). It is not a historical claim.
  16. Focused visits. The four chronic-care follow-ups (two weeks with the consultation, four weeks after the first operation, five weeks after the second, and six months) are documented as focused visits on the CMID cataract monograph and the 2021 AAO Preferred Practice Pattern, the 2017 ACP low back pain guideline, the 2015 ATA guideline, the CMID osteoarthritis monograph with the 2019 ACR guideline, the USPSTF falls recommendation and the USPSTF depression recommendation. One deviation is recorded instead of an invented result: the VF-14 functional questionnaire was not given before the first operation because he cannot read it and it was not read aloud; no harm resulted, since the indication was clear from the interview; the corrective action was to read it aloud before the second operation (58 out of 100) and to read questionnaires aloud to patients who do not read. Synthetic findings introduced: the fall between operations and the wrist sprain.
  17. Consultation. The ophthalmology referral and the consultation by Ines Delacroix-Moreno, MD (a fictional consultant), on the day of the two-week visit, are S. The consultant answers the five referral questions in line with what the record shows afterwards: bilateral nuclear cataract with glaucoma, macular degeneration and diabetic retinopathy excluded; surgery for functional impairment, right eye first; monofocal distance lenses, the left eye six weeks later, same-day bilateral surgery discussed and declined; the drops and checks with voice reminders; and no driving until the standard is met, with extra care between operations.
