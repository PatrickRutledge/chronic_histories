Assessment

  • Cause of the vision loss (H25.13). Bilateral age-related nuclear cataract, dense on the right, fully explains the blurred vision, glare and faded colors, and the acuity matches the lens opacity. Glaucoma is excluded by normal pressures and healthy discs, macular degeneration by a normal fundus and macular OCT, and diabetic retinopathy by the absence of diabetes and of retinal signs. [S]
  • Indication and order of surgery (H25.13). Surgery is indicated because the cataracts limit what he needs to do (faces, steps at dusk, driving) despite the best glasses; the decision rests on his loss of function, not on an acuity number. The right eye, the worse, goes first. The VF-14 questionnaire was not given today because it was not read aloud to him; his limits were documented by interview and the questionnaire will be read to him before the second eye. [S]
  • Lens and timing (H25.13). Phacoemulsification with a monofocal lens implant aimed at distance in each eye, from today's biometry; his corneas are regular and need no toric lens; glasses for near and fine distance afterwards. The left eye about six weeks after the right, once the first eye has healed and its result is known. Immediately sequential bilateral surgery on the same day is an option the AAO describes, with fewer trips and quicker recovery, but with the risks of a problem in both eyes at once; after discussion he chose separate days. [S]
  • Drops, visits and managing without reading (H25.13). Intracameral antibiotic at surgery, then moxifloxacin four times a day for a week and prednisolone acetate four times a day tapering over four weeks. Checks at one day, one to two weeks and one month after each eye. Bottles have distinct caps; the schedule was demonstrated and recorded as voice reminders, and he repeated it back. Same-day contact for pain, redness or falling vision. Routine preoperative laboratory tests are not needed. [S]
  • Falls and driving (Z91.81). His vision is below the driving standard now; no driving until it is met after surgery. Between the operations the two eyes will see very differently, which upsets depth judgment on steps; extra care, good light and handrails are needed, and the right lens of his glasses can be changed after the first operation to reduce the mismatch. [S]
