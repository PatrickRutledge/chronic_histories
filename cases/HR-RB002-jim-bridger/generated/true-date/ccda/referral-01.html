Historical basis. These claims are historical evidence, not findings from a modern examination.

  • James Bridger was born on March 17, 1804, in Richmond, Virginia, the son of James and Chloe Bridger. The family moved west to Missouri when he was a boy, and his mother, a brother and his father died there within a few years, leaving him an orphan at about fourteen with a younger sister. [D]
  • After an apprenticeship as a blacksmith, he joined William Ashley's fur-trapping expedition to the Rocky Mountains in 1822. He spent more than four decades in the West as a trapper, a partner in a fur company, a trader, and from 1849 to 1868 a guide and scout for emigrants, surveyors and the Army. [D]
  • In a fight with Blackfeet men in 1832 he was struck in the back by two arrows; the iron head of one could not be removed and stayed deep in his back. [D]
  • In August 1835, at the fur traders' rendezvous on the Green River, the missionary physician Marcus Whitman removed the arrowhead from his back. A companion of Whitman's described an iron arrowhead three inches long, hooked at the point where it had struck a large bone and surrounded by tissue that had grown around it; the operation was difficult, and Bridger bore it steadily while trappers and Indians watched. [D]
  • In 1843 he and his partner Louis Vasquez founded Fort Bridger, a trading post for emigrants on Blacks Fork of the Green River in what is now southwestern Wyoming. [D]
  • He married three Native women in succession: the daughter of a Flathead chief, who died in 1846; a Ute woman, who died in 1849; and in 1850 a Shoshone woman. He had several children, some of whom he sent to school in Missouri. [D]
  • He could not read, but he enjoyed having books read aloud to him; an army officer's account from his scouting years describes him listening to Shakespeare's plays read until midnight. [D]
  • By his early sixties the effects of arthritis, rheumatism and rugged frontier life had caught up with him; in 1867 at Fort Phil Kearny he was troubled by rheumatism that hampered his movements, and in 1868 he moved back to his farm near Westport, Missouri. [D]
  • In his later years he had a goiter and a rupture (hernia), which troubled him somewhat; the cause of the goiter is not recorded. [D]
  • His eyesight began to fail in the late 1860s, and from 1873 it failed rapidly, so that he could recognize friends only by the sound of their voices. The cause of his failing sight is not recorded in the sources read. [D]

Plan of Treatment

  • Vision work-up at intake: visual acuity with and without pinhole, pupils, red reflex and a functional history (faces, reading signs, driving, glare, falls); HbA1c for diabetic retinopathy risk; urgent eye care for any sudden loss, pain or redness. Guideline: Cataract in the Adult Eye Preferred Practice Pattern (2021); CMID: Clinical Monitoring Intelligence Database, cataract and rheumatoid arthritis/osteoarthritis monographs (2026) [S]
  • Falls and low vision: brighter lighting on the porch and stairs, contrast tape on step edges, a second handrail, night lights; no driving until vision meets the state standard. Guideline: Cataract in the Adult Eye Preferred Practice Pattern (2021); Falls Prevention in Community-Dwelling Older Adults: Interventions (2024) [S]
  • Low back pain: lumbar X-ray at intake to look for a retained fragment at the old wound (and to clear any later MRI); reassurance and staying active; physical therapy with exercise and motor control training, then a home program, with heat; no opioids, no muscle relaxants; a short course of an oral anti-inflammatory only if exercise is not enough. Guideline: Noninvasive Treatments for Acute, Subacute, and Chronic Low Back Pain: A Clinical Practice Guideline From the American College of Physicians (2017) [S]
  • Goiter: TSH and free T4; thyroid ultrasound with the sonographic pattern of each nodule; fine-needle aspiration only for a nodule that meets size and pattern thresholds; follow-up ultrasound at an interval set by the findings; return for trouble swallowing or breathing, voice change or rapid growth. Guideline: 2015 American Thyroid Association Management Guidelines for Adult Patients with Thyroid Nodules and Differentiated Thyroid Cancer (2015) [S]
  • Knees: quadriceps strengthening, walking and tai chi; topical diclofenac; a stick in the outside hand on slopes. Guideline: 2019 American College of Rheumatology/Arthritis Foundation Guideline for the Management of Osteoarthritis of the Hand, Hip, and Knee (2019); CMID: Clinical Monitoring Intelligence Database, cataract and rheumatoid arthritis/osteoarthritis monographs (2026) [S]
