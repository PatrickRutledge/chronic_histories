# Teaching Note — Arthur Ashe pilot

> **SYNTHETIC RECORD, NOT A REAL MEDICAL RECORD**

## Why this case matters

This case shows how historical evidence can support a transparent fictional modern episode without presenting interpretation or generated content as documented fact.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish documented (**D**), retrospective (**R**), and synthetic (**S**) content.
2. Keep historical and active problems distinct.
3. Connect measurable goals and interventions to guidelines.
4. Preserve strengths, function, identity, and agency.

## Destigmatization message

A health condition is one part of a person’s life and does not erase achievement, relationships, purpose, or agency.

## Discussion questions

- Which facts support concern without establishing a modern diagnosis?
- Which entries belong in active problems, historical problems, or both?
- What additional evidence would change an evidence grade?
- How should an EHR distinguish synthetic and historical content?

## Evidence-grade summary

- **D:** Arthur Robert Ashe Jr. was born in Richmond, Virginia, on July 10, 1943, to Mattie Cunningham Ashe and Arthur Robert Ashe Sr.; his mother died before he was seven. On July 31, 1979, while teaching tennis to inner-city youth, Ashe had a heart attack, and he then underwent quadruple coronary artery bypass surgery in 1979. When chest pain recurred, Ashe had a second bypass operation, a double bypass, in 1983, during which he received a blood transfusion. In 1988 Ashe had difficulty moving his right hand and arm; a scan showed a mass in the left side of his brain, which surgeons removed, and the diagnosis was toxoplasmosis. Testing then showed that he had HIV and AIDS. Ashe and his doctors concluded that he had almost certainly acquired HIV from a blood transfusion during his 1983 heart operation, before blood was tested for the virus. Ashe kept his diagnosis private at first, telling only a few family members and friends. On April 8, 1992, after learning that USA Today planned to report it, he announced at a press conference that he had HIV. Both of Ashe's parents had atherosclerotic heart disease at early ages. His mother died at 27 of complications from surgery, and his father had two heart attacks, at 55 and at 59. Ashe died in New York City on February 6, 1993, aged 49, of AIDS-related pneumonia. His memoir, Days of Grace, was published after his death.
- **R:** None recorded.
- **S:** Shifted identity, fictional care team, modern encounters, results, plans, and outcomes.
