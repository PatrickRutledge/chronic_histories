## Assessment and plan

1. **Acute gout of the right big toe, a first flare, drug-induced** (M10.271). A typical first gout flare of the big toe (podagra), with sudden overnight onset, redness, swelling and maximal tenderness within a day, in a man taking a thiazide diuretic, which raises serum urate. No fever, no skin break, and a single typical joint make septic arthritis unlikely, so the joint was not aspirated; he will return at once if he develops fever or the joint worsens. Serum urate can be falsely normal during a flare, so it will be measured once the flare has settled. Low-dose colchicine is chosen: an oral NSAID would add to the bleeding risk of aspirin and raise blood pressure that is not yet at target. Atorvastatin with colchicine slightly raises the risk of muscle toxicity at higher colchicine doses; the short low-dose course is acceptable with a warning about muscle pain. **S**
