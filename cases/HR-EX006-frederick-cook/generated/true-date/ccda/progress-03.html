History of Present Illness

Mr. Cook is a 65-year-old man, released from prison on parole five months ago, with stage 2 hypertension and an asymptomatic stenosis of the right internal carotid artery, who comes for his Welcome to Medicare preventive examination now that his Medicare Part B has begun. Two weeks ago he started hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg, amlodipine 5 mg, atorvastatin 40 mg and aspirin 81 mg daily; he takes them from a weekly pill box and has missed no doses. He urinates more in the mornings, and has no dizziness, ankle swelling or muscle aches. His home readings this week average 142/86. He walks for an hour most days and is writing every morning. He has had no weakness, numbness, speech trouble or loss of vision. The screening tests from his first visit were reviewed with him: hepatitis C antibody nonreactive, the interferon-gamma release assay for tuberculosis negative, the HIV test nonreactive, and the hepatitis B panel negative for infection and for immunity.
