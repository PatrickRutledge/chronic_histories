Judgment calls (dossier editorial resolutions)

  1. Index age and whereabouts. The modern episode presents him at 65. The synthetic date of birth is June 10, 1961 (the true month and day), so the shift is 96 years. The index visit on July 7, 2026 maps to July 7, 1930, four months after Time reported his parole from Leavenworth (the week before March 17, 1930), and the last visit on October 27, 2026 maps to October 27, 1930. The brief placed him in New York after release; the Time report says he went to Chicago, and his whereabouts in July to October 1930 were not verified in a fetched source, so the record does not name a city for the modern episode. He was 64 at his parole and turned 65 in June 1930; Time gave his age as 65.
  2. Thin chart. No illness of his own before 1940 was found in a fetched source. The documented health facts are his father's death from pneumonia, his work as a physician (the Belgica's scurvy, the prison hospital), and the apoplexy of May 1940 and death from a cerebral hemorrhage in August 1940. The modern episode is built around a common, defensible pattern for an older man returning from long incarceration: blood pressure found high at the first visit and a carotid bruit, which lead to stage 2 hypertension and an asymptomatic moderate carotid stenosis. Both are synthetic; no source says he had them, and the episode makes no prediction about the stroke a decade later.
  3. Depression (roster lead). The roster lists depression and a "psychological burden." No fetched source documents depression or any mental-health diagnosis. It is left out of the claims. His own words calling imprisonment "the greatest tragedy of my life" are recorded (CLM-0009, sensitive) and anchor only the modern depression and shame screening. The synthetic PHQ-9 scores (7, then 4) are below 10, the PHQ-2 is 1, no behavioral-health diagnosis is made, and no safety assessment is required.
  4. Release, sentence and time served. The Ohio State finding aid and the American Oil & Gas Historical Society give a sentence of 14 years and 9 months in 1923; the Columbia Encyclopedia says he served five years (1925 to 1930); his own reckoning is six years and three months, including sixteen months in the county jail at Fort Worth. All are recorded. The brief's "imprisoned at Leavenworth until 1930" is supported; the release was a parole, not a discharge.
  5. Stroke and death. The brief's "died after a stroke" is supported: Time reports him critically ill of apoplexy at Port Chester in May 1940, and the Ohio State exhibit gives the cause of death as a cerebral hemorrhage. The death date (August 5, 1940) is from the Library of Congress authority record and the place (New Rochelle) from the Sullivan County Historical Society. Whether the May illness and the fatal hemorrhage were one event was not established, and no retrospective diagnosis (R) is recorded.
  6. Pardon. The pardon in 1940 is documented. The president who granted it has a record in the dataset; he is not named here, because the history does not need the name.
  7. Other figures named. Robert Peary (HR-EX004) is named in CLM-0003 and CLM-0006, as the leader of the 1891 to 1892 expedition and the rival claimant to the Pole, and Roald Amundsen (HR-EX003) in CLM-0004, as his Belgica shipmate and supporter; both are listed in identity.historical_mentions. No health fact or decision of either man is recorded. The Inuit companions and the Denali companion are named or mentioned only in the documented claims.
  8. Belgica. The Ohio State exhibit says he recommended fresh seal meat for the men's scurvy; it does not mention penguin meat or any illness of his own, so none is recorded.
  9. Aliases and names. The display name is "Frederick Cook"; the alias "Frederick Albert Cook" appears in no other record. "Dr. Frederick A. Cook" is not used as an alias.
  10. Choice of condition and treatment. A carotid bruit leads to duplex ultrasound; this is evaluation of a finding, not the screening of asymptomatic adults that the USPSTF recommends against. A 50 to 69 percent asymptomatic stenosis is managed with best medical therapy because the SVS 2021 guideline reserves endarterectomy for asymptomatic stenosis of 70 to 99 percent. Low-dose aspirin for asymptomatic stenosis of 50 percent or more is a clinical judgment in line with the ESVS 2023 guideline, whose full text was not read. Yearly duplex surveillance follows the CMID stroke monograph. Stage 2 hypertension is treated with two first-line drugs under the 2025 AHA/ACC guideline. The hydrochlorothiazide-induced gout flare and the switch to losartan follow the 2020 ACR guideline. ICD-10-CM I65.21 is used for the right-sided stenosis; the left plaque below 50 percent is described in the text. Height, weight and every result are synthetic.
  11. Preventive visit type. The brief calls for an annual wellness visit at 65 or older. At 65, in the first year of Medicare Part B, Medicare covers the once-in-a-lifetime initial preventive physical examination (Welcome to Medicare, G0402), not an annual wellness visit, so the preventive visit uses the visit type initial_preventive_exam with SNOMED CT 86013001. It meets the same documentation rules (review of systems, health risk assessment, prevention plan, advance care planning, same-day PHQ-2).
  12. Re-entry screening. Hepatitis C antibody, an interferon-gamma release assay and a hepatitis B panel are drawn at intake because of the incarceration; HIV is tested at 65 under the USPSTF age range. The hepatitis C and tuberculosis results are coded with their numeric LOINC measures; the HIV and hepatitis B panel results are qualitative and are given in the note text only.
  13. Social history. His imprisonment, prison hospital work, parole terms (including the fine taken from earnings), and memoir writing are documented. The rented room, benefits, parole officer, diet, and never smoking or drinking are synthetic and not claims about his real life. Whether he still held a medical license in 1930 was not checked; the record says only that he no longer practices.
  14. Sick and preventive visits. The initial preventive physical examination on August 4, 2026 (true frame August 4, 1930) and a same-day sick visit on September 8, 2026 (true frame September 8, 1930) were added. The sick visit is a first gout flare seven weeks after starting hydrochlorothiazide, chosen because it is a common adverse effect of a drug the episode started, it teaches low-dose colchicine with a statin and aspirin, and it leads to a guideline-based drug change; it does not echo a documented illness.
  15. Focused visits. The four chronic-care follow-ups (two, six, twelve and sixteen weeks) are documented as focused visits on the CMID hypertension, hyperlipidemia and stroke monographs, the 2025 AHA/ACC hypertension guideline, the carotid guidelines, and the ACR gout guideline. The CMID's echocardiogram at diagnosis of stage 2 hypertension is done (normal). The ankle-brachial index is recorded as due at the annual wellness visit. One protocol deviation is recorded: the potassium and creatinine check ordered for one to two weeks after starting losartan was missed and drawn at four weeks, with normal results, no harm, and a corrective action.
  16. Consultation. The vascular neurology consultation by the fictional Dr. Abernathy-Mensah on the second visit is synthetic. Its recommendations (no revascularization, atorvastatin 40 mg, aspirin 81 mg, blood pressure below 130/80, no brain imaging now, a yearly duplex, and the BE FAST warning signs) are those the later visits carry out; LDL fell 54 percent to 68 mg/dL.
