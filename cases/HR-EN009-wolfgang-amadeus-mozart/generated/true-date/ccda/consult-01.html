Assessment

  • Acute post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis (N00.9). A typical acute nephritic syndrome, with red cell casts, edema, hypertension, and a rise in creatinine, appearing after a latent period of two to three weeks. There is firm evidence of a recent group A streptococcal infection and a low C3 with a normal C4. That is the classic pattern, and it makes a kidney biopsy unnecessary now. IgA nephropathy usually causes dark urine within a day or two of an infection, not weeks later, and does not lower C3. IgA vasculitis would usually bring purpura. Lupus would usually lower C4 as well. ANCA vasculitis and anti-GBM disease would be expected to run a more aggressive course with a normal C3. Ibuprofen may have added a little to the rise in creatinine. The creatinine is still rising, but he is passing more urine, his potassium is acceptable, and he has no pulmonary edema or hypertensive emergency, so outpatient care is safe with close review. The antibiotics clear the infection but do not change the course of the nephritis. [S]
  • Joint pains after streptococcal infection: acute rheumatic fever considered. In a low-risk population the 1773 revised Jones criteria need two major manifestations, or one major and two minor, with evidence of a preceding streptococcal infection. He has polyarthralgia, one minor manifestation. His fever never reached 38.5 °C, and his ESR and CRP are below the minor thresholds of 60 mm/h and 3.0 mg/dL. There is no arthritis, chorea, erythema marginatum, or nodules. The PR interval is normal, and Doppler echocardiography shows no carditis. He has no major manifestation, so the criteria are not met. Rheumatic fever and post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis seldom occur together. No secondary prophylaxis is needed. [S]
