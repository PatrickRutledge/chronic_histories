Teaching objective: Primary care and nephrology for a 28-year-old composer and pianist with acute post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis: recognizing the acute nephritic syndrome (edema, cola-colored urine, hypertension, a rising creatinine, and red cell casts) two to three weeks after a sore throat; confirming a recent group A streptococcal infection and a low C3 with a normal C4; telling it apart from IgA nephropathy and IgA vasculitis, lupus, C3 glomerulopathy, and acute rheumatic fever, with the 1773 revised Jones criteria and an echocardiogram for the joint pains; outpatient supportive care with salt and fluid restriction, a loop diuretic, and blood pressure thresholds; antibiotics for the infection; avoiding NSAIDs; knowing when a kidney biopsy is needed; and following C3, creatinine, proteinuria, and hematuria until they recover. A sick visit for a cold teaches when not to test for strep, and the difference between synpharyngitic hematuria and a post-streptococcal relapse. Built on the KDIGO 1779 glomerular diseases guideline, the AHA 1773 Jones criteria statement, the IDSA 1770 streptococcal pharyngitis guideline, and the CMID chronic kidney disease and hypertension monographs.
