## Assessment

1. **Acute post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis** (N00.9). A typical acute nephritic syndrome, with red cell casts, edema, hypertension, and a rise in creatinine, appearing after a latent period of two to three weeks. There is firm evidence of a recent group A streptococcal infection and a low C3 with a normal C4. That is the classic pattern, and it makes a kidney biopsy unnecessary now. IgA nephropathy usually causes dark urine within a day or two of an infection, not weeks later, and does not lower C3. IgA vasculitis would usually bring purpura. Lupus would usually lower C4 as well. ANCA vasculitis and anti-GBM disease would be expected to run a more aggressive course with a normal C3. Ibuprofen may have added a little to the rise in creatinine. The creatinine is still rising, but he is passing more urine, his potassium is acceptable, and he has no pulmonary edema or hypertensive emergency, so outpatient care is safe with close review. The antibiotics clear the infection but do not change the course of the nephritis. **S**
2. **Joint pains after streptococcal infection: acute rheumatic fever considered**. In a low-risk population the 1773 revised Jones criteria need two major manifestations, or one major and two minor, with evidence of a preceding streptococcal infection. He has polyarthralgia, one minor manifestation. His fever never reached 38.5 °C, and his ESR and CRP are below the minor thresholds of 60 mm/h and 3.0 mg/dL. There is no arthritis, chorea, erythema marginatum, or nodules. The PR interval is normal, and Doppler echocardiography shows no carditis. He has no major manifestation, so the criteria are not met. Rheumatic fever and post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis seldom occur together. No secondary prophylaxis is needed. **S**

## Recommendations

1. Outpatient care with review in two weeks: continue furosemide 40 mg each morning until he is back to his usual weight, then stop it.
2. Sodium below 2 g a day and about 1.5 liters of fluid a day while he is edematous; daily weights.
3. Add amlodipine only if blood pressure stays at 140/90 or higher once he is euvolemic; same-day assessment for severe headache, vision change, confusion, breathlessness, or falling urine output.
4. Complete the ten-day course of penicillin V; household contacts need testing only if they have symptoms.
5. No NSAIDs while the kidneys recover; acetaminophen for pain.
6. Creatinine and potassium in about a week; then creatinine, potassium, C3, urinalysis, and urine protein-to-creatinine ratio at two weeks and at six to eight weeks; urinalysis and urine protein-to-creatinine ratio at six months and a year; blood pressure every year after.
7. No kidney biopsy now. Biopsy if C3 is still low after eight weeks, creatinine keeps rising beyond the first one to two weeks, nephrotic-range proteinuria persists beyond four to six weeks, or new systemic features or recurrent dark urine appear.
8. No rheumatic fever prophylaxis; the echocardiogram and ECG are normal and the Jones criteria are not met.
