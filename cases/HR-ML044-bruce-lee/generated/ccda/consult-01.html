Assessment

  • Seizure, not syncope; classification (R56.9). An epileptic seizure. The prodrome of heat and faintness could fit syncope, but a minute of rhythmic jerking, vomiting and 20 minutes of confusion do not: convulsive syncope causes brief, irregular jerks and a quick recovery. The ECG gives no cardiac cause. By the ILAE 2017 classification it is an unknown-onset motor (tonic-clonic) seizure, because its onset was not witnessed and the EEG shows no focal features. [S]
  • Evaluation (R56.9). Yes, both are indicated after a first seizure. The MRI with an epilepsy protocol shows no hippocampal sclerosis, malformation, tumor or scar, and the sleep-deprived EEG has no epileptiform discharges. [S]
  • Unprovoked; not epilepsy (R56.9). An acute symptomatic seizure requires a severe metabolic disturbance, intoxication or withdrawal, or an acute brain insult. His sodium, glucose and calcium were normal, and the cannabis did not amount to intoxication by his account; cannabis is not an established epileptogenic drug. Sleep loss, heat and missed meals lower the threshold but do not make the seizure provoked. This is a first unprovoked seizure. With a single seizure, a normal EEG and MRI, and no earlier brain insult, he does not meet the ILAE definition of epilepsy. [S]
  • Antiseizure medicine (R56.9). Not now, which he chose after hearing the evidence. Adults with a first unprovoked seizure have a 21% to 45% risk of another within two years, and his normal EEG and MRI, daytime seizure and lack of a prior brain insult put him at the lower end. Immediate treatment would lower the two-year risk but not the chance of long-term remission, and side effects affect 7% to 31% of people. A second unprovoked seizure, or an epileptiform EEG on any repeat study, would mean epilepsy and starting a medicine such as levetiracetam or lamotrigine. [S]
  • Driving, work and medicine safety (R56.9). Hong Kong's licence regulations list epilepsy and liability to sudden fainting among the conditions for which a licence must be refused or cancelled; I advise no driving for at least six months, as many licensing authorities require after a first unprovoked seizure, and then a review. No work at heights, falls or swimming alone until six months; sparring and training are fine once his back allows. No cannabis for now, which he chose. Drink to thirst with salt or an electrolyte drink in the heat, not large volumes of plain water. For his back, avoid tramadol and bupropion, which lower the seizure threshold, and carisoprodol or meprobamate, whose withdrawal can cause seizures; acetaminophen, short courses of an anti-inflammatory and exercise therapy are safe. [S]
