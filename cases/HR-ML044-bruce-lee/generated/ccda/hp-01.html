  • Bruce Jun Fan Lee was born on November 27, 1940, at the Jackson Street Hospital in San Francisco's Chinatown, while his parents were on tour with the Chinese opera; his parents named him Jun Fan, and a nurse at the hospital gave him the English name Bruce. At three months old he returned with his parents to Hong Kong, where he was raised until the age of 18. [D]
  • His Chinese name was 李振藩 (Lee Jun-fan in Cantonese romanization); as a child actor he took the stage name 李小龍 (Lee Siu-lung, "Little Dragon"). [D]
  • Raised in Hong Kong, he was a child actor in some 20 films; at 13 he began studying wing chun gung fu under Yip Man, and he was also an accomplished dancer. [D]
  • In 1959 he returned to the United States, settled in Seattle, studied philosophy at the University of Washington and began teaching gung fu, opening schools in Seattle, Oakland and Los Angeles; he developed his own martial art, Jeet Kune Do ("the way of the intercepting fist"), grounded in simplicity, directness and personal freedom. He is known for the words "Be water, my friend." [D]
  • In 1970, without warming up, he lifted a 125-pound barbell in a "good morning" exercise and severely injured his back; after many tests an injury to the fourth sacral nerve was found. He was ordered to complete bed rest, told he would never do gung fu again, and stayed in bed for about six months before beginning his own recovery program; the injury became a chronic source of pain for the rest of his life. [D]
  • He returned to Hong Kong to make films for Golden Harvest: The Big Boss (1971), Fist of Fury and The Way of the Dragon, which he wrote, directed and starred in; Enter the Dragon, the first Hong Kong and Hollywood co-production, was released after his death. [D]
  • On May 10, 1973, while dubbing lines for Enter the Dragon at Golden Harvest's Hammer Hill Road studios in Hong Kong, he felt faint, collapsed in the bathroom, was helped back to the dubbing studio, vomited and fainted again; he was taken to hospital in critical condition, where a neurosurgeon diagnosed cerebral oedema and gave mannitol to reduce the swelling. He recovered. [D]
  • His biographer Matthew Polly, as quoted in the Clinical Kidney Journal, wrote that on May 10 "his body began convulsing" after he lost consciousness; a seizure was considered a potential cause of the episode, but because it was a single episode, epilepsy was not diagnosed. [D]
  • The air conditioning in the dubbing room had been turned off to avoid sound problems in the recording, and he had high temperature, weakness, vomiting and loss of consciousness, according to his biographer. [D]
  • He had used cannabis (hashish) on the day of the May 10 collapse; the Hong Kong neurosurgeon who treated him thought it was a factor, an explanation he rejected. [D]
  • His biographer reported that some months before May 1973 he had the sweat glands removed from his armpits. [D]
  • He married in 1964; a son was born in 1965 and a daughter in 1969. His father, the Cantonese opera performer and actor Lee Hoi Chuen, died in Hong Kong in 1965, a week after the birth of his first grandchild. [D]
