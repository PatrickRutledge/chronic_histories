Teaching objective: Evaluation and management after a first convulsive seizure in a 32-year-old Chinese American martial artist, actor and film-maker in Hong Kong, anchored to his documented collapse with convulsions at a film studio two months before his death. The episode covers the emergency care that preceded the index visit (recorded as history); telling a seizure from convulsive syncope (witness history, ECG and QTc); sodium, glucose, calcium, kidney function and creatine kinase; MRI with an epilepsy protocol and a sleep-deprived EEG; classifying the event as an unknown-onset motor (tonic-clonic) seizure by the ILAE 1964 classification and deciding that a single unprovoked seizure with normal studies is not epilepsy; the AAN/AES 1962 guideline on whether to start an antiseizure medicine after a first unprovoked seizure (recurrence risk 21% to 45% over two years, lower with immediate treatment but with no long-term benefit), decided with him in favor of no medicine and a clear plan for a second seizure; triggers (sleep deprivation, heat, missed meals, fluid and electrolyte balance, cannabis); medicines that lower the seizure threshold; Hong Kong driving rules; seizure first aid and work safety for a stunt performer; and his documented chronic back pain managed with exercise and pain medicines that neither lower the seizure threshold nor interact. It is taught with culturally appropriate care: his languages, his philosophy of training, and his own decisions, without stereotype.
