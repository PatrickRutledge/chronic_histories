## Assessment and plan

1. **First unprovoked seizure** (R56.9). The prolonged confusion afterward, the minute of rhythmic jerking and the vomiting point to an epileptic seizure rather than convulsive syncope, in which jerks are brief and recovery is quick; the ECG was normal. By the ILAE 1964 classification it is an unknown-onset motor (tonic-clonic) seizure, because nobody saw how it began. No acute symptomatic cause was found: sodium, glucose and calcium were normal and he was not intoxicated or withdrawing from a drug, so sleep loss, heat, missed meals and cannabis are counted as triggers, not causes. MRI and EEG are normal, there was no earlier brain injury and the seizure happened while he was awake, so his risk of another seizure is at the lower end of the 21% to 45% over two years. He does not have epilepsy. Dr. Abernathy-Leung explained that starting a medicine now would lower the two-year risk but would not change the long-term outlook, and that side effects affect 7% to 31% of people. He chose not to start one, and both clinicians agree. **S**
