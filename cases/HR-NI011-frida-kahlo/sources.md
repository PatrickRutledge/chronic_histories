## Historical evidence

1. Museo Frida Kahlo. “Biography of Frida Kahlo” (Gerardo Ochoa Sandy; edited and updated by Ximena Jordan, 2020). Supports her birth on July 6, 1907, in the Blue House in Coyoacán; polio in 1913 at age six with a thinner, shorter right leg and outward-turned foot; the September 17, 1925 bus and streetcar collision and its injuries; beginning to paint in bed; lifelong chronic pain, many operations, and about 25 corsets; stopping her classes at La Esmeralda in 1944 because of limited mobility; her father’s epilepsy and death in 1941; her mother’s death in 1932; the 1950 hospitalization; the 1953 below-knee amputation; and her death at the Blue House on July 13, 1954, at 47. https://www.museofridakahlo.org.mx/wp/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Biografias-Frida-Kahlo-ingles.pdf
2. Courtney CA, O’Hearn MA, Franck CC. “Frida Kahlo: Portrait of Chronic Pain.” *Physical Therapy* 2017;97(1):90–96. Abstract reviewed. Supports polio at six, a trolley car accident in her teens, lifelong chronic pain, numerous failed spinal surgeries, and limb amputation, and interprets her pain as chronic neuropathic pain with central sensitization (**R**). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27562644/
3. Budrys V. “Neurological deficits in the life and works of Frida Kahlo.” *European Neurology* 2006;55(1):4–10. Abstract reviewed. Lists a congenital anomaly (spina bifida), poliomyelitis, spine injury, and neuropathic pain; the spina bifida proposal is graded **R** (possible). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16432301/
4. HISTMED Master Roster v3. Internal project source. Its clinical claims were provisional and were not treated as independent corroboration.

## Editorial resolutions

- **Roster correction.** The roster files Kahlo under “19th Century,” which does not fit a birth in 1907. The case uses “Early 20th Century.” Nationality is “Mexican.”
- **Index age.** The modern episode presents Kahlo at 37, her age in 1944, when limited mobility made her stop attending her classes at La Esmeralda. The index visit is September 17, 2026, the anniversary of the 1925 accident; the true-date frame places it on September 17, 1944. Past medical history and family history include only events before then. The 1950 hospitalization, the 1953 amputation, and her death remain in the historical timeline.
- **Modern diagnosis.** The documented injuries and chronic pain are **D**. The synthetic episode codes chronic pain due to trauma (G89.21), post-traumatic ankle osteoarthritis (M19.171), and long-term opioid therapy (Z79.891). The opioid history, doses, and taper are **S** and are not a statement about her historical treatment.
- **Contested cause of the leg weakness.** The contemporaneous diagnosis was polio, so the problem list records sequelae of poliomyelitis (B91) with verification provisional. The spina bifida proposal is **R** and stays off the active problem list.
- **Mood.** PHQ-9 scores stay below 10 with item 9 negative, so no depression diagnosis is coded and no structured safety assessment is required. The plan states that one will be done if the score reaches 10 or item 9 is positive.
- **Naloxone.** The case schema has no nasal route for medications, so naloxone nasal spray is recorded in the care plan and goals rather than the medication list.
- **Not used.** The museum biography mentions opioid use in her final year; this was not recorded, for safe-messaging reasons and because it postdates the index age. Her miscarriages, the details of the pelvic wound, and her marriage history are not recorded. Proposed fibromyalgia (Martínez-Lavín et al., 2000), post-polio syndrome (Nilsson, 2004), and cauda equina syndrome (Arslantas et al., 2024) have no reviewable abstract and are not cited. The year of *The Broken Column* was not verified from a logged source. Some popular sources give the polio year as 1914; the museum’s 1913 is used.
- Kahlo died in 1954, so the recent-death review rule does not apply. Ordinary clinical, provenance, and safe-messaging reviews remain required.
