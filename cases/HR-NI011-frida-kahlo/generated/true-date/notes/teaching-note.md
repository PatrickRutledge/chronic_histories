# Teaching Note — Frida Kahlo pilot

> **SYNTHETIC RECORD, NOT A REAL MEDICAL RECORD**

## Why this case matters

This case shows how historical evidence can support a transparent fictional modern episode without presenting interpretation or generated content as documented fact.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish documented (**D**), retrospective (**R**), and synthetic (**S**) content.
2. Keep historical and active problems distinct.
3. Connect measurable goals and interventions to guidelines.
4. Preserve strengths, function, identity, and agency.

## Destigmatization message

A health condition is one part of a person’s life and does not erase achievement, relationships, purpose, or agency.

## Discussion questions

- Which facts support concern without establishing a modern diagnosis?
- Which entries belong in active problems, historical problems, or both?
- What additional evidence would change an evidence grade?
- How should an EHR distinguish synthetic and historical content?

## Evidence-grade summary

- **D:** Magdalena Carmen Frida Kahlo Calderón was born on July 6, 1907, in her parents' house in Coyoacán, Mexico City, now known as the Blue House, the daughter of Guillermo Kahlo and Matilde Calderón. Frida Kahlo died at the Blue House in Mexico City on July 13, 1954, at the age of 47. In 1913, at the age of six, Kahlo fell ill with polio; afterward her right leg was thinner, slightly shorter, and less developed, with the foot turned outward, and her father encouraged cycling and swimming. On September 17, 1925, at 18, Kahlo was riding a bus that was struck by a streetcar; a metal handrail impaled her through the hip and fractured her pelvis, and she had three fractures of the spinal column, a fractured collarbone, two rib fractures, a dislocated right shoulder, and eleven fractures of the right leg with a dislocated foot. While immobilized for several months after the accident, Kahlo began to paint, using an easel adapted for painting in bed. Kahlo lived with chronic pain for the rest of her life, underwent many operations, some with long convalescences and serious consequences, and used about 25 different corsets. In 1944, because of increasingly limited mobility, Kahlo had to stop attending her classes at the La Esmeralda school of painting and sculpture, and some students continued to work with her at the Blue House. In 1950 Kahlo spent nearly the whole year in hospital with an infection after a graft to her spinal column. In 1953 Kahlo's right leg was amputated below the knee because of the threat of gangrene. Kahlo's father, Guillermo Kahlo, had epilepsy and died in 1941; her mother, Matilde Calderón, died in 1932.
- **R:** A neurologist's retrospective review proposes that Kahlo also had a congenital spinal anomaly (spina bifida). Her pain has been interpreted retrospectively as including chronic neuropathic pain and central sensitization.
- **S:** Shifted identity, fictional care team, modern encounters, results, plans, and outcomes.
