## Editorial resolutions

- **Roster correction.** The roster phrase “grief (3 of 4 sons died)” is not retained as written. Two sons, Eddie (1850) and Willie (1862), died during Lincoln’s lifetime and are sourced. The roster’s third death is not used, because no logged source supports it.
- **Index age.** The modern episode illustrates Lincoln at 32, the age of his best-documented depressive episode in January 1841. Past medical history and family history therefore include only events before 1841. Later events remain in the historical timeline.
- **Contested diagnoses.** Chronic depression, Marfan syndrome or another marfanoid disorder, and the 1863 smallpox interpretation are graded **R**. Marfan syndrome is not placed on the active problem list.
- **Treatment choice.** The 2022 VA/DoD guideline recommends psychotherapy or pharmacotherapy as monotherapy for uncomplicated MDD, and combination treatment for severe, persistent, or recurrent MDD. The only possible earlier episode (1835) is disputed and is not logged, so the episode is treated as uncomplicated. The synthetic plan is sertraline monotherapy chosen through shared decision-making.
- **Safe messaging.** No historical self-harm claim is recorded. The safety assessment is synthetic and records no current ideation. The assassination is stated as a fact without graphic detail.
- **Not used.** The 1835 episode after Ann Rutledge’s death, and accounts of friends’ concern for his safety in 1841, rest on later recollections that were not verified for this pilot. *The Atlantic* (Shenk, 2005) could not be retrieved and is not cited.
- **Review reopened.** On November 3, 2026 the owner asked for the pilot to be brought up to the batch standard. Release status returned to draft, and the clinical and safe-messaging reviews were reopened; the November 2 approval covered only the earlier content. Provenance review and claim release statuses are unchanged, because no historical claim was added or altered.
- **Focused visits.** The six-week (ENC-002) and twelve-week (ENC-003) follow-ups are documented as focused chronic-care visits on the CMID depression monograph, with the VA/DoD MDD and suicide-risk guidelines: PHQ-9 at least monthly in acute treatment and every two to four weeks in continuation, suicide-risk reassessment at every visit (C-SSRS Since Last Visit and PHQ-9 item 9) with review of the existing collaborative safety plan, adherence and adverse-effect review, the SSRI tolerability check at two weeks, a bipolar screen at the initial workup, weight and blood pressure, and the mental status examination. Lithium, anticonvulsant, antipsychotic, and AIMS monitoring are recorded as not indicated. Synthetic findings introduced: sleep, concentration, and activity detail consistent with the goal outcomes; about one missed dose every two weeks (consistent with the six-of-seven-days goal being met); PHQ-9 item 9 scored 0 at both visits, consistent with the safety assessment; no sexual or other adverse effects beyond the recorded early nausea; evening walks with an unnamed law colleague. The dose stayed at 50 mg at the partial response, as the record shows, with an increase planned only if the score was not below 5 at twelve weeks. Protocol deviations recorded honestly, without invented results: (1) no SSRI tolerability check at two weeks (no contact between intake and six weeks; no harm, nausea resolved by week three); (2) no PHQ-9 at four weeks; (3) the interim nurse telephone PHQ-9 planned at six weeks was not made, leaving seven weeks unmeasured before the twelve-week review (no harm; continuation-phase PHQ-9s then booked, the first at the preventive visit); (4) no Mood Disorder Questionnaire at intake, covered by the psychiatric interview at the consultation, which found no bipolar features.

The whole-year shift is 185 years, so the true-date frame places them on October 9 and October 27, 1841, when Lincoln was 32, after the documented January 1841 letter and long before any later event in the timeline; no documented event is contradicted.
